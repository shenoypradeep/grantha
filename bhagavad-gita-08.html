अष्टमोऽध्यायः
Aṣṭamo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 8 · Akṣarabrahmayoga
अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
किं तद्ब्रह्म किमध्यात्मं किं कर्म पुरुषोत्तम । अधिभूतं च किं प्रोक्तमधिदैवं किमुच्यते ॥ १ ॥
kiṃ tadbrahma kimadhyātmaṃ kiṃ karma puruṣottama | adhibhūtaṃ ca kiṃ proktam adhidaivaṃ kimucyate || 1 ||
Padaccheda
किम् तत् ब्रह्म किम् अध्यात्मम् किम् कर्म पुरुषोत्तम अधिभूतम् च किम् प्रोक्तम् अधिदैवम् किम् उच्यते
kim tat brahma kim adhyātmam kim karma puruṣottama adhibhūtam ca kim proktam adhidaivam kim ucyate
Word by word
Arjuna said: What is that Brahman? What is it that concerns the Self? What is action, O supreme Puruṣa? What is said to be that which concerns beings, and what is called that which concerns the gods?

Note Śaṅkara: the Lord gave Arjuna the seeds of these questions at the end of the last adhyāya (7.29–30). He has no separate comment on verses 1 and 2.

अधियज्ञः कथं कोऽत्र देहेऽस्मिन्मधुसूदन । प्रयाणकाले च कथं ज्ञेयोऽसि नियतात्मभिः ॥ २ ॥
adhiyajñaḥ kathaṃ ko 'tra dehe 'sminmadhusūdana | prayāṇakāle ca kathaṃ jñeyo 'si niyatātmabhiḥ || 2 ||
Padaccheda
अधियज्ञः कथम् कः अत्र देहे अस्मिन् मधुसूदन प्रयाण-काले च कथम् ज्ञेयः असि नियत-आत्मभिः
adhiyajñaḥ katham kaḥ atra dehe asmin madhusūdana prayāṇa-kāle ca katham jñeyaḥ asi niyata-ātmabhiḥ
Word by word
Who is it that concerns sacrifice here in this body, and how, O slayer of Madhu? And how are you to be known at the time of departure by those of controlled mind?
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
अक्षरं ब्रह्म परमं स्वभावोऽध्यात्ममुच्यते । भूतभावोद्भवकरो विसर्गः कर्मसंज्ञितः ॥ ३ ॥
akṣaraṃ brahma paramaṃ svabhāvo 'dhyātmamucyate | bhūtabhāvodbhavakaro visargaḥ karmasaṃjñitaḥ || 3 ||
Padaccheda
अक्षरम् ब्रह्म परमम् स्वभावः अध्यात्मम् उच्यते भूत-भाव-उद्भव-करः विसर्गः कर्म-संज्ञितः
akṣaram brahma paramam svabhāvaḥ adhyātmam ucyate bhūta-bhāva-udbhava-karaḥ visargaḥ karma-saṃjñitaḥ
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: Brahman is the supreme imperishable; its own being is called what concerns the Self; the offering that brings about the arising of beings is named action.

Note Śaṅkara: the imperishable is the supreme Self, as in the Veda (‘at the command of this imperishable, O Gārgī’); the syllable Om is not meant, since ‘supreme’ fits Brahman better. svabhāva is the being of that same supreme Brahman as the inward Self in each body: the reality that, taking the body as its seat, appears as the inward Self and ends in the supreme Brahman. visarga is the giving up of an oblation of rice or cake to a deity: sacrifice. From it, as from a seed, beings moving and unmoving arise by way of rain and the rest.

अधिभूतं क्षरो भावः पुरुषश्चाधिदैवतम् । अधियज्ञोऽहमेवात्र देहे देहभृतां वर ॥ ४ ॥
adhibhūtaṃ kṣaro bhāvaḥ puruṣaścādhidaivatam | adhiyajño 'hamevātra dehe dehabhṛtāṃ vara || 4 ||
Padaccheda
अधिभूतम् क्षरः भावः पुरुषः च अधिदैवतम् अधियज्ञः अहम् एव अत्र देहे देहभृताम् वर
adhibhūtam kṣaraḥ bhāvaḥ puruṣaḥ ca adhidaivatam adhiyajñaḥ aham eva atra dehe dehabhṛtām vara
Word by word
What concerns beings is perishable existence; the Puruṣa is what concerns the gods; and I myself am what concerns sacrifice, here in the body, O best of the embodied.

Note Śaṅkara: perishable existence is whatever has a birth. The Puruṣa, so called because all is filled by him or because he lies in the city of the body, is Hiraṇyagarbha within the sun, who sustains the organs of all living beings. adhiyajña is the deity called Viṣṇu who presides over all sacrifices, for the Veda says ‘sacrifice is Viṣṇu’; and that Viṣṇu is the Lord himself. Sacrifice is performed by means of the body and so has the body as its seat.

अन्तकाले च मामेव स्मरन्मुक्त्वा कलेवरम् । यः प्रयाति स मद्भावं याति नास्त्यत्र संशयः ॥ ५ ॥
antakāle ca māmeva smaranmuktvā kalevaram | yaḥ prayāti sa madbhāvaṃ yāti nāstyatra saṃśayaḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
अन्त-काले च माम् एव स्मरन् मुक्त्वा कलेवरम् यः प्रयाति सः मद्-भावम् याति न अस्ति अत्र संशयः
anta-kāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram yaḥ prayāti saḥ mad-bhāvam yāti na asti atra saṃśayaḥ
Word by word
And he who departs, leaving the body at the time of death, remembering me alone, goes to my being; there is no doubt about this.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘me’ is the supreme Lord, Viṣṇu; ‘my being’ is the reality of Viṣṇu.

यं यं वापि स्मरन्भावं त्यजत्यन्ते कलेवरम् । तं तमेवैति कौन्तेय सदा तद्भावभावितः ॥ ६ ॥
yaṃ yaṃ vāpi smaranbhāvaṃ tyajatyante kalevaram | taṃ tamevaiti kaunteya sadā tadbhāvabhāvitaḥ || 6 ||
Padaccheda
यम् यम् वा अपि स्मरन् भावम् त्यजति अन्ते कलेवरम् तम् तम् एव एति कौन्तेय सदा तद्-भाव-भावितः
yam yam vā api smaran bhāvam tyajati ante kalevaram tam tam eva eti kaunteya sadā tad-bhāva-bhāvitaḥ
Word by word
Or whatever being one remembers when leaving the body at the end, to that alone he goes, O son of Kuntī, having always dwelt on that being.

Note Śaṅkara: the rule does not hold for the Lord only. The being is some particular deity. He goes to it because he has made it familiar by constantly calling it to mind.

तस्मात्सर्वेषु कालेषु मामनुस्मर युध्य च । मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्मामेवैष्यस्यसंशयः ॥ ७ ॥
tasmātsarveṣu kāleṣu māmanusmara yudhya ca | mayyarpitamanobuddhirmāmevaiṣyasyasaṃśayaḥ || 7 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मात् सर्वेषु कालेषु माम् अनुस्मर युध्य च मयि अर्पित-मनः-बुद्धिः माम् एव एष्यसि असंशयः
tasmāt sarveṣu kāleṣu mām anusmara yudhya ca mayi arpita-manaḥ-buddhiḥ mām eva eṣyasi asaṃśayaḥ
Word by word
Therefore remember me at all times, and fight. With mind and understanding given over to me, you will come to me alone, without doubt.

Note Śaṅkara: because the last thought is what brings about the next body. He is to remember as scripture teaches, and to fight, which is his own dharma. ‘Me’ is Vāsudeva.

अभ्यासयोगयुक्तेन चेतसा नान्यगामिना । परमं पुरुषं दिव्यं याति पार्थानुचिन्तयन् ॥ ८ ॥
abhyāsayogayuktena cetasā nānyagāminā | paramaṃ puruṣaṃ divyaṃ yāti pārthānucintayan || 8 ||
Padaccheda
अभ्यास-योग-युक्तेन चेतसा न-अन्य-गामिना परमम् पुरुषम् दिव्यम् याति पार्थ अनुचिन्तयन्
abhyāsa-yoga-yuktena cetasā na-anya-gāminā paramam puruṣam divyam yāti pārtha anucintayan
Word by word
With a mind engaged in the yoga of practice, that goes to nothing else, meditating, one goes to the supreme divine Puruṣa, O Pārtha.

Note Śaṅkara: practice is the repetition of the same thought about the one object, the Lord, on whom the mind is fixed, with no thought of another kind coming between; that practice itself is the yoga. divya: dwelling in heaven, in the orb of the sun. Meditating: following in thought what scripture and the teacher have taught.

कविं पुराणमनुशासितारमणोरणीयांसमनुस्मरेद्यः । सर्वस्य धातारमचिन्त्यरूपमादित्यवर्णं तमसः परस्तात् ॥ ९ ॥
kaviṃ purāṇamanuśāsitāramaṇoraṇīyāṃsamanusmaredyaḥ | sarvasya dhātāramacintyarūpamādityavarṇaṃ tamasaḥ parastāt || 9 ||
Padaccheda
कविम् पुराणम् अनुशासितारम् अणोः अणीयांसम् अनुस्मरेत् यः सर्वस्य धातारम् अचिन्त्य-रूपम् आदित्य-वर्णम् तमसः परस्तात्
kavim purāṇam anuśāsitāram aṇoḥ aṇīyāṃsam anusmaret yaḥ sarvasya dhātāram acintya-rūpam āditya-varṇam tamasaḥ parastāt
Word by word
He who meditates on the all-knowing seer, the ancient, the ruler, subtler than the subtle, the dispenser of all, of form beyond thought, shining like the sun, beyond darkness;

Note Śaṅkara: this describes the Puruṣa he goes to. kavi: all-knowing. The ruler of the whole world. He dispenses the fruits of action, in all their variety, to living beings. His form, though it exists, cannot be conceived by anyone. Like the sun's, his radiance is the light of eternal consciousness. The darkness is the delusion that is ignorance. The verse is construed with ‘goes’ in verse 8.

प्रयाणकाले मनसाचलेन भक्त्या युक्तो योगबलेन चैव । भ्रुवोर्मध्ये प्राणमावेश्य सम्यक्स तं परं पुरुषमुपैति दिव्यम् ॥ १० ॥
prayāṇakāle manasācalena bhaktyā yukto yogabalena caiva | bhruvormadhye prāṇamāveśya samyaksa taṃ paraṃ puruṣamupaiti divyam || 10 ||
Padaccheda
प्रयाण-काले मनसा अचलेन भक्त्या युक्तः योग-बलेन च एव भ्रुवोः मध्ये प्राणम् आवेश्य सम्यक् सः तम् परम् पुरुषम् उपैति दिव्यम्
prayāṇa-kāle manasā acalena bhaktyā yuktaḥ yoga-balena ca eva bhruvoḥ madhye prāṇam āveśya samyak saḥ tam param puruṣam upaiti divyam
Word by word
at the time of departure, with an unmoving mind, endowed with devotion and with the strength of yoga, having rightly placed the life-breath between the eyebrows, he reaches that supreme divine Puruṣa.

Note Śaṅkara: the strength of yoga is the steadiness of mind that comes from the store of impressions left by absorption. First the mind is mastered in the lotus of the heart; then, by the channel that goes upward, mastering stage after stage, the breath is placed between the eyebrows. divya here: of the nature of light.

यदक्षरं वेदविदो वदन्ति विशन्ति यद्यतयो वीतरागाः । यदिच्छन्तो ब्रह्मचर्यं चरन्ति तत्ते पदं संग्रहेण प्रवक्ष्ये ॥ ११ ॥
yadakṣaraṃ vedavido vadanti viśanti yadyatayo vītarāgāḥ | yadicchanto brahmacaryaṃ caranti tatte padaṃ saṃgraheṇa pravakṣye || 11 ||
Padaccheda
यत् अक्षरम् वेद-विदः वदन्ति विशन्ति यत् यतयः वीत-रागाः यत् इच्छन्तः ब्रह्मचर्यम् चरन्ति तत् ते पदम् संग्रहेण प्रवक्ष्ये
yat akṣaram veda-vidaḥ vadanti viśanti yat yatayaḥ vīta-rāgāḥ yat icchantaḥ brahmacaryam caranti tat te padam saṃgraheṇa pravakṣye
Word by word
That imperishable of which the knowers of the Veda speak, which the ascetics free from attachment enter, and desiring which they lead the life of a celibate student: that goal I shall declare to you in brief.

Note Śaṅkara: the knowers of the Veda speak of it by denying every attribute: ‘not gross, not subtle’ and so on. The ascetics are the renouncers; they enter it when right vision is gained. ‘Desiring’: to know it. The verses that follow teach the worship of Om, as the name or symbol of the supreme Brahman, together with the fixing of the mind in yoga, for those of dull and middling understanding; its fruit is liberation after a time. He rests this on the Praśna Upaniṣad (5.1–5: Om is the higher and the lower Brahman; he who meditates on the supreme Puruṣa with the three-measured Om is led to the world of Brahmā) and the Kaṭha (1.2.14–15: ‘that goal which all the Vedas declare … is Om’); the same worship is to be told here of the Brahman set forth in 8.9 and in this verse, with whatever follows from it.

सर्वद्वाराणि संयम्य मनो हृदि निरुध्य च । मूर्ध्न्याधायात्मनः प्राणमास्थितो योगधारणाम् ॥ १२ ॥
sarvadvārāṇi saṃyamya mano hṛdi nirudhya ca | mūrdhnyādhāyātmanaḥ prāṇamāsthito yogadhāraṇām || 12 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-द्वाराणि संयम्य मनः हृदि निरुध्य च मूर्ध्नि आधाय आत्मनः प्राणम् आस्थितः योग-धारणाम्
sarva-dvārāṇi saṃyamya manaḥ hṛdi nirudhya ca mūrdhni ādhāya ātmanaḥ prāṇam āsthitaḥ yoga-dhāraṇām
Word by word
Having closed all the gates, confined the mind in the heart, and placed his own life-breath in the head, engaged in the fixing of the mind in yoga,

Note Śaṅkara: the gates are the gates of perception. The mind is brought to stillness in the lotus of the heart; with the mind so mastered he rises by the channel that goes upward from the heart.

ओमित्येकाक्षरं ब्रह्म व्याहरन्मामनुस्मरन् । यः प्रयाति त्यजन्देहं स याति परमां गतिम् ॥ १३ ॥
omityekākṣaraṃ brahma vyāharanmāmanusmaran | yaḥ prayāti tyajandehaṃ sa yāti paramāṃ gatim || 13 ||
Padaccheda
ॐ इति एक-अक्षरम् ब्रह्म व्याहरन् माम् अनुस्मरन् यः प्रयाति त्यजन् देहम् सः याति परमाम् गतिम्
om iti eka-akṣaram brahma vyāharan mām anusmaran yaḥ prayāti tyajan deham saḥ yāti paramām gatim
Word by word
uttering Om, the one syllable that is Brahman, and remembering me, he who departs, leaving the body, goes to the supreme goal.

Note Śaṅkara: Om is the name of Brahman; ‘me’ is the Lord, its meaning. ‘Leaving the body’ qualifies the departure: the self departs by leaving the body, not by any destruction of its own nature.

अनन्यचेताः सततं यो मां स्मरति नित्यशः । तस्याहं सुलभः पार्थ नित्ययुक्तस्य योगिनः ॥ १४ ॥
ananyacetāḥ satataṃ yo māṃ smarati nityaśaḥ | tasyāhaṃ sulabhaḥ pārtha nityayuktasya yoginaḥ || 14 ||
Padaccheda
अन्-अन्य-चेताः सततम् यः माम् स्मरति नित्यशः तस्य अहम् सुलभः पार्थ नित्य-युक्तस्य योगिनः
an-anya-cetāḥ satatam yaḥ mām smarati nityaśaḥ tasya aham sulabhaḥ pārtha nitya-yuktasya yoginaḥ
Word by word
He who remembers me constantly and all his life, with mind on nothing else: for that ever-collected yogī I am easy to attain, O Pārtha.

Note Śaṅkara: satatam means without a break, nityaśaḥ for a long time: not for six months or a year, but without a break as long as he lives.

मामुपेत्य पुनर्जन्म दुःखालयमशाश्वतम् । नाप्नुवन्ति महात्मानः संसिद्धिं परमां गताः ॥ १५ ॥
māmupetya punarjanma duḥkhālayamaśāśvatam | nāpnuvanti mahātmānaḥ saṃsiddhiṃ paramāṃ gatāḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
माम् उपेत्य पुनर्जन्म दुःख-आलयम् अशाश्वतम् न आप्नुवन्ति महात्मानः संसिद्धिम् परमाम् गताः
mām upetya punarjanma duḥkha-ālayam aśāśvatam na āpnuvanti mahātmānaḥ saṃsiddhim paramām gatāḥ
Word by word
Having reached me, the great souls, who have attained the highest perfection, do not undergo rebirth, the impermanent abode of sorrow.

Note Śaṅkara: this is what comes of his being easy to attain. Birth is the abode of sorrows of every kind. The great souls are the ascetics; the highest perfection is liberation. Those who do not reach the Lord return.

आ ब्रह्मभुवनाल्लोकाः पुनरावर्तिनोऽर्जुन । मामुपेत्य तु कौन्तेय पुनर्जन्म न विद्यते ॥ १६ ॥
ā brahmabhuvanāllokāḥ punarāvartino 'rjuna | māmupetya tu kaunteya punarjanma na vidyate || 16 ||
Padaccheda
आ ब्रह्म-भुवनात् लोकाः पुनरावर्तिनः अर्जुन माम् उपेत्य तु कौन्तेय पुनर्जन्म न विद्यते
ā brahma-bhuvanāt lokāḥ punarāvartinaḥ arjuna mām upetya tu kaunteya punarjanma na vidyate
Word by word
The worlds, up to the world of Brahmā, are subject to return, O Arjuna; but on reaching me, O son of Kuntī, there is no rebirth.

Note Śaṅkara: up to and including the world of Brahmā. They are subject to return because they are limited by time, as the next verse shows.

सहस्रयुगपर्यन्तमहर्यद्ब्रह्मणो विदुः । रात्रिं युगसहस्रान्तां तेऽहोरात्रविदो जनाः ॥ १७ ॥
sahasrayugaparyantamaharyadbrahmaṇo viduḥ | rātriṃ yugasahasrāntāṃ te 'horātravido janāḥ || 17 ||
Padaccheda
सहस्र-युग-पर्यन्तम् अहः यत् ब्रह्मणः विदुः रात्रिम् युग-सहस्र-अन्ताम् ते अहर्-रात्र-विदः जनाः
sahasra-yuga-paryantam ahaḥ yat brahmaṇaḥ viduḥ rātrim yuga-sahasra-antām te ahar-rātra-vidaḥ janāḥ
Word by word
Those who know that the day of Brahmā lasts a thousand ages, and that his night ends after a thousand ages, are the people who know day and night.

Note Śaṅkara: Brahmā is Prajāpati, Virāj. Those who know day and night are those who know the reckoning of time.

अव्यक्ताद्व्यक्तयः सर्वाः प्रभवन्त्यहरागमे । रात्र्यागमे प्रलीयन्ते तत्रैवाव्यक्तसंज्ञके ॥ १८ ॥
avyaktādvyaktayaḥ sarvāḥ prabhavantyaharāgame | rātryāgame pralīyante tatraivāvyaktasaṃjñake || 18 ||
Padaccheda
अव्यक्तात् व्यक्तयः सर्वाः प्रभवन्ति अहर्-आगमे रात्रि-आगमे प्रलीयन्ते तत्र एव अव्यक्त-संज्ञके
avyaktāt vyaktayaḥ sarvāḥ prabhavanti ahar-āgame rātri-āgame pralīyante tatra eva avyakta-saṃjñake
Word by word
At the coming of day all manifest things arise from the unmanifest; at the coming of night they dissolve into that same which is called the unmanifest.

Note Śaṅkara: the unmanifest here is Prajāpati's state of sleep. The manifest things are all creatures, moving and unmoving. Day is his waking, night his sleep.

भूतग्रामः स एवायं भूत्वा भूत्वा प्रलीयते । रात्र्यागमेऽवशः पार्थ प्रभवत्यहरागमे ॥ १९ ॥
bhūtagrāmaḥ sa evāyaṃ bhūtvā bhūtvā pralīyate | rātryāgame 'vaśaḥ pārtha prabhavatyaharāgame || 19 ||
Padaccheda
भूत-ग्रामः सः एव अयम् भूत्वा भूत्वा प्रलीयते रात्रि-आगमे अवशः पार्थ प्रभवति अहर्-आगमे
bhūta-grāmaḥ saḥ eva ayam bhūtvā bhūtvā pralīyate rātri-āgame avaśaḥ pārtha prabhavati ahar-āgame
Word by word
This same multitude of beings, coming to be again and again, dissolves helplessly at the coming of night, O Pārtha, and arises at the coming of day.

Note Śaṅkara: the same that existed in the former age, and no other. This is said so that no one should meet with the fruit of what he has not done or lose the fruit of what he has done, so that the teaching about bondage and liberation should have its purpose, and to produce dispassion towards the cycle of birth and death, in which beings, under the sway of action rooted in ignorance, arise and dissolve without freedom.

परस्तस्मात्तु भावोऽन्योऽव्यक्तोऽव्यक्तात्सनातनः । यः स सर्वेषु भूतेषु नश्यत्सु न विनश्यति ॥ २० ॥
parastasmāttu bhāvo 'nyo 'vyakto 'vyaktātsanātanaḥ | yaḥ sa sarveṣu bhūteṣu naśyatsu na vinaśyati || 20 ||
Padaccheda
परः तस्मात् तु भावः अन्यः अव्यक्तः अव्यक्तात् सनातनः यः सः सर्वेषु भूतेषु नश्यत्सु न विनश्यति
paraḥ tasmāt tu bhāvaḥ anyaḥ avyaktaḥ avyaktāt sanātanaḥ yaḥ saḥ sarveṣu bhūteṣu naśyatsu na vinaśyati
Word by word
But beyond that unmanifest there is another being, unmanifest and eternal, which does not perish when all beings perish.

Note Śaṅkara: the nature of the imperishable, to be reached by this path of yoga, is now stated. This being is the supreme Brahman called the imperishable. It is distinct from, and quite unlike, the unmanifest named before, which is ignorance, the seed of the multitude of beings. It is itself unmanifest in that it is beyond the reach of the senses. ‘All beings’: Brahmā and the rest.

अव्यक्तोऽक्षर इत्युक्तस्तमाहुः परमां गतिम् । यं प्राप्य न निवर्तन्ते तद्धाम परमं मम ॥ २१ ॥
avyakto 'kṣara ityuktas tamāhuḥ paramāṃ gatim | yaṃ prāpya na nivartante taddhāma paramaṃ mama || 21 ||
Padaccheda
अव्यक्तः अक्षरः इति उक्तः तम् आहुः परमाम् गतिम् यम् प्राप्य न निवर्तन्ते तत् धाम परमम् मम
avyaktaḥ akṣaraḥ iti uktaḥ tam āhuḥ paramām gatim yam prāpya na nivartante tat dhāma paramam mama
Word by word
It is called the unmanifest, the imperishable; they call it the supreme goal. That which, once reached, they do not return from is my supreme abode.

Note Śaṅkara: they do not return to the cycle of birth and death. It is the highest state of Viṣṇu.

पुरुषः स परः पार्थ भक्त्या लभ्यस्त्वनन्यया । यस्यान्तःस्थानि भूतानि येन सर्वमिदं ततम् ॥ २२ ॥
puruṣaḥ sa paraḥ pārtha bhaktyā labhyastvananyayā | yasyāntaḥsthāni bhūtāni yena sarvamidaṃ tatam || 22 ||
Padaccheda
पुरुषः सः परः पार्थ भक्त्या लभ्यः तु अन्-अन्यया यस्य अन्तः-स्थानि भूतानि येन सर्वम् इदम् ततम्
puruṣaḥ saḥ paraḥ pārtha bhaktyā labhyaḥ tu an-anyayā yasya antaḥ-sthāni bhūtāni yena sarvam idam tatam
Word by word
That supreme Puruṣa, O Pārtha, within whom beings dwell and by whom all this is pervaded, is to be attained by devotion directed to no other.

Note Śaṅkara: this is the means of attaining it. The devotion is of the nature of knowledge and has the Self as its object. Beings, as effects, lie within their cause. He pervades the whole world as space pervades a pot and the like.

यत्र काले त्वनावृत्तिमावृत्तिं चैव योगिनः । प्रयाता यान्ति तं कालं वक्ष्यामि भरतर्षभ ॥ २३ ॥
yatra kāle tvanāvṛttimāvṛttiṃ caiva yoginaḥ | prayātā yānti taṃ kālaṃ vakṣyāmi bharatarṣabha || 23 ||
Padaccheda
यत्र काले तु अनावृत्तिम् आवृत्तिम् च एव योगिनः प्रयाताः यान्ति तम् कालम् वक्ष्यामि भरत-ऋषभ
yatra kāle tu anāvṛttim āvṛttim ca eva yoginaḥ prayātāḥ yānti tam kālam vakṣyāmi bharata-ṛṣabha
Word by word
The time at which yogīs, departing, go to no return, and that at which they go to return: that time I shall tell, O best of the Bharatas.

Note Śaṅkara: the northern path has to be told for the yogīs under discussion, who meditate on Brahman in Om and are liberated after a time; the path of return is told in order to praise the other. ‘Yogīs’ here includes men of ritual action, called so in a secondary sense.

अग्निर्ज्योतिरहः शुक्लः षण्मासा उत्तरायणम् । तत्र प्रयाता गच्छन्ति ब्रह्म ब्रह्मविदो जनाः ॥ २४ ॥
agnirjyotirahaḥ śuklaḥ ṣaṇmāsā uttarāyaṇam | tatra prayātā gacchanti brahma brahmavido janāḥ || 24 ||
Padaccheda
अग्निः ज्योतिः अहः शुक्लः षष्-मासाः उत्तरायणम् तत्र प्रयाताः गच्छन्ति ब्रह्म ब्रह्म-विदः जनाः
agniḥ jyotiḥ ahaḥ śuklaḥ ṣaṣ-māsāḥ uttarāyaṇam tatra prayātāḥ gacchanti brahma brahma-vidaḥ janāḥ
Word by word
Fire, light, day, the bright fortnight, the six months of the sun's northern course: departing by that, people who know Brahman go to Brahman.

Note Śaṅkara: these are the deities presiding over each, who form the path; the word ‘time’ is used because time predominates in the list, as a wood is called a mango grove. tatra: on that path. Those who know Brahman are here those devoted to meditation on Brahman; they go to Brahman by stages. For those steadfast in right vision, who are liberated at once, there is no going or returning anywhere: their life-breaths do not depart, as the Veda says.

Note The opening words of this bhāṣya passage were not in the text I read.

धूमो रात्रिस्तथा कृष्णः षण्मासा दक्षिणायनम् । तत्र चान्द्रमसं ज्योतिर्योगी प्राप्य निवर्तते ॥ २५ ॥
dhūmo rātristathā kṛṣṇaḥ ṣaṇmāsā dakṣiṇāyanam | tatra cāndramasaṃ jyotiryogī prāpya nivartate || 25 ||
Padaccheda
धूमः रात्रिः तथा कृष्णः षष्-मासाः दक्षिणायनम् तत्र चान्द्रमसम् ज्योतिः योगी प्राप्य निवर्तते
dhūmaḥ rātriḥ tathā kṛṣṇaḥ ṣaṣ-māsāḥ dakṣiṇāyanam tatra cāndramasam jyotiḥ yogī prāpya nivartate
Word by word
Smoke, night, likewise the dark fortnight, the six months of the sun's southern course: by that the yogī reaches the light of the moon and returns.

Note Śaṅkara: again the presiding deities. The yogī here is the man of ritual action who performs sacrifices and the like; the light of the moon is his fruit, and when he has enjoyed it and it is exhausted he returns here.

शुक्लकृष्णे गती ह्येते जगतः शाश्वते मते । एकया यात्यनावृत्तिमन्ययावर्तते पुनः ॥ २६ ॥
śuklakṛṣṇe gatī hyete jagataḥ śāśvate mate | ekayā yātyanāvṛttim anyayāvartate punaḥ || 26 ||
Padaccheda
शुक्ल-कृष्णे गती हि एते जगतः शाश्वते मते एकया याति अनावृत्तिम् अन्यया आवर्तते पुनः
śukla-kṛṣṇe gatī hi ete jagataḥ śāśvate mate ekayā yāti anāvṛttim anyayā āvartate punaḥ
Word by word
For these two paths of the world, the bright and the dark, are held to be everlasting. By the one, one goes to no return; by the other, one returns again.

Note Śaṅkara: bright because it reveals knowledge, dark for want of that. ‘Of the world’ means of those qualified for knowledge and for ritual action, since the two paths cannot belong to everyone. They are everlasting because the cycle of birth and death is everlasting.

नैते सृती पार्थ जानन्योगी मुह्यति कश्चन । तस्मात्सर्वेषु कालेषु योगयुक्तो भवार्जुन ॥ २७ ॥
naite sṛtī pārtha jānanyogī muhyati kaścana | tasmātsarveṣu kāleṣu yogayukto bhavārjuna || 27 ||
Padaccheda
न एते सृती पार्थ जानन् योगी मुह्यति कश्चन तस्मात् सर्वेषु कालेषु योग-युक्तः भव अर्जुन
na ete sṛtī pārtha jānan yogī muhyati kaścana tasmāt sarveṣu kāleṣu yoga-yuktaḥ bhava arjuna
Word by word
Knowing these two paths, O Pārtha, no yogī is deluded. Therefore be collected in yoga at all times, O Arjuna.

Note Śaṅkara: knowing that one leads to the cycle of birth and death and the other to liberation.

वेदेषु यज्ञेषु तपःसु चैव दानेषु यत्पुण्यफलं प्रदिष्टम् । अत्येति तत्सर्वमिदं विदित्वा योगी परं स्थानमुपैति चाद्यम् ॥ २८ ॥
vedeṣu yajñeṣu tapaḥsu caiva dāneṣu yatpuṇyaphalaṃ pradiṣṭam | atyeti tatsarvamidaṃ viditvā yogī paraṃ sthānamupaiti cādyam || 28 ||
Padaccheda
वेदेषु यज्ञेषु तपःसु च एव दानेषु यत् पुण्य-फलम् प्रदिष्टम् अत्येति तत् सर्वम् इदम् विदित्वा योगी परम् स्थानम् उपैति च आद्यम्
vedeṣu yajñeṣu tapaḥsu ca eva dāneṣu yat puṇya-phalam pradiṣṭam atyeti tat sarvam idam viditvā yogī param sthānam upaiti ca ādyam
Word by word
Having known this, the yogī goes beyond all the fruit of merit that is declared for the Vedas, for sacrifices, for austerities and for gifts, and reaches the supreme, primal state.

Note Śaṅkara: the Vedas rightly studied, sacrifices performed in full, austerities well practised, gifts rightly given. ‘This’ is what has been taught in settling the seven questions, rightly understood and practised. The supreme state is that of the Lord; primal: existing at the beginning, the cause, Brahman.
