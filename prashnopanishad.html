ॐ भद्रं कर्णेभिः शृणुयाम देवा भद्रं पश्येमाक्षभिर्यजत्राः । स्थिरैरङ्गैस्तुष्टुवांसस्तनूभिर्व्यशेम देवहितं यदायुः ॥ स्वस्ति न इन्द्रो वृद्धश्रवाः स्वस्ति नः पूषा विश्ववेदाः । स्वस्ति नस्तार्क्ष्यो अरिष्टनेमिः स्वस्ति नो बृहस्पतिर्दधातु ॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥
oṃ bhadraṃ karṇebhiḥ śṛṇuyāma devā bhadraṃ paśyemākṣabhiryajatrāḥ | sthirairaṅgaistuṣṭuvāṃsastanūbhirvyaśema devahitaṃ yadāyuḥ || svasti na indro vṛddhaśravāḥ svasti naḥ pūṣā viśvavedāḥ | svasti nastārkṣyo ariṣṭanemiḥ svasti no bṛhaspatirdadhātu || oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||

ॐ भद्रम् कर्णेभिः शृणुयाम देवाः भद्रम् पश्येम अक्षभिः यजत्राः स्थिरैः अङ्गैः तुष्टुवांसः तनूभिः व्यशेम देव-हितम् यत् आयुः स्वस्ति नः इन्द्रः वृद्ध-श्रवाः स्वस्ति नः पूषा विश्व-वेदाः स्वस्ति नः तार्क्ष्यः अरिष्ट-नेमिः स्वस्ति नः बृहस्पतिः दधातु ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः
oṃ bhadram karṇebhiḥ śṛṇuyāma devāḥ bhadram paśyema akṣabhiḥ yajatrāḥ sthiraiḥ aṅgaiḥ tuṣṭuvāṃsaḥ tanūbhiḥ vyaśema deva-hitam yat āyuḥ svasti naḥ indraḥ vṛddha-śravāḥ svasti naḥ pūṣā viśva-vedāḥ svasti naḥ tārkṣyaḥ ariṣṭa-nemiḥ svasti naḥ bṛhaspatiḥ dadhātu oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ
Word by word
Om. May we hear with our ears what is auspicious, O gods; may we see with our eyes what is auspicious, O you who are worthy of worship. Praising you with firm limbs and bodies, may we attain the life that the gods have appointed. May Indra of great fame grant us well-being; may Pūṣan who knows all grant us well-being; may Tārkṣya of the unharmed wheel-rim grant us well-being; may Bṛhaspati grant us well-being. Om. Peace, peace, peace.

प्रथमः प्रश्नः
Prathamaḥ praśnaḥ · First question
सुकेशा च भारद्वाजः शैब्यश्च सत्यकामः सौर्यायणी च गार्ग्यः कौसल्यश्चाश्वलायनो भार्गवो वैदर्भिः कबन्धी कात्यायनस्ते हैते ब्रह्मपरा ब्रह्मनिष्ठा परं ब्रह्मान्वेषमाणा एष ह वै तत्सर्वं वक्ष्यतीति ते ह समित्पाणयो भगवन्तं पिप्पलादमुपसन्नाः ॥ १ ॥
sukeśā ca bhāradvājaḥ śaibyaś ca satyakāmaḥ sauryāyaṇī ca gārgyaḥ kausalyaś cāśvalāyano bhārgavo vaidarbhiḥ kabandhī kātyāyanas te haite brahmaparā brahmaniṣṭhā paraṃ brahmānveṣamāṇā eṣa ha vai tatsarvaṃ vakṣyatīti te ha samitpāṇayo bhagavantaṃ pippalādam upasannāḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
सुकेशा च भारद्वाजः शैब्यः च सत्यकामः सौर्यायणी च गार्ग्यः कौसल्यः च आश्वलायनः भार्गवः वैदर्भिः कबन्धी कात्यायनः ते ह एते ब्रह्म-पराः ब्रह्म-निष्ठाः परम् ब्रह्म अन्वेषमाणाः एषः ह वै तत् सर्वम् वक्ष्यति इति ते ह समित्-पाणयः भगवन्तम् पिप्पलादम् उपसन्नाः
sukeśā ca bhāradvājaḥ śaibyaḥ ca satyakāmaḥ sauryāyaṇī ca gārgyaḥ kausalyaḥ ca āśvalāyanaḥ bhārgavaḥ vaidarbhiḥ kabandhī kātyāyanaḥ te ha ete brahma-parāḥ brahma-niṣṭhāḥ param brahma anveṣamāṇāḥ eṣaḥ ha vai tat sarvam vakṣyati iti te ha samit-pāṇayaḥ bhagavantam pippalādam upasannāḥ
Word by word
Sukeśan Bhāradvāja, Satyakāma Śaibya, Sauryāyaṇin Gārgya, Kausalya Āśvalāyana, Bhārgava Vaidarbhi and Kabandhin Kātyāyana: these, intent on (the lower) Brahman and steadfast in it, seeking the supreme Brahman, thought ‘he will tell us all that’, and with fuel in hand they approached the venerable Pippalāda.

Introduction Introducing the Upaniṣad: this Brāhmaṇa is begun as restating at length what the mantra has said. The story of the seers' questions and the answers is there to praise the knowledge: it is to be received by those who have lived a year with the teacher in brahmacarya and the like, and to be taught by teachers who, like Pippalāda, are all but omniscient, not by anyone whatever. And since it points to brahmacarya and the other means, these are to be practised.

Bhāṣya Sukeśan by name, the son of Bharadvāja; Satyakāma by name, the son of Śibi; Sauryāyaṇin, the son of Sūrya's son (sauryāyaṇī is a Vedic form for sauryāyaṇiḥ), of the line of Garga; Kausalya by name, the son of Aśvala; Bhārgava, of the line of Bhṛgu, born in Vidarbha; Kabandhin by name, the young descendant of Katya, the form being used of one whose great-grandfather is living.

Bhāṣya Brahmaparāḥ: they had taken the lower Brahman for the highest; brahmaniṣṭhāḥ: they were given to its practice. Seeking the supreme Brahman, that which is eternal and to be known, and resolved to strive for it as they wished, they went to the teacher to learn it.

तान्ह स ऋषिरुवाच । भूय एव तपसा ब्रह्मचर्येण श्रद्धया संवत्सरं संवत्स्यथ । यथाकामं प्रश्नान्पृच्छत । यदि विज्ञास्यामः सर्वं ह वो वक्ष्याम इति ॥ २ ॥
tān ha sa ṛṣir uvāca | bhūya eva tapasā brahmacaryeṇa śraddhayā saṃvatsaraṃ saṃvatsyatha | yathākāmaṃ praśnān pṛcchata | yadi vijñāsyāmaḥ sarvaṃ ha vo vakṣyāma iti || 2 ||
Padaccheda
तान् ह सः ऋषिः उवाच भूयः एव तपसा ब्रह्मचर्येण श्रद्धया संवत्सरम् संवत्स्यथ यथा-कामम् प्रश्नान् पृच्छत यदि विज्ञास्यामः सर्वम् ह वः वक्ष्यामः इति
tān ha saḥ ṛṣiḥ uvāca bhūyaḥ eva tapasā brahmacaryeṇa śraddhayā saṃvatsaram saṃvatsyatha yathā-kāmam praśnān pṛcchata yadi vijñāsyāmaḥ sarvam ha vaḥ vakṣyāmaḥ iti
Word by word
The seer said to them: ‘Dwell here a year more, with austerity, brahmacarya and faith. Then ask questions as you wish. If we know, we shall tell you all.’

Bhāṣya ‘again’: though you are ascetics already. tapas is restraint of the senses; here brahmacarya above all; śraddhā is the conviction that it is so, with reverence. ‘Dwell’: intent on serving the teacher well. ‘As you wish’: each on the matter he wishes to know. The word ‘if’ shows the absence of pride, not ignorance or doubt, as is plain from his settling the questions.

अथ कबन्धी कात्यायन उपेत्य पप्रच्छ । भगवन्कुतो ह वा इमाः प्रजाः प्रजायन्त इति ॥ ३ ॥
atha kabandhī kātyāyana upetya papraccha | bhagavan kuto ha vā imāḥ prajāḥ prajāyanta iti || 3 ||
Padaccheda
अथ कबन्धी कात्यायनः उपेत्य पप्रच्छ भगवन् कुतः ह वै इमाः प्रजाः प्रजायन्ते इति
atha kabandhī kātyāyanaḥ upetya papraccha bhagavan kutaḥ ha vai imāḥ prajāḥ prajāyante iti
Word by word
Then (after the year) Kabandhin Kātyāyana approached and asked: ‘Venerable sir, from where are these creatures born?’

Bhāṣya ‘then’: after the year. The creatures are brāhmaṇas and the rest. The question is asked so that the effect of the lower knowledge and of action combined, and the course they lead to, may be told.

तस्मै स होवाच । प्रजाकामो वै प्रजापतिः । स तपोऽतप्यत । स तपस्तप्त्वा स मिथुनमुत्पादयते रयिं च प्राणं चेति । एतौ मे बहुधा प्रजाः करिष्यत इति ॥ ४ ॥
tasmai sa hovāca | prajākāmo vai prajāpatiḥ | sa tapo 'tapyata | sa tapas taptvā sa mithunam utpādayate rayiṃ ca prāṇaṃ ceti | etau me bahudhā prajāḥ kariṣyata iti || 4 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मै सः ह उवाच प्रजा-कामः वै प्रजापतिः सः तपः अतप्यत सः तपः तप्त्वा सः मिथुनम् उत्पादयते रयिम् च प्राणम् च इति एतौ मे बहुधा प्रजाः करिष्यतः इति
tasmai saḥ ha uvāca prajā-kāmaḥ vai prajāpatiḥ saḥ tapaḥ atapyata saḥ tapaḥ taptvā saḥ mithunam utpādayate rayim ca prāṇam ca iti etau me bahudhā prajāḥ kariṣyataḥ iti
Word by word
He said to him: ‘Prajāpati, desiring offspring, reflected (on the knowledge of the Veda). Having reflected, he produced a pair, food and prāṇa, thinking: these two will make creatures for me in many ways.’

Bhāṣya Prajāpati: he who, with the knowledge ‘being the Self of all, I shall create the world’ and acting on it, filled with that thought, came to be at the beginning of the kalpa as Hiraṇyagarbha, the lord of the creatures to be created, moving and unmoving. tapas: he reflected on the knowledge cultivated in an earlier birth, whose object is what the Veda reveals. The pair is the means of creation: rayi is Soma, food; prāṇa is Agni, the eater. Thinking so, he made the sun and the moon, in the order in which the world-egg arose.

आदित्यो ह वै प्राणो रयिरेव चन्द्रमाः । रयिर्वा एतत्सर्वं यन्मूर्तं चामूर्तं च । तस्मान्मूर्तिरेव रयिः ॥ ५ ॥
ādityo ha vai prāṇo rayir eva candramāḥ | rayir vā etat sarvaṃ yanmūrtaṃ cāmūrtaṃ ca | tasmān mūrtir eva rayiḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
आदित्यः ह वै प्राणः रयिः एव चन्द्रमाः रयिः वै एतत् सर्वम् यत् मूर्तम् च अमूर्तम् च तस्मात् मूर्तिः एव रयिः
ādityaḥ ha vai prāṇaḥ rayiḥ eva candramāḥ rayiḥ vai etat sarvam yat mūrtam ca amūrtam ca tasmāt mūrtiḥ eva rayiḥ
Word by word
The sun is prāṇa; the moon is food. All this is food, what has form and what is formless; therefore (when the two are set apart) form itself is food.

Bhāṣya The sun is prāṇa, the eater, fire; the moon is rayi, food, Soma. This is one thing, eater and food: Prajāpati is one and yet a pair, the difference being made by what is chief and what is subordinate. All this, the gross with form and the subtle without form, in the shape of eater and food, is rayi. So when the two are set apart, what is other than the formless, that is, form, is rayi, because it is eaten by the formless.

Introduction Going on to the next mantra: so too the formless prāṇa, the eater, is everything, even what is eaten. How?

अथादित्य उदयन्यत्प्राचीं दिशं प्रविशति तेन प्राच्यान्प्राणान्रश्मिषु संनिधत्ते । यद्दक्षिणां यत्प्रतीचीं यदुदीचीं यदधो यदूर्ध्वं यदन्तरा दिशो यत्सर्वं प्रकाशयति तेन सर्वान्प्राणान्रश्मिषु संनिधत्ते ॥ ६ ॥
athāditya udayan yatprācīṃ diśaṃ praviśati tena prācyān prāṇān raśmiṣu saṃnidhatte | yad dakṣiṇāṃ yat pratīcīṃ yad udīcīṃ yad adho yad ūrdhvaṃ yad antarā diśo yat sarvaṃ prakāśayati tena sarvān prāṇān raśmiṣu saṃnidhatte || 6 ||
Padaccheda
अथ आदित्यः उदयन् यत् प्राचीम् दिशम् प्रविशति तेन प्राच्यान् प्राणान् रश्मिषु संनिधत्ते यत् दक्षिणाम् यत् प्रतीचीम् यत् उदीचीम् यत् अधः यत् ऊर्ध्वम् यत् अन्तरा दिशः यत् सर्वम् प्रकाशयति तेन सर्वान् प्राणान् रश्मिषु संनिधत्ते
atha ādityaḥ udayan yat prācīm diśam praviśati tena prācyān prāṇān raśmiṣu saṃnidhatte yat dakṣiṇām yat pratīcīm yat udīcīm yat adhaḥ yat ūrdhvam yat antarā diśaḥ yat sarvam prakāśayati tena sarvān prāṇān raśmiṣu saṃnidhatte
Word by word
Now when the sun, rising, enters the eastern quarter, he thereby takes the living things of the east into his rays. When he enters the southern, the western, the northern, below, above and the quarters between, when he illumines all, he thereby takes all living things into his rays.

Bhāṣya ‘rising’: coming within sight of living things. ‘Enters’: pervades with his own light. By that pervading he places the living things (prāṇān: prāṇinaḥ) in his rays, which are his own shining and pervade all: that is, he makes them his own self. The quarters between are the corner quarters.

स एष वैश्वानरो विश्वरूपः प्राणोऽग्निरुदयते । तदेतदृचाभ्युक्तम् ॥ ७ ॥
sa eṣa vaiśvānaro viśvarūpaḥ prāṇo 'gnir udayate | tad etad ṛcābhyuktam || 7 ||
Padaccheda
सः एषः वैश्वानरः विश्व-रूपः प्राणः अग्निः उदयते तत् एतत् ऋचा अभ्युक्तम्
saḥ eṣaḥ vaiśvānaraḥ viśva-rūpaḥ prāṇaḥ agniḥ udayate tat etat ṛcā abhyuktam
Word by word
He, this Vaiśvānara of all forms, prāṇa, fire, rises. This same thing is declared by a verse:

Bhāṣya He, the eater, prāṇa, is Vaiśvānara, the Self of all, and of all forms because he is the Self of the universe; as prāṇa and fire, that same eater rises every day, making all the quarters his own.

विश्वरूपं हरिणं जातवेदसं परायणं ज्योतिरेकं तपन्तम् । सहस्ररश्मिः शतधा वर्तमानः प्राणः प्रजानामुदयत्येष सूर्यः ॥ ८ ॥
viśvarūpaṃ hariṇaṃ jātavedasaṃ parāyaṇaṃ jyotir ekaṃ tapantam | sahasraraśmiḥ śatadhā vartamānaḥ prāṇaḥ prajānām udayaty eṣa sūryaḥ || 8 ||
Padaccheda
विश्व-रूपम् हरिणम् जातवेदसम् परायणम् ज्योतिः एकम् तपन्तम् सहस्र-रश्मिः शतधा वर्तमानः प्राणः प्रजानाम् उदयति एषः सूर्यः
viśva-rūpam hariṇam jātavedasam parāyaṇam jyotiḥ ekam tapantam sahasra-raśmiḥ śatadhā vartamānaḥ prāṇaḥ prajānām udayati eṣaḥ sūryaḥ
Word by word
(The wise have known him,) of all forms, rayed, all-knowing, the final resort, the one light, giving heat. Of a thousand rays, existing in a hundred ways, the life of creatures, this sun rises.

Bhāṣya Hariṇam: having rays. jātavedasam: in whom knowledge has arisen. parāyaṇam: the resting place of all life. ‘The one light’: the eye of all living things, without a second. ‘The wise, the knowers of Brahman, have known the sun as their own Self’ is to be supplied. ‘In a hundred ways’: in many ways, by the difference of living things.

Introduction Going on to the next mantra: the moon, which is form and food, and the sun, which is formless, prāṇa, the eater, are one pair, and it is all. How will the pair make creatures?

संवत्सरो वै प्रजापतिः । तस्यायने दक्षिणं चोत्तरं च । तद्ये ह वै तदिष्टापूर्ते कृतमित्युपासते ते चान्द्रमसमेव लोकमभिजयन्ते । त एव पुनरावर्तन्ते । तस्मादेत ऋषयः प्रजाकामा दक्षिणं प्रतिपद्यन्ते । एष ह वै रयिर्यः पितृयाणः ॥ ९ ॥
saṃvatsaro vai prajāpatiḥ | tasyāyane dakṣiṇaṃ cottaraṃ ca | tad ye ha vai tadiṣṭāpūrte kṛtam ity upāsate te cāndramasam eva lokam abhijayante | ta eva punar āvartante | tasmād eta ṛṣayaḥ prajākāmā dakṣiṇaṃ pratipadyante | eṣa ha vai rayir yaḥ pitṛyāṇaḥ || 9 ||
Padaccheda
संवत्सरः वै प्रजापतिः तस्य अयने दक्षिणम् च उत्तरम् च तत् ये ह वै तत् इष्टापूर्ते कृतम् इति उपासते ते चान्द्रमसम् एव लोकम् अभिजयन्ते ते एव पुनः आवर्तन्ते तस्मात् एते ऋषयः प्रजा-कामाः दक्षिणम् प्रतिपद्यन्ते एषः ह वै रयिः यः पितृयाणः
saṃvatsaraḥ vai prajāpatiḥ tasya ayane dakṣiṇam ca uttaram ca tat ye ha vai tat iṣṭāpūrte kṛtam iti upāsate te cāndramasam eva lokam abhijayante te eva punaḥ āvartante tasmāt ete ṛṣayaḥ prajā-kāmāḥ dakṣiṇam pratipadyante eṣaḥ ha vai rayiḥ yaḥ pitṛyāṇaḥ
Word by word
The year is Prajāpati. It has two paths, the southern and the northern. Those who devote themselves to sacrifice and works of public good, to what is made, win only the world of the moon; they return again. Therefore these seers who desire offspring attain the southern. This path of the fathers is food.

Bhāṣya That same pair is time: the year is Prajāpati because it is brought about by the pair, for the year is the sum of the lunar days and of the days and nights that moon and sun bring about; not being other than them, it too is the pair of rayi and prāṇa. The second Muṇḍaka says that, as sparks from a well-kindled fire, all beings are born from the supreme imperishable and merge in it again. What are those beings that are divided out of the imperishable? How, once divided, do they merge in it? What is the mark of that imperishable? Bhāṣya ‘in this person’, who has head, hands and the rest, which organs sleep, that is, cease from their own work, and which stay awake and do their own work? When the work of waking and of dream has ceased, whose is the happiness that is serene, without effort, unobstructed? And at that time, in what are all, having ceased from the work of waking and dream, established together, become one, past telling apart, like the juices in honey or the rivers that have entered the sea?

Bhāṣya It may be objected that organs which have ceased from their work, like sickles and other tools laid down, simply rest each in itself: whence the questioner's surmise that the organs of sleeping men go to oneness in something? The surmise is right. The organs are composite, serve the ends of an owner and are dependent in waking; so in sleep too it is reasonable that, being composite, they come together in something on which they depend. The question fits that surmise. Bhāṣya An objection: in experiencing this greatness the mind is the instrument of the experiencer; how can it be said to experience it of itself? It is the knower of the field that is independent. The answer: there is no fault. The independence of the knower of the field is itself made by the adjunct, mind; in truth he neither sleeps nor wakes of himself. His waking and his dream are made by the adjunct, mind, as the Vājasaneyaka says: ‘becoming dream, he thinks as it were, he moves as it were’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.3.7). So it is right to speak of the mind as independent in experiencing this greatness.

Bhāṣya Some say that if at the time of dream the knower of the field has the mind as adjunct, his being his own light is contradicted. Not so: their error comes from not knowing the sense of scripture. All talk of being one's own light and the like, up to liberation, lies within ignorance and is produced by the adjuncts, mind and the rest: ‘where there is as it were another, there one may see another’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.3.31), ‘there is contact with the elements’, ‘but where all has become the Self alone, by what should one see whom?’ (2.4.14). So this is a doubt of those who know Brahman dimly, not of those who know the one Self.

Bhāṣya But then the qualification ‘here this Puruṣa is his own light’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.3.9) would have no purpose. The reply: that is saying too little. By ‘he lies in the space within the heart’ (2.1.17), which bounds him within the heart, his being his own light would be contradicted all the more. ‘Granted that this fault may be; still in dream, by his being his own light alone, half the burden is taken off.’ No: there too scripture says ‘he lies in the purītat’ (2.1.19), so there is a connection with the channels of the purītat, and the idea of taking off half the burden is false. How then ‘here this Puruṣa is his own light’? If it is said that that scripture, being of another branch, need not be regarded: no, for one sense is accepted; the one Self is the sense of all the Vedāntas, what they mean to teach and what is to be known. So it is right to show how the Self's being its own light in dream is possible, since scripture reveals the truth as it is.

Bhāṣya Then hear the sense of scripture, giving up all conceit; by conceit it cannot be known in a hundred years by all those who think themselves learned. As one who sleeps in the space of the heart, in the purītat and in the channels has no connection with them and can be shown apart from them, so that the Self's being its own light is not contradicted, so too here: in the mind, which holds the impressions that have arisen from ignorance, desire and action, the seer, wholly apart from all effects and instruments, sees through ignorance the impressions caused by action as if they were other things; and because he is other than the impressions, which are what is seen, his being his own light cannot be denied even by the proudest logician. So it is well said that, when the organs are merged in the mind and the mind is not merged, he, made of mind, sees dreams.

Bhāṣya How does he experience greatness? A friend or a son seen before: scented by that impression, he thinks, through ignorance, that he sees the son or friend who has arisen from the impression; so too he hears as it were, and experiences again as it were what was experienced in other places and quarters. ‘Seen’: in this birth; ‘unseen’: seen in another birth, for there can be no impression of what was never seen at all. So with the heard and the unheard. ‘Experienced’: in this birth, by the mind alone; ‘unexperienced’: experienced by the mind in another birth. The real is real water and the like; the unreal is the water of a mirage and the like. In short he sees all, and being all he sees: with all the impressions of the mind as his adjunct, the god mind, the self of all the organs, sees dreams.

He said to them: ‘Thus far only do I know this supreme Brahman; there is nothing higher than this.’

Bhāṣya Having taught the pupils thus, Pippalāda said: this much only is the supreme Brahman that is to be known; there is nothing higher than it to be known. He said so to remove the pupils' doubt that something unknown might remain, and to give them the conviction that they had gained their end.

They, honouring him, (said): ‘You indeed are our father, who take us across to the farther shore of ignorance.’ Homage to the supreme seers! Homage to the supreme seers!

Bhāṣya The pupils, taught by the teacher and having gained their end, seeing no price that could be paid for the knowledge, honoured him by strewing handfuls of flowers at his feet and bowing their heads. ‘You are our father’, since by knowledge you have begotten the body that is Brahman, eternal, ageless, deathless, fearless; you take us by the raft of knowledge across the great ocean of ignorance, of wrong knowledge, with its sea-beasts, birth, old age, death, disease and pain, to the other shore, called liberation, from which there is no return. So fatherhood towards us is more fitly yours than another's: the other father begets the body only, and yet he is the most to be honoured in the world; what need be said of one who gives fearlessness without end? ‘Homage to the supreme seers’, the founders of the tradition of the knowledge of Brahman; the repetition is to show reverence.
