मुण्डकोपनिषद्

Muṇḍakopaniṣad

Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad

Atharvaveda · meanings following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

Three muṇḍakas, six khaṇḍas, 65 mantras: the lower and the higher knowledge, the rites and their limit; the imperishable from which all comes forth, and the bow of Om; the two birds, the means to the Self, and the knower who becomes Brahman. Meanings and translation follow Śaṅkara's bhāṣya.

SourceSrirangam edition, 1910

Om. May we hear with our ears what is auspicious, O gods; may we see with our eyes what is auspicious, O you who are worthy of worship. May Indra of great fame grant us well-being; may Pūṣan who knows all grant us well-being; may Tārkṣya of the unharmed wheel-rim grant us well-being; may Bṛhaspati grant us well-being. Om. प्रथममुण्डके प्रथमः खण्डः
Prathamamuṇḍake prathamaḥ khaṇḍaḥ · Muṇḍaka 1 · First khaṇḍa
ब्रह्मा देवानां प्रथमः संबभूव विश्वस्य कर्ता भुवनस्य गोप्ता । स ब्रह्मविद्यां सर्वविद्याप्रतिष्ठामथर्वाय ज्येष्ठपुत्राय प्राह ॥ १ ॥
brahmā devānāṃ prathamaḥ saṃbabhūva viśvasya kartā bhuvanasya goptā | sa brahmavidyāṃ sarvavidyāpratiṣṭhām atharvāya jyeṣṭhaputrāya prāha || 1 ||
Padaccheda
ब्रह्मा देवानाम् प्रथमः संबभूव विश्वस्य कर्ता भुवनस्य गोप्ता सः ब्रह्म-विद्याम् सर्व-विद्या-प्रतिष्ठाम् अथर्वाय ज्येष्ठ-पुत्राय प्राह
brahmā devānām prathamaḥ saṃbabhūva viśvasya kartā bhuvanasya goptā saḥ brahma-vidyām sarva-vidyā-pratiṣṭhām atharvāya jyeṣṭha-putrāya prāha
Word by word
Brahmā came into being first among the gods, the maker of the universe, the protector of the world. He taught the knowledge of Brahman, the support of all knowledge, to Atharvan, his eldest son.

Introduction Introducing the Upaniṣad: at the start the Upaniṣad itself states its relation, the line of teachers who handed the knowledge down, in order to praise it: so great are those by whom it was gained with effort, as the means to the highest good; praised, it wins the hearer's eagerness. The relation of means and end it states later, ‘the knot of the heart is cut’ (2.2.9). It first shows that the lower knowledge, the Ṛgveda and the rest, which is only injunction and prohibition, does not remove ignorance and the other faults that cause the round of birth and death (1.2.8 and after); then it teaches the knowledge of Brahman, to be gained by the teacher's grace after dispassion toward all that means and ends can win (‘having examined the worlds’, 1.2.12); and it states the fruit more than once: ‘he who knows Brahman becomes Brahman’ (3.2.9), ‘they are all freed, immortal beyond’ (3.2.6).

Bhāṣya On the same: though men of every stage of life are entitled to knowledge as such, it is the knowledge of Brahman resting on renunciation that is the means to liberation, not knowledge joined with rites; the Upaniṣad shows this by ‘living on alms’ (1.2.11) and ‘by the yoga of renunciation’ (3.2.6). Knowledge and action are also opposed: no rite can be performed, even in a dream, together with the vision of the oneness of Brahman and the Self; and since knowledge has no set time and no fixed occasion, it cannot be confined to a time (leaving the rest for rites). That householders are found handing on this knowledge is a mere mark and cannot overturn what reason has settled: not even a hundred injunctions could put darkness and light in one place, still less mere marks.

Bhāṣya On the name: it is called upaniṣad because, in those who approach this knowledge of Brahman as their own self, with faith and devotion, it loosens (śātayati) the host of evils, conception, birth, old age, disease; or leads them to the supreme Brahman; or utterly destroys ignorance, the cause of the round of birth and death: so the root sad with upa and ni is remembered to mean.

Bhāṣya Brahmā: the great one, surpassing all others in dharma, knowledge, dispassion and lordship. prathamaḥ: foremost in qualities, or first in time. saṃbabhūva: came forth of his own freedom, not as others are born under the power of dharma and adharma (Manu 1.7). The epithets that follow praise the knowledge through its teacher. brahmavidyā: the knowledge of Brahman, the supreme Self, for it is described as that ‘by which one knows the imperishable Puruṣa’ (1.2.13); or the knowledge told by Brahmā, the first-born. sarvavidyāpratiṣṭhā: the support of all knowledge, because it is the cause by which all knowledge comes forth; or because by it alone what all knowledge knows is known (‘by which the unheard becomes heard’, Chāndogya 6.1.3). jyeṣṭhaputra: among Brahmā's many ways of creating, Atharvan was created first in one of them.

अथर्वणे यां प्रवदेत ब्रह्माथर्वा तां पुरोवाचाङ्गिरे ब्रह्मविद्याम् । स भारद्वाजाय सत्यवहाय प्राह भारद्वाजोऽङ्गिरसे परावराम् ॥ २ ॥
atharvaṇe yāṃ pravadeta brahmātharvā tāṃ purovācāṅgire brahmavidyām | sa bhāradvājāya satyavahāya prāha bhāradvājo 'ṅgirase parāvarām || 2 ||
Padaccheda
अथर्वणे याम् प्रवदेत ब्रह्मा अथर्वा ताम् पुरा उवाच अङ्गिरे ब्रह्म-विद्याम् सः भारद्वाजाय सत्यवहाय प्राह भारद्वाजः अङ्गिरसे परावराम्
atharvaṇe yām pravadeta brahmā atharvā tām purā uvāca aṅgire brahma-vidyām saḥ bhāradvājāya satyavahāya prāha bhāradvājaḥ aṅgirase parāvarām
Word by word
The knowledge of Brahman which Brahmā taught to Atharvan, Atharvan of old told to Aṅgir; he taught it to Satyavaha of the line of Bharadvāja; and Bhāradvāja (taught) to Aṅgiras this (knowledge) handed down from higher to lower.

Bhāṣya Pravadeta stands for the past, prāvadat, ‘taught’. aṅgir: one named Aṅgir. bhāradvāja: of the line (gotra) of Bharadvāja; satyavaha is his name. aṅgirase: to Aṅgiras, his pupil or son. parāvarā: received by each lower from each higher; or reaching over every kind of knowledge, higher and lower. ‘Taught’ is carried over to the last clause.

Reading Many editions read satyavāhāya; the edition and the bhāṣya both read satyavahāya.

शौनको ह वै महाशालोऽङ्गिरसं विधिवदुपसन्नः पप्रच्छ । कस्मिन्नु भगवो विज्ञाते सर्वमिदं विज्ञातं भवतीति ॥ ३ ॥
śaunako ha vai mahāśālo 'ṅgirasaṃ vidhivad upasannaḥ papraccha | kasmin nu bhagavo vijñāte sarvam idaṃ vijñātaṃ bhavatīti || 3 ||
Padaccheda
शौनकः ह वै महाशालः अङ्गिरसम् विधिवत् उपसन्नः पप्रच्छ कस्मिन् नु भगवः विज्ञाते सर्वम् इदम् विज्ञातम् भवति इति
śaunakaḥ ha vai mahāśālaḥ aṅgirasam vidhivat upasannaḥ papraccha kasmin nu bhagavaḥ vijñāte sarvam idam vijñātam bhavati iti
Word by word
Śaunaka, the great householder, having approached Aṅgiras in due form, asked: ‘Revered sir, what is it which being known, all this becomes known?’

Bhāṣya Śaunaka, son of Śunaka, a great householder. vidhivat: as the scripture lays down. Since the word is not used of those before Śaunaka and Aṅgiras, it seems that for them the manner of approach was not fixed; the word sets the limit from which it holds; or, like a lamp set in the middle, it lights both ways, for the rule of approach holds for us as well. nu marks doubt. Śaunaka had heard the saying of the wise, ‘when the one is known, one knows all’, and wishing to know what that one is he asked in doubt; or he asked from what is seen in the world, where the many pieces of gold are known by knowing the one gold: is there likewise one cause of all the variety of the world, by knowing which all is known? To the objection that he should first have asked whether there is such a thing, and only then ‘in what’: the question means just that, put shortly to spare words.

तस्मै स होवाच । द्वे विद्ये वेदितव्ये इति ह स्म यद्ब्रह्मविदो वदन्ति परा चैवापरा च ॥ ४ ॥
tasmai sa hovāca | dve vidye veditavye iti ha sma yad brahmavido vadanti parā caivāparā ca || 4 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मै सः ह उवाच द्वे विद्ये वेदितव्ये इति ह स्म यत् ब्रह्म-विदः वदन्ति परा च एव अपरा च
tasmai saḥ ha uvāca dve vidye veditavye iti ha sma yat brahma-vidaḥ vadanti parā ca eva aparā ca
Word by word
He said to him: ‘Two knowledges are to be known, as those who know Brahman say: the higher and the lower.’

Bhāṣya Brahmavidaḥ: those who know the meaning of the Veda and see the highest truth. parā: the knowledge of the supreme Self; aparā: the knowledge of dharma and adharma, their means and their fruits. Śaunaka asked what, being known, makes one know all, and Aṅgiras speaks of what was not asked; there is no fault, for the answer keeps an order. The lower knowledge is ignorance and must be set aside first, since when its objects are known nothing is known in truth; by the rule that the opposing view is cleared away before the conclusion is stated.

तत्रापरा ऋग्वेदो यजुर्वेदः सामवेदोऽथर्ववेदः शिक्षा कल्पो व्याकरणं निरुक्तं छन्दो ज्योतिषमिति । अथ परा यया तदक्षरमधिगम्यते ॥ ५ ॥
tatrāparā ṛgvedo yajurvedaḥ sāmavedo 'tharvavedaḥ śikṣā kalpo vyākaraṇaṃ niruktaṃ chando jyotiṣam iti | atha parā yayā tad akṣaram adhigamyate || 5 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र अपरा ऋग्वेदः यजुर्वेदः सामवेदः अथर्ववेदः शिक्षा कल्पः व्याकरणम् निरुक्तम् छन्दः ज्योतिषम् इति अथ परा यया तत् अक्षरम् अधिगम्यते
tatra aparā ṛgvedaḥ yajurvedaḥ sāmavedaḥ atharvavedaḥ śikṣā kalpaḥ vyākaraṇam niruktam chandaḥ jyotiṣam iti atha parā yayā tat akṣaram adhigamyate
Word by word
Of these the lower is the Ṛgveda, the Yajurveda, the Sāmaveda, the Atharvaveda, phonetics, ritual, grammar, etymology, metre and astronomy. And the higher is that by which the imperishable is attained.

Bhāṣya The four Vedas and the six limbs are the lower knowledge. adhigamyate: is attained, for adhi with gam mostly means attaining; and attaining the supreme is not other than knowing it, since the removal of ignorance is itself the attaining of the supreme, nothing else.

Bhāṣya On an objection: if the higher knowledge stands outside the Ṛgveda and the rest, how can it be higher, or a means to liberation? The law books call the teachings outside the Veda false views, to be rejected as fruitless (Manu 12.95); and the Upaniṣads would fall outside the Veda; while if they are within it, naming them apart is pointless. No: what is meant here by ‘the higher knowledge’ is chiefly the knowing of the imperishable that is to be known from the Upaniṣads, not the mass of words of the Upaniṣads; whereas ‘Veda’ everywhere means the mass of words. Even when the words are learnt, the imperishable is not attained without a further effort, going to a teacher and the rest, and dispassion; so the knowledge of Brahman is named apart, ‘and the higher’.

यत्तदद्रेश्यमग्राह्यमगोत्रमवर्णमचक्षुःश्रोत्रं तदपाणिपादम् । नित्यं विभुं सर्वगतं सुसूक्ष्मं तदव्ययं यद्भूतयोनिं परिपश्यन्ति धीराः ॥ ६ ॥
yat tad adreśyam agrāhyam agotram avarṇam acakṣuḥśrotraṃ tad apāṇipādam | nityaṃ vibhuṃ sarvagataṃ susūkṣmaṃ tad avyayaṃ yad bhūtayoniṃ paripaśyanti dhīrāḥ || 6 ||
Padaccheda
यत् तत् अद्रेश्यम् अग्राह्यम् अगोत्रम् अवर्णम् अचक्षुः-श्रोत्रम् तत् अपाणि-पादम् नित्यम् विभुम् सर्वगतम् सुसूक्ष्मम् तत् अव्ययम् यत् भूत-योनिम् परिपश्यन्ति धीराः
yat tat adreśyam agrāhyam agotram avarṇam acakṣuḥ-śrotram tat apāṇi-pādam nityam vibhum sarvagatam susūkṣmam tat avyayam yat bhūta-yonim paripaśyanti dhīrāḥ
Word by word
That which is invisible, ungraspable, without lineage, without colour, without eye or ear, without hands or feet; eternal, manifold (in all beings), present everywhere, most subtle; that undecaying one which the wise see on every side as the source of beings: (that is the imperishable).

Introduction In an injunction, what is to be done, the agnihotra and the rest, stands apart from knowing the meaning of the sentence, for it needs agent and other factors to be brought together. Here, in the higher knowledge, it is not so: it is complete in the very knowing of the sentence, since there is nothing beyond resting in the knowledge of what the words alone make known. So the mantra describes the higher knowledge through the imperishable and its marks.

Bhāṣya Adreśyam: invisible, beyond reach of all the organs of knowledge, for seeing that goes outward passes through the five senses. agrāhyam: not the object of the organs of action. agotram: gotra is lineage, root; it has no root to which it is joined. avarṇam: without the properties of substance, grossness and the like, or whiteness and the like. acakṣuḥśrotram: since it is called ‘all-knowing’ (1.1.9), it might be thought to work, like those in the round of birth, through eye, ear and the other organs; that is denied (‘he sees without eyes, hears without ears’, Śvetāśvatara 3.19). apāṇipādam: without organs of action. Being neither grasped nor grasping, it is eternal, imperishable. vibhum: it becomes (bhavati) manifold (vi-), as all the kinds of living beings from Brahmā down to plants. sarvagatam: pervading, like space. susūkṣmam: most subtle, being free of sound and the rest, which make space, wind and the other elements each grosser than the one before. avyayam: it does not decay; having no limbs it does not decay as a body loses a limb, nor as a king loses his treasury, nor through its qualities, being without qualities and the self of all. bhūtayoni: the cause of beings, as the earth is of the moving and unmoving. paripaśyanti: see everywhere as the Self of all. dhīrāḥ: the discerning. The knowledge by which such an imperishable is attained is the higher knowledge.

यथोर्णनाभिः सृजते गृह्णते च यथा पृथिव्यामोषधयः संभवन्ति । यथा सतः पुरुषात्केशलोमानि तथाक्षरात्संभवतीह विश्वम् ॥ ७ ॥
yathorṇanābhiḥ sṛjate gṛhṇate ca yathā pṛthivyām oṣadhayaḥ saṃbhavanti | yathā sataḥ puruṣāt keśalomāni tathākṣarāt saṃbhavatīha viśvam || 7 ||
Padaccheda
यथा ऊर्णनाभिः सृजते गृह्णते च यथा पृथिव्याम् ओषधयः संभवन्ति यथा सतः पुरुषात् केश-लोमानि तथा अक्षरात् संभवति इह विश्वम्
yathā ūrṇanābhiḥ sṛjate gṛhṇate ca yathā pṛthivyām oṣadhayaḥ saṃbhavanti yathā sataḥ puruṣāt keśa-lomāni tathā akṣarāt saṃbhavati iha viśvam
Word by word
As the spider sends forth and takes back (its threads), as plants spring up on the earth, as the hair of head and body grows from a living man, so the universe here comes forth from the imperishable.

Bhāṣya The imperishable was called the source of beings; how, is shown by examples. The spider, needing no other cause, itself sends out threads not other than its own body, and takes them back into itself; plants, rice and the rest, spring from the earth and are not other than it; hair grows from a living man and is unlike him. So from the imperishable, which needs no other cause, comes forth the whole world in this round of birth and death, both like it and unlike it. Several examples are given to make it easy to grasp.

तपसा चीयते ब्रह्म ततोऽन्नमभिजायते । अन्नात्प्राणो मनः सत्यं लोकाः कर्मसु चामृतम् ॥ ८ ॥
tapasā cīyate brahma tato 'nnam abhijāyate | annāt prāṇo manaḥ satyaṃ lokāḥ karmasu cāmṛtam || 8 ||
Padaccheda
तपसा चीयते ब्रह्म ततः अन्नम् अभिजायते अन्नात् प्राणः मनः सत्यम् लोकाः कर्मसु च अमृतम्
tapasā cīyate brahma tataḥ annam abhijāyate annāt prāṇaḥ manaḥ satyam lokāḥ karmasu ca amṛtam
Word by word
By austerity (by knowledge) Brahman swells; from it food (the unmanifest) is born; from food prāṇa (Hiraṇyagarbha), mind, the true (the five elements), the worlds; and in actions the immortal (their fruit).

Bhāṣya The mantra states the order in which the world comes forth from Brahman: not all at once, as a fistful of jujube fruit is thrown. tapas: knowledge, the knowing of how to create. cīyate: Brahman, wishing to bring forth the world, swells, as a seed swells into a sprout or a father swells with joy at a son; it grows full with the knowledge and power of creating, sustaining and withdrawing. annam: ‘what is eaten’, the unmanifest, the common cause of all in the round of birth, in the state of being about to be manifested. prāṇa: Hiraṇyagarbha, informed by Brahman's power of knowledge and action, common to the world, the sprout from the seed that is ignorance, desire, action and the elements, the self of the world. manas: what intends, imagines, doubts and decides. satyam: the five elements, space and the rest. lokāḥ: the seven worlds, bhūḥ and the rest, in the order of the cosmic egg. karmasu: in them the actions of men and others, by caste and stage of life; and in actions, as their occasion, the immortal: the fruit of action, called immortal because it does not perish as long as the actions do not perish, even in hundreds of crores of kalpas.

yaḥ sarvajñaḥ sarvavid yasya jñānamayaṃ tapaḥ | tasmād etad brahma nāma rūpam annaṃ ca jāyate || 9 ||
Padaccheda
यः सर्वज्ञः सर्ववित् यस्य ज्ञानमयम् तपः तस्मात् एतत् ब्रह्म नाम रूपम् अन्नम् च जायते
yaḥ sarvajñaḥ sarvavit yasya jñānamayam tapaḥ tasmāt etat brahma nāma rūpam annam ca jāyate

Introduction Introducing the khaṇḍa: the lower knowledge, the Vedas with their limbs, has been named (1.1.5), and the higher, by which the imperishable is attained, has been described (1.1.6–9). The round, the object of the lower knowledge, made of the differences of agent, means, act and fruit, is without beginning or end, sorrow by nature, to be left by each embodied being, an unbroken stream like a river; its stilling, liberation, the object of the higher, is without beginning or end, ageless, deathless, immortal, fearless, pure, clear, resting in itself, the supreme bliss, without a second. Having examined the worlds built up by action, a brāhmaṇa should come to dispassion: the unmade is not (won) by what is done. To know that, he should go, fuel in hand, to a teacher alone, one versed in the Veda and established in Brahman.

Introduction Now, to show who is fit for the higher knowledge, one who has turned away from the whole round of means and ends.

Bhāṣya Lokān: the worlds that are the fruit of the rites of the lower knowledge, which are enjoined only for one who has the natural faults of ignorance, desire and action, the worlds of the southern and northern paths, and the hells, animal births and ghostly states that come of neglecting what is enjoined and doing what is forbidden. Having examined them by perception, inference, comparison and scripture, and settled their nature on every side: from the unmanifest down to the plants, manifest and unmanifest, causing one another like seed and sprout, crowded with countless evils, pithless as the stem of a plantain, like illusion, a mirage, a city in the clouds, a dream, a bubble, foam, perishing every moment; built up by action prompted by ignorance and desire, made by dharma and adharma; having put them behind him. brāhmaṇaḥ: the brāhmaṇa is named because he especially is fit for the knowledge of Brahman by renouncing everything.

Bhāṣya On the dispassion: nis with vid here means dispassion. Its form: in this round there is nothing at all that is unmade; all worlds are built by action, and so impermanent; nothing permanent exists here, and all action is a means only to what is impermanent, for all that action brings about is of four kinds, produced, reached, modified or purified; action has no further object. ‘I seek what is permanent, immortal, fearless, unchanging, unmoving, constant, not its opposite: what use is action, full of toil, a means of harm?’ So turned away, to know the fearless, auspicious, unmade, permanent state, he should go to a teacher alone, one endowed with calm, restraint and the rest; ‘alone’ means that even one learned in scripture should not seek the knowledge of Brahman on his own. samitpāṇiḥ: carrying a load of fuel. śrotriya: rich in study and in understanding what is heard. brahmaniṣṭha: one who, giving up all rites, rests in the one, non-dual Brahman alone, as one says ‘resting in japa, in austerity’; one engaged in rites cannot rest in Brahman, since action and knowledge of the Self are opposed. Having approached such a teacher in due form and won his favour, he should ask about the imperishable, true Puruṣa.

तस्मै स विद्वानुपसन्नाय सम्यक्प्रशान्तचित्ताय शमान्विताय । येनाक्षरं पुरुषं वेद सत्यं प्रोवाच तां तत्त्वतो ब्रह्मविद्याम् ॥ १३ ॥
tasmai sa vidvān upasannāya samyak praśāntacittāya śamānvitāya | yenākṣaraṃ puruṣaṃ veda satyaṃ provāca tāṃ tattvato brahmavidyām || 13 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मै सः विद्वान् उपसन्नाय सम्यक् प्रशान्त-चित्ताय शम-अन्विताय येन अक्षरम् पुरुषम् वेद सत्यम् प्रोवाच ताम् तत्त्वतः ब्रह्म-विद्याम्
tasmai saḥ vidvān upasannāya samyak praśānta-cittāya śama-anvitāya yena akṣaram puruṣam veda satyam provāca tām tattvataḥ brahma-vidyām
Word by word
To him who has approached rightly, whose mind is calm, who is endowed with calm (of the senses), the knowing one should teach in truth that knowledge of Brahman by which one knows the imperishable, the true Puruṣa.

Bhāṣya Vidvān: the teacher who knows Brahman. samyak: as the scripture lays down. praśāntacitta: free of pride and the like; śamānvita: with the outer senses withdrawn, turned away from everything. puruṣa: so called because it is full (pūrṇa) and lies in the city (puri śayana) of the body; satya: being by nature the highest truth, undecaying; akṣara: because it does not flow away, is not hurt, does not waste. provāca stands for prabrūyāt, ‘should teach’: the rule binds the teacher too, to bring across the great ocean of ignorance a good pupil who has come to him as is right.

द्वितीयमुण्डके प्रथमः खण्डः
Dvitīyamuṇḍake prathamaḥ khaṇḍaḥ · Muṇḍaka 2 · First khaṇḍa
तदेतत्सत्यं यथा सुदीप्तात्पावकाद्विस्फुलिङ्गाः सहस्रशः प्रभवन्ते सरूपाः । तथाक्षराद्विविधाः सोम्य भावाः प्रजायन्ते तत्र चैवापियन्ति ॥ १ ॥
tad etat satyaṃ yathā sudīptāt pāvakād visphuliṅgāḥ sahasraśaḥ prabhavante sarūpāḥ | tathākṣarād vividhāḥ somya bhāvāḥ prajāyante tatra caivāpiyanti || 1 ||
Padaccheda
तत् एतत् सत्यम् यथा सुदीप्तात् पावकात् विस्फुलिङ्गाः सहस्रशः प्रभवन्ते सरूपाः तथा अक्षरात् विविधाः सोम्य भावाः प्रजायन्ते तत्र च एव अपियन्ति
tat etat satyam yathā sudīptāt pāvakāt visphuliṅgāḥ sahasraśaḥ prabhavante sarūpāḥ tathā akṣarāt vividhāḥ somya bhāvāḥ prajāyante tatra ca eva apiyanti
Word by word
That is the truth: as from a blazing fire sparks of like form come forth by thousands, so, dear one, from the imperishable beings of many kinds (the jīvas) are born, and into it they return.

Introduction Introducing the khaṇḍa: all that the lower knowledge brings about has been told. The imperishable called Puruṣa, the true, from which as its root this round comes forth and into which it dissolves, and which being known all this is known, is the object of the higher knowledge of Brahman; that is now to be told.

Bhāṣya The ‘truth’ of the lower knowledge, the fruit of rites, is true only relatively; this is the object of the higher knowledge, being the highest reality; the other is untrue, being the object of ignorance. Since the imperishable is wholly beyond the senses, an example is given so that it may be grasped as if perceived. Sparks, parts of the fire, come forth from a well-kindled fire in thousands, of the same nature as fire. bhāvāḥ: the jīvas, various because they follow the variety of bodies that are their adjuncts; as the spaces enclosed by pots and the like arise as the pots arise, so the jīvas are born as the bodily adjuncts made of name and form arise, and dissolve into the imperishable as those adjuncts dissolve, as the enclosed spaces when the pots are gone. As space is the cause of the rise and end of the enclosed spaces only through the pots, so the imperishable is the cause of the birth and end of the jīvas only through the bodily adjuncts made by name and form.

दिव्यो ह्यमूर्तः पुरुषः सबाह्याभ्यन्तरो ह्यजः । अप्राणो ह्यमनाः शुभ्रो ह्यक्षरात्परतः परः ॥ २ ॥
divyo hy amūrtaḥ puruṣaḥ sabāhyābhyantaro hy ajaḥ | aprāṇo hy amanāḥ śubhro hy akṣarāt parataḥ paraḥ || 2 ||
Padaccheda
दिव्यः हि अमूर्तः पुरुषः स-बाह्य-अभ्यन्तरः हि अजः अप्राणः हि अमनाः शुभ्रः हि अक्षरात् परतः परः
divyaḥ hi amūrtaḥ puruṣaḥ sa-bāhya-abhyantaraḥ hi ajaḥ aprāṇaḥ hi amanāḥ śubhraḥ hi akṣarāt parataḥ paraḥ
Word by word
For Puruṣa is self-luminous, formless, both without and within, unborn, without prāṇa, without mind, pure, higher than the high imperishable (the unmanifest).

Introduction Now it states the very nature of the imperishable, free of every distinction made by adjuncts, without any form, like space; described as ‘not this, not this’: higher than the imperishable called the unmanifest, the seed of name and form, which is high only with regard to its own effects.

Bhāṣya Divyaḥ: shining, being self-luminous; or dwelling in its own self; or not of the world. amūrtaḥ: without any form. puruṣaḥ: full, or lying in the city of the body. sabāhyābhyantaraḥ: together with what is outside and inside. ajaḥ: not born from anything, since there is nothing besides it to cause its birth, as wind causes bubbles on water, or pots the divisions of space. Since all changes have birth at their root, denying birth denies them all: so it is also ageless, deathless, imperishable, constant, fearless.

Bhāṣya On aprāṇaḥ and amanāḥ: though, to those who see the differences of adjuncts, the body and the rest, it appears as having prāṇa, mind, senses and objects, as space appears to have a surface and dirt, to those who see its true nature it has no prāṇa, no moving air with its different powers of action, and no mind, made of intending and the rest, with its different powers of knowing. So all the breaths, the organs of action and their objects, intellect and mind, the organs of knowledge and their objects, are denied, as another text says, ‘it seems to think, it seems to move’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.3.7). Free of both kinds of adjunct, it is śubhra, pure. akṣarāt parataḥ paraḥ: ‘the imperishable’ is the unmanifest, marked by the adjunct of the seed of name and form, high because it is known as the seed of all effects and organs; higher than that high imperishable is Puruṣa without adjuncts, on which that imperishable, called space, is woven warp and woof.

Bhāṣya On how it is without prāṇa: if prāṇa and the rest existed in their own nature before creation, as Puruṣa does, Puruṣa would have prāṇa through them; but they do not; so the supreme Puruṣa is without them, as Devadatta is sonless while his son is not yet born.

एतस्माज्जायते प्राणो मनः सर्वेन्द्रियाणि च । खं वायुर्ज्योतिरापः पृथिवी विश्वस्य धारिणी ॥ ३ ॥
etasmāj jāyate prāṇo manaḥ sarvendriyāṇi ca | khaṃ vāyur jyotir āpaḥ pṛthivī viśvasya dhāriṇī || 3 ||
Padaccheda
एतस्मात् जायते प्राणः मनः सर्व-इन्द्रियाणि च खम् वायुः ज्योतिः आपः पृथिवी विश्वस्य धारिणी
etasmāt jāyate prāṇaḥ manaḥ sarva-indriyāṇi ca kham vāyuḥ jyotiḥ āpaḥ pṛthivī viśvasya dhāriṇī
Word by word
From him are born prāṇa, mind and all the senses; space, wind, fire, water, and the earth, the supporter of all.

Bhāṣya Why prāṇa and the rest are not (in him): from this Puruṣa, marked by the adjunct of the seed of name and form, is born prāṇa, an object of ignorance, a modification, a mere name, untrue (‘a modification is a name, resting on speech’, Chāndogya 6.1.4). The supreme does not come to have prāṇa by a prāṇa that is untrue and an object of ignorance, any more than a sonless man has a son through a son seen in a dream. So also mind, all the senses and their objects are born from him; hence it is proved that he is truly, not figuratively, without prāṇa and the rest. As they are not in truth before they arise, so too when they have dissolved.

Bhāṣya As the organs, so the elements, the causes of body and objects: kham, space; vāyu, the outer wind with its kinds, āvaha and the rest; jyotis, fire; āpaḥ, water; pṛthivī, the earth that bears all. These, with sound, touch, form, taste and smell, each having its own quality together with those of the ones before, are born from him.

अग्निर्मूर्धा चक्षुषी चन्द्रसूर्यौ दिशः श्रोत्रे वाग्विवृताश्च वेदाः । वायुः प्राणो हृदयं विश्वमस्य पद्भ्यां पृथिवी ह्येष सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा ॥ ४ ॥
agnir mūrdhā cakṣuṣī candrasūryau diśaḥ śrotre vāg vivṛtāś ca vedāḥ | vāyuḥ prāṇo hṛdayaṃ viśvam asya padbhyāṃ pṛthivī hy eṣa sarvabhūtāntarātmā || 4 ||
Padaccheda
अग्निः मूर्धा चक्षुषी चन्द्र-सूर्यौ दिशः श्रोत्रे वाक् विवृताः च वेदाः वायुः प्राणः हृदयम् विश्वम् अस्य पद्भ्याम् पृथिवी हि एषः सर्व-भूत-अन्तरात्मा
agniḥ mūrdhā cakṣuṣī candra-sūryau diśaḥ śrotre vāk vivṛtāḥ ca vedāḥ vāyuḥ prāṇaḥ hṛdayam viśvam asya padbhyām pṛthivī hi eṣaḥ sarva-bhūta-antarātmā
Word by word
Fire (heaven) is his head, the moon and the sun his eyes, the quarters his ears, the disclosed Vedas his speech, wind his prāṇa, the universe his heart; from his feet (came) the earth. He is the inner self of all beings.

Introduction Having stated briefly the imperishable, the true Puruṣa without qualities, the object of the higher knowledge, in ‘self-luminous, formless’ (2.1.2), the Upaniṣad goes on to state the same with qualities, at length; a thing told briefly and then at length is easily grasped, as in a sūtra and its commentary. Virāj, who is born within the egg from Hiraṇyagarbha, the first-born prāṇa, though known as other because other principles stand between, is also born from this Puruṣa and made of him: to show this the mantra describes him.

Bhāṣya Agni: the heavenly world (‘that world, Gautama, is a fire’, Chāndogya 5.4.1) is his head; the moon and the sun his eyes; ‘his’ is to be carried to each item. vāg vivṛtāś ca vedāḥ: his speech is the well-known Vedas, made manifest. hṛdayam: his inner organ is the whole world; for the world is only a modification of the inner organ, since it is seen to dissolve into the mind in deep sleep and in waking to spread out from it again like sparks from a fire. From his feet the earth was born. This god is Viṣṇu, the infinite, the first embodied one, whose body is the three worlds, the inner self of all beings: he it is who in all beings sees, hears, thinks and knows, the self of all the organs.

tasmād agniḥ samidho yasya sūryaḥ somāt parjanya oṣadhayaḥ pṛthivyām | pumān retaḥ siñcati yoṣitāyāṃ bahvīḥ prajāḥ puruṣāt saṃprasūtāḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मात् अग्निः समिधः यस्य सूर्यः सोमात् पर्जन्यः ओषधयः पृथिव्याम् पुमान् रेतः सिञ्चति योषितायाम् बह्वीः प्रजाः पुरुषात् संप्रसूताः
tasmāt agniḥ samidhaḥ yasya sūryaḥ somāt parjanyaḥ oṣadhayaḥ pṛthivyām pumān retaḥ siñcati yoṣitāyām bahvīḥ prajāḥ puruṣāt saṃprasūtāḥ

From him comes the fire (heaven) whose fuel is the sun; from soma (the moon) the rain-cloud; (from it) the plants on the earth; (from them) the man pours seed into the woman: so many creatures are born from Puruṣa.

Introduction The creatures that go round by way of the five fires (Chāndogya 5.4–8) also are born from that Puruṣa.

Bhāṣya From the supreme Puruṣa comes the fire that is a particular station of creatures, the heavenly world; its fuel is the sun, for the sun kindles heaven as fuel kindles fire. From the soma produced in that heavenly fire comes the rain-cloud, the second fire; from it the plants on the earth; from the plants, offered in the fire that is man and become his substance, the man-fire pours seed into the woman-fire. In this order many creatures, brāhmaṇas and the rest, are born from the supreme Puruṣa.

tasmāt ṛcaḥ sāma yajūṃṣi dīkṣā yajñāḥ ca sarve kratavaḥ dakṣiṇāḥ ca saṃvatsaraḥ ca yajamānaḥ ca lokāḥ somaḥ yatra pavate yatra sūryaḥ
Word by word
From him come the verses, the chants and the formulas, consecration, all sacrifices and rites (with sacrificial posts), and the priests' fees; the year, the sacrificer, and the worlds where the moon purifies and where the sun (shines).

Bhāṣya The means of rites and their fruits also come from him. ṛcaḥ: mantras with a fixed number of syllables and feet, in metres such as the gāyatrī; sāma: sung with five or seven parts, with stobhas and the like; yajūṃṣi: in the form of prose, without fixed syllables or feet: so the mantras of three kinds. dīkṣā: the special observances of the agent, the muñja girdle and the rest. yajñāḥ: the agnihotra and the rest; kratavaḥ: those with a sacrificial post. dakṣiṇāḥ: from one cow up to one's whole property without limit. saṃvatsaraḥ: time, as a part of the rite; yajamānaḥ: the agent. lokāḥ: the worlds that are the fruit of his rites, where soma purifies the worlds and where the sun burns: reached by the two paths, of the southern and the northern course, the fruits of those without knowledge and of those with it.

tasmāc ca devā bahudhā saṃprasūtāḥ sādhyā manuṣyāḥ paśavo vayāṃsi | prāṇāpānau vrīhiyavau tapaś ca śraddhā satyaṃ brahmacaryaṃ vidhiś ca || 7 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मात् च देवाः बहुधा संप्रसूताः साध्याः मनुष्याः पशवः वयांसि प्राण-अपानौ व्रीहि-यवौ तपः च श्रद्धा सत्यम् ब्रह्मचर्यम् विधिः च
tasmāt ca devāḥ bahudhā saṃprasūtāḥ sādhyāḥ manuṣyāḥ paśavaḥ vayāṃsi prāṇa-apānau vrīhi-yavau tapaḥ ca śraddhā satyam brahmacaryam vidhiḥ ca
Word by word
From him are born the gods in their many kinds, the Sādhyas, men, cattle and birds; prāṇa and apāna, rice and barley; austerity, faith, truth, chastity and rule.

Bhāṣya The gods who have a part in the rites, in many groups, the Vasus and the rest; the Sādhyas, a class of gods; men, who are entitled to rites; cattle, of village and forest; birds. prāṇāpānau: the life of men and the rest; vrīhiyavau: rice and barley, for oblations. tapas: part of the rite, which refines the man, and also an independent means to its own fruit. śraddhā: the clearness of mind and conviction that comes before every effort toward a human end. satyam: avoiding falsehood, saying things as they are without causing pain. brahmacarya: abstaining from sexual union. vidhiḥ: the manner of doing.

sapta prāṇāḥ prabhavanti tasmāt saptārciṣaḥ samidhaḥ sapta homāḥ | sapteme lokā yeṣu caranti prāṇā guhāśayā nihitāḥ sapta sapta || 8 ||
Padaccheda
सप्त प्राणाः प्रभवन्ति तस्मात् सप्त अर्चिषः समिधः सप्त होमाः सप्त इमे लोकाः येषु चरन्ति प्राणाः गुहा-शयाः निहिताः सप्त सप्त
sapta prāṇāḥ prabhavanti tasmāt sapta arciṣaḥ samidhaḥ sapta homāḥ sapta ime lokāḥ yeṣu caranti prāṇāḥ guhā-śayāḥ nihitāḥ sapta sapta
Word by word
From him come forth the seven prāṇas (the senses of the head), their seven flames, seven fuels (their objects), seven offerings (their cognitions), and these seven worlds (their seats) in which the prāṇas move, lying in the cave, placed seven by seven (in each creature).

Bhāṣya Sapta prāṇāḥ: the seven in the head. arciṣaḥ: their lights, each showing its own object. samidhaḥ: the seven objects, for the senses are kindled by objects. homāḥ: their cognitions of the objects (‘his knowing is what he offers’, Taittirīya Nārāyaṇa 80). lokāḥ: the seats of the senses, in which the prāṇas move; this qualifies the prāṇas, to show that prāṇa, apāna and the other breaths are not meant. guhāśayāḥ: lying in the cave, the body or the heart, in sleep. nihitāḥ: set by the creator, seven by seven, in each creature.

Bhāṣya Summing up: the rites of the knowing, who sacrifice to the Self, with their fruits, and the rites of the unknowing, with their means and fruits, all come forth from the supreme, all-knowing Puruṣa: that is the sense of the section.

What is this universe? Action, the agnihotra and the rest; tapas, knowledge; and the fruit made by them: all this is the effect of Brahman. Introduction Introducing the khaṇḍa: how is the imperishable, which is without form, to be known?

Bhāṣya Āviḥ: shining forth. saṃnihitam: through the adjuncts, speech and the rest, it appears as if perceiving sound and the other objects (‘it blazes, it shines’, says another text); manifest through the properties of its adjuncts, seeing, hearing, thinking, knowing, it is perceived in the heart of all beings, set firmly there. guhācaram nāma: it is known as ‘moving in the cave’ because it moves there in the forms of seeing, hearing and the rest. mahat: being greater than all; padam: what is reached by all, the ground of all things. Why so great an abode: because in it all is set, as spokes in the hub of a wheel: what moves, birds and the like; what breathes, men, cattle and the like, with prāṇa and apāna; what winks; and, by ‘ca’, what does not wink.

Bhāṣya Jānatha: know this ground of all, pupils, as your own Self. sadasat: it is of the nature of being and non-being, the formed and the formless, gross and subtle, for apart from it they are not. vareṇyam: to be sought, for it alone, being eternal, is to be desired by all. paraṃ vijñānāt prajānām: beyond the knowledge of creatures, out of reach of worldly knowing. variṣṭham: best of all excellent things, for Brahman alone is free of every fault.

हिरण्मये परे कोशे विरजम् ब्रह्म निष्कलम् तत् शुभ्रम् ज्योतिषाम् ज्योतिः तत् यत् आत्म-विदः विदुः

In the highest golden sheath (the light of the understanding) is the stainless Brahman, without parts. It is pure, the light of lights; it is what the knowers of the Self know.

Bhāṣya This and the next two mantras sum up what has been said. hiraṇmaye: shining, the light of the knowing of the intellect; kośe: a sheath, as of a sword, being the place where the nature of the Self is realized; pare: highest, being innermost of all. virajam: free of the dust and stain of ignorance and every other fault; brahma: being greatest and the Self of all; niṣkalam: from which all parts have gone, partless. jyotiṣāṃ jyotiḥ: the light even of fire and the other lights; their being lights is owed to the light of consciousness of Brahman, the Self within them; that is the supreme light, the light of the Self, which no other lights. ātmavidaḥ: the discerning who know their own Self as the witness of the cognitions of sound and other objects know it, following the inward cognition; not others, who follow outward things.

na tatra sūryo bhāti na candratārakaṃ nemā vidyuto bhānti kuto 'yam agniḥ | tam eva bhāntam anubhāti sarvaṃ tasya bhāsā sarvam idaṃ vibhāti || 11 ||
Padaccheda
न तत्र सूर्यः भाति न चन्द्र-तारकम् न इमाः विद्युतः भान्ति कुतः अयम् अग्निः तम् एव भान्तम् अनुभाति सर्वम् तस्य भासा सर्वम् इदम् विभाति
na tatra sūryaḥ bhāti na candra-tārakam na imāḥ vidyutaḥ bhānti kutaḥ ayam agniḥ tam eva bhāntam anubhāti sarvam tasya bhāsā sarvam idam vibhāti
Word by word
There the sun does not shine, nor the moon and stars; these lightnings do not shine; how then this fire? Everything shines after him alone as he shines; by his light all this shines.

Bhāṣya How it is the light of lights: in Brahman, his own Self, the sun, though it lights all, does not shine, that is, does not light Brahman; for it lights all else, the not-self, by Brahman's light, and has no power of its own to light it. So with the moon and stars and lightning; how then this fire within our reach? All this world that shines shines after the supreme Lord, who shines, being light by nature; as water or a firebrand burns after the fire that burns, by contact with it, not of itself. Since Brahman shines and shines variously through the varied light in its effects, its being light by nature is known; what has no light of its own cannot give light to another: pots and the like are not seen to light others, but the sun and other luminous things are.

brahmaivedam amṛtaṃ purastād brahma paścād brahma dakṣiṇataś cottareṇa | adhaś cordhvaṃ ca prasṛtaṃ brahmaivedaṃ viśvam idaṃ variṣṭham || 12 ||
Padaccheda
ब्रह्म एव इदम् अमृतम् पुरस्तात् ब्रह्म पश्चात् ब्रह्म दक्षिणतः च उत्तरेण अधः च ऊर्ध्वम् च प्रसृतम् ब्रह्म एव इदम् विश्वम् इदम् वरिष्ठम्
brahma eva idam amṛtam purastāt brahma paścāt brahma dakṣiṇataḥ ca uttareṇa adhaḥ ca ūrdhvam ca prasṛtam brahma eva idam viśvam idam variṣṭham
Word by word
Brahman alone, the immortal, is this (that appears) in front; Brahman behind, Brahman to the south and to the north; spread out below and above. Brahman alone is this universe, the best.

Bhāṣya Brahman, the light of lights, alone is true; all else is its modification, a mere name resting on speech, untrue: this, set out at length with reasons, is summed up by this mantra, which stands as the conclusion. What appears in front, as if it were not Brahman, to those whose sight is ignorance, is Brahman alone; so behind, to the south, to the north, below and above: what spreads out everywhere in the form of effects, appearing as if other, with name and form. In a word, this whole world is Brahman alone, the best. Every notion of what is not Brahman is mere ignorance, like the notion of a snake in a rope; Brahman alone, one, is the truth in the highest sense: this is the teaching of the Veda.

Padaccheda
द्वा सुपर्णा सयुजा सखाया समानम् वृक्षम् परिषस्वजाते तयोः अन्यः पिप्पलम् स्वादु अत्ति अनश्नन् अन्यः अभिचाकशीति
dvā suparṇā sayujā sakhāyā samānam vṛkṣam pariṣasvajāte tayoḥ anyaḥ pippalam svādu atti anaśnan anyaḥ abhicākaśīti
Word by word
Two birds, companions and friends, cling to the same tree. Of the two, one eats the sweet fruit; the other, not eating, looks on.

Introduction Introducing the muṇḍaka: the higher knowledge has been told, by which the imperishable, true Puruṣa is attained, whose attainment utterly destroys the knot of the heart and the other causes of the round; and the yoga that is the means of seeing it has been told under the figure of taking up the bow and the rest. Now the means that help it, truth and the others, are to be told; and the truth is settled again, chiefly, in another way. Because it is so very hard to fathom, a mantra that is like a sūtra is set down to settle the highest reality, though this has already been done.

Bhāṣya Suparṇā: of beautiful flight, or simply birds. sayujā: always joined together. sakhāyā: of the same name, with the same cause of manifestation. samānaṃ vṛkṣam: one and the same tree, as the seat where both are perceived: the body, called a tree because it is cut down; they cling to it, like two birds to a tree, for the enjoying of its fruit. This is the aśvattha tree with its root above and its branches below, sprung from the root that is the unmanifest, called the field, the seat of the fruits of the actions of all beings. The two that cling to it, like birds, are the self whose adjunct is the subtle body, the seat of the impressions of ignorance, desire and action, and the Lord.

Bhāṣya Of the two, one, the knower of the field, resting on the tree of the subtle body, eats without discernment the fruit produced by action, pleasure and pain, sweet with many varied tastes. The other, the Lord, ever pure, conscious and free by nature, all-knowing, whose adjunct is sattva, does not eat; he impels both the eaten and the eater by his mere being as the eternal witness; not eating, he only looks on, for his impelling is only seeing, like a king's.

समाने वृक्षे पुरुषो निमग्नोऽनीशया शोचति मुह्यमानः । जुष्टं यदा पश्यत्यन्यमीशमस्य महिमानमिति वीतशोकः ॥ २ ॥
samāne vṛkṣe puruṣo nimagno 'nīśayā śocati muhyamānaḥ | juṣṭaṃ yadā paśyaty anyam īśam asya mahimānam iti vītaśokaḥ || 2 ||
Padaccheda
समाने वृक्षे पुरुषः निमग्नः अनीशया शोचति मुह्यमानः जुष्टम् यदा पश्यति अन्यम् ईशम् अस्य महिमानम् इति वीत-शोकः
samāne vṛkṣe puruṣaḥ nimagnaḥ anīśayā śocati muhyamānaḥ juṣṭam yadā paśyati anyam īśam asya mahimānam iti vīta-śokaḥ
Word by word
On the same tree a man (the jīva), sunk (in it), grieves, bewildered by his helplessness. When he sees the other, the Lord, worshipped (by many), and (knows) ‘this greatness is his’, he is freed from grief.

Bhāṣya On that tree, the body, the enjoyer, the jīva, weighed down by the heavy load of ignorance, desire, the fruits of action, attachment and the rest, sinks like a gourd in the sea: he takes the body for himself, thinking ‘this is I, the son of that man, the grandson of this one, lean, stout, with qualities, without them, happy, unhappy’, and that there is nothing else; he is born and dies, and is joined to and parted from kin and friends. anīśā: helplessness, the wretchedness of ‘I can do nothing; my son is lost, my wife is dead, what is my life to me?’ By it he grieves, bewildered, falling into inner anxiety through many kinds of ill, for want of discernment.

Bhāṣya On the same: going round in this way through the births of ghost, animal, man and the rest, at some time, by the force of pure dharma gathered over many births, he is shown the path of yoga by some most compassionate one; endowed with non-violence, truth, chastity, the renouncing of all, calm, restraint and the rest, his self collected, he sees, meditating, the other, unlike the self marked by the adjunct of the tree: the Lord, not in the round, beyond hunger and thirst, grief and delusion, age and death, ruler of the whole world, worshipped by the many paths of yoga and by men of rites. ‘I am this Self, the same in all, dwelling in all beings, not the other, bounded by the adjuncts that ignorance has made, which is illusion; and this greatness, the world, is the glory of me, the supreme Lord’: when he so sees, he is freed from the whole ocean of grief: he has done what was to be done.

Padaccheda
यदा पश्यः पश्यते रुक्म-वर्णम् कर्तारम् ईशम् पुरुषम् ब्रह्म-योनिम् तदा विद्वान् पुण्य-पापे विधूय निरञ्जनः परमम् साम्यम् उपैति
yadā paśyaḥ paśyate rukma-varṇam kartāram īśam puruṣam brahma-yonim tadā vidvān puṇya-pāpe vidhūya nirañjanaḥ paramam sāmyam upaiti
Word by word
When the seer sees the maker, the Lord, Puruṣa, of golden hue (self-luminous), Brahman the source (of all), then the knower, shaking off merit and demerit, stainless, attains the supreme sameness (that is non-duality).

Bhāṣya Another mantra states the same at more length. paśyaḥ: the seer, the knowing seeker. rukmavarṇa: self-luminous by nature, or with an undying light like that of gold. The maker of the whole world, the Lord, Puruṣa. brahmayoni: Brahman and the source; or the source of the lower Brahman (Brahmā). Then the knower, shaking off, burning away with their root, merit and demerit, the actions that bind, becomes stainless, without taint, free of the afflictions, and attains the supreme, unsurpassed sameness, non-duality; for all sameness within duality falls short of it, so this supreme sameness is non-duality.

प्राणो ह्येष यः सर्वभूतैर्विभाति विजानन्विद्वान्भवते नातिवादी । आत्मक्रीड आत्मरतिः क्रियावानेष ब्रह्मविदां वरिष्ठः ॥ ४ ॥
prāṇo hy eṣa yaḥ sarvabhūtair vibhāti vijānan vidvān bhavate nātivādī | ātmakrīḍa ātmaratiḥ kriyāvān eṣa brahmavidāṃ variṣṭhaḥ || 4 ||
Padaccheda
प्राणः हि एषः यः सर्व-भूतैः विभाति विजानन् विद्वान् भवते न अतिवादी आत्म-क्रीडः आत्म-रतिः क्रियावान् एषः ब्रह्म-विदाम् वरिष्ठः
prāṇaḥ hi eṣaḥ yaḥ sarva-bhūtaiḥ vibhāti vijānan vidvān bhavate na ativādī ātma-krīḍaḥ ātma-ratiḥ kriyāvān eṣaḥ brahma-vidām variṣṭhaḥ
Word by word
This is prāṇa (the prāṇa of prāṇa), who shines as all beings. Knowing him, the wise man does not speak of anything beyond. Playing in the Self, delighting in the Self, active (in knowledge and meditation): he is the best of the knowers of Brahman.

Bhāṣya Prāṇa: the supreme Lord, the prāṇa of prāṇa, the subject here, who shines variously in all beings, from Brahmā down to a tuft of grass, that is, as their self; the instrumental marks the way he is. ativādin: one whose habit is to speak going beyond all others. One who knows this prāṇa of prāṇa directly as ‘I am this’, not by the mere understanding of a sentence, is not an ativādin: when it is seen that all is the Self and nothing else is, what is there to speak of beyond? He who sees something other speaks going beyond it; this knower sees nothing other than the Self, hears nothing other, knows nothing other.

Bhāṣya Ātmakrīḍa: whose play is in the Self alone, not in wife, sons and the rest; ātmarati: whose delight is in the Self alone. Play needs outward means; delight is mere joy and needs none: that is the difference. kriyāvān: active in knowledge, meditation, dispassion and the like. If the words are read as one compound, ‘one whose action is delight in the Self’, either the bahuvrīhi or the possessive suffix is redundant.

Bhāṣya On an objection: some take kriyāvān to teach the joining of the agnihotra and other rites with the knowledge of Brahman. That conflicts with the plain words ‘he is the best of the knowers of Brahman’; one active in outward rites cannot be one who plays and delights in the Self; only one who has turned from outward action plays in the Self, for the two are opposed, as darkness and light cannot be in one place together. So the teaching of a joining of knowledge and rites here is idle talk; and the Upaniṣad says ‘give up other words’ (2.2.5) and ‘by the yoga of renunciation’ (3.2.6). The active one here is the renouncer active in knowledge, meditation and the like, who keeps the bounds unbroken; such a one, not speaking beyond, playing and delighting in the Self, active, established in Brahman, is the chief of all the knowers of Brahman.

सत्येन लभ्यस्तपसा ह्येष आत्मा सम्यग्ज्ञानेन ब्रह्मचर्येण नित्यम् । अन्तःशरीरे ज्योतिर्मयो हि शुभ्रो यं पश्यन्ति यतयः क्षीणदोषाः ॥ ५ ॥

Padaccheda
सत्येन लभ्यः तपसा हि एषः आत्मा सम्यक्-ज्ञानेन ब्रह्मचर्येण नित्यम् अन्तः-शरीरे ज्योतिर्मयः हि शुभ्रः यम् पश्यन्ति यतयः क्षीण-दोषाः
satyena labhyaḥ tapasā hi eṣaḥ ātmā samyak-jñānena brahmacaryeṇa nityam antaḥ-śarīre jyotirmayaḥ hi śubhraḥ yam paśyanti yatayaḥ kṣīṇa-doṣāḥ
Word by word
This Self is to be gained by truth, by austerity, by right knowledge, by chastity, (practised) always: (the Self) within the body, made of light, pure, whom the renouncers see, their faults destroyed.

Bhāṣya Now truth and the other means that help right knowledge are enjoined for the mendicant, all of them chiefly turning away. satyena: by giving up untruth, false speech. tapasā: by the one-pointing of senses and mind (‘the one-pointedness of mind and senses is the highest austerity’, Mokṣadharma 250.4); this, favourable to the Self by turning toward the seeing of it, is the highest austerity, not the cāndrāyaṇa and the like. samyagjñāna: the seeing of the Self as it is; brahmacarya: abstaining from sexual union. nityam: always; it is to be joined to each, like a lamp set within, ‘always by truth, always by austerity, always by right knowledge’ (so too ‘in whom there is no crookedness, no untruth, no deceit’, Praśna 1.16).

Bhāṣya Where is the Self to be gained by these means? Within the body, in the space of the lotus of the heart, made of light, of golden hue, pure: whom the yatis, renouncers given to striving, see, their faults, anger and the other stains of mind, destroyed. That Self is gained by renouncers through truth and the other means practised always, not by truth and the rest practised now and then. This is said in praise of truth and the other means.

सत्यमेव जयते नानृतं सत्येन पन्था विततो देवयानः । येनाक्रमन्त्यृषयो ह्याप्तकामा यत्र तत्सत्यस्य परमं निधानम् ॥ ६ ॥
satyam eva jayate nānṛtaṃ satyena panthā vitato devayānaḥ | yenākramanty ṛṣayo hy āptakāmā yatra tat satyasya paramaṃ nidhānam || 6 ||
Padaccheda
सत्यम् एव जयते न अनृतम् सत्येन पन्थाः विततः देवयानः येन आक्रमन्ति ऋषयः हि आप्त-कामाः यत्र तत् सत्यस्य परमम् निधानम्
satyam eva jayate na anṛtam satyena panthāḥ vitataḥ devayānaḥ yena ākramanti ṛṣayaḥ hi āpta-kāmāḥ yatra tat satyasya paramam nidhānam
Word by word
Truth alone wins, not untruth. By truth is laid out the path, the way of the gods, by which the seers, their desires fulfilled, ascend to where that supreme treasure of truth is.

Bhāṣya Satyam: the truthful man wins, not the liar; truth and untruth by themselves, not resting in a person, can neither win nor lose. It is well known in the world that the liar is overcome by the truthful, not the reverse; so truth is a strong means. Scripture too shows that truth is the best of means: by truth, by keeping to speech that tells things as they are, the path called the way of the gods is laid out, spread without a break; by it the seers, men of vision, free of deceit, illusion, guile, egoism, pretence and untruth, their thirst gone, ascend to where that highest reality is, the end to be gained by truth, the best of means, the supreme treasure, stored there as the human goal.

बृहच्च तद्दिव्यमचिन्त्यरूपं सूक्ष्माच्च तत्सूक्ष्मतरं विभाति । दूरात्सुदूरे तदिहान्तिके च पश्यत्स्विहैव निहितं गुहायाम् ॥ ७ ॥
bṛhac ca tad divyam acintyarūpaṃ sūkṣmāc ca tat sūkṣmataraṃ vibhāti | dūrāt sudūre tad ihāntike ca paśyatsv ihaiva nihitaṃ guhāyām || 7 ||
Padaccheda
बृहत् च तत् दिव्यम् अचिन्त्य-रूपम् सूक्ष्मात् च तत् सूक्ष्मतरम् विभाति दूरात् सुदूरे तत् इह अन्तिके च पश्यत्सु इह एव निहितम् गुहायाम्
bṛhat ca tat divyam acintya-rūpam sūkṣmāt ca tat sūkṣmataram vibhāti dūrāt sudūre tat iha antike ca paśyatsu iha eva nihitam guhāyām
Word by word
Great is that, self-luminous, of unthinkable form; subtler than the subtle, it shines (in many forms). It is farther than the far, and near, here (in the body); here, in those who see, it is set in the cave (of the intellect).

Bhāṣya What it is and what its nature: bṛhat: great, this Brahman, the subject, to be gained by truth and the other means, since it pervades all. divyam: self-luminous, beyond the senses; so its form cannot be thought. Subtler than space and the other subtle things, for its subtlety is unsurpassed, being the cause of all. vibhāti: it shines variously in the forms of sun, moon and the rest. It is farther than the far, wholly out of reach of those who do not know; and near, here in the body, being the Self of those who know, and the innermost of all (‘within space’, says scripture). paśyatsu: in conscious beings, it is set, perceived by yogins as the one who sees and the rest; in the cave, the intellect, where the knowers mark it hidden; though it is there, those who do not know, covered by ignorance, do not mark it.

He is not grasped by the eye, nor by speech, nor by the other senses, nor by austerity or rites. When one is pure in mind by the clearness of knowledge, then, meditating, one sees him, without parts.

Bhāṣya Again the means proper to it alone is told. He is not grasped by the eye, being without form; nor by speech, not being nameable; nor by the other devas, the senses; though austerity is the means to gain all things, he is not grasped by it, nor by Vedic rites, the agnihotra and the rest, however great. jñānaprasāda: knowledge, the knowing of all beings, though by nature able to make the Self known, does not make known the principle of the Self, ever present, while it is unclear and impure, stained by attachment to outward objects and the like, as a mirror covered with dirt or water stirred up. When the stain of attachment born of contact of senses with objects is taken away, and it stands clear, clean and still like a mirror or water, that is the clearness of knowledge. By it one becomes pure in the inner organ, fit to see Brahman; then, meditating with a one-pointed mind, having truth and the other means and having withdrawn the senses, he sees that Self, free of all division of parts.

To him the Self reveals its own supreme nature, hidden by ignorance; it shows itself when knowledge is there, as a pot appears when there is light. So praying for the Self alone, giving up all else, is the means of gaining it.

Bhāṣya Vedāntavijñāna: the knowledge born of the Vedānta; its meaning, the supreme Self to be known, they have well ascertained. saṃnyāsayoga: the yoga that is the giving up of all rites, resting in Brahman alone; by it they are yatis, given to striving, pure in mind. parāntakāla: the times of death of those in the round are ‘ends’ that are not final; compared with them, for seekers of liberation the time of leaving the body at the close of the round is the final end. brahmalokeṣu: Brahman itself is the world, brahmaloka; the plural is because the seekers are many, and the one is seen and reached as if many; so ‘in Brahman’. parāmṛtāḥ: those whose self is the supreme immortal, Brahman, become Brahman while still living. parimucyanti: they cease on every side, like the going out of a lamp or the space in a broken pot; all of them are freed, and need not go to another place.

Bhāṣya On the same: so scripture and tradition: ‘as the track of birds in the sky or of a fish in water is not seen, so is the going of those who know’ (Mokṣadharma 181.9); ‘not travelling the roads, they cross to the far shore of the roads’. Going bounded by place belongs to the round alone, being the effect of limited means; Brahman, being all, is not to be reached by going to a place. If Brahman were bounded by place it would, like a solid thing, have a beginning and an end, depend on another, have parts, and be impermanent and made; Brahman cannot be such; so reaching it cannot be bounded by place either.

Bhāṣya The knowers of Brahman hold liberation to be only the removal of the bondage of the round, ignorance and the rest, not something produced. At liberation the parts that made up the body, prāṇa and the rest, go to their own sources, each into its cause; pratiṣṭhāḥ is accusative plural. They are the well-known fifteen named in the last question (Praśna 6.4). The gods resting in the body, in the eye and the other organs, go into their corresponding deities, the sun and the rest. karmāṇi: the actions done by the seeker whose fruit has not begun, for those whose fruit has begun are spent by being undergone. vijñānamaya ātmā: the self that, taking as itself the intellect and other adjuncts made by ignorance, has entered here into the various bodies like the reflection of the sun in water and the like; called ‘consisting of understanding’, mostly made of it, since actions are for its fruit. Bhāṣya In his family no one is born who does not know Brahman. He crosses grief, the pain of mind from the loss of many cherished things, already while living; he crosses evil, called dharma and adharma; freed from the knots of the cave, the knots of ignorance in the heart, he becomes immortal, as was said in ‘the knot of the heart is cut’ (2.2.9).

तदेतदृचाभ्युक्तम् । क्रियावन्तः श्रोत्रिया ब्रह्मनिष्ठाः स्वयं जुह्वत एकर्षिं श्रद्धयन्तः । तेषामेवैतां ब्रह्मविद्यां वदेत शिरोव्रतं विधिवद्यैस्तु चीर्णम् ॥ १० ॥
tad etad ṛcābhyuktam | kriyāvantaḥ śrotriyā brahmaniṣṭhāḥ svayaṃ juhvata ekarṣiṃ śraddhayantaḥ | teṣām evaitāṃ brahmavidyāṃ vadeta śirovrataṃ vidhivad yais tu cīrṇam || 10 ||
Padaccheda
तत् एतत् ऋचा अभ्युक्तम् क्रियावन्तः श्रोत्रियाः ब्रह्म-निष्ठाः स्वयम् जुह्वते एकर्षिम् श्रद्धयन्तः तेषाम् एव एताम् ब्रह्म-विद्याम् वदेत शिरः-व्रतम् विधिवत् यैः तु चीर्णम्
tat etat ṛcā abhyuktam kriyāvantaḥ śrotriyāḥ brahma-niṣṭhāḥ svayam juhvate ekarṣim śraddhayantaḥ teṣām eva etām brahma-vidyām vadeta śiraḥ-vratam vidhivat yaiḥ tu cīrṇam
Word by word
This is declared by a verse: ‘To those alone who perform the rites, are versed in the Veda and devoted to (the lower) Brahman, who themselves, with faith, offer into the fire called Ekarṣi, and by whom the vow of the head has been duly kept, one should tell this knowledge of Brahman.’

Introduction Now the Upaniṣad closes by showing the rule for handing on the knowledge of Brahman; that rule is declared by a verse.

Bhāṣya Kriyāvantaḥ: engaged in doing the rites described. brahmaniṣṭhāḥ: given to the lower Brahman and wishing to know the higher. ekarṣi: the fire named Ekarṣi, into which they themselves offer with faith. To them alone, refined in self and fit vessels, one should tell this knowledge of Brahman; and to those by whom the vow of the head, the carrying of fire on the head, has been duly kept, as is well known among the followers of the Atharvaveda as their Vedic vow.

तदेतत्सत्यमृषिरङ्गिराः पुरोवाच नैतदचीर्णव्रतोऽधीते । नमः परमऋषिभ्यो नमः परमऋषिभ्यः ॥ ११ ॥
tad etat satyam ṛṣir aṅgirāḥ purovāca naitad acīrṇavrato 'dhīte | namaḥ paramaṛṣibhyo namaḥ paramaṛṣibhyaḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
तत् एतत् सत्यम् ऋषिः अङ्गिराः पुरा उवाच न एतत् अचीर्ण-व्रतः अधीते नमः परम-ऋषिभ्यः नमः परम-ऋषिभ्यः
tat etat satyam ṛṣiḥ aṅgirāḥ purā uvāca na etat acīrṇa-vrataḥ adhīte namaḥ parama-ṛṣibhyaḥ namaḥ parama-ṛṣibhyaḥ
Word by word
This truth the seer Aṅgiras told of old (to Śaunaka). One who has not kept the vow does not study it. Homage to the supreme seers! Homage to the supreme seers!

Bhāṣya This imperishable, true Puruṣa the seer Aṅgiras of old told to Śaunaka, who had approached him in due form and asked; so too another should tell it, in the same way, to one who seeks the good, seeks liberation, and has approached him in due form. One who has not kept the vow does not study this even as a text; for only to one who has kept the vow is the knowledge refined to bear fruit. The knowledge of Brahman is complete. Homage to the supreme seers, Brahmā and the others, from whom it has come down in succession, who saw the supreme Brahman directly and knew it; homage again. The repetition is out of great reverence and marks the end of the Upaniṣad.

Word by word
Om. May we hear with our ears what is auspicious, O gods; may we see with our eyes what is auspicious, O you who are worthy of worship. Praising you with firm limbs and bodies, may we attain the life that the gods have appointed. May Indra of great fame grant us well-being; may Pūṣan who knows all grant us well-being; may Tārkṣya of the unharmed wheel-rim grant us well-being; may Bṛhaspati grant us well-being. Om. Peace, peace, peace.
