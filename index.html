ग्रन्थालयः

Vedic Space Granthālaya

Sanskrit texts, verse by verse

The Granthālaya is the library of Vedic Space: a place to read Sanskrit texts slowly, one verse at a time, with what is needed to understand each word.

Contents
Every verse is given in Devanāgarī and in IAST transliteration, with the padaccheda, a word-by-word meaning carrying the lemma and grammar of each word, and a plain English translation. Notes mark the places where a commentator's reading shapes the meaning. The library now holds 11 texts and 1,335 verses.
Sources
The Sanskrit texts and their grammatical analysis come from the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit (Oliver Hellwig). Śaṅkara's bhāṣya is read in the GRETIL e-texts. Bhaja Govindam is transcribed from the Srirangam edition of Śaṅkara's works (1910), which is in the public domain. Dictionary entries are those of Monier-Williams and Apte, from the Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries. The word analysis, meanings and translations are drafts prepared by Vedic Space with the help of AI (Claude), and are marked on each page as awaiting review; where a text has Śaṅkara's bhāṣya, they follow it. Each page names its sources and the people behind them, with links back to the original files.
Licence
The Granthālaya is free to read and is for non-commercial use only. Vedic Space's additions (Devanāgarī rendering, padaccheda, word-by-word meanings and translations) are released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; each source keeps its own licence, named on the page that uses it.
Navigation
Choose a text from the catalogue below. On a page, three switches show or hide the IAST, the padaccheda and the word-by-word meaning. A word in the word-by-word section opens its dictionary entry. Focus mode shows one verse at a time; longer texts have a section list in the toolbar.
Intention
To keep the reader close to the Sanskrit itself: to read, to chant and to contemplate. The translations are plain and stay near the words of the text. The library grows as each text is prepared and reviewed.

ऐतरेयोपनिषद्Aitareya Upaniṣad

Ṛgveda · three adhyāyas, 33 mantras · following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

In the beginning this was the Self alone: the creation of the worlds, their guardians and food, and the Self's entry into the body; the three births of man and Vāmadeva's knowledge in the womb; and the Self as consciousness, which is Brahman.

ईशावास्योपनिषद्Īśa Upaniṣad

Śukla Yajurveda · 18 mantras · following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

All this is to be covered by the Lord: the Self that is one and unmoving, the two paths of knowledge and of rites, and the prayer of the dying worshipper.

कठोपनिषद्Kaṭha Upaniṣad

Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · six vallīs, 119 verses · following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

Naciketas goes to the house of Death and asks what becomes of a person after dying; Yama's answer is the teaching of the Self.

केनोपनिषद्Kena Upaniṣad

Sāmaveda · four khaṇḍas, 35 mantras · following Śaṅkara's pada-bhāṣya

By whom is the mind sent forth? Brahman as the ear of the ear and the mind of the mind, known in each cognition; the story of the gods, the venerable being and Umā; and the footing of the teaching in austerity, restraint and truth.

माण्डूक्योपनिषद्Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

Atharvaveda · 12 mantras, with Gauḍapāda's 29 kārikās · following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

The syllable Om as all that is, and the four quarters of the Self: waking, dream, deep sleep and the fourth, which is the Self to be known. Gauḍapāda's kārikās of the Āgama-prakaraṇa stand in their places among the mantras.

मुण्डकोपनिषद्Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad

Atharvaveda · three muṇḍakas, 65 mantras · following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

Śaunaka asks what, being known, makes all known: the lower knowledge of the Vedas and their rites, and the higher, by which the imperishable is reached; the bow of Om; the two birds on one tree; and the knower who becomes Brahman.

प्रश्नोपनिषद्Praśna Upaniṣad

Atharvaveda · six praśnas, 67 mantras · following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

Six seekers put six questions to Pippalāda: on the origin of creatures, on prāṇa, on sleep and dream, on Om, and on the Puruṣa of sixteen parts.

तैत्तिरीयोपनिषद्Taittirīya Upaniṣad

Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · three vallīs, 31 anuvākas · following Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

Pronunciation, the great conjunctions and the teacher's parting charge; Brahman as real, knowledge, infinite, and the five selves from food to bliss; Bhṛgu's search, by austerity, for Brahman as bliss.

भगवद्गीताBhagavad Gītā
