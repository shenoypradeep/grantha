द्वितीयोऽध्यायः
Dvitīyo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 2 · Sāṃkhyayoga
संजय उवाचsaṃjaya uvāca
तं तथा कृपयाविष्टमश्रुपूर्णाकुलेक्षणम् । विषीदन्तमिदं वाक्यमुवाच मधुसूदनः ॥ १ ॥
taṃ tathā kṛpayāviṣṭam aśrupūrṇākulekṣaṇam | viṣīdantam idaṃ vākyam uvāca madhusūdanaḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
तम् तथा कृपया आविष्टम् अश्रु-पूर्ण-आकुल-ईक्षणम् विषीदन्तम् इदम् वाक्यम् उवाच मधुसूदनः
tam tathā kṛpayā āviṣṭam aśru-pūrṇa-ākula-īkṣaṇam viṣīdantam idam vākyam uvāca madhusūdanaḥ
Word by word
Saṃjaya said: To him who was thus overcome by compassion, despairing, his troubled eyes filled with tears, Madhusūdana spoke these words:
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् । अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥ २ ॥
kutas tvā kaśmalam idaṃ viṣame samupasthitam | anāryajuṣṭam asvargyam akīrtikaram arjuna || 2 ||
Padaccheda
कुतः त्वा कश्मलम् इदम् विषमे समुपस्थितम् अन्-आर्य-जुष्टम् अ-स्वर्ग्यम् अ-कीर्ति-करम् अर्जुन
kutaḥ tvā kaśmalam idam viṣame samupasthitam an-ārya-juṣṭam a-svargyam a-kīrti-karam arjuna
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: Whence has this faintness of heart come upon you at this time of crisis, O Arjuna? It is unworthy of a noble man, it does not lead to heaven, and it brings disgrace.
क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते । क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परंतप ॥ ३ ॥
klaibyaṃ mā sma gamaḥ pārtha naitat tvayy upapadyate | kṣudraṃ hṛdayadaurbalyaṃ tyaktvottiṣṭha paraṃtapa || 3 ||
Padaccheda
क्लैब्यम् मा स्म गमः पार्थ न एतत् त्वयि उपपद्यते क्षुद्रम् हृदय-दौर्बल्यम् त्यक्त्वा उत्तिष्ठ परंतप
klaibyam mā sma gamaḥ pārtha na etat tvayi upapadyate kṣudram hṛdaya-daurbalyam tyaktvā uttiṣṭha paraṃtapa
Word by word
Do not yield to unmanliness, O Pārtha; it does not befit you. Cast off this petty weakness of heart and arise, O scorcher of foes.
अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
कथं भीष्ममहं संख्ये द्रोणं च मधुसूदन । इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजार्हावरिसूदन ॥ ४ ॥
kathaṃ bhīṣmam ahaṃ saṃkhye droṇaṃ ca madhusūdana | iṣubhiḥ pratiyotsyāmi pūjārhāv arisūdana || 4 ||
Padaccheda
कथम् भीष्मम् अहम् संख्ये द्रोणम् च मधुसूदन इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजा-अर्हौ अरि-सूदन
katham bhīṣmam aham saṃkhye droṇam ca madhusūdana iṣubhiḥ pratiyotsyāmi pūjā-arhau ari-sūdana
Word by word
Arjuna said: How shall I fight in battle with arrows against Bhīṣma and Droṇa, O Madhusūdana, who are worthy of worship, O slayer of enemies?
गुरूनहत्वा हि महानुभावाञ्श्रेयो भोक्तुं भैक्षमपीह लोके । हत्वार्थकामांस्तु गुरूनिहैव भुञ्जीय भोगान्रुधिरप्रदिग्धान् ॥ ५ ॥
gurūnahatvā hi mahānubhāvāñśreyo bhoktuṃ bhaikṣamapīha loke | hatvārthakāmāṃstu gurūnihaiva bhuñjīya bhogānrudhirapradigdhān || 5 ||
Padaccheda
गुरून् अ-हत्वा हि महा-अनुभावान् श्रेयः भोक्तुम् भैक्षम् अपि इह लोके हत्वा अर्थ-कामान् तु गुरून् इह एव भुञ्जीय भोगान् रुधिर-प्रदिग्धान्
gurūn a-hatvā hi mahā-anubhāvān śreyaḥ bhoktum bhaikṣam api iha loke hatvā artha-kāmān tu gurūn iha eva bhuñjīya bhogān rudhira-pradigdhān
Word by word
It is better to live in this world even on food got by begging than to kill these teachers of great dignity. If I killed my teachers, though they seek their own gain, I should enjoy here only pleasures smeared with blood.
न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः । यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषामस्तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥ ६ ॥
na caitadvidmaḥ kataranno garīyo yadvā jayema yadi vā no jayeyuḥ | yāneva hatvā na jijīviṣāmaste 'vasthitāḥ pramukhe dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ || 6 ||
Padaccheda
न च एतत् विद्मः कतरत् नः गरीयः यत् वा जयेम यदि वा नः जयेयुः यान् एव हत्वा न जिजीविषामः ते अवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः
na ca etat vidmaḥ katarat naḥ garīyaḥ yat vā jayema yadi vā naḥ jayeyuḥ yān eva hatvā na jijīviṣāmaḥ te avasthitāḥ pramukhe dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ
Word by word
We do not know which is better for us: that we should conquer them, or that they should conquer us. The very sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, after killing whom we would not wish to live, stand before us.

Note jijīviṣāmaḥ is a desiderative form; it is labelled here as a present.

कार्पण्यदोषोपहतस्वभावः पृच्छामि त्वां धर्मसंमूढचेताः । यच्छ्रेयः स्यान्निश्चितं ब्रूहि तन्मे शिष्यस्तेऽहं शाधि मां त्वां प्रपन्नम् ॥ ७ ॥
kārpaṇyadoṣopahatasvabhāvaḥ pṛcchāmi tvāṃ dharmasaṃmūḍhacetāḥ | yacchreyaḥ syānniścitaṃ brūhi tanme śiṣyaste 'haṃ śādhi māṃ tvāṃ prapannam || 7 ||
Padaccheda
कार्पण्य-दोष-उपहत-स्वभावः पृच्छामि त्वाम् धर्म-संमूढ-चेताः यत् श्रेयः स्यात् निश्चितम् ब्रूहि तत् मे शिष्यः ते अहम् शाधि माम् त्वाम् प्रपन्नम्
kārpaṇya-doṣa-upahata-svabhāvaḥ pṛcchāmi tvām dharma-saṃmūḍha-cetāḥ yat śreyaḥ syāt niścitam brūhi tat me śiṣyaḥ te aham śādhi mām tvām prapannam
Word by word
My nature is overcome by the fault of faint-heartedness, and my mind is confused about dharma. I ask you: tell me for certain what is better. I am your pupil; instruct me, who have taken refuge in you.
न हि प्रपश्यामि ममापनुद्याद्यच्छोकमुच्छोषणमिन्द्रियाणाम् । अवाप्य भूमावसपत्नमृद्धं राज्यं सुराणामपि चाधिपत्यम् ॥ ८ ॥
na hi prapaśyāmi mamāpanudyādyacchokamucchoṣaṇamindriyāṇām | avāpya bhūmāvasapatnamṛddhaṃ rājyaṃ surāṇāmapi cādhipatyam || 8 ||
Padaccheda
न हि प्रपश्यामि मम अपनुद्यात् यत् शोकम् उच्छोषणम् इन्द्रियाणाम् अवाप्य भूमौ असपत्नम् ऋद्धम् राज्यम् सुराणाम् अपि च आधिपत्यम्
na hi prapaśyāmi mama apanudyāt yat śokam ucchoṣaṇam indriyāṇām avāpya bhūmau asapatnam ṛddham rājyam surāṇām api ca ādhipatyam
Word by word
For I do not see what would dispel this grief that dries up my senses, even if I obtained an unrivalled and prosperous kingdom on earth, and lordship over the gods.
संजय उवाचsaṃjaya uvāca
एवमुक्त्वा हृषीकेशं गुडाकेशः परंतप । न योत्स्य इति गोविन्दमुक्त्वा तूष्णीं बभूव ह ॥ ९ ॥
evam uktvā hṛṣīkeśaṃ guḍākeśaḥ paraṃtapa | na yotsya iti govindam uktvā tūṣṇīṃ babhūva ha || 9 ||
Padaccheda
एवम् उक्त्वा हृषीकेशम् गुडाकेशः परंतप न योत्स्ये इति गोविन्दम् उक्त्वा तूष्णीम् बभूव ह
evam uktvā hṛṣīkeśam guḍākeśaḥ paraṃtapa na yotsye iti govindam uktvā tūṣṇīm babhūva ha
Word by word
Saṃjaya said: Having spoken thus to Hṛṣīkeśa, O scorcher of foes, Guḍākeśa said to Govinda, “I will not fight”, and fell silent.

Reading The critical edition reads paraṃtapa, a vocative addressed to Dhṛtarāṣṭra. The commonly recited text has paraṃtapaḥ, a nominative describing Arjuna.

तमुवाच हृषीकेशः प्रहसन्निव भारत । सेनयोरुभयोर्मध्ये विषीदन्तमिदं वचः ॥ १० ॥
tam uvāca hṛṣīkeśaḥ prahasann iva bhārata | senayor ubhayor madhye viṣīdantam idaṃ vacaḥ || 10 ||
Padaccheda
तम् उवाच हृषीकेशः प्रहसन् इव भारत सेनयोः उभयोः मध्ये विषीदन्तम् इदम् वचः
tam uvāca hṛṣīkeśaḥ prahasan iva bhārata senayoḥ ubhayoḥ madhye viṣīdantam idam vacaḥ
Word by word
To him who was despairing there between the two armies, O Bhārata, Hṛṣīkeśa, as though smiling, spoke these words:

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara's commentary begins here. The passage from 1.2 up to ‘I will not fight’ shows, he says, how grief and delusion, the seed of the cycle of birth and death, arise in living beings. Seeing that Arjuna, sunk in a sea of grief, could be lifted out by nothing but knowledge of the Self, the Lord begins that teaching. The settled meaning of the Gītā, he holds, is that liberation comes from knowledge of the truth alone, not from knowledge combined with action.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
अशोच्यानन्वशोचस्त्वं प्रज्ञावादांश्च भाषसे । गतासूनगतासूंश्च नानुशोचन्ति पण्डिताः ॥ ११ ॥
aśocyānanvaśocastvaṃ prajñāvādāṃśca bhāṣase | gatāsūn agatāsūṃś ca nānuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
अ-शोच्यान् अन्वशोचः त्वम् प्रज्ञा-वादान् च भाषसे गतासून् अ-गतासून् च न अनुशोचन्ति पण्डिताः
a-śocyān anvaśocaḥ tvam prajñā-vādān ca bhāṣase gatāsūn a-gatāsūn ca na anuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: You have grieved for those who are not to be grieved for, and yet you speak the words of the wise. The wise grieve neither for the dead nor for the living.

Introduction The text from 1.2 to 2.9 is to be read as showing how grief and delusion, the seed of the cycle of birth and death, arise. Arjuna's grief and delusion come from the mistaken notions ‘I am theirs, they are mine’ about kingdom, teachers, sons, friends and kinsmen. His discernment overpowered by them, he turned away from battle, his own dharma as a warrior, and set about another's, living on alms. So do all beings whose minds are seized by grief and delusion give up their own dharma and take to what is forbidden; and even their action in their own dharma is done with desire for its fruit and with the sense of ‘I’, so that dharma and adharma pile up and the cycle does not cease. Grief and delusion cease by nothing other than knowledge of the Self preceded by the renunciation of all action; wishing to teach that, the Lord makes Arjuna the occasion, for the good of all.

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara then sets out and rejects the view that liberation comes not from steadfastness in knowledge alone but from knowledge joined with Vedic and smṛti rites. His grounds: the Lord speaks of two separate understandings, the Sāṃkhya (2.11–31, the truth of the Self, which is no doer) and Yoga (the practice of the means before that understanding is born), and of two paths resting on them (2.39, 3.3), because knowledge and action, resting on the notions of non-doer and doer, of one and many, cannot be in one man at once; the Bṛhadāraṇyaka likewise enjoins rites for the man of desire and renunciation for him who wants only the Self. Arjuna's question at 3.1–2 (‘if you hold understanding better than action … tell me the one’) would make no sense had both been taught together, as no one told to eat what is sweet and cool asks which of the two cures bile; and the Lord's answer, ‘two paths were taught by me’, would not fit.

Bhāṣya When one who knows the truth still acts, for the holding together of the world, that is not action that could be joined with knowledge: like the Lord's own conduct as a warrior, it is without the sense of ‘I’ and without aim at its fruit. ‘Janaka and others reached perfection by action alone’ (3.20) means either that, being knowers, they reached it without giving up action, or, if they were not knowers, that by action offered to the Lord they reached purity of mind or the rise of knowledge. So the settled sense of the Gītā is that liberation is from knowledge of the truth alone, not from knowledge joined with action. Seeing no rescue for Arjuna, sunk in a sea of grief, but knowledge of the Self, the Lord in compassion begins.

Bhāṣya Bhīṣma, Droṇa and the others are not to be grieved for, because their conduct is good and because in their true nature they are eternal. The paṇḍitas are the knowers of the Self: paṇḍā is the understanding that has the Self for its object. Arjuna shows foolishness and learning together, like one out of his mind.

न त्वेवाहं जातु नासं न त्वं नेमे जनाधिपाः । न चैव न भविष्यामः सर्वे वयमतः परम् ॥ १२ ॥
na tvevāhaṃ jātu nāsaṃ na tvaṃ neme janādhipāḥ | na caiva na bhaviṣyāmaḥ sarve vayamataḥ param || 12 ||
Padaccheda
न तु एव अहम् जातु न आसम् न त्वम् न इमे जनाधिपाः न च एव न भविष्यामः सर्वे वयम् अतः परम्
na tu eva aham jātu na āsam na tvam na ime janādhipāḥ na ca eva na bhaviṣyāmaḥ sarve vayam ataḥ param
Word by word
Never was there a time when I did not exist, nor you, nor these rulers of men; nor shall any of us ever cease to be hereafter.

Bhāṣya In all three times we are eternal in our nature as the Self, as space is through the making and breaking of pots. The plural follows the difference of bodies; it is not meant to teach that there are many selves.

देहिनोऽस्मिन्यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा । तथा देहान्तरप्राप्तिर्धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति ॥ १३ ॥
dehino 'sminyathā dehe kaumāraṃ yauvanaṃ jarā | tathā dehāntaraprāptir dhīrastatra na muhyati || 13 ||
Padaccheda
देहिनः अस्मिन् यथा देहे कौमारम् यौवनम् जरा तथा देह-अन्तर-प्राप्तिः धीरः तत्र न मुह्यति
dehinaḥ asmin yathā dehe kaumāram yauvanam jarā tathā deha-antara-prāptiḥ dhīraḥ tatra na muhyati
Word by word
As the embodied Self passes in this body through childhood, youth and old age, so it passes to another body. The wise man is not deluded by that.

Bhāṣya The Self does not perish when one stage ends nor is it born when the next begins; without changing, it gains each stage, and in the same way another body.

मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः । आगमापायिनोऽनित्यास्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत ॥ १४ ॥
mātrāsparśāstu kaunteya śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkhadāḥ | āgamāpāyino 'nityās tāṃstitikṣasva bhārata || 14 ||
Padaccheda
मात्रा-स्पर्शाः तु कौन्तेय शीत-उष्ण-सुख-दुःख-दाः आगम-अपायिनः अनित्याः तान् तितिक्षस्व भारत
mātrā-sparśāḥ tu kaunteya śīta-uṣṇa-sukha-duḥkha-dāḥ āgama-apāyinaḥ anityāḥ tān titikṣasva bhārata
Word by word
The contacts of the senses with their objects, O son of Kuntī, give cold and heat, pleasure and pain. They come and go, and do not last. Endure them, O Bhārata.

Bhāṣya Mātrāḥ are the senses, by which sound and the other objects are measured; their sparśāḥ are contacts with those objects. Or else sparśāḥ are the objects themselves. Cold and heat are named apart because each is sometimes pleasant and sometimes painful, whereas pleasure and pain are fixed in nature. To endure them is neither to rejoice nor to despair over them.

Note titikṣasva is a desiderative form; it is labelled here as an imperative.

यं हि न व्यथयन्त्येते पुरुषं पुरुषर्षभ । समदुःखसुखं धीरं सोऽमृतत्वाय कल्पते ॥ १५ ॥
yaṃ hi na vyathayantyete puruṣaṃ puruṣarṣabha | samaduḥkhasukhaṃ dhīraṃ so 'mṛtatvāya kalpate || 15 ||
Padaccheda
यम् हि न व्यथयन्ति एते पुरुषम् पुरुष-ऋषभ सम-दुःख-सुखम् धीरम् सः अमृत-त्वाय कल्पते
yam hi na vyathayanti ete puruṣam puruṣa-ṛṣabha sama-duḥkha-sukham dhīram saḥ amṛta-tvāya kalpate
Word by word
The wise man whom these do not trouble, O best of men, who is the same in pain and pleasure: he is fit for immortality.

Bhāṣya He is not shaken because he sees the eternal Self. Immortality here is liberation.

नासतो विद्यते भावो नाभावो विद्यते सतः । उभयोरपि दृष्टोऽन्तस्त्वनयोस्तत्त्वदर्शिभिः ॥ १६ ॥
nāsato vidyate bhāvo nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ | ubhayorapi dṛṣṭo 'ntastvanayostattvadarśibhiḥ || 16 ||
Padaccheda
न असतः विद्यते भावः न अभावः विद्यते सतः उभयोः अपि दृष्टः अन्तः तु अनयोः तत्त्व-दर्शिभिः
na asataḥ vidyate bhāvaḥ na abhāvaḥ vidyate sataḥ ubhayoḥ api dṛṣṭaḥ antaḥ tu anayoḥ tattva-darśibhiḥ
Word by word
The unreal has no being, and the real never ceases to be. The truth about both has been seen by those who see things as they are.

Bhāṣya The unreal is cold, heat and the like, with their causes: every effect, being a modification that is never found apart from its cause, as a pot is never found apart from clay. That of which the awareness never fails is real; that of which it fails is unreal. In ‘the pot is’, ‘the cloth is’, the awareness of pot and cloth comes and goes, the awareness of being does not. The real is the Self.

Bhāṣya Anta is the settled conclusion, that the real is only real and the unreal only unreal. In tattva, ‘that’ (tad) is a name of Brahman, which is all; tattva is its true nature. Bhāṣya Avināśin is free of the last change of state, destruction; nitya, of transformation; aja, of birth; avyaya, of decline. The question is a denial: he slays no one in any way and causes no one to be slain. Killing is only an example: since the reason given, changelessness, holds equally everywhere, what the Lord means is that every action is denied for the knower.

Bhāṣya It may be objected that one who knows a changeless post can still act. But here the knower is the Self itself; knowing does not belong to the aggregate of body and organs, so what is left is that the uncompounded, changeless Self is the knower. As the Self, changeless all the while, is imagined through ignorance to be the perceiver of sound and the rest that the understanding brings, because it is not told apart from the activity of the understanding, so it is called ‘knower’ through the knowledge that tells Self from not-Self, which is also an activity of the understanding and not ultimately real. The actions that scripture enjoins are therefore enjoined for one who does not know.

Bhāṣya Nor can it be said that knowledge too is enjoined only for one who does not know, so that there is no difference. When the sense of an injunction such as the agnihotra is understood, something remains to be performed by one who thinks ‘I am the doer, this is my duty’; when the sense of ‘it is not born’ is understood, nothing remains to be performed: only the knowledge ‘I am not the doer, not the enjoyer’ arises. So the knower who sees the changeless Self, and the seeker of liberation, are entitled only to the renunciation of all action. Hence the two paths of 3.3, the two ways Vyāsa taught his son (Mahābhārata, Śāntiparvan), and 3.27–28 and 5.13.

Bhāṣya Some who think themselves learned say that the knowledge ‘I am the one Self, changeless, not a doer, free of the six changes beginning with birth’ arises in no one. Then the teaching ‘it is not born’ would be pointless. If scripture gives knowledge of dharma and adharma and of the doer's passage to another body, why not of that same Self's changelessness? Nor is the Self beyond every instrument: ‘by the mind alone it is to be seen’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.4.19); the mind refined by scripture, the teacher's instruction, calm and restraint is the instrument for seeing it. Knowledge, once arisen, necessarily removes the contrary ignorance shown in 2.19; and to be the doer, the object or the prompter of an action is the work of ignorance in every action alike.

Bhāṣya On 5.13, ‘having renounced all actions with the mind’: this is not limited to mental actions, since speech and body act only after the mind; nor does it leave out the mental causes of scriptural actions, since the verse adds ‘neither acting nor causing to act’; nor is it said of a dying man, since he ‘sits in the city of nine gates’; nor can it be construed ‘laying actions down in the body’, since sitting needs a place and renouncing does not, and saṃnyāsa here means giving up, not depositing. So in the Gītā the knower of the Self is entitled to renunciation alone, not to action.

atha cet tvam imam dharmyam saṃgrāmam na kariṣyasi tataḥ svadharmam kīrtim ca hitvā pāpam avāpsyasi
Word by word
But if you will not wage this battle, which accords with dharma, then, having abandoned your own dharma and your fame, you will incur sin.

Bhāṣya The fame is that which Arjuna won by his encounter with Mahādeva and the like.

अकीर्तिं चापि भूतानि कथयिष्यन्ति तेऽव्ययाम् । संभावितस्य चाकीर्तिर्मरणादतिरिच्यते ॥ ३४ ॥

Padaccheda
अकीर्तिम् च अपि भूतानि कथयिष्यन्ति ते अव्ययाम् संभावितस्य च अकीर्तिः मरणात् अतिरिच्यते
akīrtim ca api bhūtāni kathayiṣyanti te avyayām saṃbhāvitasya ca akīrtiḥ maraṇāt atiricyate
Word by word
People will also tell of your undying disgrace; and for one who has been honoured, disgrace is worse than death.
भयाद्रणादुपरतं मंस्यन्ते त्वां महारथाः । येषां च त्वं बहुमतो भूत्वा यास्यसि लाघवम् ॥ ३५ ॥
bhayādraṇāduparataṃ maṃsyante tvāṃ mahārathāḥ | yeṣāṃ ca tvaṃ bahumato bhūtvā yāsyasi lāghavam || 35 ||
Padaccheda
भयात् रणात् उपरतम् मंस्यन्ते त्वाम् महा-रथाः येषाम् च त्वम् बहु-मतः भूत्वा यास्यसि लाघवम्
bhayāt raṇāt uparatam maṃsyante tvām mahā-rathāḥ yeṣām ca tvam bahu-mataḥ bhūtvā yāsyasi lāghavam
Word by word
The great chariot-warriors will think that you withdrew from the battle out of fear; and you, who were highly esteemed by them, will come to be held in contempt.

Bhāṣya They will think that he withdrew for fear of Karṇa and the others, not out of compassion.

अवाच्यवादांश्च बहून्वदिष्यन्ति तवाहिताः । निन्दन्तस्तव सामर्थ्यं ततो दुःखतरं नु किम् ॥ ३६ ॥
avācyavādāṃśca bahūnvadiṣyanti tavāhitāḥ | nindantastava sāmarthyaṃ tato duḥkhataraṃ nu kim || 36 ||
Padaccheda
अवाच्य-वादान् च बहून् वदिष्यन्ति तव अहिताः निन्दन्तः तव सामर्थ्यम् ततः दुःखतरम् नु किम्
avācya-vādān ca bahūn vadiṣyanti tava ahitāḥ nindantaḥ tava sāmarthyam tataḥ duḥkhataram nu kim
Word by word
Your enemies will say many things not fit to be spoken, deriding your prowess. What could be more painful than that?
हतो वा प्राप्स्यसि स्वर्गं जित्वा वा भोक्ष्यसे महीम् । तस्मादुत्तिष्ठ कौन्तेय युद्धाय कृतनिश्चयः ॥ ३७ ॥
hato vā prāpsyasi svargaṃ jitvā vā bhokṣyase mahīm | tasmāduttiṣṭha kaunteya yuddhāya kṛtaniścayaḥ || 37 ||
Padaccheda
हतः वा प्राप्स्यसि स्वर्गम् जित्वा वा भोक्ष्यसे महीम् तस्मात् उत्तिष्ठ कौन्तेय युद्धाय कृत-निश्चयः
hataḥ vā prāpsyasi svargam jitvā vā bhokṣyase mahīm tasmāt uttiṣṭha kaunteya yuddhāya kṛta-niścayaḥ
Word by word
Slain, you will gain heaven; victorious, you will enjoy the earth. Therefore arise, O son of Kuntī, resolved to fight.

Bhāṣya Either way he gains. The resolve is: ‘I shall conquer the enemy, or I shall die.’

सुखदुःखे समे कृत्वा लाभालाभौ जयाजयौ । ततो युद्धाय युज्यस्व नैवं पापमवाप्स्यसि ॥ ३८ ॥
sukhaduḥkhe same kṛtvā lābhālābhau jayājayau | tato yuddhāya yujyasva naivaṃ pāpamavāpsyasi || 38 ||
Padaccheda
सुख-दुःखे समे कृत्वा लाभ-अलाभौ जय-अजयौ ततः युद्धाय युज्यस्व न एवम् पापम् अवाप्स्यसि
sukha-duḥkhe same kṛtvā lābha-alābhau jaya-ajayau tataḥ yuddhāya yujyasva na evam pāpam avāpsyasi
Word by word
Treating pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat alike, make ready for battle. In this way you will incur no sin.

Bhāṣya To treat them alike is to have neither attachment nor aversion. This advice is given in passing, for one who is fighting because fighting is his own dharma.

एषा तेऽभिहिता सांख्ये बुद्धिर्योगे त्विमां शृणु । बुद्ध्या युक्तो यया पार्थ कर्मबन्धं प्रहास्यसि ॥ ३९ ॥
eṣā te 'bhihitā sāṃkhye buddhir yoge tvimāṃ śṛṇu | buddhyā yukto yayā pārtha karmabandhaṃ prahāsyasi || 39 ||
Padaccheda
एषा ते अभिहिता सांख्ये बुद्धिः योगे तु इमाम् शृणु बुद्ध्या युक्तः यया पार्थ कर्म-बन्धम् प्रहास्यसि
eṣā te abhihitā sāṃkhye buddhiḥ yoge tu imām śṛṇu buddhyā yuktaḥ yayā pārtha karma-bandham prahāsyasi
Word by word
This understanding has been told to you in regard to the discernment of the truth. Now hear it in regard to yoga: endowed with this understanding, O Pārtha, you will cast off the bondage of action.

Bhāṣya Sāṃkhya is the discernment of the supreme reality; the understanding concerning it is the knowledge that directly removes grief, delusion and the other causes of the cycle of birth and death. yoga is the means to it: the yoga of action, performed without attachment as worship of the Lord after giving up the pairs of opposites, and the yoga of absorption (samādhi). The bondage of action is action itself, as merit and demerit; it is cast off through the knowledge that comes by the Lord's grace.

नेहाभिक्रमनाशोऽस्ति प्रत्यवायो न विद्यते । स्वल्पमप्यस्य धर्मस्य त्रायते महतो भयात् ॥ ४० ॥
nehābhikramanāśo 'sti pratyavāyo na vidyate | svalpam apy asya dharmasya trāyate mahato bhayāt || 40 ||
Padaccheda
न इह अभिक्रम-नाशः अस्ति प्रत्यवायः न विद्यते सु-अल्पम् अपि अस्य धर्मस्य त्रायते महतः भयात्
na iha abhikrama-nāśaḥ asti pratyavāyaḥ na vidyate su-alpam api asya dharmasya trāyate mahataḥ bhayāt
Word by word
Here nothing that has been begun is lost, and no contrary result follows. Even a little of this dharma saves one from great fear.

Bhāṣya ‘here’: on the path to liberation, the yoga of action. A beginning in it is not wasted, as a beginning in farming may be, nor does harm come of it, as may happen with medical treatment. The great fear is the fear of the cycle of birth and death.

व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिरेकेह कुरुनन्दन । बहुशाखा ह्यनन्ताश्च बुद्धयोऽव्यवसायिनाम् ॥ ४१ ॥
vyavasāyātmikā buddhirekeha kurunandana | bahuśākhā hyanantāśca buddhayo 'vyavasāyinām || 41 ||
Padaccheda
व्यवसाय-आत्मिका बुद्धिः एका इह कुरु-नन्दन बहु-शाखाः हि अनन्ताः च बुद्धयः अव्यवसायिनाम्
vyavasāya-ātmikā buddhiḥ ekā iha kuru-nandana bahu-śākhāḥ hi anantāḥ ca buddhayaḥ avyavasāyinām
Word by word
Here, O joy of the Kurus, the understanding that is resolute is one; but the thoughts of the irresolute are many-branched and endless.

Bhāṣya ‘here’: on the path to the highest good. The resolute understanding is one because it arises from a right means of knowledge, and it sets aside the many contrary notions. The irresolute are those without the discerning understanding that such a means of knowledge gives; through the spreading of their notions the cycle of birth and death goes on without end.

यामिमां पुष्पितां वाचं प्रवदन्त्यविपश्चितः । वेदवादरताः पार्थ नान्यदस्तीति वादिनः ॥ ४२ ॥
yāmimāṃ puṣpitāṃ vācaṃ pravadanty avipaścitaḥ | vedavādaratāḥ pārtha nānyadastīti vādinaḥ || 42 ||
Padaccheda
याम् इमाम् पुष्पिताम् वाचम् प्रवदन्ति अ-विपश्चितः वेद-वाद-रताः पार्थ न अन्यत् अस्ति इति वादिनः
yām imām puṣpitām vācam pravadanti a-vipaścitaḥ veda-vāda-ratāḥ pārtha na anyat asti iti vādinaḥ
Word by word
The undiscerning, O Pārtha, who delight in the words of the Veda and say that there is nothing else, utter this flowery speech:

Bhāṣya Flowery: lovely like a tree in bloom, pleasant to hear. The ‘words of the Veda’ are its many passages of praise that set out results and the means to them. ‘Nothing else’: nothing other than rites, which are the means to heaven and such results.

कामात्मानः स्वर्गपरा जन्मकर्मफलप्रदाम् । क्रियाविशेषबहुलां भोगैश्वर्यगतिं प्रति ॥ ४३ ॥
kāmātmānaḥ svargaparā janmakarmaphalapradām | kriyāviśeṣabahulāṃ bhogaiśvaryagatiṃ prati || 43 ||
Padaccheda
काम-आत्मानः स्वर्ग-पराः जन्म-कर्म-फल-प्रदाम् क्रिया-विशेष-बहुलाम् भोग-ऐश्वर्य-गतिम् प्रति
kāma-ātmānaḥ svarga-parāḥ janma-karma-phala-pradām kriyā-viśeṣa-bahulām bhoga-aiśvarya-gatim prati
Word by word
full of desire, holding heaven to be the highest goal, they utter speech that gives birth as the fruit of action, and is full of particular rites for gaining enjoyment and power.

Bhāṣya The words describing the speech go with ‘they utter’ in the previous verse. Uttering it, the deluded keep turning in the cycle of birth and death.

भोगैश्वर्यप्रसक्तानां तयापहृतचेतसाम् । व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिः समाधौ न विधीयते ॥ ४४ ॥
bhogaiśvaryaprasaktānāṃ tayāpahṛtacetasām | vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ samādhau na vidhīyate || 44 ||
Padaccheda
भोग-ऐश्वर्य-प्रसक्तानाम् तया अपहृत-चेतसाम् व्यवसाय-आत्मिका बुद्धिः समाधौ न विधीयते
bhoga-aiśvarya-prasaktānām tayā apahṛta-cetasām vyavasāya-ātmikā buddhiḥ samādhau na vidhīyate
Word by word
In those who are attached to enjoyment and power, and whose minds are carried away by that speech, the resolute understanding does not form in the mind.

Bhāṣya Samādhi here is the inner organ, the intellect, ‘that in which everything is placed for the person's experience’. The resolute understanding, whether of sāṃkhya or of yoga, does not arise in it.

त्रैगुण्यविषया वेदा निस्त्रैगुण्यो भवार्जुन । निर्द्वंद्वो नित्यसत्त्वस्थो निर्योगक्षेम आत्मवान् ॥ ४५ ॥
traiguṇyaviṣayā vedā nistraiguṇyo bhavārjuna | nirdvaṃdvo nityasattvastho niryogakṣema ātmavān || 45 ||
Padaccheda
त्रैगुण्य-विषयाः वेदाः निस्त्रैगुण्यः भव अर्जुन निर्द्वंद्वः नित्य-सत्त्व-स्थः निर्योगक्षेमः आत्मवान्
traiguṇya-viṣayāḥ vedāḥ nistraiguṇyaḥ bhava arjuna nirdvaṃdvaḥ nitya-sattva-sthaḥ niryogakṣemaḥ ātmavān
Word by word
The Vedas have the world of the three guṇas as their subject; be free from the three guṇas, O Arjuna: free from the pairs of opposites, always abiding in sattva, free from getting and keeping, and self-possessed.

Bhāṣya Traiguṇya is the cycle of birth and death, which the Vedas set forth. To be free of it is to be without desire. The pairs of opposites are things that cause pleasure and pain, each with its contrary. yoga is acquiring what one has not; kṣema is guarding what one has; one whose chief concern they are can hardly strive for the good. ātmavān: vigilant. This is advice for one who is doing his own dharma.

Note The guṇas are the three constituents of Prakṛti (sattva, rajas and tamas), which the Gītā explains in later adhyāyas; the Sanskrit names are kept here.

यावानर्थ उदपाने सर्वतः संप्लुतोदके । तावान्सर्वेषु वेदेषु ब्राह्मणस्य विजानतः ॥ ४६ ॥
yāvānartha udapāne sarvataḥ saṃplutodake | tāvānsarveṣu vedeṣu brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ || 46 ||
Padaccheda
यावान् अर्थः उदपाने सर्वतः संप्लुत-उदके तावान् सर्वेषु वेदेषु ब्राह्मणस्य विजानतः
yāvān arthaḥ udapāne sarvataḥ saṃpluta-udake tāvān sarveṣu vedeṣu brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ
Word by word
Whatever use there is in a well is all found where water floods on every side; in the same way, whatever use there is in all the Vedas is found for a brahmin who knows.

Bhāṣya Whatever purpose, such as bathing and drinking, is served by wells and ponds is all contained in the purpose served by a flood of water. So the fruit of all the rites taught in the Vedas is contained in the fruit of knowledge that belongs to the brahmin, the renouncer who knows the supreme truth. Hence, until one is fit for the path of knowledge, one who is entitled to rites must perform them, though they are like the well and the pond.

कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन । मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ ४७ ॥
karmaṇyevādhikāraste mā phaleṣu kadācana | mā karmaphalaheturbhūr mā te saṅgo 'stvakarmaṇi || 47 ||
Padaccheda
कर्मणि एव अधिकारः ते मा फलेषु कदाचन मा कर्म-फल-हेतुः भूः मा ते सङ्गः अस्तु अकर्मणि
karmaṇi eva adhikāraḥ te mā phaleṣu kadācana mā karma-phala-hetuḥ bhūḥ mā te saṅgaḥ astu akarmaṇi
Word by word
Your entitlement is to action alone, never to its fruits. Do not be one who brings about the fruit of action, and do not be attached to inaction.

Bhāṣya ‘to action alone’, and not to the path of knowledge. Never, in any state, is there to be craving for the fruit of action. One who acts out of such craving becomes the cause of that fruit, which is rebirth. And he should not think, ‘if the fruit is not wanted, why act at all?’, and so grow fond of not acting.

योगस्थः कुरु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा धनंजय । सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योः समो भूत्वा समत्वं योग उच्यते ॥ ४८ ॥
yogasthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā dhanaṃjaya | siddhyasiddhyoḥ samo bhūtvā samatvaṃ yoga ucyate || 48 ||
Padaccheda
योग-स्थः कुरु कर्माणि सङ्गम् त्यक्त्वा धनंजय सिद्धि-असिद्ध्योः समः भूत्वा सम-त्वम् योगः उच्यते
yoga-sthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgam tyaktvā dhanaṃjaya siddhi-asiddhyoḥ samaḥ bhūtvā sama-tvam yogaḥ ucyate
Word by word
Established in yoga, O Dhanaṃjaya, perform actions, giving up attachment and remaining the same in success and failure. Evenness is called yoga.

Bhāṣya Actions are to be done for the Lord alone, giving up even the attachment of ‘may the Lord be pleased with me’. Success is the gaining of knowledge that comes from purity of mind when action is done without craving for its fruit; failure is the opposite.

दूरेण ह्यवरं कर्म बुद्धियोगाद्धनंजय । बुद्धौ शरणमन्विच्छ कृपणाः फलहेतवः ॥ ४९ ॥
dūreṇa hyavaraṃ karma buddhiyogāddhanaṃjaya | buddhau śaraṇamanviccha kṛpaṇāḥ phalahetavaḥ || 49 ||
Padaccheda
दूरेण हि अवरम् कर्म बुद्धि-योगात् धनंजय बुद्धौ शरणम् अन्विच्छ कृपणाः फल-हेतवः
dūreṇa hi avaram karma buddhi-yogāt dhanaṃjaya buddhau śaraṇam anviccha kṛpaṇāḥ phala-hetavaḥ
Word by word
Action done for its fruit, O Dhanaṃjaya, is far inferior to action joined with evenness of mind. Seek refuge in that understanding. Pitiable are those who act for the fruit.

Bhāṣya Action done by one who seeks its fruit is inferior because it is a cause of birth and death. The understanding in which refuge is to be sought is that of yoga, or the understanding of sāṃkhya which ripens from it: one is to take refuge in knowledge of the supreme truth.

बुद्धियुक्तो जहातीह उभे सुकृतदुष्कृते । तस्माद्योगाय युज्यस्व योगः कर्मसु कौशलम् ॥ ५० ॥
buddhiyukto jahātīha ubhe sukṛtaduṣkṛte | tasmādyogāya yujyasva yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam || 50 ||
Padaccheda
बुद्धि-युक्तः जहाति इह उभे सुकृत-दुष्कृते तस्मात् योगाय युज्यस्व योगः कर्मसु कौशलम्
buddhi-yuktaḥ jahāti iha ubhe sukṛta-duṣkṛte tasmāt yogāya yujyasva yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam
Word by word
One endowed with this understanding casts off both merit and demerit here in this world. Therefore strive for yoga: yoga is skill in action.

Bhāṣya He casts them off through purity of mind and the gaining of knowledge. The skill is this: evenness of mind in success and failure, with the mind given over to the Lord, while one is engaged in the actions that are one's own dharma. By that evenness, actions whose nature is to bind lose that nature.

कर्मजं बुद्धियुक्ता हि फलं त्यक्त्वा मनीषिणः । जन्मबन्धविनिर्मुक्ताः पदं गच्छन्त्यनामयम् ॥ ५१ ॥
karmajaṃ buddhiyuktā hi phalaṃ tyaktvā manīṣiṇaḥ | janmabandhavinirmuktāḥ padaṃ gacchantyanāmayam || 51 ||
Padaccheda
कर्म-जम् बुद्धि-युक्ताः हि फलम् त्यक्त्वा मनीषिणः जन्म-बन्ध-विनिर्मुक्ताः पदम् गच्छन्ति अनामयम्
karma-jam buddhi-yuktāḥ hi phalam tyaktvā manīṣiṇaḥ janma-bandha-vinirmuktāḥ padam gacchanti anāmayam
Word by word
For the wise, endowed with this understanding, give up the fruit born of action; freed from the bondage of birth, they go to the state that is free from all ill.

Bhāṣya The fruit born of action is the gaining of bodies, wished for or not. Having become men of knowledge, they are freed from the bondage of birth while still living, and reach the highest state of Viṣṇu, called liberation. Or else, he adds, the ‘understanding’ spoken of from verse 49 onwards is the vision of the supreme truth itself, since it is said to remove merit and demerit directly.

यदा ते मोहकलिलं बुद्धिर्व्यतितरिष्यति । तदा गन्तासि निर्वेदं श्रोतव्यस्य श्रुतस्य च ॥ ५२ ॥
yadā te mohakalilaṃ buddhirvyatitariṣyati | tadā gantāsi nirvedaṃ śrotavyasya śrutasya ca || 52 ||
Padaccheda
यदा ते मोह-कलिलम् बुद्धिः व्यतितरिष्यति तदा गन्तासि निर्वेदम् श्रोतव्यस्य श्रुतस्य च
yadā te moha-kalilam buddhiḥ vyatitariṣyati tadā gantāsi nirvedam śrotavyasya śrutasya ca
Word by word
When your understanding crosses beyond the mire of delusion, then you will come to dispassion towards what is yet to be heard and what has been heard.

Bhāṣya The mire is the confusion, of the nature of delusion, that clouds the discernment of the Self from what is not the Self and turns the mind towards objects. When the understanding has become very pure, what has been heard and what remains to be heard both appear fruitless.

श्रुतिविप्रतिपन्ना ते यदा स्थास्यति निश्चला । समाधावचला बुद्धिस्तदा योगमवाप्स्यसि ॥ ५३ ॥
śrutivipratipannā te yadā sthāsyati niścalā | samādhāvacalā buddhis tadā yogamavāpsyasi || 53 ||
Padaccheda
श्रुति-विप्रतिपन्ना ते यदा स्थास्यति निश्चला समाधौ अचला बुद्धिः तदा योगम् अवाप्स्यसि
śruti-vipratipannā te yadā sthāsyati niścalā samādhau acalā buddhiḥ tadā yogam avāpsyasi
Word by word
When your understanding, bewildered by all that it has heard, stands unmoving and steady in the Self, then you will attain yoga.

Bhāṣya The mind is scattered by hearing the many texts that set out ends and means, some urging action and some withdrawal. samādhi here is the Self, ‘that in which the mind is settled’. The yoga then attained is the absorption that is discerning wisdom.

अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
स्थितप्रज्ञस्य का भाषा समाधिस्थस्य केशव । स्थितधीः किं प्रभाषेत किमासीत व्रजेत किम् ॥ ५४ ॥
sthitaprajñasya kā bhāṣā samādhisthasya keśava | sthitadhīḥ kiṃ prabhāṣeta kimāsīta vrajeta kim || 54 ||
Padaccheda
स्थित-प्रज्ञस्य का भाषा समाधि-स्थस्य केशव स्थित-धीः किम् प्रभाषेत किम् आसीत व्रजेत किम्
sthita-prajñasya kā bhāṣā samādhi-sthasya keśava sthita-dhīḥ kim prabhāṣeta kim āsīta vrajeta kim
Word by word
Arjuna said: What is the description, O Keśava, of one whose wisdom is firm, who is established in absorption? How would one of firm understanding speak, how would he sit, how would he walk?

Bhāṣya Sthitaprajña is one in whom the wisdom ‘I am the supreme Brahman’ is firmly established. bhāṣā: how is he spoken of by others? The verse asks for the marks of such a person. From here to the end of the adhyāya these marks are taught, and they are also the means, to be won by effort, for one who seeks that state.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् । आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥ ५५ ॥
prajahāti yadā kāmān sarvān pārtha manogatān | ātmany evātmanā tuṣṭaḥ sthitaprajñas tadocyate || 55 ||
Padaccheda
प्रजहाति यदा कामान् सर्वान् पार्थ मनः-गतान् आत्मनि एव आत्मना तुष्टः स्थित-प्रज्ञः तदा उच्यते
prajahāti yadā kāmān sarvān pārtha manaḥ-gatān ātmani eva ātmanā tuṣṭaḥ sthita-prajñaḥ tadā ucyate
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: When a man gives up completely all the desires that have entered the mind, O Pārtha, and is content in the Self alone by the Self, then he is called one of firm wisdom.

Bhāṣya Content in the inward Self by himself alone, looking to no outward gain, satisfied by the nectar of seeing the supreme truth. The wisdom is that born of discerning the Self from what is not the Self. Such a one is the renouncer who has given up the desire for sons, wealth and worlds, and delights in the Self.

दुःखेष्वनुद्विग्नमनाः सुखेषु विगतस्पृहः । वीतरागभयक्रोधः स्थितधीर्मुनिरुच्यते ॥ ५६ ॥
duḥkheṣvanudvignamanāḥ sukheṣu vigataspṛhaḥ | vītarāgabhayakrodhaḥ sthitadhīrmunirucyate || 56 ||
Padaccheda
दुःखेषु अनुद्विग्न-मनाः सुखेषु विगत-स्पृहः वीत-राग-भय-क्रोधः स्थित-धीः मुनिः उच्यते
duḥkheṣu anudvigna-manāḥ sukheṣu vigata-spṛhaḥ vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ sthita-dhīḥ muniḥ ucyate
Word by word
One whose mind is not shaken in sorrows, who is free from longing in pleasures, and free from attachment, fear and anger, is called a sage of firm understanding.

Bhāṣya His longing does not grow with pleasures as fire grows when fuel is added. The muni is the renouncer.

यः सर्वत्रानभिस्नेहस्तत्तत्प्राप्य शुभाशुभम् । नाभिनन्दति न द्वेष्टि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ५७ ॥
yaḥ sarvatrānabhisnehastattatprāpya śubhāśubham | nābhinandati na dveṣṭi tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā || 57 ||
Padaccheda
यः सर्वत्र अनभिस्नेहः तत् तत् प्राप्य शुभ-अशुभम् न अभिनन्दति न द्वेष्टि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता
yaḥ sarvatra anabhisnehaḥ tat tat prāpya śubha-aśubham na abhinandati na dveṣṭi tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā
Word by word
He who is without attachment everywhere, who neither rejoices nor hates on meeting with this or that, good or evil: his wisdom is firmly established.

Bhāṣya Without attachment even to the body and to life.

यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ५८ ॥
yadā saṃharate cāyaṃ kūrmo 'ṅgānīva sarvaśaḥ | indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyastasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā || 58 ||
Padaccheda
यदा संहरते च अयम् कूर्मः अङ्गानि इव सर्वशः इन्द्रियाणि इन्द्रिय-अर्थेभ्यः तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता
yadā saṃharate ca ayam kūrmaḥ aṅgāni iva sarvaśaḥ indriyāṇi indriya-arthebhyaḥ tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā
Word by word
When he draws in his senses from their objects on every side, as a tortoise draws in its limbs, his wisdom is firmly established.

Bhāṣya ‘he’ is the ascetic who has entered on the path of knowledge; the tortoise draws in its limbs out of fear.

विषया विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः । रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते ॥ ५९ ॥
viṣayā vinivartante nirāhārasya dehinaḥ | rasavarjaṃ raso 'pyasya paraṃ dṛṣṭvā nivartate || 59 ||
Padaccheda
विषयाः विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः रस-वर्जम् रसः अपि अस्य परम् दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते
viṣayāḥ vinivartante nirāhārasya dehinaḥ rasa-varjam rasaḥ api asya param dṛṣṭvā nivartate
Word by word
The objects of sense turn away from an embodied being who does not feed on them, but the taste for them remains. Even that taste turns away from him when he has seen the supreme.

Bhāṣya ‘objects’ here stands for the senses, which withdraw even in a foolish man who practises hard austerity and takes in no objects; the subtle liking for objects is not thereby given up. rasa is well known in the sense of attachment. It ceases for the ascetic who has realised the supreme truth, Brahman, and abides as ‘I am that’. Without right vision the liking is not rooted out; so the wisdom that is right vision must be made firm.

यततो ह्यपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः । इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभं मनः ॥ ६० ॥
yatato hyapi kaunteya puruṣasya vipaścitaḥ | indriyāṇi pramāthīni haranti prasabhaṃ manaḥ || 60 ||
Padaccheda
यततः हि अपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभम् मनः
yatataḥ hi api kaunteya puruṣasya vipaścitaḥ indriyāṇi pramāthīni haranti prasabham manaḥ
Word by word
For the turbulent senses, O son of Kuntī, carry away by force the mind even of a discerning man who is striving.

Bhāṣya So one who would make right vision firm must first bring the senses under control. They agitate a man who turns towards objects, and then carry off his mind openly, even while he looks on with discernment.

तानि सर्वाणि संयम्य युक्त आसीत मत्परः । वशे हि यस्येन्द्रियाणि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ६१ ॥
tāni sarvāṇi saṃyamya yukta āsīta matparaḥ | vaśe hi yasyendriyāṇi tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā || 61 ||
Padaccheda
तानि सर्वाणि संयम्य युक्तः आसीत मद्-परः वशे हि यस्य इन्द्रियाणि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता
tāni sarvāṇi saṃyamya yuktaḥ āsīta mad-paraḥ vaśe hi yasya indriyāṇi tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā
Word by word
Having restrained them all, one should sit collected, holding me to be the highest; for he whose senses are under control has wisdom that is firmly established.

Bhāṣya Matpara: one for whom ‘I, Vāsudeva, the inward Self of all, am the highest’; he should sit thinking ‘I am not other than he’. The senses come under control by the strength of practice.

ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते । सङ्गात्संजायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥ ६२ ॥
dhyāyato viṣayānpuṃsaḥ saṅgasteṣūpajāyate | saṅgātsaṃjāyate kāmaḥ kāmātkrodho 'bhijāyate || 62 ||
Padaccheda
ध्यायतः विषयान् पुंसः सङ्गः तेषु उपजायते सङ्गात् संजायते कामः कामात् क्रोधः अभिजायते
dhyāyataḥ viṣayān puṃsaḥ saṅgaḥ teṣu upajāyate saṅgāt saṃjāyate kāmaḥ kāmāt krodhaḥ abhijāyate
Word by word
When a man dwells in thought on the objects of sense, attachment to them arises. From attachment desire is born, and from desire anger springs up.

Bhāṣya This and the next verse give the root of every evil for one who is on the way to ruin. Anger arises from desire when desire is thwarted by something. Attachment (saṅga) is fondness for the objects; desire (kāma) is thirst.

क्रोधाद्भवति संमोहः संमोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः । स्मृतिभ्रंशाद्बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥ ६३ ॥
krodhādbhavati saṃmohaḥ saṃmohātsmṛtivibhramaḥ | smṛtibhraṃśādbuddhināśo buddhināśātpraṇaśyati || 63 ||
Padaccheda
क्रोधात् भवति संमोहः संमोहात् स्मृति-विभ्रमः स्मृति-भ्रंशात् बुद्धि-नाशः बुद्धि-नाशात् प्रणश्यति
krodhāt bhavati saṃmohaḥ saṃmohāt smṛti-vibhramaḥ smṛti-bhraṃśāt buddhi-nāśaḥ buddhi-nāśāt praṇaśyati
Word by word
From anger comes delusion; from delusion, the failing of memory; from the loss of memory, the ruin of understanding; and from the ruin of understanding he perishes.

Bhāṣya Delusion is the failure to discern what ought and ought not to be done: an angry man abuses even his teacher. The memory that fails is that formed by the teaching of scripture and teacher; it does not arise when the occasion for it comes. The ruin of understanding is the unfitness of the mind to tell right from wrong. A man is a man only so long as his mind is fit for this; he ‘perishes’ in that he becomes unfit for any human end.

रागद्वेषवियुक्तैस्तु विषयानिन्द्रियैश्चरन् । आत्मवश्यैर्विधेयात्मा प्रसादमधिगच्छति ॥ ६४ ॥
rāgadveṣaviyuktaistu viṣayānindriyaiścaran | ātmavaśyairvidheyātmā prasādamadhigacchati || 64 ||
Padaccheda
राग-द्वेष-वियुक्तैः तु विषयान् इन्द्रियैः चरन् आत्म-वश्यैः विधेय-आत्मा प्रसादम् अधिगच्छति
rāga-dveṣa-viyuktaiḥ tu viṣayān indriyaiḥ caran ātma-vaśyaiḥ vidheya-ātmā prasādam adhigacchati
Word by word
But one whose mind is obedient, who moves among objects with senses that are freed from attachment and aversion and are under his own control, attains serenity.

Bhāṣya Now the cause of liberation is told. The senses naturally act from attachment and aversion; the seeker of liberation experiences, with senses free of these, only the objects that cannot be avoided. prasāda is clearness, well-being.

प्रसादे सर्वदुःखानां हानिरस्योपजायते । प्रसन्नचेतसो ह्याशु बुद्धिः पर्यवतिष्ठते ॥ ६५ ॥
prasāde sarvaduḥkhānāṃ hānirasyopajāyate | prasannacetaso hyāśu buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate || 65 ||
Padaccheda
प्रसादे सर्व-दुःखानाम् हानिः अस्य उपजायते प्रसन्न-चेतसः हि आशु बुद्धिः पर्यवतिष्ठते
prasāde sarva-duḥkhānām hāniḥ asya upajāyate prasanna-cetasaḥ hi āśu buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate
Word by word
In serenity all his sorrows come to an end; for the understanding of one whose mind is serene quickly becomes steady.

Bhāṣya The understanding settles on every side, like space; it becomes unmoving as the very nature of the Self. Therefore one should deal, with senses free of attachment and aversion, only with unavoidable objects that scripture does not forbid.

नास्ति बुद्धिरयुक्तस्य न चायुक्तस्य भावना । न चाभावयतः शान्तिरशान्तस्य कुतः सुखम् ॥ ६६ ॥
nāsti buddhirayuktasya na cāyuktasya bhāvanā | na cābhāvayataḥ śāntir aśāntasya kutaḥ sukham || 66 ||
Padaccheda
न अस्ति बुद्धिः अयुक्तस्य न च अयुक्तस्य भावना न च अभावयतः शान्तिः अ-शान्तस्य कुतः सुखम्
na asti buddhiḥ ayuktasya na ca ayuktasya bhāvanā na ca abhāvayataḥ śāntiḥ a-śāntasya kutaḥ sukham
Word by word
There is no understanding for one who is not collected, and no contemplation; without contemplation there is no peace; and for one without peace, how can there be happiness?

Bhāṣya The understanding is that which has the nature of the Self for its object; bhāvanā is steady application to the knowledge of the Self. Happiness is the turning of the senses away from the thirst for enjoying objects; that thirst is sorrow itself.

Note abhāvayataḥ is a causative present participle with a-; it is labelled here as a present participle.

इन्द्रियाणां हि चरतां यन्मनोऽनुविधीयते । तदस्य हरति प्रज्ञां वायुर्नावमिवाम्भसि ॥ ६७ ॥
indriyāṇāṃ hi caratāṃ yanmano 'nuvidhīyate | tadasya harati prajñāṃ vāyurnāvamivāmbhasi || 67 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियाणाम् हि चरताम् यत् मनः अनुविधीयते तत् अस्य हरति प्रज्ञाम् वायुः नावम् इव अम्भसि
indriyāṇām hi caratām yat manaḥ anuvidhīyate tat asya harati prajñām vāyuḥ nāvam iva ambhasi
Word by word
For the mind that follows after the roaming senses carries away his wisdom, as the wind carries away a boat on the water.

Bhāṣya The wisdom is that born of discerning the Self from what is not the Self. As the wind drives a boat off its course, so the mind takes wisdom from the Self and turns it to objects.

तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ६८ ॥
tasmādyasya mahābāho nigṛhītāni sarvaśaḥ | indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyastasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā || 68 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मात् यस्य महा-बाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः इन्द्रियाणि इन्द्रिय-अर्थेभ्यः तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता
tasmāt yasya mahā-bāho nigṛhītāni sarvaśaḥ indriyāṇi indriya-arthebhyaḥ tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā
Word by word
Therefore, O mighty-armed, he whose senses are held back in every way from their objects has wisdom that is firmly established.

Bhāṣya This sums up what was argued in several ways from verse 60.

या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी । यस्यां जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतो मुनेः ॥ ६९ ॥
yā niśā sarvabhūtānāṃ tasyāṃ jāgarti saṃyamī | yasyāṃ jāgrati bhūtāni sā niśā paśyato muneḥ || 69 ||
Padaccheda
या निशा सर्व-भूतानाम् तस्याम् जागर्ति संयमी यस्याम् जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतः मुनेः
yā niśā sarva-bhūtānām tasyām jāgarti saṃyamī yasyām jāgrati bhūtāni sā niśā paśyataḥ muneḥ
Word by word
What is night for all beings, in that the self-controlled one is awake; and that in which beings are awake is night for the sage who sees.

Bhāṣya The ‘night’ of all beings is the supreme truth, the object of the one of firm wisdom: to the ignorant it is dark, as day is to creatures of the night. The self-controlled yogī has awakened in it from the sleep of ignorance. That in which beings are said to be awake is the night of ignorance, with its division into knower and known, in which they are really asleep, like dreamers.

Bhāṣya Hence rites are enjoined only in the state of ignorance, not in the state of knowledge. He concludes that the knower of the Self is entitled only to the renunciation of all action.

आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् । तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे स शान्तिमाप्नोति न कामकामी ॥ ७० ॥
āpūryamāṇamacalapratiṣṭhaṃ samudramāpaḥ praviśanti yadvat | tadvatkāmā yaṃ praviśanti sarve sa śāntimāpnoti na kāmakāmī || 70 ||
Padaccheda
आपूर्यमाणम् अचल-प्रतिष्ठम् समुद्रम् आपः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् तद्वत् कामाः यम् प्रविशन्ति सर्वे सः शान्तिम् आप्नोति न काम-कामी
āpūryamāṇam acala-pratiṣṭham samudram āpaḥ praviśanti yadvat tadvat kāmāḥ yam praviśanti sarve saḥ śāntim āpnoti na kāma-kāmī
Word by word
As waters enter the ocean, which is being filled yet stays unmoved, so he whom all desires enter attains peace; not so one who desires the objects of desire.

Bhāṣya The waters enter the ocean without changing it. So desires, even in the presence of their objects, enter such a man without disturbing him; they dissolve in the Self and do not bring him under their sway. The peace he attains is liberation.

विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान्पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः । निर्ममो निरहंकारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति ॥ ७१ ॥
vihāya kāmānyaḥ sarvānpumāṃścarati niḥspṛhaḥ | nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ sa śāntimadhigacchati || 71 ||
Padaccheda
विहाय कामान् यः सर्वान् पुमान् चरति निःस्पृहः निर्ममः निरहंकारः सः शान्तिम् अधिगच्छति
vihāya kāmān yaḥ sarvān pumān carati niḥspṛhaḥ nirmamaḥ nirahaṃkāraḥ saḥ śāntim adhigacchati
Word by word
The man who abandons all desires and moves about free from longing, without the sense of ‘mine’ and without the sense of ‘I’, attains peace.

Bhāṣya He is the renouncer, whose only activity is what keeps the body alive, and who longs not even for that. nirmama: without the notion ‘this is mine’ even of the few things needed for life. nirahaṃkāra: without self-regard on account of learning and the like. The peace is the end of all the sorrow of worldly existence, called nirvāṇa: he becomes Brahman.

एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति । स्थित्वास्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥ ७२ ॥
eṣā brāhmī sthitiḥ pārtha naināṃ prāpya vimuhyati | sthitvāsyāmantakāle 'pi brahmanirvāṇamṛcchati || 72 ||
Padaccheda
एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ न एनाम् प्राप्य विमुह्यति स्थित्वा अस्याम् अन्त-काले अपि ब्रह्म-निर्वाणम् ऋच्छति
eṣā brāhmī sthitiḥ pārtha na enām prāpya vimuhyati sthitvā asyām anta-kāle api brahma-nirvāṇam ṛcchati
Word by word
This is the state of Brahman, O Pārtha. Having attained it, one is no longer deluded. Abiding in it even at the last hour, one attains the bliss of Brahman.

Bhāṣya The state of Brahman is to renounce all action and abide as Brahman. brahmanirvāṇa is the bliss of Brahman, liberation. If even one who reaches this state in the last stage of life attains it, how much more one who renounces straight from the student's life and abides in Brahman as long as he lives.
