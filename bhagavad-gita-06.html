षष्ठोऽध्यायः
Ṣaṣṭho 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 6 · Ātmasaṃyamayoga
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
अनाश्रितः कर्मफलं कार्यं कर्म करोति यः । स संन्यासी च योगी च न निरग्निर्न चाक्रियः ॥ १ ॥
anāśritaḥ karmaphalaṃ kāryaṃ karma karoti yaḥ | sa saṃnyāsī ca yogī ca na niragnirna cākriyaḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
अनाश्रितः कर्म-फलम् कार्यम् कर्म करोति यः सः संन्यासी च योगी च न निरग्निः न च अक्रियः
anāśritaḥ karma-phalam kāryam karma karoti yaḥ saḥ saṃnyāsī ca yogī ca na niragniḥ na ca akriyaḥ
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: He who performs the action that is to be done without depending on its fruit is a renouncer and a yogī; not he who is merely without the fires and without rites.

Note Śaṅkara: without thirst for the fruit of action, he performs the obligatory rites such as the Agnihotra. Yoga is the collectedness of the mind. He, and not only the one who has given up the sacred fires and the rites, such as austerity and giving, is to be thought of as a renouncer and a yogī. Both names are used of him in a secondary sense, in praise: he renounces the intention for the fruit, and he acts as a step towards yoga.

यं संन्यासमिति प्राहुर्योगं तं विद्धि पाण्डव । न ह्यसंन्यस्तसंकल्पो योगी भवति कश्चन ॥ २ ॥
yaṃ saṃnyāsamiti prāhuryogaṃ taṃ viddhi pāṇḍava | na hyasaṃnyastasaṃkalpo yogī bhavati kaścana || 2 ||
Padaccheda
यम् संन्यासम् इति प्राहुः योगम् तम् विद्धि पाण्डव न हि असंन्यस्त-संकल्पः योगी भवति कश्चन
yam saṃnyāsam iti prāhuḥ yogam tam viddhi pāṇḍava na hi asaṃnyasta-saṃkalpaḥ yogī bhavati kaścana
Word by word
What they call renunciation, know that to be yoga, O son of Pāṇḍu; for no one becomes a yogī who has not renounced intention.

Note Śaṅkara: true renunciation is the giving up of all actions and their fruits; yoga here is the performance of action. The two are alike in the agent: the true renouncer gives up the intention regarding all actions and their fruits, and the man of action, even while acting, gives up the intention regarding the fruit. That intention distracts the mind, so no one who keeps it can be collected.

आरुरुक्षोर्मुनेर्योगं कर्म कारणमुच्यते । योगारूढस्य तस्यैव शमः कारणमुच्यते ॥ ३ ॥
ārurukṣormuneryogaṃ karma kāraṇamucyate | yogārūḍhasya tasyaiva śamaḥ kāraṇamucyate || 3 ||
Padaccheda
आरुरुक्षोः मुनेः योगम् कर्म कारणम् उच्यते योग-आरूढस्य तस्य एव शमः कारणम् उच्यते
ārurukṣoḥ muneḥ yogam karma kāraṇam ucyate yoga-ārūḍhasya tasya eva śamaḥ kāraṇam ucyate
Word by word
For the sage who wishes to ascend to yoga, action is said to be the means; for the same man, when he has ascended to yoga, quiescence is said to be the means.

Note Śaṅkara: the yoga of action without regard for fruit is the outer means to the yoga of meditation. The one who wishes to ascend cannot yet remain in meditation; the sage is one who has renounced the fruit of action. Quiescence is withdrawal from all actions: the more he withdraws from actions, the more the mind of the unwearied man of conquered senses becomes collected.

यदा हि नेन्द्रियार्थेषु न कर्मस्वनुषज्जते । सर्वसंकल्पसंन्यासी योगारूढस्तदोच्यते ॥ ४ ॥
yadā hi nendriyārtheṣu na karmasvanuṣajjate | sarvasaṃkalpasaṃnyāsī yogārūḍhastadocyate || 4 ||
Padaccheda
यदा हि न इन्द्रिय-अर्थेषु न कर्मसु अनुषज्जते सर्व-संकल्प-संन्यासी योग-आरूढः तदा उच्यते
yadā hi na indriya-artheṣu na karmasu anuṣajjate sarva-saṃkalpa-saṃnyāsī yoga-ārūḍhaḥ tadā ucyate
Word by word
For when he is attached neither to the objects of the senses nor to actions, having renounced all intentions, then he is said to have ascended to yoga.

Note Śaṅkara: he has no sense that the objects, or actions of any kind, are something he must pursue. All desires have their root in intention, and all action comes from desire, as scripture and the law books say; so by ‘renounced all intentions’ the Lord has him give up all desires and all actions.

उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् । आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः ॥ ५ ॥
uddharedātmanātmānaṃ nātmānamavasādayet | ātmaiva hyātmano bandhur ātmaiva ripurātmanaḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
उद्धरेत् आत्मना आत्मानम् न आत्मानम् अवसादयेत् आत्मा एव हि आत्मनः बन्धुः आत्मा एव रिपुः आत्मनः
uddharet ātmanā ātmānam na ātmānam avasādayet ātmā eva hi ātmanaḥ bandhuḥ ātmā eva ripuḥ ātmanaḥ
Word by word
One should raise oneself by oneself, and not let oneself sink; for oneself alone is one's friend, oneself alone one's enemy.

Note Śaṅkara: raise the self that is sunk in the ocean of the cycle of birth and death, that is, bring it to the state of one who has ascended to yoga. No other friend brings release; even a kinsman, a seat of the bonds of affection, stands in the way of liberation. An outward enemy too is set in motion by oneself.

बन्धुरात्मात्मनस्तस्य येनात्मैवात्मना जितः । अनात्मनस्तु शत्रुत्वे वर्तेतात्मैव शत्रुवत् ॥ ६ ॥
bandhurātmātmanastasya yenātmaivātmanā jitaḥ | anātmanastu śatrutve vartetātmaiva śatruvat || 6 ||
Padaccheda
बन्धुः आत्मा आत्मनः तस्य येन आत्मा एव आत्मना जितः अनात्मनः तु शत्रुत्वे वर्तेत आत्मा एव शत्रुवत्
bandhuḥ ātmā ātmanaḥ tasya yena ātmā eva ātmanā jitaḥ anātmanaḥ tu śatrutve varteta ātmā eva śatruvat
Word by word
The self is a friend of him who has conquered himself by himself; but for one who has not conquered himself, the self would act in enmity, like an enemy.

Note Śaṅkara: the self that is conquered is the aggregate of body and organs; he has mastered his senses.

जितात्मनः प्रशान्तस्य परमात्मा समाहितः । शीतोष्णसुखदुःखेषु तथा मानावमानयोः ॥ ७ ॥
jitātmanaḥ praśāntasya paramātmā samāhitaḥ | śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkheṣu tathā mānāvamānayoḥ || 7 ||
Padaccheda
जित-आत्मनः प्रशान्तस्य परम-आत्मा समाहितः शीत-उष्ण-सुख-दुःखेषु तथा मान-अवमानयोः
jita-ātmanaḥ praśāntasya parama-ātmā samāhitaḥ śīta-uṣṇa-sukha-duḥkheṣu tathā māna-avamānayoḥ
Word by word
For one who has conquered himself and is tranquil, the supreme Self is directly present, in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, and likewise in honour and dishonour.

Note Śaṅkara: for such a renouncer, whose mind is serene, the supreme Self is present as his very own Self; and he should be the same in cold and heat and the rest.

ज्ञानविज्ञानतृप्तात्मा कूटस्थो विजितेन्द्रियः । युक्त इत्युच्यते योगी समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चनः ॥ ८ ॥
jñānavijñānatṛptātmā kūṭastho vijitendriyaḥ | yukta ityucyate yogī samaloṣṭāśmakāñcanaḥ || 8 ||
Padaccheda
ज्ञान-विज्ञान-तृप्त-आत्मा कूटस्थः विजित-इन्द्रियः युक्तः इति उच्यते योगी सम-लोष्ट-अश्म-काञ्चनः
jñāna-vijñāna-tṛpta-ātmā kūṭasthaḥ vijita-indriyaḥ yuktaḥ iti ucyate yogī sama-loṣṭa-aśma-kāñcanaḥ
Word by word
The yogī whose mind is satisfied with knowledge and realization, who is unshaken, with senses conquered, and to whom a clod, a stone and gold are the same, is said to be collected.

Note Śaṅkara: jñāna is the thorough knowledge of the things taught in scripture; vijñāna is making what is so known a matter of one's own experience.

सुहृन्मित्रार्युदासीनमध्यस्थद्वेष्यबन्धुषु । साधुष्वपि च पापेषु समबुद्धिर्विशिष्यते ॥ ९ ॥
suhṛnmitrāryudāsīnamadhyasthadveṣyabandhuṣu | sādhuṣvapi ca pāpeṣu samabuddhirviśiṣyate || 9 ||
Padaccheda
सुहृद्-मित्र-अरि-उदासीन-मध्यस्थ-द्वेष्य-बन्धुषु साधुषु अपि च पापेषु सम-बुद्धिः विशिष्यते
suhṛd-mitra-ari-udāsīna-madhyastha-dveṣya-bandhuṣu sādhuṣu api ca pāpeṣu sama-buddhiḥ viśiṣyate
Word by word
One whose understanding is the same towards well-wishers, friends, enemies, the indifferent, the neutral, the hateful and kinsmen, and towards the good and even the sinful, excels.

Note Śaṅkara: a well-wisher helps without looking for a return; a friend has affection; the indifferent takes no side; the neutral wishes both opponents well. The good follow scripture; the sinful do what is forbidden. He does not occupy his mind with who a man is or what he does. He is the best of those who have ascended to yoga. Śaṅkara records another reading, vimucyate, ‘is freed’.

योगी युञ्जीत सततमात्मानं रहसि स्थितः । एकाकी यतचित्तात्मा निराशीरपरिग्रहः ॥ १० ॥
yogī yuñjīta satatamātmānaṃ rahasi sthitaḥ | ekākī yatacittātmā nirāśīraparigrahaḥ || 10 ||
Padaccheda
योगी युञ्जीत सततम् आत्मानम् रहसि स्थितः एकाकी यत-चित्त-आत्मा निराशीः अपरिग्रहः
yogī yuñjīta satatam ātmānam rahasi sthitaḥ ekākī yata-citta-ātmā nirāśīḥ aparigrahaḥ
Word by word
The yogī should constantly concentrate his mind, remaining in a solitary place, alone, with mind and body controlled, free from expectation and without possessions.

Note Śaṅkara: the yogī is the one who meditates. A solitary place is a mountain cave or the like. ‘In solitude’ and ‘alone’ imply that he has taken to renunciation.

शुचौ देशे प्रतिष्ठाप्य स्थिरमासनमात्मनः । नात्युच्छ्रितं नातिनीचं चैलाजिनकुशोत्तरम् ॥ ११ ॥
śucau deśe pratiṣṭhāpya sthiramāsanamātmanaḥ | nātyucchritaṃ nātinīcaṃ cailājinakuśottaram || 11 ||
Padaccheda
शुचौ देशे प्रतिष्ठाप्य स्थिरम् आसनम् आत्मनः न अति-उच्छ्रितम् न अति-नीचम् चैल-अजिन-कुश-उत्तरम्
śucau deśe pratiṣṭhāpya sthiram āsanam ātmanaḥ na ati-ucchritam na ati-nīcam caila-ajina-kuśa-uttaram
Word by word
Having set up in a clean place his own firm seat, neither too high nor too low, covered with cloth, a skin and kuśa grass,

Note Śaṅkara: clean by nature or made so, and secluded. The cloth, skin and grass are laid in the reverse of the order in which they are named.

तत्रैकाग्रं मनः कृत्वा यतचित्तेन्द्रियक्रियः । उपविश्यासने युञ्ज्याद्योगमात्मविशुद्धये ॥ १२ ॥
tatraikāgraṃ manaḥ kṛtvā yatacittendriyakriyaḥ | upaviśyāsane yuñjyādyogamātmaviśuddhaye || 12 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र एक-अग्रम् मनः कृत्वा यत-चित्त-इन्द्रिय-क्रियः उपविश्य आसने युञ्ज्यात् योगम् आत्म-विशुद्धये
tatra eka-agram manaḥ kṛtvā yata-citta-indriya-kriyaḥ upaviśya āsane yuñjyāt yogam ātma-viśuddhaye
Word by word
sitting there on the seat, having made the mind one-pointed, with the activities of mind and senses controlled, he should practise yoga for the purification of the mind.

Note Śaṅkara: the mind is made one-pointed by withdrawing it from all objects.

समं कायशिरोग्रीवं धारयन्नचलं स्थिरः । सम्प्रेक्ष्य नासिकाग्रं स्वं दिशश्चानवलोकयन् ॥ १३ ॥
samaṃ kāyaśirogrīvaṃ dhārayannacalaṃ sthiraḥ | samprekṣya nāsikāgraṃ svaṃ diśaścānavalokayan || 13 ||
Padaccheda
समम् काय-शिरः-ग्रीवम् धारयन् अचलम् स्थिरः सम्प्रेक्ष्य नासिका-अग्रम् स्वम् दिशः च अनवलोकयन्
samam kāya-śiraḥ-grīvam dhārayan acalam sthiraḥ samprekṣya nāsikā-agram svam diśaḥ ca anavalokayan
Word by word
Holding body, head and neck straight and unmoving, steady, gazing as it were at the tip of his own nose and not looking about in any direction,

Note Śaṅkara: the word iva, ‘as it were’, is to be supplied. Gazing at the tip of the nose is not what is enjoined here; what is meant is the drawing in of the sight of the eyes. If the gaze itself were meant, the mind would be fixed there and not on the Self, on which it is to be fixed (verse 25).

प्रशान्तात्मा विगतभीर्ब्रह्मचारिव्रते स्थितः । मनः संयम्य मच्चित्तो युक्त आसीत मत्परः ॥ १४ ॥
praśāntātmā vigatabhīrbrahmacārivrate sthitaḥ | manaḥ saṃyamya maccitto yukta āsīta matparaḥ || 14 ||
Padaccheda
प्रशान्त-आत्मा विगत-भीः ब्रह्मचारि-व्रते स्थितः मनः संयम्य मद्-चित्तः युक्तः आसीत मद्-परः
praśānta-ātmā vigata-bhīḥ brahmacāri-vrate sthitaḥ manaḥ saṃyamya mad-cittaḥ yuktaḥ āsīta mad-paraḥ
Word by word
with mind tranquil, free from fear, firm in the vow of a celibate student, having restrained the mind, he should sit collected, with his thought on me, holding me as the highest.

Note Śaṅkara: the vow is continence, service of the teacher, living on alms and the like. To restrain the mind is to withdraw its movements. ‘Me’ is the supreme Lord. A man in love has his thought on a woman, but does not hold her as the highest; this one has both his thought on the Lord and the Lord as the highest.

युञ्जन्नेवं सदात्मानं योगी नियतमानसः । शान्तिं निर्वाणपरमां मत्संस्थामधिगच्छति ॥ १५ ॥
yuñjannevaṃ sadātmānaṃ yogī niyatamānasaḥ | śāntiṃ nirvāṇaparamāṃ matsaṃsthāmadhigacchati || 15 ||
Padaccheda
युञ्जन् एवम् सदा आत्मानम् योगी नियत-मानसः शान्तिम् निर्वाण-परमाम् मद्-संस्थाम् अधिगच्छति
yuñjan evam sadā ātmānam yogī niyata-mānasaḥ śāntim nirvāṇa-paramām mad-saṃsthām adhigacchati
Word by word
Always concentrating the mind thus, the yogī of controlled mind attains the peace that culminates in liberation and rests in me.

Note Śaṅkara: peace is cessation; nirvāṇa is liberation, in which that peace ends. matsaṃsthā: dependent on me.

नात्यश्नतस्तु योगोऽस्ति न चैकान्तमनश्नतः । न चातिस्वप्नशीलस्य जाग्रतो नैव चार्जुन ॥ १६ ॥
nātyaśnatastu yogo 'sti na caikāntam anaśnataḥ | na cātisvapnaśīlasya jāgrato naiva cārjuna || 16 ||
Padaccheda
न अत्यश्नतः तु योगः अस्ति न च एकान्तम् अन्-अश्नतः न च अति-स्वप्न-शीलस्य जाग्रतः न एव च अर्जुन
na atyaśnataḥ tu yogaḥ asti na ca ekāntam an-aśnataḥ na ca ati-svapna-śīlasya jāgrataḥ na eva ca arjuna
Word by word
Yoga is not for one who eats too much, nor for one who does not eat at all; not for one given to too much sleep, nor for one who keeps awake, O Arjuna.

Note Śaṅkara: too much is more than the measure that suits oneself, as the Veda says: food that matches oneself protects and does not harm, more harms, less does not protect. Or it is more than the measure laid down in the teaching of yoga: half the stomach for food, a third for water, and a fourth left free for the movement of air.

युक्ताहारविहारस्य युक्तचेष्टस्य कर्मसु । युक्तस्वप्नावबोधस्य योगो भवति दुःखहा ॥ १७ ॥
yuktāhāravihārasya yuktaceṣṭasya karmasu | yuktasvapnāvabodhasya yogo bhavati duḥkhahā || 17 ||
Padaccheda
युक्त-आहार-विहारस्य युक्त-चेष्टस्य कर्मसु युक्त-स्वप्न-अवबोधस्य योगः भवति दुःख-हा
yukta-āhāra-vihārasya yukta-ceṣṭasya karmasu yukta-svapna-avabodhasya yogaḥ bhavati duḥkha-hā
Word by word
For one who is measured in food and movement, measured in effort in actions, and measured in sleep and waking, yoga becomes the destroyer of sorrow.

Note Śaṅkara: vihāra is walking about. Yoga destroys all the sorrow of the cycle of birth and death.

यदा विनियतं चित्तमात्मन्येवावतिष्ठते । निःस्पृहः सर्वकामेभ्यो युक्त इत्युच्यते तदा ॥ १८ ॥
yadā viniyataṃ cittamātmanyevāvatiṣṭhate | niḥspṛhaḥ sarvakāmebhyo yukta ityucyate tadā || 18 ||
Padaccheda
यदा विनियतम् चित्तम् आत्मनि एव अवतिष्ठते निःस्पृहः सर्व-कामेभ्यः युक्तः इति उच्यते तदा
yadā viniyatam cittam ātmani eva avatiṣṭhate niḥspṛhaḥ sarva-kāmebhyaḥ yuktaḥ iti ucyate tadā
Word by word
When the well-controlled mind rests in the Self alone, and he is free from longing for all objects of desire, then he is said to be collected.

Note Śaṅkara: the mind, become one-pointed, leaves the thought of outward things and rests in the Self alone. The objects of desire are both seen and unseen.

यथा दीपो निवातस्थो नेङ्गते सोपमा स्मृता । योगिनो यतचित्तस्य युञ्जतो योगमात्मनः ॥ १९ ॥
yathā dīpo nivātastho neṅgate sopamā smṛtā | yogino yatacittasya yuñjato yogamātmanaḥ || 19 ||
Padaccheda
यथा दीपः निवात-स्थः न इङ्गते सा उपमा स्मृता योगिनः यत-चित्तस्य युञ्जतः योगम् आत्मनः
yathā dīpaḥ nivāta-sthaḥ na iṅgate sā upamā smṛtā yoginaḥ yata-cittasya yuñjataḥ yogam ātmanaḥ
Word by word
‘As a lamp in a windless place does not flicker’: that is the comparison thought of for the yogī of controlled mind who practises concentration on the Self.

Note Śaṅkara: the comparison is for the collected mind of the yogī, and was thought of by those who know yoga and have seen the ways of the mind.

यत्रोपरमते चित्तं निरुद्धं योगसेवया । यत्र चैवात्मनात्मानं पश्यन्नात्मनि तुष्यति ॥ २० ॥
yatroparamate cittaṃ niruddhaṃ yogasevayā | yatra caivātmanātmānaṃ paśyannātmani tuṣyati || 20 ||
Padaccheda
यत्र उपरमते चित्तम् निरुद्धम् योग-सेवया यत्र च एव आत्मना आत्मानम् पश्यन् आत्मनि तुष्यति
yatra uparamate cittam niruddham yoga-sevayā yatra ca eva ātmanā ātmānam paśyan ātmani tuṣyati
Word by word
When the mind, restrained by the practice of yoga, comes to rest, and when, seeing the Self by the mind, he is content in the Self;

Note Śaṅkara: yatra means ‘at which time’. By the mind made pure by concentration he perceives the Self, the supreme consciousness whose nature is light. Verses 20 to 23 form one sentence describing yoga.

सुखमात्यन्तिकं यत्तद्बुद्धिग्राह्यमतीन्द्रियम् । वेत्ति यत्र न चैवायं स्थितश्चलति तत्त्वतः ॥ २१ ॥
sukhamātyantikaṃ yattadbuddhigrāhyamatīndriyam | vetti yatra na caivāyaṃ sthitaścalati tattvataḥ || 21 ||
Padaccheda
सुखम् आत्यन्तिकम् यत् तत् बुद्धि-ग्राह्यम् अतीन्द्रियम् वेत्ति यत्र न च एव अयम् स्थितः चलति तत्त्वतः
sukham ātyantikam yat tat buddhi-grāhyam atīndriyam vetti yatra na ca eva ayam sthitaḥ calati tattvataḥ
Word by word
when he knows that endless happiness which is grasped by the understanding and is beyond the senses, and, established there, never moves from the truth;

Note Śaṅkara: grasped by the understanding alone, without the senses, and not produced by objects. Established in the nature of the Self, the knower does not fall away from it.

यं लब्ध्वा चापरं लाभं मन्यते नाधिकं ततः । यस्मिन्स्थितो न दुःखेन गुरुणापि विचाल्यते ॥ २२ ॥
yaṃ labdhvā cāparaṃ lābhaṃ manyate nādhikaṃ tataḥ | yasminsthito na duḥkhena guruṇāpi vicālyate || 22 ||
Padaccheda
यम् लब्ध्वा च अपरम् लाभम् मन्यते न अधिकम् ततः यस्मिन् स्थितः न दुःखेन गुरुणा अपि विचाल्यते
yam labdhvā ca aparam lābham manyate na adhikam tataḥ yasmin sthitaḥ na duḥkhena guruṇā api vicālyate
Word by word
having gained which he thinks no other gain greater than that, and established in which he is not shaken even by heavy sorrow:

Note Śaṅkara: the gain is the gain of the Self. Heavy sorrow is such as the blow of a weapon.

तं विद्याद्दुःखसंयोगवियोगं योगसंज्ञितम् । स निश्चयेन योक्तव्यो योगोऽनिर्विण्णचेतसा ॥ २३ ॥
taṃ vidyādduḥkhasaṃyogaviyogaṃ yogasaṃjñitam | sa niścayena yoktavyo yogo 'nirviṇṇacetasā || 23 ||
Padaccheda
तम् विद्यात् दुःख-संयोग-वियोगम् योग-संज्ञितम् सः निश्चयेन योक्तव्यः योगः अनिर्विण्ण-चेतसा
tam vidyāt duḥkha-saṃyoga-viyogam yoga-saṃjñitam saḥ niścayena yoktavyaḥ yogaḥ anirviṇṇa-cetasā
Word by word
that severing of union with sorrow one should know as what is called yoga. That yoga is to be practised with resolve and with an undespairing mind.

Note Śaṅkara: this particular state of the Self, marked by all that has been said since verse 20, is called yoga, ‘union’, by a name that says the opposite of what it is, a disunion. Having summed up its fruit, the Lord again enjoins its practice, to teach that resolve and freedom from despair are means to it.

संकल्पप्रभवान्कामांस्त्यक्त्वा सर्वानशेषतः । मनसैवेन्द्रियग्रामं विनियम्य समन्ततः ॥ २४ ॥
saṃkalpaprabhavānkāmāṃstyaktvā sarvānaśeṣataḥ | manasaivendriyagrāmaṃ viniyamya samantataḥ || 24 ||
Padaccheda
संकल्प-प्रभवान् कामान् त्यक्त्वा सर्वान् अशेषतः मनसा एव इन्द्रिय-ग्रामम् विनियम्य समन्ततः
saṃkalpa-prabhavān kāmān tyaktvā sarvān aśeṣataḥ manasā eva indriya-grāmam viniyamya samantataḥ
Word by word
Having given up without remainder all the desires that arise from intention, and having restrained the group of the senses on every side by the mind itself,

Note Śaṅkara: by the mind joined with discernment.

शनैः शनैरुपरमेद्बुद्ध्या धृतिगृहीतया । आत्मसंस्थं मनः कृत्वा न किंचिदपि चिन्तयेत् ॥ २५ ॥
śanaiḥ śanairuparamedbuddhyā dhṛtigṛhītayā | ātmasaṃsthaṃ manaḥ kṛtvā na kiṃcidapi cintayet || 25 ||
Padaccheda
शनैः शनैः उपरमेत् बुद्ध्या धृति-गृहीतया आत्म-संस्थम् मनः कृत्वा न किंचित् अपि चिन्तयेत्
śanaiḥ śanaiḥ uparamet buddhyā dhṛti-gṛhītayā ātma-saṃstham manaḥ kṛtvā na kiṃcit api cintayet
Word by word
he should come to rest little by little, by the understanding held with firmness; having made the mind rest in the Self, he should not think of anything at all.

Note Śaṅkara: not suddenly. The mind rests in the Self in the thought ‘the Self alone is all; there is nothing else’. This is the highest rule of yoga.

यतो यतो निश्चरति मनश्चञ्चलमस्थिरम् । ततस्ततो नियम्यैतदात्मन्येव वशं नयेत् ॥ २६ ॥
yato yato niścarati manaścañcalamasthiram | tatastato niyamyaitadātmanyeva vaśaṃ nayet || 26 ||
Padaccheda
यतः यतः निश्चरति मनः चञ्चलम् अस्थिरम् ततः ततः नियम्य एतत् आत्मनि एव वशम् नयेत्
yataḥ yataḥ niścarati manaḥ cañcalam asthiram tataḥ tataḥ niyamya etat ātmani eva vaśam nayet
Word by word
From whatever cause the restless, unsteady mind wanders away, from that he should hold it back and bring it under the control of the Self alone.

Note Śaṅkara: the causes are sound and the other objects. He holds the mind back from each by examining what it really is, so that it is seen as a mere appearance, and by cultivating dispassion.

प्रशान्तमनसं ह्येनं योगिनं सुखमुत्तमम् । उपैति शान्तरजसं ब्रह्मभूतमकल्मषम् ॥ २७ ॥
praśāntamanasaṃ hyenaṃ yoginaṃ sukhamuttamam | upaiti śāntarajasaṃ brahmabhūtamakalmaṣam || 27 ||
Padaccheda
प्रशान्त-मनसम् हि एनम् योगिनम् सुखम् उत्तमम् उपैति शान्त-रजसम् ब्रह्म-भूतम् अकल्मषम्
praśānta-manasam hi enam yoginam sukham uttamam upaiti śānta-rajasam brahma-bhūtam akalmaṣam
Word by word
For the highest happiness comes to this yogī whose mind is tranquil, whose rajas is stilled, who has become Brahman and is stainless.

Note Śaṅkara: his rajas, the afflictions such as delusion, is worn away. brahmabhūta: liberated while living, sure that ‘all is Brahman alone’. Stainless: free of merit, demerit and the like.

युञ्जन्नेवं सदात्मानं योगी विगतकल्मषः । सुखेन ब्रह्मसंस्पर्शमत्यन्तं सुखमश्नुते ॥ २८ ॥
yuñjannevaṃ sadātmānaṃ yogī vigatakalmaṣaḥ | sukhena brahmasaṃsparśamatyantaṃ sukhamaśnute || 28 ||
Padaccheda
युञ्जन् एवम् सदा आत्मानम् योगी विगत-कल्मषः सुखेन ब्रह्म-संस्पर्शम् अत्यन्तम् सुखम् अश्नुते
yuñjan evam sadā ātmānam yogī vigata-kalmaṣaḥ sukhena brahma-saṃsparśam atyantam sukham aśnute
Word by word
Always concentrating the mind thus, the yogī, freed from stain, attains with ease the boundless happiness of contact with Brahman.

Note Śaṅkara: free of the obstacles to yoga. atyanta: going beyond any end, unsurpassed.

सर्वभूतस्थमात्मानं सर्वभूतानि चात्मनि । ईक्षते योगयुक्तात्मा सर्वत्र समदर्शनः ॥ २९ ॥
sarvabhūtasthamātmānaṃ sarvabhūtāni cātmani | īkṣate yogayuktātmā sarvatra samadarśanaḥ || 29 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-भूत-स्थम् आत्मानम् सर्व-भूतानि च आत्मनि ईक्षते योग-युक्त-आत्मा सर्वत्र सम-दर्शनः
sarva-bhūta-stham ātmānam sarva-bhūtāni ca ātmani īkṣate yoga-yukta-ātmā sarvatra sama-darśanaḥ
Word by word
He whose mind is collected by yoga, seeing the same everywhere, sees the Self present in all beings and all beings in the Self.

Note Śaṅkara: this shows the fruit of yoga, the vision of oneness with Brahman, which cuts off the whole cycle of birth and death. He sees his own Self in all beings, and all beings, from Brahmā down to a tuft of grass, made one in the Self. In all beings, unequal as they are, his knowledge is of the same undifferentiated oneness of Brahman and the Self.

यो मां पश्यति सर्वत्र सर्वं च मयि पश्यति । तस्याहं न प्रणश्यामि स च मे न प्रणश्यति ॥ ३० ॥
yo māṃ paśyati sarvatra sarvaṃ ca mayi paśyati | tasyāhaṃ na praṇaśyāmi sa ca me na praṇaśyati || 30 ||
Padaccheda
यः माम् पश्यति सर्वत्र सर्वम् च मयि पश्यति तस्य अहम् न प्रणश्यामि सः च मे न प्रणश्यति
yaḥ mām paśyati sarvatra sarvam ca mayi paśyati tasya aham na praṇaśyāmi saḥ ca me na praṇaśyati
Word by word
He who sees me everywhere and sees all in me: to him I am not lost, and he is not lost to me.

Note Śaṅkara: this is the fruit of the vision of the oneness of the Self. ‘Me’ is Vāsudeva, the Self of all. ‘Not lost’: the Lord does not pass out of his sight, nor he out of the Lord's, since he and the Lord are one Self, and one's own Self is always dear to oneself.

सर्वभूतस्थितं यो मां भजत्येकत्वमास्थितः । सर्वथा वर्तमानोऽपि स योगी मयि वर्तते ॥ ३१ ॥
sarvabhūtasthitaṃ yo māṃ bhajatyekatvamāsthitaḥ | sarvathā vartamāno 'pi sa yogī mayi vartate || 31 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-भूत-स्थितम् यः माम् भजति एकत्वम् आस्थितः सर्वथा वर्तमानः अपि सः योगी मयि वर्तते
sarva-bhūta-sthitam yaḥ mām bhajati ekatvam āsthitaḥ sarvathā vartamānaḥ api saḥ yogī mayi vartate
Word by word
He who, established in oneness, worships me as present in all beings: in whatever way he lives, that yogī lives in me.

Note Śaṅkara: the first half restates the right vision of the previous verse; its fruit, liberation, is then stated. However he lives, the man of right vision abides in the Lord, the highest state of Viṣṇu; he is ever free, and nothing bars him from liberation.

आत्मौपम्येन सर्वत्र समं पश्यति योऽर्जुन । सुखं वा यदि वा दुःखं स योगी परमो मतः ॥ ३२ ॥
ātmaupamyena sarvatra samaṃ paśyati yo 'rjuna | sukhaṃ vā yadi vā duḥkhaṃ sa yogī paramo mataḥ || 32 ||
Padaccheda
आत्म-औपम्येन सर्वत्र समम् पश्यति यः अर्जुन सुखम् वा यदि वा दुःखम् सः योगी परमः मतः
ātma-aupamyena sarvatra samam paśyati yaḥ arjuna sukham vā yadi vā duḥkham saḥ yogī paramaḥ mataḥ
Word by word
He who, by comparison with himself, sees pleasure or pain as the same everywhere, O Arjuna, is held to be the highest yogī.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘as pleasure is welcome to me, so it is to all living beings; as pain is unwelcome to me, so it is to all’. Seeing this, he acts against no one: he harms none. Such a one, harmless and steadfast in right vision, is the best among all yogīs.

अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
योऽयं योगस्त्वया प्रोक्तः साम्येन मधुसूदन । एतस्याहं न पश्यामि चञ्चलत्वात्स्थितिं स्थिराम् ॥ ३३ ॥
yo 'yaṃ yogastvayā proktaḥ sāmyena madhusūdana | etasyāhaṃ na paśyāmi cañcalatvātsthitiṃ sthirām || 33 ||
Padaccheda
यः अयम् योगः त्वया प्रोक्तः साम्येन मधुसूदन एतस्य अहम् न पश्यामि चञ्चलत्वात् स्थितिम् स्थिराम्
yaḥ ayam yogaḥ tvayā proktaḥ sāmyena madhusūdana etasya aham na paśyāmi cañcalatvāt sthitim sthirām
Word by word
Arjuna said: This yoga that you have taught as sameness, O slayer of Madhu: I do not see a firm footing for it, because of restlessness.

Note Śaṅkara: the restlessness is that of the mind.

चञ्चलं हि मनः कृष्ण प्रमाथि बलवद्दृढम् । तस्याहं निग्रहं मन्ये वायोरिव सुदुष्करम् ॥ ३४ ॥
cañcalaṃ hi manaḥ kṛṣṇa pramāthi balavaddṛḍham | tasyāhaṃ nigrahaṃ manye vāyoriva suduṣkaram || 34 ||
Padaccheda
चञ्चलम् हि मनः कृष्ण प्रमाथि बलवत् दृढम् तस्य अहम् निग्रहम् मन्ये वायोः इव सु-दुष्करम्
cañcalam hi manaḥ kṛṣṇa pramāthi balavat dṛḍham tasya aham nigraham manye vāyoḥ iva su-duṣkaram
Word by word
For the mind is restless, O Kṛṣṇa, turbulent, strong and unyielding. I think its restraint is very hard to achieve, like that of the wind.

Note Śaṅkara: Kṛṣṇa is from kṛṣ, ‘to scrape away’: he draws away sin and other faults from his devotees. The mind is turbulent because it throws body and senses into disorder and subjects them to another. Its restraint is harder even than that of the wind.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम् । अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते ॥ ३५ ॥
asaṃśayaṃ mahābāho mano durnigrahaṃ calam | abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate || 35 ||
Padaccheda
असंशयम् महा-बाहो मनः दुर्निग्रहम् चलम् अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते
asaṃśayam mahā-bāho manaḥ durnigraham calam abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: Without doubt, O mighty-armed, the mind is restless and hard to restrain; but by practice, O son of Kuntī, and by dispassion it is held.

Note Śaṅkara: practice is the repetition of the same thought on some one ground of the mind. Dispassion is freedom from thirst for pleasures seen and unseen, gained by repeatedly seeing their faults.

असंयतात्मना योगो दुष्प्राप इति मे मतिः । वश्यात्मना तु यतता शक्योऽवाप्तुमुपायतः ॥ ३६ ॥
asaṃyatātmanā yogo duṣprāpa iti me matiḥ | vaśyātmanā tu yatatā śakyo 'vāptumupāyataḥ || 36 ||
Padaccheda
असंयत-आत्मना योगः दुष्प्रापः इति मे मतिः वश्य-आत्मना तु यतता शक्यः अवाप्तुम् उपायतः
asaṃyata-ātmanā yogaḥ duṣprāpaḥ iti me matiḥ vaśya-ātmanā tu yatatā śakyaḥ avāptum upāyataḥ
Word by word
Yoga is hard to attain by one whose mind is not controlled; this is my view. But by one whose mind is mastered and who strives, it can be attained by the right means.

Note Śaṅkara: controlled, and mastered, by practice and dispassion; these are the means.

अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
अयतिः श्रद्धयोपेतो योगाच्चलितमानसः । अप्राप्य योगसंसिद्धिं कां गतिं कृष्ण गच्छति ॥ ३७ ॥
ayatiḥ śraddhayopeto yogāccalitamānasaḥ | aprāpya yogasaṃsiddhiṃ kāṃ gatiṃ kṛṣṇa gacchati || 37 ||
Padaccheda
अयतिः श्रद्धया उपेतः योगात् चलित-मानसः अ-प्राप्य योग-संसिद्धिम् काम् गतिम् कृष्ण गच्छति
ayatiḥ śraddhayā upetaḥ yogāt calita-mānasaḥ a-prāpya yoga-saṃsiddhim kām gatim kṛṣṇa gacchati
Word by word
Arjuna said: One who has faith but does not strive, whose mind has strayed from yoga and who has not attained perfection in yoga: to what end does he go, O Kṛṣṇa?

Note Śaṅkara: on taking up the practice of yoga he has renounced the actions that win this world and the next, and he has not gained right vision, the fruit of perfection in yoga and the means to liberation. Arjuna fears he is lost if his mind strays from the path at the time of death.

कच्चिन्नोभयविभ्रष्टश्छिन्नाभ्रमिव नश्यति । अप्रतिष्ठो महाबाहो विमूढो ब्रह्मणः पथि ॥ ३८ ॥
kaccinnobhayavibhraṣṭaśchinnābhramiva naśyati | apratiṣṭho mahābāho vimūḍho brahmaṇaḥ pathi || 38 ||
Padaccheda
कच्चित् न उभय-विभ्रष्टः छिन्न-अभ्रम् इव नश्यति अप्रतिष्ठः महा-बाहो विमूढः ब्रह्मणः पथि
kaccit na ubhaya-vibhraṣṭaḥ chinna-abhram iva naśyati apratiṣṭhaḥ mahā-bāho vimūḍhaḥ brahmaṇaḥ pathi
Word by word
Fallen from both, does he not perish like a broken cloud, O mighty-armed, without support and bewildered on the path to Brahman?

Note Śaṅkara: ‘both’ are the path of action and the path of yoga.

एतन्मे संशयं कृष्ण छेत्तुमर्हस्यशेषतः । त्वदन्यः संशयस्यास्य छेत्ता न ह्युपपद्यते ॥ ३९ ॥
etanme saṃśayaṃ kṛṣṇa chettumarhasyaśeṣataḥ | tvadanyaḥ saṃśayasyāsya chettā na hyupapadyate || 39 ||
Padaccheda
एतत् मे संशयम् कृष्ण छेत्तुम् अर्हसि अशेषतः त्वत् अन्यः संशयस्य अस्य छेत्ता न हि उपपद्यते
etat me saṃśayam kṛṣṇa chettum arhasi aśeṣataḥ tvat anyaḥ saṃśayasya asya chettā na hi upapadyate
Word by word
You should cut away this doubt of mine completely, O Kṛṣṇa; for no remover of this doubt other than you is to be found.

Note Śaṅkara: no other, whether seer or god.

Note etat is neuter although saṃśayam is masculine; the text is kept as it stands.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
पार्थ नैवेह नामुत्र विनाशस्तस्य विद्यते । न हि कल्याणकृत्कश्चिद्दुर्गतिं तात गच्छति ॥ ४० ॥
pārtha naiveha nāmutra vināśastasya vidyate | na hi kalyāṇakṛtkaściddurgatiṃ tāta gacchati || 40 ||
Padaccheda
पार्थ न एव इह न अमुत्र विनाशः तस्य विद्यते न हि कल्याण-कृत् कश्चित् दुर्गतिम् तात गच्छति
pārtha na eva iha na amutra vināśaḥ tasya vidyate na hi kalyāṇa-kṛt kaścit durgatim tāta gacchati
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: O Pārtha, neither here nor hereafter is there ruin for him; for no one who does good, my son, goes to a bad end.

Note Śaṅkara: ruin would be a birth lower than the one before; it does not befall one fallen from yoga. tāta is said of a father, who extends himself in his son, and so of a son too; a pupil also is called a son.

प्राप्य पुण्यकृतांल्लोकानुषित्वा शाश्वतीः समाः । शुचीनां श्रीमतां गेहे योगभ्रष्टोऽभिजायते ॥ ४१ ॥
prāpya puṇyakṛtāṃllokānuṣitvā śāśvatīḥ samāḥ | śucīnāṃ śrīmatāṃ gehe yogabhraṣṭo 'bhijāyate || 41 ||
Padaccheda
प्राप्य पुण्य-कृताम् लोकान् उषित्वा शाश्वतीः समाः शुचीनाम् श्रीमताम् गेहे योग-भ्रष्टः अभिजायते
prāpya puṇya-kṛtām lokān uṣitvā śāśvatīḥ samāḥ śucīnām śrīmatām gehe yoga-bhraṣṭaḥ abhijāyate
Word by word
Having reached the worlds of the doers of merit and dwelt there for unending years, one fallen from yoga is born in the house of the pure and prosperous.

Note Śaṅkara: the context shows that he is a renouncer who had set out on the path of yoga. The doers of merit are those who perform the horse sacrifice and the like. He is born again when the enjoyment of those worlds is exhausted. The pure are those who act as scripture directs.

अथवा योगिनामेव कुले भवति धीमताम् । एतद्धि दुर्लभतरं लोके जन्म यदीदृशम् ॥ ४२ ॥
athavā yogināmeva kule bhavati dhīmatām | etaddhi durlabhataraṃ loke janma yadīdṛśam || 42 ||
Padaccheda
अथवा योगिनाम् एव कुले भवति धीमताम् एतत् हि दुर्लभतरम् लोके जन्म यत् ईदृशम्
athavā yoginām eva kule bhavati dhīmatām etat hi durlabhataram loke janma yat īdṛśam
Word by word
Or else he is born in a family of wise yogīs themselves; a birth such as this is harder to gain in the world.

Note Śaṅkara: a family of yogīs who are poor, as distinct from that of the prosperous; such a birth is harder to gain than the former.

तत्र तं बुद्धिसंयोगं लभते पौर्वदेहिकम् । यतते च ततो भूयः संसिद्धौ कुरुनन्दन ॥ ४३ ॥
tatra taṃ buddhisaṃyogaṃ labhate paurvadehikam | yatate ca tato bhūyaḥ saṃsiddhau kurunandana || 43 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र तम् बुद्धि-संयोगम् लभते पौर्वदेहिकम् यतते च ततः भूयः संसिद्धौ कुरु-नन्दन
tatra tam buddhi-saṃyogam labhate paurvadehikam yatate ca tataḥ bhūyaḥ saṃsiddhau kuru-nandana
Word by word
There he regains that union with the understanding which was his in his former body, and from there he strives still more for perfection, O joy of the Kurus.

Note Śaṅkara: tataḥ: because of the impression left by what he did before, he strives more than before.

पूर्वाभ्यासेन तेनैव ह्रियते ह्यवशोऽपि सः । जिज्ञासुरपि योगस्य शब्दब्रह्मातिवर्तते ॥ ४४ ॥
pūrvābhyāsena tenaiva hriyate hyavaśo 'pi saḥ | jijñāsurapi yogasya śabdabrahmātivartate || 44 ||
Padaccheda
पूर्व-अभ्यासेन तेन एव ह्रियते हि अवशः अपि सः जिज्ञासुः अपि योगस्य शब्दब्रह्म अतिवर्तते
pūrva-abhyāsena tena eva hriyate hi avaśaḥ api saḥ jijñāsuḥ api yogasya śabdabrahma ativartate
Word by word
For by that very former practice he is carried on, even against his will. Even one who merely wishes to know of yoga goes beyond the Veda.

Note Śaṅkara: the practice of a former birth. If he has done no act of adharma stronger than the impression left by yoga, that impression carries him on; if he has, it is overpowered, but when that act is exhausted it begins to work of itself, and is not lost however long it waits. śabdabrahma here is the fruit of performing the rites taught in the Veda: even the seeker who set out on the path of yoga and fell from it will pass beyond that; how much more one who knows yoga and practises it steadfastly.

प्रयत्नाद्यतमानस्तु योगी संशुद्धकिल्बिषः । अनेकजन्मसंसिद्धस्ततो याति परां गतिम् ॥ ४५ ॥
prayatnādyatamānastu yogī saṃśuddhakilbiṣaḥ | anekajanmasaṃsiddhastato yāti parāṃ gatim || 45 ||
Padaccheda
प्रयत्नात् यतमानः तु योगी संशुद्ध-किल्बिषः अनेक-जन्म-संसिद्धः ततः याति पराम् गतिम्
prayatnāt yatamānaḥ tu yogī saṃśuddha-kilbiṣaḥ aneka-janma-saṃsiddhaḥ tataḥ yāti parām gatim
Word by word
But the yogī who strives with effort, cleansed of sin and perfected through many births, then goes to the supreme goal.

Note Śaṅkara: striving harder. He gathers impressions little by little over many births and is perfected by what is so gathered; then, having gained right vision, he goes to the supreme goal.

तपस्विभ्योऽधिको योगी ज्ञानिभ्योऽपि मतोऽधिकः । कर्मिभ्यश्चाधिको योगी तस्माद्योगी भवार्जुन ॥ ४६ ॥
tapasvibhyo 'dhiko yogī jñānibhyo 'pi mato 'dhikaḥ | karmibhyaścādhiko yogī tasmādyogī bhavārjuna || 46 ||
Padaccheda
तपस्विभ्यः अधिकः योगी ज्ञानिभ्यः अपि मतः अधिकः कर्मिभ्यः च अधिकः योगी तस्मात् योगी भव अर्जुन
tapasvibhyaḥ adhikaḥ yogī jñānibhyaḥ api mataḥ adhikaḥ karmibhyaḥ ca adhikaḥ yogī tasmāt yogī bhava arjuna
Word by word
The yogī is superior to ascetics; he is held superior even to men of learning; and the yogī is superior to men of ritual action. Therefore be a yogī, O Arjuna.

Note Śaṅkara: jñāna here is learning in the meaning of the scriptures; karma is the Agnihotra and the like.

योगिनामपि सर्वेषां मद्गतेनान्तरात्मना । श्रद्धावान्भजते यो मां स मे युक्ततमो मतः ॥ ४७ ॥
yogināmapi sarveṣāṃ madgatenāntarātmanā | śraddhāvānbhajate yo māṃ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ || 47 ||
Padaccheda
योगिनाम् अपि सर्वेषाम् मद्-गतेन अन्तरात्मना श्रद्धावान् भजते यः माम् सः मे युक्ततमः मतः
yoginām api sarveṣām mad-gatena antarātmanā śraddhāvān bhajate yaḥ mām saḥ me yuktatamaḥ mataḥ
Word by word
And of all yogīs, he who worships me with faith, with his inner self gone to me, is held by me to be the most collected.

Note Śaṅkara: the inner self is the mind.
