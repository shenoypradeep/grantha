पञ्चदशोऽध्यायः
Pañcadaśo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 15 · Puruṣottamayoga
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
ऊर्ध्वमूलमधःशाखमश्वत्थं प्राहुरव्ययम् । छन्दांसि यस्य पर्णानि यस्तं वेद स वेदवित् ॥ १ ॥
ūrdhvamūlamadhaḥśākhamaśvatthaṃ prāhuravyayam | chandāṃsi yasya parṇāni yastaṃ veda sa vedavit || 1 ||
Padaccheda
ऊर्ध्व-मूलम् अधः-शाखम् अश्वत्थम् प्राहुः अव्ययम् छन्दांसि यस्य पर्णानि यः तम् वेद सः वेद-वित्
ūrdhva-mūlam adhaḥ-śākham aśvattham prāhuḥ avyayam chandāṃsi yasya parṇāni yaḥ tam veda saḥ veda-vit
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: They speak of the undecaying holy fig tree with its root above and its branches below, whose leaves are the Vedic hymns. He who knows it is a knower of the Veda.

Bhāṣya The tree is the cycle of birth and death, described to bring about dispassion. Its root is Brahman with its power of illusion, the unmanifest, called ‘above’ because it is subtle, the cause, eternal and great; compare Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.3.1. Its branches are mahat, the sense of ‘I’, the subtle elements and the rest. aśvattha: ‘it will not stand even till tomorrow’ (na śvo 'pi sthātā), perishing every moment; yet it is undecaying, since the illusion of this cycle has gone on without beginning. As leaves protect a tree, the Vedas protect this tree, for they make known right and wrong, their causes and their fruits. He who knows the tree with its root knows the meaning of the Veda; nothing else remains to be known.

अधश्चोर्ध्वं प्रसृतास्तस्य शाखा गुणप्रवृद्धा विषयप्रवालाः । अधश्च मूलान्यनुसंततानि कर्मानुबन्धीनि मनुष्यलोके ॥ २ ॥
adhaścordhvaṃ prasṛtāstasya śākhā guṇapravṛddhā viṣayapravālāḥ | adhaśca mūlānyanusaṃtatāni karmānubandhīni manuṣyaloke || 2 ||
Padaccheda
अधः च ऊर्ध्वम् प्रसृताः तस्य शाखाः गुण-प्रवृद्धाः विषय-प्रवालाः अधः च मूलानि अनुसंततानि कर्म-अनुबन्धीनि मनुष्य-लोके
adhaḥ ca ūrdhvam prasṛtāḥ tasya śākhāḥ guṇa-pravṛddhāḥ viṣaya-pravālāḥ adhaḥ ca mūlāni anusaṃtatāni karma-anubandhīni manuṣya-loke
Word by word
Its branches spread downwards and upwards, nourished by the guṇas, with sense objects for shoots; and downwards its roots stretch out, followed by action, in the world of men.

Bhāṣya Downwards, from men to the unmoving; upwards, as far as the abode of Brahmā: the fruits of knowledge and action, according to each one's deeds and learning. The sense objects sprout like shoots from the bodies that are those branches. The chief root was given in verse 1; these secondary roots are the latent impressions of attachment, aversion and the like, born of the fruits of action, from which acts of dharma and adharma follow; they spread especially in the world of men, for it is here that men are entitled to act.

न रूपमस्येह तथोपलभ्यते नान्तो न चादिर्न च संप्रतिष्ठा । अश्वत्थमेनं सुविरूढमूलमसङ्गशस्त्रेण दृढेन छित्त्वा ॥ ३ ॥
na rūpamasyeha tathopalabhyate nānto na cādirna ca saṃpratiṣṭhā | aśvatthamenaṃ suvirūḍhamūlamasaṅgaśastreṇa dṛḍhena chittvā || 3 ||
Padaccheda
न रूपम् अस्य इह तथा उपलभ्यते न अन्तः न च आदिः न च संप्रतिष्ठा अश्वत्थम् एनम् सु-विरूढ-मूलम् असङ्ग-शस्त्रेण दृढेन छित्त्वा
na rūpam asya iha tathā upalabhyate na antaḥ na ca ādiḥ na ca saṃpratiṣṭhā aśvattham enam su-virūḍha-mūlam asaṅga-śastreṇa dṛḍhena chittvā
Word by word
Its form is not perceived here as such, nor its end, nor its beginning, nor its continuance. Having cut this holy fig tree, whose roots have grown firm, with the strong axe of non-attachment,

Bhāṣya It is not found to be as described, being like a dream, mirage water, a magic show or a city in the sky: seen and then gone. Non-attachment is rising above the desire for sons, wealth and the worlds; the axe is made strong by the resolve to turn towards the supreme Self and sharpened on the whetstone of repeated discernment. The tree is to be uprooted with its seed.

ततः पदं तत्परिमार्गितव्यं यस्मिन्गता न निवर्तन्ति भूयः । तमेव चाद्यं पुरुषं प्रपद्ये यतः प्रवृत्तिः प्रसृता पुराणी ॥ ४ ॥
tataḥ padaṃ tatparimārgitavyaṃ yasmingatā na nivartanti bhūyaḥ | tameva cādyaṃ puruṣaṃ prapadye yataḥ pravṛttiḥ prasṛtā purāṇī || 4 ||
Padaccheda
ततः पदम् तत् परिमार्गितव्यम् यस्मिन् गताः न निवर्तन्ति भूयः तम् एव च आद्यम् पुरुषम् प्रपद्ये यतः प्रवृत्तिः प्रसृता पुराणी
tataḥ padam tat parimārgitavyam yasmin gatāḥ na nivartanti bhūyaḥ tam eva ca ādyam puruṣam prapadye yataḥ pravṛttiḥ prasṛtā purāṇī
Word by word
then that abode is to be sought, having gone to which they do not return again: ‘I take refuge in that very primal Puruṣa, from whom the ancient going forth has streamed.’

Bhāṣya The abode is that of Viṣṇu; to seek it is to know it. How is it to be sought? By taking refuge in him, with the words given. The going forth is that of the illusory tree of the cycle of birth and death, which has come out from that Puruṣa as a magic show from a magician.

निर्मानमोहा जितसङ्गदोषा अध्यात्मनित्या विनिवृत्तकामाः । द्वंद्वैर्विमुक्ताः सुखदुःखसंज्ञैर्गच्छन्त्यमूढाः पदमव्ययं तत् ॥ ५ ॥
nirmānamohā jitasaṅgadoṣā adhyātmanityā vinivṛttakāmāḥ | dvaṃdvairvimuktāḥ sukhaduḥkhasaṃjñair gacchanty amūḍhāḥ padamavyayaṃ tat || 5 ||
Padaccheda
निर्मान-मोहाः जित-सङ्ग-दोषाः अध्यात्म-नित्याः विनिवृत्त-कामाः द्वंद्वैः विमुक्ताः सुख-दुःख-संज्ञैः गच्छन्ति अमूढाः पदम् अव्ययम् तत्
nirmāna-mohāḥ jita-saṅga-doṣāḥ adhyātma-nityāḥ vinivṛtta-kāmāḥ dvaṃdvaiḥ vimuktāḥ sukha-duḥkha-saṃjñaiḥ gacchanti amūḍhāḥ padam avyayam tat
Word by word
Free from pride and delusion, having conquered the fault of attachment, ever intent on the Self, their desires ceased, freed from the pairs of opposites known as pleasure and pain, the undeluded go to that undecaying abode.

Bhāṣya ‘intent on the Self’: ever given to reflecting on the nature of the supreme Self. Their desires have ceased wholly, leaving no trace; they are renouncers.

न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः । यद्गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते तद्धाम परमं मम ॥ ६ ॥
na tadbhāsayate sūryo na śaśāṅko na pāvakaḥ | yadgatvā na nivartante taddhāma paramaṃ mama || 6 ||
Padaccheda
न तत् भासयते सूर्यः न शशाङ्कः न पावकः यत् गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते तत् धाम परमम् मम
na tat bhāsayate sūryaḥ na śaśāṅkaḥ na pāvakaḥ yat gatvā na nivartante tat dhāma paramam mama
Word by word
The sun does not illumine it, nor the moon, nor fire. That to which they go and do not return is my supreme abode.

Bhāṣya ‘that’ at the beginning goes with ‘abode’. The abode is of the nature of light; the sun does not illumine it, though it has the power to light up everything. It is the supreme abode of Viṣṇu.

ममैवांशो जीवलोके जीवभूतः सनातनः । मनःषष्ठानीन्द्रियाणि प्रकृतिस्थानि कर्षति ॥ ७ ॥
mamaivāṃśo jīvaloke jīvabhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ | manaḥṣaṣṭhānīndriyāṇi prakṛtisthāni karṣati || 7 ||
Padaccheda
मम एव अंशः जीव-लोके जीव-भूतः सनातनः मनः-षष्ठानि इन्द्रियाणि प्रकृति-स्थानि कर्षति
mama eva aṃśaḥ jīva-loke jīva-bhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ manaḥ-ṣaṣṭhāni indriyāṇi prakṛti-sthāni karṣati
Word by word
A part of my own self, eternal, become the jīva in the world of living beings, draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, which are seated in nature.

Bhāṣya (objection) every going ends in a return; how is there none from that abode? (Reply) the jīva, known as doer and experiencer, is a part of me, the supreme Self, as the sun reflected in water is a part of the sun and returns to it when the water is gone, or as the space in a pot, when the pot is gone, is one with space. The supreme Self has no parts; the ‘part’ is imagined, marked off by a limiting adjunct made by ignorance. ‘Seated in nature’: in their own places, the ear-hole and the rest.

शरीरं यदवाप्नोति यच्चाप्युत्क्रामतीश्वरः । गृहीत्वैतानि संयाति वायुर्गन्धानिवाशयात् ॥ ८ ॥
śarīraṃ yadavāpnoti yaccāpyutkrāmatīśvaraḥ | gṛhītvaitāni saṃyāti vāyurgandhānivāśayāt || 8 ||
Padaccheda
शरीरम् यत् अवाप्नोति यत् च अपि उत्क्रामति ईश्वरः गृहीत्वा एतानि संयाति वायुः गन्धान् इव आशयात्
śarīram yat avāpnoti yat ca api utkrāmati īśvaraḥ gṛhītvā etāni saṃyāti vāyuḥ gandhān iva āśayāt
Word by word
When the lord takes a body, and when he leaves it, he goes taking these with him, as the wind takes scents from their seat.

Bhāṣya Yat is ‘when’. The lord is the jīva, master of the aggregate of body and organs. When he leaves, he draws them (verse 7); when he passes from the earlier body to another, he goes with the six. Their seat is a flower and the like.

श्रोत्रं चक्षुः स्पर्शनं च रसनं घ्राणमेव च । अधिष्ठाय मनश्चायं विषयानुपसेवते ॥ ९ ॥
śrotraṃ cakṣuḥ sparśanaṃ ca rasanaṃ ghrāṇameva ca | adhiṣṭhāya manaścāyaṃ viṣayānupasevate || 9 ||
Padaccheda
श्रोत्रम् चक्षुः स्पर्शनम् च रसनम् घ्राणम् एव च अधिष्ठाय मनः च अयम् विषयान् उपसेवते
śrotram cakṣuḥ sparśanam ca rasanam ghrāṇam eva ca adhiṣṭhāya manaḥ ca ayam viṣayān upasevate
Word by word
Presiding over hearing, sight, touch, taste and smell, and the mind, he enjoys the objects.

Bhāṣya He presides over the mind, the sixth, together with each sense in turn, and, dwelling in the body, enjoys sound and the other objects.

उत्क्रामन्तं स्थितं वापि भुञ्जानं वा गुणान्वितम् । विमूढा नानुपश्यन्ति पश्यन्ति ज्ञानचक्षुषः ॥ १० ॥
utkrāmantaṃ sthitaṃ vāpi bhuñjānaṃ vā guṇānvitam | vimūḍhā nānupaśyanti paśyanti jñānacakṣuṣaḥ || 10 ||
Padaccheda
उत्क्रामन्तम् स्थितम् वा अपि भुञ्जानम् वा गुण-अन्वितम् विमूढाः न अनुपश्यन्ति पश्यन्ति ज्ञान-चक्षुषः
utkrāmantam sthitam vā api bhuñjānam vā guṇa-anvitam vimūḍhāḥ na anupaśyanti paśyanti jñāna-cakṣuṣaḥ
Word by word
The deluded do not see him as he leaves or stays or experiences, joined with the guṇas; those who have the eye of knowledge see.

Bhāṣya Leaving the body taken before, staying in the body, perceiving sound and the rest, joined with the guṇas in the form of pleasure, pain and delusion: though he thus comes fully within the range of sight, they do not see him, deluded in many ways, their minds dragged by the enjoyment of objects seen and unseen. The Lord says this in pity. The eye is knowledge born of the means of knowledge: a discerning sight.

यतन्तो योगिनश्चैनं पश्यन्त्यात्मन्यवस्थितम् । यतन्तोऽप्यकृतात्मानो नैनं पश्यन्त्यचेतसः ॥ ११ ॥
yatanto yoginaścainaṃ paśyantyātmanyavasthitam | yatanto 'pyakṛtātmāno nainaṃ paśyantyacetasaḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
यतन्तः योगिनः च एनम् पश्यन्ति आत्मनि अवस्थितम् यतन्तः अपि अकृत-आत्मानः न एनम् पश्यन्ति अचेतसः
yatantaḥ yoginaḥ ca enam paśyanti ātmani avasthitam yatantaḥ api akṛta-ātmānaḥ na enam paśyanti acetasaḥ
Word by word
The yogins who strive see him dwelling in themselves; but those whose minds are not refined, the undiscerning, do not see him even though they strive.

Bhāṣya The yogins are those of collected mind; they see the Self as ‘I am this’, dwelling in their own understanding. The others, though they strive with scripture and other means of knowledge, have not refined themselves by austerity and mastery of the senses, have not turned from bad conduct, and their arrogance is not stilled.

यदादित्यगतं तेजो जगद्भासयतेऽखिलम् । यच्चन्द्रमसि यच्चाग्नौ तत्तेजो विद्धि मामकम् ॥ १२ ॥
yadādityagataṃ tejo jagadbhāsayate 'khilam | yaccandramasi yaccāgnau tattejo viddhi māmakam || 12 ||
Padaccheda
यत् आदित्य-गतम् तेजः जगत् भासयते अखिलम् यत् चन्द्रमसि यत् च अग्नौ तत् तेजः विद्धि मामकम्
yat āditya-gatam tejaḥ jagat bhāsayate akhilam yat candramasi yat ca agnau tat tejaḥ viddhi māmakam
Word by word
The light that is in the sun and illumines the whole world, that which is in the moon and that which is in fire: know that light to be mine.

Bhāṣya Verses 12–15 state in brief the Lord's glories, to show that the abode is the Self of all and the ground of all dealings. Or the light is the light of consciousness. That is the same in all things moving and unmoving; the sun and the others are named because in them sattva is very bright and the light shows more fully, as a face is not reflected in wood or a wall but is in a mirror, more or less as the mirror is clear.

गामाविश्य च भूतानि धारयाम्यहमोजसा । पुष्णामि चौषधीः सर्वाः सोमो भूत्वा रसात्मकः ॥ १३ ॥
gāmāviśya ca bhūtāni dhārayāmyahamojasā | puṣṇāmi cauṣadhīḥ sarvāḥ somo bhūtvā rasātmakaḥ || 13 ||
Padaccheda
गाम् आविश्य च भूतानि धारयामि अहम् ओजसा पुष्णामि च ओषधीः सर्वाः सोमः भूत्वा रस-आत्मकः
gām āviśya ca bhūtāni dhārayāmi aham ojasā puṣṇāmi ca oṣadhīḥ sarvāḥ somaḥ bhūtvā rasa-ātmakaḥ
Word by word
Entering the earth, I uphold beings by my strength; and having become the moon, whose nature is sap, I nourish all plants.

Bhāṣya The strength is the Lord's own, free of desire and passion, which has entered the earth to hold up the world, so that the heavy earth neither falls nor breaks apart. The plants are rice, barley and the rest. Soma is the store of all saps; he nourishes the plants by making his own saps enter them.

अहं वैश्वानरो भूत्वा प्राणिनां देहमाश्रितः । प्राणापानसमायुक्तः पचाम्यन्नं चतुर्विधम् ॥ १४ ॥
ahaṃ vaiśvānaro bhūtvā prāṇināṃ dehamāśritaḥ | prāṇāpānasamāyuktaḥ pacāmyannaṃ caturvidham || 14 ||
Padaccheda
अहम् वैश्वानरः भूत्वा प्राणिनाम् देहम् आश्रितः प्राण-अपान-समायुक्तः पचामि अन्नम् चतुर्विधम्
aham vaiśvānaraḥ bhūtvā prāṇinām deham āśritaḥ prāṇa-apāna-samāyuktaḥ pacāmi annam caturvidham
Word by word
Having become the fire of digestion, dwelling in the body of living beings, joined with the out-breath and the in-breath, I digest food of four kinds.

Bhāṣya Vaiśvānara is the fire in the belly: ‘this fire that is within man, by which the food that is eaten is digested’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.9.1). The four kinds are what is chewed, what is swallowed, what is sucked and what is licked. The eater is the fire and the food is Soma; one who sees all this as fire and Soma is not tainted by any fault in food.

सर्वस्य चाहं हृदि संनिविष्टो मत्तः स्मृतिर्ज्ञानमपोहनं च । वेदैश्च सर्वैरहमेव वेद्यो वेदान्तकृद्वेदविदेव चाहम् ॥ १५ ॥
sarvasya cāhaṃ hṛdi saṃniviṣṭo mattaḥ smṛtirjñānamapohanaṃ ca | vedaiśca sarvairahameva vedyo vedāntakṛdvedavideva cāham || 15 ||
Padaccheda
सर्वस्य च अहम् हृदि संनिविष्टः मत्तः स्मृतिः ज्ञानम् अपोहनम् च वेदैः च सर्वैः अहम् एव वेद्यः वेदान्त-कृत् वेद-वित् एव च अहम्
sarvasya ca aham hṛdi saṃniviṣṭaḥ mattaḥ smṛtiḥ jñānam apohanam ca vedaiḥ ca sarvaiḥ aham eva vedyaḥ vedānta-kṛt veda-vit eva ca aham
Word by word
And I am seated in the heart of everyone; from me come memory, knowledge and their loss. I alone am to be known by all the Vedas; I am the maker of the Vedānta and the knower of the Veda.

Bhāṣya I am seated in the understanding of every living being as its Self. Memory and knowledge come to those of good deeds in accord with those deeds, and are lost to those of evil deeds in accord with theirs. vedānta-kṛt: the maker of the tradition of the meaning of the Vedānta.

द्वाविमौ पुरुषौ लोके क्षरश्चाक्षर एव च । क्षरः सर्वाणि भूतानि कूटस्थोऽक्षर उच्यते ॥ १६ ॥
dvāvimau puruṣau loke kṣaraścākṣara eva ca | kṣaraḥ sarvāṇi bhūtāni kūṭastho 'kṣara ucyate || 16 ||
Padaccheda
द्वौ इमौ पुरुषौ लोके क्षरः च अक्षरः एव च क्षरः सर्वाणि भूतानि कूटस्थः अक्षरः उच्यते
dvau imau puruṣau loke kṣaraḥ ca akṣaraḥ eva ca kṣaraḥ sarvāṇi bhūtāni kūṭasthaḥ akṣaraḥ ucyate
Word by word
There are these two Puruṣas in the world, the perishable and the imperishable. The perishable is all beings; the unshaken is called the imperishable.

Bhāṣya The Lord now sets out his true nature, free of limiting adjuncts, by gathering all that has been taught into three groups. The perishable is the whole mass of modifications. The imperishable is the Lord's power of illusion, the seed from which the perishable Puruṣa arises, the seat of the impressions of desire and action of the many beings in the cycle of birth and death. kūṭastha: standing like a heap; or kūṭa is illusion, deceit, crookedness, and it stands in these many forms. It is called imperishable because the seed of that cycle is endless.

उत्तमः पुरुषस्त्वन्यः परमात्मेत्युदाहृतः । यो लोकत्रयमाविश्य बिभर्त्यव्यय ईश्वरः ॥ १७ ॥
uttamaḥ puruṣastvanyaḥ paramātmetyudāhṛtaḥ | yo lokatrayamāviśya bibhartyavyaya īśvaraḥ || 17 ||
Padaccheda
उत्तमः पुरुषः तु अन्यः परम-आत्मा इति उदाहृतः यः लोकत्रयम् आविश्य बिभर्ति अव्ययः ईश्वरः
uttamaḥ puruṣaḥ tu anyaḥ parama-ātmā iti udāhṛtaḥ yaḥ lokatrayam āviśya bibharti avyayaḥ īśvaraḥ
Word by word
But the highest Puruṣa is another, called the supreme Self, the undecaying Lord who, having entered the three worlds, upholds them.

Bhāṣya Wholly different from those two, untouched by the faults of the two adjuncts, the perishable and the imperishable. He is supreme in relation to the selves made by ignorance, the body and the rest, and the Self as the inner consciousness of all beings; so the Upaniṣads call him the supreme Self. He enters the three worlds, earth, mid-region and heaven, by his power of consciousness and upholds them merely by being. The Lord is the all-knowing one called Nārāyaṇa.

यस्मात्क्षरमतीतोऽहमक्षरादपि चोत्तमः । अतोऽस्मि लोके वेदे च प्रथितः पुरुषोत्तमः ॥ १८ ॥
yasmātkṣaramatīto 'hamakṣarādapi cottamaḥ | ato 'smi loke vede ca prathitaḥ puruṣottamaḥ || 18 ||
Padaccheda
यस्मात् क्षरम् अतीतः अहम् अक्षरात् अपि च उत्तमः अतः अस्मि लोके वेदे च प्रथितः पुरुष-उत्तमः
yasmāt kṣaram atītaḥ aham akṣarāt api ca uttamaḥ ataḥ asmi loke vede ca prathitaḥ puruṣa-uttamaḥ
Word by word
Because I have gone beyond the perishable and am higher even than the imperishable, I am famed in the world and in the Veda as the supreme Puruṣa.

Bhāṣya The perishable is the illusory tree of the cycle of birth and death, called the aśvattha; the imperishable is the seed of that tree. Devotees know me by this name, and poets use it in their poems.

यो मामेवमसंमूढो जानाति पुरुषोत्तमम् । स सर्वविद्भजति मां सर्वभावेन भारत ॥ १९ ॥
yo māmevamasaṃmūḍho jānāti puruṣottamam | sa sarvavidbhajati māṃ sarvabhāvena bhārata || 19 ||
Padaccheda
यः माम् एवम् असंमूढः जानाति पुरुष-उत्तमम् सः सर्व-वित् भजति माम् सर्व-भावेन भारत
yaḥ mām evam asaṃmūḍhaḥ jānāti puruṣa-uttamam saḥ sarva-vit bhajati mām sarva-bhāvena bhārata
Word by word
He who, undeluded, thus knows me as the supreme Puruṣa, he, knowing all, worships me with his whole being, O descendant of Bharata.

Bhāṣya He knows me as ‘I am this’. He is all-knowing, since he knows all as the Self; he worships me, who dwell in all beings, sarva-bhāvena: as the Self of all.

इति गुह्यतमं शास्त्रमिदमुक्तं मयानघ । एतद्बुद्ध्वा बुद्धिमान्स्यात्कृतकृत्यश्च भारत ॥ २० ॥
iti guhyatamaṃ śāstramidamuktaṃ mayānagha | etadbuddhvā buddhimānsyātkṛtakṛtyaśca bhārata || 20 ||
Padaccheda
इति गुह्यतमम् शास्त्रम् इदम् उक्तम् मया अनघ एतत् बुद्ध्वा बुद्धिमान् स्यात् कृतकृत्यः च भारत
iti guhyatamam śāstram idam uktam mayā anagha etat buddhvā buddhimān syāt kṛtakṛtyaḥ ca bhārata
Word by word
Thus this most secret teaching has been told by me, O sinless one. Having understood it, one would be wise and would have done all that is to be done, O descendant of Bharata.

Bhāṣya Though the whole Gītā is called the śāstra, here this chapter is so called in praise, for the whole meaning of the Gītā, and of the Veda too, is summed up in it (15.1, 15.15). When the truth of the Lord is known, all that one has to do is done, and not otherwise: ‘all action without exception ends in knowledge’ (4.33); he also cites Manu 12.93.
