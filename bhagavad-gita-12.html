द्वादशोऽध्यायः
Dvādaśo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 12 · Bhaktiyoga
अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
एवं सततयुक्ता ये भक्तास्त्वां पर्युपासते । ये चाप्यक्षरमव्यक्तं तेषां के योगवित्तमाः ॥ १ ॥
evaṃ satatayuktā ye bhaktāstvāṃ paryupāsate | ye cāpyakṣaramavyaktaṃ teṣāṃ ke yogavittamāḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
एवम् सतत-युक्ताः ये भक्ताः त्वाम् पर्युपासते ये च अपि अक्षरम् अव्यक्तम् तेषाम् के योग-वित्-तमाः
evam satata-yuktāḥ ye bhaktāḥ tvām paryupāsate ye ca api akṣaram avyaktam teṣām ke yoga-vit-tamāḥ
Word by word
Arjuna said: Those devotees who, ever collected in this way, worship you, and those who worship the imperishable, the unmanifest: which of them are the better knowers of yoga?

Note Śaṅkara: evam refers to the verse just before (11.55): engaged without break, with collected mind, in the Lord's work and the rest. ‘You’ = the form of the universe as it was shown; paryupāsate = meditate on. The others have given up all desires and renounced all actions; the imperishable is Brahman, unmanifest because free of every limiting adjunct and beyond the reach of the senses.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
मय्यावेश्य मनो ये मां नित्ययुक्ता उपासते । श्रद्धया परयोपेतास्ते मे युक्ततमा मताः ॥ २ ॥
mayyāveśya mano ye māṃ nityayuktā upāsate | śraddhayā parayopetāste me yuktatamā matāḥ || 2 ||
Padaccheda
मयि आवेश्य मनः ये माम् नित्य-युक्ताः उपासते श्रद्धया परया उपेताः ते मे युक्ततमाः मताः
mayi āveśya manaḥ ye mām nitya-yuktāḥ upāsate śraddhayā parayā upetāḥ te me yuktatamāḥ matāḥ
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: Those who fix the mind on me and worship me, ever collected, endowed with supreme faith, I hold to be the most collected.

Note Śaṅkara: let the worshippers of the imperishable, who see rightly, stand aside for now; what is to be said of them comes later. ‘On me’ = on the supreme Lord whose form is the universe. They pass day and night without break with their thought on him; so it is fitting to call them the most collected.

ये त्वक्षरमनिर्देश्यमव्यक्तं पर्युपासते । सर्वत्रगमचिन्त्यं च कूटस्थमचलं ध्रुवम् ॥ ३ ॥
ye tv akṣaram anirdeśyam avyaktaṃ paryupāsate | sarvatragam acintyaṃ ca kūṭasthamacalaṃ dhruvam || 3 ||
Padaccheda
ये तु अक्षरम् अनिर्देश्यम् अव्यक्तम् पर्युपासते सर्वत्र-गम् अचिन्त्यम् च कूट-स्थम् अचलम् ध्रुवम्
ye tu akṣaram anirdeśyam avyaktam paryupāsate sarvatra-gam acintyam ca kūṭa-stham acalam dhruvam
Word by word
But those who worship the imperishable, which cannot be pointed out, the unmanifest, present everywhere, unthinkable, unshaken, unmoving, constant,

Note Śaṅkara: it cannot be pointed out because, being unmanifest, it is beyond the reach of words; unmanifest = not made manifest by any means of knowledge. Worship (upāsana) is to draw near the object of worship by making it the object of thought as the scriptures teach, and to stay so for a long time in an even flow of the same thought, like a stream of oil. sarvatragam = pervading like space. kūṭastha: kūṭa is a thing fair to see and faulty within, here illusion (māyā), the seed of the cycle of birth and death; the imperishable stands in it as its overseer. Or: standing like a heap, and hence unmoving.

संनियम्येन्द्रियग्रामं सर्वत्र समबुद्धयः । ते प्राप्नुवन्ति मामेव सर्वभूतहिते रताः ॥ ४ ॥
saṃniyamyendriyagrāmaṃ sarvatra samabuddhayaḥ | te prāpnuvanti māmeva sarvabhūtahite ratāḥ || 4 ||
Padaccheda
संनियम्य इन्द्रिय-ग्रामम् सर्वत्र सम-बुद्धयः ते प्राप्नुवन्ति माम् एव सर्व-भूत-हिते रताः
saṃniyamya indriya-grāmam sarvatra sama-buddhayaḥ te prāpnuvanti mām eva sarva-bhūta-hite ratāḥ
Word by word
having restrained the group of the senses, their understanding the same at all times, delighting in the good of all beings: they reach me alone.

Note Śaṅkara: sarvatra = at all times; their understanding is the same when the pleasant or the unpleasant comes. That they reach me needs no saying, for ‘the man of knowledge is my very Self’ (7.18); of those who are the Lord's own nature one cannot speak of being the most collected or not.

क्लेशोऽधिकतरस्तेषामव्यक्तासक्तचेतसाम् । अव्यक्ता हि गतिर्दुःखं देहवद्भिरवाप्यते ॥ ५ ॥
kleśo 'dhikatarasteṣāmavyaktāsaktacetasām | avyaktā hi gatirduḥkhaṃ dehavadbhiravāpyate || 5 ||
Padaccheda
क्लेशः अधिकतरः तेषाम् अव्यक्त-आसक्त-चेतसाम् अव्यक्ता हि गतिः दुःखम् देहवद्भिः अवाप्यते
kleśaḥ adhikataraḥ teṣām avyakta-āsakta-cetasām avyaktā hi gatiḥ duḥkham dehavadbhiḥ avāpyate
Word by word
The hardship is greater for those whose minds are set on the unmanifest, for the unmanifest goal is reached with difficulty by the embodied.

Note Śaṅkara: the hardship of those devoted to the Lord's work is great indeed; but greater is that of those who see the highest truth and are one with the imperishable, for it lies in giving up the identification with the body. dehavadbhiḥ = by those who identify themselves with the body.

ये तु सर्वाणि कर्माणि मयि संन्यस्य मत्पराः । अनन्येनैव योगेन मां ध्यायन्त उपासते ॥ ६ ॥
ye tu sarvāṇi karmāṇi mayi saṃnyasya matparāḥ | ananyenaiva yogena māṃ dhyāyanta upāsate || 6 ||
Padaccheda
ये तु सर्वाणि कर्माणि मयि संन्यस्य मत्-पराः अनन्येन एव योगेन माम् ध्यायन्तः उपासते
ye tu sarvāṇi karmāṇi mayi saṃnyasya mat-parāḥ ananyena eva yogena mām dhyāyantaḥ upāsate
Word by word
But those who lay down all actions in me, hold me as the supreme and worship me, meditating on me with undivided concentration,

Note Śaṅkara: ananya = having no other support than the Self, the God whose form is the universe; yogena = by concentration (samādhi).

तेषामहं समुद्धर्ता मृत्युसंसारसागरात् । भवामि नचिरात्पार्थ मय्यावेशितचेतसाम् ॥ ७ ॥
teṣāmahaṃ samuddhartā mṛtyusaṃsārasāgarāt | bhavāmi nacirātpārtha mayyāveśitacetasām || 7 ||
Padaccheda
तेषाम् अहम् समुद्धर्ता मृत्यु-संसार-सागरात् भवामि नचिरात् पार्थ मयि आवेशित-चेतसाम्
teṣām aham samuddhartā mṛtyu-saṃsāra-sāgarāt bhavāmi nacirāt pārtha mayi āveśita-cetasām
Word by word
of them, whose minds are fixed on me, O Pārtha, I become before long the deliverer from the ocean of death and rebirth.

Note Śaṅkara: the cycle of birth and death (saṃsāra), bound up with death, is like an ocean because it is hard to cross; nacirāt = quickly.

मय्येव मन आधत्स्व मयि बुद्धिं निवेशय । निवसिष्यसि मय्येव अत ऊर्ध्वं न संशयः ॥ ८ ॥
mayyeva mana ādhatsva mayi buddhiṃ niveśaya | nivasiṣyasi mayyeva ata ūrdhvaṃ na saṃśayaḥ || 8 ||
Padaccheda
मयि एव मनः आधत्स्व मयि बुद्धिम् निवेशय निवसिष्यसि मयि एव अतः ऊर्ध्वम् न संशयः
mayi eva manaḥ ādhatsva mayi buddhim niveśaya nivasiṣyasi mayi eva ataḥ ūrdhvam na saṃśayaḥ
Word by word
Place your mind on me alone, settle your understanding in me; hereafter you will dwell in me alone, there is no doubt.

Note Śaṅkara: the mind is what intends and wavers, the understanding what determines; ‘you will dwell in me’ = as my very Self; ata ūrdhvam = after the fall of the body.

Note mayy eva ata ūrdhvam is printed without sandhi, as in the source.

अथ चित्तं समाधातुं न शक्नोषि मयि स्थिरम् । अभ्यासयोगेन ततो मामिच्छाप्तुं धनंजय ॥ ९ ॥
atha cittaṃ samādhātuṃ na śaknoṣi mayi sthiram | abhyāsayogena tato māmicchāptuṃ dhanaṃjaya || 9 ||
Padaccheda
अथ चित्तम् समाधातुम् न शक्नोषि मयि स्थिरम् अभ्यास-योगेन ततः माम् इच्छ आप्तुम् धनंजय
atha cittam samādhātum na śaknoṣi mayi sthiram abhyāsa-yogena tataḥ mām iccha āptum dhanaṃjaya
Word by word
If you are not able to fix your mind steadily on me, then seek to reach me by the yoga of practice, O Dhanaṃjaya.

Note Śaṅkara: practice is to draw the mind back from every side and set it again and again on one support; the yoga that follows on it is concentration. ‘Me’ = whose form is the universe.

अभ्यासेऽप्यसमर्थोऽसि मत्कर्मपरमो भव । मदर्थमपि कर्माणि कुर्वन्सिद्धिमवाप्स्यसि ॥ १० ॥
abhyāse 'pyasamartho 'si matkarmaparamo bhava | madarthamapi karmāṇi kurvansiddhimavāpsyasi || 10 ||
Padaccheda
अभ्यासे अपि असमर्थः असि मत्-कर्म-परमः भव मत्-अर्थम् अपि कर्माणि कुर्वन् सिद्धिम् अवाप्स्यसि
abhyāse api asamarthaḥ asi mat-karma-paramaḥ bhava mat-artham api karmāṇi kurvan siddhim avāpsyasi
Word by word
If you are unable even for practice, be intent on work for me; even by doing actions for my sake you will attain perfection.

Note Śaṅkara: matkarmaparama = one for whom work done for the Lord is the chief thing. Perfection is attained by way of purity of mind, yoga and knowledge.

अथैतदप्यशक्तोऽसि कर्तुं मद्योगमाश्रितः । सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं ततः कुरु यतात्मवान् ॥ ११ ॥
athaitadapyaśakto 'si kartuṃ madyogamāśritaḥ | sarvakarmaphalatyāgaṃ tataḥ kuru yatātmavān || 11 ||
Padaccheda
अथ एतत् अपि अशक्तः असि कर्तुम् मत्-योगम् आश्रितः सर्व-कर्म-फल-त्यागम् ततः कुरु यत-आत्मवान्
atha etat api aśaktaḥ asi kartum mat-yogam āśritaḥ sarva-karma-phala-tyāgam tataḥ kuru yata-ātmavān
Word by word
If you are unable to do even this, then, resorting to yoga in me and with your mind restrained, give up the fruit of all actions.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘this’ = being intent on work for the Lord; madyoga = performing actions while laying them down in the Lord.

श्रेयो हि ज्ञानमभ्यासाज्ज्ञानाद्ध्यानं विशिष्यते । ध्यानात्कर्मफलत्यागस्त्यागाच्छान्तिरनन्तरम् ॥ १२ ॥
śreyo hi jñānamabhyāsājjñānāddhyānaṃ viśiṣyate | dhyānātkarmaphalatyāgas tyāgācchāntiranantaram || 12 ||
Padaccheda
श्रेयः हि ज्ञानम् अभ्यासात् ज्ञानात् ध्यानम् विशिष्यते ध्यानात् कर्म-फल-त्यागः त्यागात् शान्तिः अनन्तरम्
śreyaḥ hi jñānam abhyāsāt jñānāt dhyānam viśiṣyate dhyānāt karma-phala-tyāgaḥ tyāgāt śāntiḥ anantaram
Word by word
For knowledge is better than practice; meditation is better than knowledge; the giving up of the fruit of action is better than meditation; from giving up, peace follows at once.

Note Śaṅkara: the practice is one without discrimination, the meditation one joined with knowledge; peace = the ceasing of the cycle of birth and death together with its cause. The verse praises the giving up of the fruit of all actions, which was taught to the ignorant man engaged in action only when he cannot follow the means taught before, not as the first means. It is praised by likeness: immortality is taught to follow the giving up of all desires, and the fruits of action are desires; so brahmins of today are praised because the brahmin Agastya drank the ocean.

अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च । निर्ममो निरहंकारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी ॥ १३ ॥
adveṣṭā sarvabhūtānāṃ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca | nirmamo nirahaṃkāraḥ samaduḥkhasukhaḥ kṣamī || 13 ||
Padaccheda
अद्वेष्टा सर्व-भूतानाम् मैत्रः करुणः एव च निर्ममः निरहंकारः सम-दुःख-सुखः क्षमी
adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānām maitraḥ karuṇaḥ eva ca nirmamaḥ nirahaṃkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī
Word by word
He who hates no being, who is friendly and compassionate, without the sense of ‘mine’ and the sense of ‘I’, the same in pain and pleasure, forbearing,

Note Śaṅkara: from here to the end of the adhyāya the Lord tells the group of qualities, the direct cause of immortality, of the worshippers of the imperishable: renouncers who have given up all desires and are established in right vision. He hates nothing, not even what causes him pain, for he sees all beings as his Self; karuṇa = one who gives all beings freedom from fear, a renouncer; kṣamī = he stays unchanged when abused or struck.

संतुष्टः सततं योगी यतात्मा दृढनिश्चयः । मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्यो मद्भक्तः स मे प्रियः ॥ १४ ॥
saṃtuṣṭaḥ satataṃ yogī yatātmā dṛḍhaniścayaḥ | mayyarpitamanobuddhiryo madbhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ || 14 ||
Padaccheda
संतुष्टः सततम् योगी यत-आत्मा दृढ-निश्चयः मयि अर्पित-मनः-बुद्धिः यः मत्-भक्तः सः मे प्रियः
saṃtuṣṭaḥ satatam yogī yata-ātmā dṛḍha-niścayaḥ mayi arpita-manaḥ-buddhiḥ yaḥ mat-bhaktaḥ saḥ me priyaḥ
Word by word
always content, a yogī, self-restrained, of firm conviction, with mind and understanding given over to me: he who is thus devoted to me is dear to me.

Note Śaṅkara: content whether or not he gets what keeps the body alive; yogī = of collected mind; yatātmā = of restrained nature; the firm conviction is about the truth of the Self. What was hinted in the seventh adhyāya, ‘I am exceedingly dear to the man of knowledge and he is dear to me’ (7.17), is set out here.

यस्मान्नोद्विजते लोको लोकान्नोद्विजते च यः । हर्षामर्षभयोद्वेगैर्मुक्तो यः स च मे प्रियः ॥ १५ ॥
yasmānnodvijate loko lokānnodvijate ca yaḥ | harṣāmarṣabhayodvegairmukto yaḥ sa ca me priyaḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
यस्मात् न उद्विजते लोकः लोकात् न उद्विजते च यः हर्ष-अमर्ष-भय-उद्वेगैः मुक्तः यः सः च मे प्रियः
yasmāt na udvijate lokaḥ lokāt na udvijate ca yaḥ harṣa-amarṣa-bhaya-udvegaiḥ muktaḥ yaḥ saḥ ca me priyaḥ
Word by word
He from whom the world does not shrink and who does not shrink from the world, who is free from elation, intolerance, fear and agitation, he too is dear to me.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘from whom’ = from the renouncer; udvijate = is distressed, disturbed. harṣa = the uplift of the mind on gaining what is dear, marked by hair standing on end, tears and the like; amarṣa = want of forbearance.

अनपेक्षः शुचिर्दक्ष उदासीनो गतव्यथः । सर्वारम्भपरित्यागी यो मद्भक्तः स मे प्रियः ॥ १६ ॥
anapekṣaḥ śucirdakṣa udāsīno gatavyathaḥ | sarvārambhaparityāgī yo madbhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ || 16 ||
Padaccheda
अनपेक्षः शुचिः दक्षः उदासीनः गत-व्यथः सर्व-आरम्भ-परित्यागी यः मत्-भक्तः सः मे प्रियः
anapekṣaḥ śuciḥ dakṣaḥ udāsīnaḥ gata-vyathaḥ sarva-ārambha-parityāgī yaḥ mat-bhaktaḥ saḥ me priyaḥ
Word by word
He who expects nothing, who is pure, able, impartial, free from fear, who gives up all undertakings and is devoted to me, is dear to me.

Note Śaṅkara: without longing for the body, the senses, their objects and their connections; pure with outer and inner purity; dakṣa = able to see at once and rightly what to do in matters that arise; udāsīna = the ascetic who takes the side of no friend or other; undertakings = actions caused by desire, for enjoying fruits here and hereafter.

यो न हृष्यति न द्वेष्टि न शोचति न काङ्क्षति । शुभाशुभपरित्यागी भक्तिमान्यः स मे प्रियः ॥ १७ ॥
yo na hṛṣyati na dveṣṭi na śocati na kāṅkṣati | śubhāśubhaparityāgī bhaktimānyaḥ sa me priyaḥ || 17 ||
Padaccheda
यः न हृष्यति न द्वेष्टि न शोचति न काङ्क्षति शुभ-अशुभ-परित्यागी भक्तिमान् यः सः मे प्रियः
yaḥ na hṛṣyati na dveṣṭi na śocati na kāṅkṣati śubha-aśubha-parityāgī bhaktimān yaḥ saḥ me priyaḥ
Word by word
He who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves nor desires, who gives up good and evil and is full of devotion, is dear to me.

Note Śaṅkara: he does not rejoice on gaining the pleasant, hate on meeting the unpleasant, grieve on parting from what is dear, or desire what he has not got; good and evil = good and evil actions.

समः शत्रौ च मित्रे च तथा मानावमानयोः । शीतोष्णसुखदुःखेषु समः सङ्गविवर्जितः ॥ १८ ॥
samaḥ śatrau ca mitre ca tathā mānāvamānayoḥ | śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkheṣu samaḥ saṅgavivarjitaḥ || 18 ||
Padaccheda
समः शत्रौ च मित्रे च तथा मान-अवमानयोः शीत-उष्ण-सुख-दुःखेषु समः सङ्ग-विवर्जितः
samaḥ śatrau ca mitre ca tathā māna-avamānayoḥ śīta-uṣṇa-sukha-duḥkheṣu samaḥ saṅga-vivarjitaḥ
Word by word
The same towards enemy and friend, and likewise in honour and dishonour, the same in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, free from attachment,

Note Śaṅkara reads mānāpamānayoḥ and glosses: in worship and contempt; free from attachment everywhere.

तुल्यनिन्दास्तुतिर्मौनी संतुष्टो येन केनचित् । अनिकेतः स्थिरमतिर्भक्तिमान्मे प्रियो नरः ॥ १९ ॥
tulyanindāstutirmaunī saṃtuṣṭo yena kenacit | aniketaḥ sthiramatirbhaktimānme priyo naraḥ || 19 ||
Padaccheda
तुल्य-निन्दा-स्तुतिः मौनी संतुष्टः येन केनचित् अनिकेतः स्थिर-मतिः भक्तिमान् मे प्रियः नरः
tulya-nindā-stutiḥ maunī saṃtuṣṭaḥ yena kenacit aniketaḥ sthira-matiḥ bhaktimān me priyaḥ naraḥ
Word by word
to whom blame and praise are equal, silent, content with anything whatever, homeless, of steady mind, full of devotion: that man is dear to me.

Note Śaṅkara: maunī = of restrained speech; content with whatever merely keeps the body alive, for which he cites ‘clothed with anything, fed with anything, lying down anywhere: him the gods know as a brahmin’ (Mahābhārata, Śāntiparvan); aniketa = having no fixed dwelling; his steady mind is set on the highest truth.

ये तु धर्म्यामृतमिदं यथोक्तं पर्युपासते । श्रद्दधाना मत्परमा भक्तास्तेऽतीव मे प्रियाः ॥ २० ॥
ye tu dharmyāmṛtamidaṃ yathoktaṃ paryupāsate | śraddadhānā matparamā bhaktāste 'tīva me priyāḥ || 20 ||
Padaccheda
ये तु धर्म्य-अमृतम् इदम् यथा उक्तम् पर्युपासते श्रद्दधानाः मत्-परमाः भक्ताः ते अतीव मे प्रियाः
ye tu dharmya-amṛtam idam yathā uktam paryupāsate śraddadhānāḥ mat-paramāḥ bhaktāḥ te atīva me priyāḥ
Word by word
But those who practise this nectar of dharma as it has been told, having faith and holding me as the supreme goal, those devotees are exceedingly dear to me.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘those’ = the renouncers; dharmyāmṛtam = what does not depart from dharma and is nectar because it is the cause of immortality; paryupāsate = practise; ‘me’ = the Self that is the imperishable, as described; their devotion is the highest, that is, knowledge of the highest truth. This closes what was hinted at 7.17. Since one who practises it is exceedingly dear to the Lord, the seeker of liberation should practise it with effort.
