पञ्चमोऽध्यायः
Pañcamo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 5 · Karmasaṃnyāsayoga
अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
संन्यासं कर्मणां कृष्ण पुनर्योगं च शंससि । यच्छ्रेय एतयोरेकं तन्मे ब्रूहि सुनिश्चितम् ॥ १ ॥
saṃnyāsaṃ karmaṇāṃ kṛṣṇa punaryogaṃ ca śaṃsasi | yacchreya etayorekaṃ tanme brūhi suniścitam || 1 ||
Padaccheda
संन्यासम् कर्मणाम् कृष्ण पुनः योगम् च शंससि यत् श्रेयः एतयोः एकम् तत् मे ब्रूहि सु-निश्चितम्
saṃnyāsam karmaṇām kṛṣṇa punaḥ yogam ca śaṃsasi yat śreyaḥ etayoḥ ekam tat me brūhi su-niścitam
Word by word
Arjuna said: You praise the renunciation of actions, O Kṛṣṇa, and then again yoga. Tell me for certain which one of these two is the better.

Bhāṣya The actions are those enjoined by scripture, and yoga here is their performance. Since one person cannot do both at once, Arjuna asks for one of them.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
संन्यासः कर्मयोगश्च निःश्रेयसकरावुभौ । तयोस्तु कर्मसंन्यासात्कर्मयोगो विशिष्यते ॥ २ ॥
saṃnyāsaḥ karmayogaśca niḥśreyasakarāvubhau | tayostu karmasaṃnyāsātkarmayogo viśiṣyate || 2 ||
Padaccheda
संन्यासः कर्म-योगः च निःश्रेयस-करौ उभौ तयोः तु कर्म-संन्यासात् कर्म-योगः विशिष्यते
saṃnyāsaḥ karma-yogaḥ ca niḥśreyasa-karau ubhau tayoḥ tu karma-saṃnyāsāt karma-yogaḥ viśiṣyate
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: Renunciation and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good; but of the two, the yoga of action is better than the renunciation of action.

Bhāṣya Both lead to liberation by giving rise to knowledge. The yoga of action is praised as better than mere renunciation, that is, renunciation without knowledge.

ज्ञेयः स नित्यसंन्यासी यो न द्वेष्टि न काङ्क्षति । निर्द्वंद्वो हि महाबाहो सुखं बन्धात्प्रमुच्यते ॥ ३ ॥
jñeyaḥ sa nityasaṃnyāsī yo na dveṣṭi na kāṅkṣati | nirdvaṃdvo hi mahābāho sukhaṃ bandhātpramucyate || 3 ||
Padaccheda
ज्ञेयः सः नित्य-संन्यासी यः न द्वेष्टि न काङ्क्षति निर्द्वंद्वः हि महा-बाहो सुखम् बन्धात् प्रमुच्यते
jñeyaḥ saḥ nitya-saṃnyāsī yaḥ na dveṣṭi na kāṅkṣati nirdvaṃdvaḥ hi mahā-bāho sukham bandhāt pramucyate
Word by word
He who neither hates nor desires is to be known as a constant renouncer; for one free from the pairs of opposites, O mighty-armed, is easily released from bondage.

Bhāṣya This is the one who practises the yoga of action; though engaged in action, he is a constant renouncer. He does not hate pain or what brings it, nor desire pleasure or what brings it.

सांख्ययोगौ पृथग्बालाः प्रवदन्ति न पण्डिताः । एकमप्यास्थितः सम्यगुभयोर्विन्दते फलम् ॥ ४ ॥
sāṃkhyayogau pṛthagbālāḥ pravadanti na paṇḍitāḥ | ekamapyāsthitaḥ samyagubhayorvindate phalam || 4 ||
Padaccheda
सांख्य-योगौ पृथक् बालाः प्रवदन्ति न पण्डिताः एकम् अपि आस्थितः सम्यक् उभयोः विन्दते फलम्
sāṃkhya-yogau pṛthak bālāḥ pravadanti na paṇḍitāḥ ekam api āsthitaḥ samyak ubhayoḥ vindate phalam
Word by word
The childish, not the wise, speak of discernment and yoga as distinct. One who has rightly kept to even one of them gains the fruit of both.

Bhāṣya ‘distinct’ means having different and opposed fruits. The fruit of both is the same highest good. Arjuna asked about bare renunciation and bare yoga of action; the Lord, without leaving these aside, adds what he himself intends: renunciation joined with knowledge is called sāṃkhya, and the yoga of action joined with evenness of mind, the means to that knowledge, is called yoga.

यत्सांख्यैः प्राप्यते स्थानं तद्योगैरपि गम्यते । एकं सांख्यं च योगं च यः पश्यति स पश्यति ॥ ५ ॥
yatsāṃkhyaiḥ prāpyate sthānaṃ tadyogairapi gamyate | ekaṃ sāṃkhyaṃ ca yogaṃ ca yaḥ paśyati sa paśyati || 5 ||
Padaccheda
यत् सांख्यैः प्राप्यते स्थानम् तत् योगैः अपि गम्यते एकम् सांख्यम् च योगम् च यः पश्यति सः पश्यति
yat sāṃkhyaiḥ prāpyate sthānam tat yogaiḥ api gamyate ekam sāṃkhyam ca yogam ca yaḥ paśyati saḥ paśyati
Word by word
The state that is attained by the men of discernment is reached also by the men of action. He who sees discernment and yoga as one, sees.

Bhāṣya The men of discernment are the renouncers steadfast in knowledge, and the state is liberation. yogaiḥ stands for yogibhiḥ: those who perform actions as a means to knowledge, offering them to the Lord and seeking no fruit for themselves. They reach it by way of the knowledge of the truth and renunciation. The two are one because their fruit is one.

संन्यासस्तु महाबाहो दुःखमाप्तुमयोगतः । योगयुक्तो मुनिर्ब्रह्म नचिरेणाधिगच्छति ॥ ६ ॥
saṃnyāsastu mahābāho duḥkhamāptumayogataḥ | yogayukto munirbrahma nacireṇādhigacchati || 6 ||
Padaccheda
संन्यासः तु महा-बाहो दुःखम् आप्तुम् अ-योगतः योग-युक्तः मुनिः ब्रह्म नचिरेण अधिगच्छति
saṃnyāsaḥ tu mahā-bāho duḥkham āptum a-yogataḥ yoga-yuktaḥ muniḥ brahma nacireṇa adhigacchati
Word by word
But renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without yoga. The sage disciplined in yoga attains Brahman before long.

Bhāṣya This is why the yoga of action was called better. The renunciation meant is the true one. The yoga is the Vedic yoga of action, offered to the Lord and without regard for fruit. muni: one who reflects on the nature of the Lord. Brahman here means that true renunciation, marked by steadfastness in the knowledge of the supreme Self, as the Veda says ‘renunciation is Brahman’.

योगयुक्तो विशुद्धात्मा विजितात्मा जितेन्द्रियः । सर्वभूतात्मभूतात्मा कुर्वन्नपि न लिप्यते ॥ ७ ॥
yogayukto viśuddhātmā vijitātmā jitendriyaḥ | sarvabhūtātmabhūtātmā kurvannapi na lipyate || 7 ||
Padaccheda
योग-युक्तः विशुद्ध-आत्मा विजित-आत्मा जित-इन्द्रियः सर्व-भूत-आत्म-भूत-आत्मा कुर्वन् अपि न लिप्यते
yoga-yuktaḥ viśuddha-ātmā vijita-ātmā jita-indriyaḥ sarva-bhūta-ātma-bhūta-ātmā kurvan api na lipyate
Word by word
Disciplined in yoga, pure in mind, with the body mastered and the senses conquered, his Self become the Self of all beings, he is not stained though he acts.

Bhāṣya Ātman means the mind in the first compound and the body in the second. His inward Self is the Self of all beings from Brahmā down to a tuft of grass: he has right vision. Acting to hold the world together, he is not bound by actions.

नैव किंचित्करोमीति युक्तो मन्येत तत्त्ववित् । पश्यञ्शृण्वन्स्पृशञ्जिघ्रन्नश्नन्गच्छन्स्वपञ्श्वसन् ॥ ८ ॥
naiva kiṃcitkaromīti yukto manyeta tattvavit | paśyañśṛṇvanspṛśañjighrannaśnangacchansvapañśvasan || 8 ||
Padaccheda
न एव किंचित् करोमि इति युक्तः मन्येत तत्त्व-वित् पश्यन् शृण्वन् स्पृशन् जिघ्रन् अश्नन् गच्छन् स्वपन् श्वसन्
na eva kiṃcit karomi iti yuktaḥ manyeta tattva-vit paśyan śṛṇvan spṛśan jighran aśnan gacchan svapan śvasan
Word by word
‘I do nothing at all’: so the collected knower of the truth should think, while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing,

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara comments on verses 8 and 9 together. The knower of the truth knows the true nature of the Self.

प्रलपन्विसृजन्गृह्णन्नुन्मिषन्निमिषन्नपि । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेषु वर्तन्त इति धारयन् ॥ ९ ॥
pralapanvisṛjangṛhṇannunmiṣannimiṣannapi | indriyāṇīndriyārtheṣu vartanta iti dhārayan || 9 ||
Padaccheda
प्रलपन् विसृजन् गृह्णन् उन्मिषन् निमिषन् अपि इन्द्रियाणि इन्द्रिय-अर्थेषु वर्तन्ते इति धारयन्
pralapan visṛjan gṛhṇan unmiṣan nimiṣan api indriyāṇi indriya-artheṣu vartante iti dhārayan
Word by word
speaking, letting go, grasping, opening and closing the eyes: holding that it is the senses that move among the objects of the senses.

Bhāṣya One who thus sees only inaction in all the movements of body and organs is entitled to the renunciation of all action alone, since he sees that action does not exist. A man who ran to a mirage to drink, taking it for water, does not go there to drink once he knows there is no water.

ब्रह्मण्याधाय कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा करोति यः । लिप्यते न स पापेन पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा ॥ १० ॥
brahmaṇyādhāya karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā karoti yaḥ | lipyate na sa pāpena padmapatramivāmbhasā || 10 ||
Padaccheda
ब्रह्मणि आधाय कर्माणि सङ्गम् त्यक्त्वा करोति यः लिप्यते न सः पापेन पद्म-पत्रम् इव अम्भसा
brahmaṇi ādhāya karmāṇi saṅgam tyaktvā karoti yaḥ lipyate na saḥ pāpena padma-patram iva ambhasā
Word by word
He who acts having placed his actions in Brahman and given up attachment is not stained by sin, as a lotus leaf is not by water.

Bhāṣya This is the one who does not know the truth and is engaged in the yoga of action. Brahman here is the Lord: he places all actions in him, thinking ‘I act for his sake’, as a servant acts for his master, and gives up attachment even to liberation as a fruit.

कायेन मनसा बुद्ध्या केवलैरिन्द्रियैरपि । योगिनः कर्म कुर्वन्ति सङ्गं त्यक्त्वात्मशुद्धये ॥ ११ ॥
kāyena manasā buddhyā kevalairindriyairapi | yoginaḥ karma kurvanti saṅgaṃ tyaktvātmaśuddhaye || 11 ||
Padaccheda
कायेन मनसा बुद्ध्या केवलैः इन्द्रियैः अपि योगिनः कर्म कुर्वन्ति सङ्गम् त्यक्त्वा आत्म-शुद्धये
kāyena manasā buddhyā kevalaiḥ indriyaiḥ api yoginaḥ karma kurvanti saṅgam tyaktvā ātma-śuddhaye
Word by word
With the body, the mind, the understanding and also the mere senses, men of action perform action, having given up attachment, for the purification of the mind.

Bhāṣya Kevala, ‘mere’, means free of the sense of ‘mine’: ‘I act for the Lord alone, not for a fruit of my own’. The word goes with the body, mind and understanding as well. The only fruit of such action is purity of mind; therefore Arjuna, whose place is there, should act.

युक्तः कर्मफलं त्यक्त्वा शान्तिमाप्नोति नैष्ठिकीम् । अयुक्तः कामकारेण फले सक्तो निबध्यते ॥ १२ ॥
yuktaḥ karmaphalaṃ tyaktvā śāntimāpnoti naiṣṭhikīm | ayuktaḥ kāmakāreṇa phale sakto nibadhyate || 12 ||
Padaccheda
युक्तः कर्म-फलम् त्यक्त्वा शान्तिम् आप्नोति नैष्ठिकीम् अ-युक्तः काम-कारेण फले सक्तः निबध्यते
yuktaḥ karma-phalam tyaktvā śāntim āpnoti naiṣṭhikīm a-yuktaḥ kāma-kāreṇa phale saktaḥ nibadhyate
Word by word
The collected man, having given up the fruit of action, attains the peace born of steadfastness. One who is not collected, attached to the fruit by the prompting of desire, is bound.

Bhāṣya Collected in the thought ‘I act for the Lord, not for a fruit of my own’. The peace is liberation; it comes in stages: purity of mind, the gaining of knowledge, the renunciation of all action, steadfastness in knowledge.

सर्वकर्माणि मनसा संन्यस्यास्ते सुखं वशी । नवद्वारे पुरे देही नैव कुर्वन्न कारयन् ॥ १३ ॥
sarvakarmāṇi manasā saṃnyasyāste sukhaṃ vaśī | navadvāre pure dehī naiva kurvanna kārayan || 13 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-कर्माणि मनसा संन्यस्य आस्ते सुखम् वशी नव-द्वारे पुरे देही न एव कुर्वन् न कारयन्
sarva-karmāṇi manasā saṃnyasya āste sukham vaśī nava-dvāre pure dehī na eva kurvan na kārayan
Word by word
Having renounced all actions with the mind, the self-controlled embodied one sits at ease in the city of nine gates, neither acting nor causing to act.

Bhāṣya This is the one who sees the truth. He gives up all actions, obligatory, occasional, optional and forbidden, with the mind, that is, by the discerning understanding that sees inaction in action. The city is the body: seven gates in the head for perception and two below. Only one who sees the Self as distinct from the body can have the notion ‘I sit in the body’, as in a house. Doing and causing to do do not belong to the Self by its own nature. Śaṅkara discusses this at some length.

न कर्तृत्वं न कर्माणि लोकस्य सृजति प्रभुः । न कर्मफलसंयोगं स्वभावस्तु प्रवर्तते ॥ १४ ॥
na kartṛtvaṃ na karmāṇi lokasya sṛjati prabhuḥ | na karmaphalasaṃyogaṃ svabhāvastu pravartate || 14 ||
Padaccheda
न कर्तृत्वम् न कर्माणि लोकस्य सृजति प्रभुः न कर्म-फल-संयोगम् स्वभावः तु प्रवर्तते
na kartṛtvam na karmāṇi lokasya sṛjati prabhuḥ na karma-phala-saṃyogam svabhāvaḥ tu pravartate
Word by word
The lord creates neither agency nor actions for people, nor the connection of action with its fruit; it is nature that acts.

Bhāṣya The lord here is the Self. It does not make anyone an agent, saying ‘do this’, nor make the things people most want to make, such as chariots, pots and palaces, nor join the maker with the fruit. What acts is nature: prakṛti, māyā, which is ignorance, to be described in 7.14.

नादत्ते कस्यचित्पापं न चैव सुकृतं विभुः । अज्ञानेनावृतं ज्ञानं तेन मुह्यन्ति जन्तवः ॥ १५ ॥
nādatte kasyacitpāpaṃ na caiva sukṛtaṃ vibhuḥ | ajñānenāvṛtaṃ jñānaṃ tena muhyanti jantavaḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
न आदत्ते कस्यचित् पापम् न च एव सुकृतम् विभुः अज्ञानेन आवृतम् ज्ञानम् तेन मुह्यन्ति जन्तवः
na ādatte kasyacit pāpam na ca eva sukṛtam vibhuḥ ajñānena āvṛtam jñānam tena muhyanti jantavaḥ
Word by word
The all-pervading one takes on neither the sin nor the merit of anyone. Knowledge is covered by ignorance; by that creatures are deluded.

Bhāṣya Not even of a devotee. Why then do devotees offer worship, sacrifice, gifts and the like? Because discerning knowledge is covered by ignorance, so that those bound to the cycle of birth and death are deluded into thinking ‘I act, I cause to act, I shall enjoy’.

ज्ञानेन तु तदज्ञानं येषां नाशितमात्मनः । तेषामादित्यवज्ज्ञानं प्रकाशयति तत्परम् ॥ १६ ॥
jñānena tu tadajñānaṃ yeṣāṃ nāśitamātmanaḥ | teṣāmādityavajjñānaṃ prakāśayati tatparam || 16 ||
Padaccheda
ज्ञानेन तु तत् अज्ञानम् येषाम् नाशितम् आत्मनः तेषाम् आदित्यवत् ज्ञानम् प्रकाशयति तत् परम्
jñānena tu tat ajñānam yeṣām nāśitam ātmanaḥ teṣām ādityavat jñānam prakāśayati tat param
Word by word
But for those whose ignorance is destroyed by knowledge of the Self, that knowledge, like the sun, reveals the supreme reality.

Bhāṣya He construes ātmanaḥ with jñānena, the discerning knowledge that has the Self as its object. As the sun lights up all forms, so this knowledge lights up the whole of what is to be known, the supreme reality.

तद्बुद्धयस्तदात्मानस्तन्निष्ठास्तत्परायणाः । गच्छन्त्यपुनरावृत्तिं ज्ञाननिर्धूतकल्मषाः ॥ १७ ॥
tadbuddhayastadātmānastanniṣṭhāstatparāyaṇāḥ | gacchantyapunarāvṛttiṃ jñānanirdhūtakalmaṣāḥ || 17 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-बुद्धयः तत्-आत्मानः तत्-निष्ठाः तत्-परायणाः गच्छन्ति अपुनरावृत्तिम् ज्ञान-निर्धूत-कल्मषाः
tat-buddhayaḥ tat-ātmānaḥ tat-niṣṭhāḥ tat-parāyaṇāḥ gacchanti apunarāvṛttim jñāna-nirdhūta-kalmaṣāḥ
Word by word
With their understanding in that, with that as their Self, steadfast in that, with that as their final goal, they go where there is no return, their stains shaken off by knowledge.

Bhāṣya ‘that’ is Brahman. Steadfast: having renounced all actions, they abide in Brahman alone and delight in the Self only. No return: no further connection with a body. The stains are sin and the other faults that cause the cycle of birth and death. These are the ascetics.

विद्याविनयसम्पन्ने ब्राह्मणे गवि हस्तिनि । शुनि चैव श्वपाके च पण्डिताः समदर्शिनः ॥ १८ ॥
vidyāvinayasampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini | śuni caiva śvapāke ca paṇḍitāḥ samadarśinaḥ || 18 ||
Padaccheda
विद्या-विनय-सम्पन्ने ब्राह्मणे गवि हस्तिनि शुनि च एव श्वपाके च पण्डिताः सम-दर्शिनः
vidyā-vinaya-sampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini śuni ca eva śvapāke ca paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ
Word by word
In a brahmin endowed with learning and humility, in a cow, in an elephant, in a dog and in an outcaste, the wise see the same.

Bhāṣya Vinaya is calmness. The brahmin of the best refinement is of sattva, the cow of rajas, the elephant and the rest wholly of tamas. The wise are those whose habit it is to see in them the one changeless Brahman, untouched by the guṇas and by the tendencies born of them.

इहैव तैर्जितः सर्गो येषां साम्ये स्थितं मनः । निर्दोषं हि समं ब्रह्म तस्माद्ब्रह्मणि ते स्थिताः ॥ १९ ॥
ihaiva tairjitaḥ sargo yeṣāṃ sāmye sthitaṃ manaḥ | nirdoṣaṃ hi samaṃ brahma tasmādbrahmaṇi te sthitāḥ || 19 ||
Padaccheda
इह एव तैः जितः सर्गः येषाम् साम्ये स्थितम् मनः निर्दोषम् हि समम् ब्रह्म तस्मात् ब्रह्मणि ते स्थिताः
iha eva taiḥ jitaḥ sargaḥ yeṣām sāmye sthitam manaḥ nirdoṣam hi samam brahma tasmāt brahmaṇi te sthitāḥ
Word by word
Here itself birth is conquered by those whose mind rests in sameness. For Brahman is flawless and the same; therefore they abide in Brahman.

Bhāṣya This answers the objection from the law books that one who honours equals and unequals alike is at fault. ‘Here itself’: while still living. Sameness is Brahman, the same in all beings. Brahman is untouched by the faults of an outcaste and the like, though the foolish think otherwise, and is not divided by qualities, being without qualities. The rule of the law book applies to those who take the body for the Self and concerns action; the subject here, from verse 13 to the end of the adhyāya, is the renunciation of all action.

न प्रहृष्येत्प्रियं प्राप्य नोद्विजेत्प्राप्य चाप्रियम् । स्थिरबुद्धिरसंमूढो ब्रह्मविद्ब्रह्मणि स्थितः ॥ २० ॥
na prahṛṣyetpriyaṃ prāpya nodvijetprāpya cāpriyam | sthirabuddhirasaṃmūḍho brahmavidbrahmaṇi sthitaḥ || 20 ||
Padaccheda
न प्रहृष्येत् प्रियम् प्राप्य न उद्विजेत् प्राप्य च अप्रियम् स्थिर-बुद्धिः असंमूढः ब्रह्म-वित् ब्रह्मणि स्थितः
na prahṛṣyet priyam prāpya na udvijet prāpya ca apriyam sthira-buddhiḥ asaṃmūḍhaḥ brahma-vit brahmaṇi sthitaḥ
Word by word
He should not rejoice on getting what is pleasant, nor be troubled on getting what is unpleasant: of steady understanding, undeluded, the knower of Brahman abiding in Brahman.

Bhāṣya The pleasant and unpleasant bring joy and dejection to those who take the body for the Self, not to one who sees the Self alone. His steady understanding is the undoubting conviction that the Self is one, the same and flawless in all beings. Abiding in Brahman, he does no action: he has renounced all action.

बाह्यस्पर्शेष्वसक्तात्मा विन्दत्यात्मनि यत्सुखम् । स ब्रह्मयोगयुक्तात्मा सुखमक्षयमश्नुते ॥ २१ ॥
bāhyasparśeṣv asaktātmā vindatyātmani yatsukham | sa brahmayogayuktātmā sukhamakṣayamaśnute || 21 ||
Padaccheda
बाह्य-स्पर्शेषु असक्त-आत्मा विन्दति आत्मनि यत् सुखम् सः ब्रह्म-योग-युक्त-आत्मा सुखम् अक्षयम् अश्नुते
bāhya-sparśeṣu asakta-ātmā vindati ātmani yat sukham saḥ brahma-yoga-yukta-ātmā sukham akṣayam aśnute
Word by word
With mind unattached to outward objects, he finds the happiness that is in the Self; with mind collected in absorption in Brahman, he attains unfailing happiness.

Bhāṣya Sparśa, ‘what is touched’, means sound and the other objects of the senses. Yoga here is absorption (samādhi). One who wants unfailing happiness in the Self should turn the senses back from the momentary pleasure of outward objects.

ये हि संस्पर्शजा भोगा दुःखयोनय एव ते । आद्यन्तवन्तः कौन्तेय न तेषु रमते बुधः ॥ २२ ॥
ye hi saṃsparśajā bhogā duḥkhayonaya eva te | ādyantavantaḥ kaunteya na teṣu ramate budhaḥ || 22 ||
Padaccheda
ये हि संस्पर्श-जाः भोगाः दुःख-योनयः एव ते आदि-अन्तवन्तः कौन्तेय न तेषु रमते बुधः
ye hi saṃsparśa-jāḥ bhogāḥ duḥkha-yonayaḥ eva te ādi-antavantaḥ kaunteya na teṣu ramate budhaḥ
Word by word
For the enjoyments that are born of contact are only sources of pain; they have a beginning and an end, O son of Kuntī. The wise man does not delight in them.

Bhāṣya Born of the contact of the senses with their objects, and sources of pain because they are made by ignorance; ‘only’ shows that it is so in the next world as in this. Their beginning is the contact and their end the separation, so they last only the moment between. Delight in objects is seen only in the utterly deluded, as in cattle.

शक्नोतीहैव यः सोढुं प्राक्शरीरविमोक्षणात् । कामक्रोधोद्भवं वेगं स युक्तः स सुखी नरः ॥ २३ ॥
śaknotīhaiva yaḥ soḍhuṃ prākśarīravimokṣaṇāt | kāmakrodhodbhavaṃ vegaṃ sa yuktaḥ sa sukhī naraḥ || 23 ||
Padaccheda
शक्नोति इह एव यः सोढुम् प्राक् शरीर-विमोक्षणात् काम-क्रोध-उद्भवम् वेगम् सः युक्तः सः सुखी नरः
śaknoti iha eva yaḥ soḍhum prāk śarīra-vimokṣaṇāt kāma-krodha-udbhavam vegam saḥ yuktaḥ saḥ sukhī naraḥ
Word by word
The man who is able, here itself, before release from the body, to withstand the surge that arises from desire and anger: he is a yogī, he is happy.

Bhāṣya ‘before release from the body’ means up to death; the surge has countless causes and is not to be trusted as long as one lives. Desire is the thirst for a pleasant object that is perceived, heard of or remembered; anger is the aversion to what is contrary and painful. The surge of desire is an agitation of the mind marked by the hair standing on end and a glad face; that of anger by trembling, sweating, biting the lips and red eyes.

योऽन्तःसुखोऽन्तरारामस्तथान्तर्ज्योतिरेव यः । स योगी ब्रह्मनिर्वाणं ब्रह्मभूतोऽधिगच्छति ॥ २४ ॥
yo 'ntaḥsukho 'ntarārāmastathāntarjyotireva yaḥ | sa yogī brahmanirvāṇaṃ brahmabhūto 'dhigacchati || 24 ||
Padaccheda
यः अन्तः-सुखः अन्तर्-आरामः तथा अन्तर्-ज्योतिः एव यः सः योगी ब्रह्म-निर्वाणम् ब्रह्म-भूतः अधिगच्छति
yaḥ antaḥ-sukhaḥ antar-ārāmaḥ tathā antar-jyotiḥ eva yaḥ saḥ yogī brahma-nirvāṇam brahma-bhūtaḥ adhigacchati
Word by word
He who has his happiness within, his delight within, and likewise his light within only: that yogī, having become Brahman, attains the bliss of Brahman.

Bhāṣya ‘within’ is in the Self. brahmanirvāṇa is repose in Brahman, liberation; he attains it here, while still living.

लभन्ते ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृषयः क्षीणकल्मषाः । छिन्नद्वैधा यतात्मानः सर्वभूतहिते रताः ॥ २५ ॥
labhante brahmanirvāṇamṛṣayaḥ kṣīṇakalmaṣāḥ | chinnadvaidhā yatātmānaḥ sarvabhūtahite ratāḥ || 25 ||
Padaccheda
लभन्ते ब्रह्म-निर्वाणम् ऋषयः क्षीण-कल्मषाः छिन्न-द्वैधाः यत-आत्मानः सर्व-भूत-हिते रताः
labhante brahma-nirvāṇam ṛṣayaḥ kṣīṇa-kalmaṣāḥ chinna-dvaidhāḥ yata-ātmānaḥ sarva-bhūta-hite ratāḥ
Word by word
The seers whose sins are worn away, whose doubts are cut through, who are self-controlled and delight in the good of all beings, gain the bliss of Brahman.

Bhāṣya The seers are the renouncers of right vision. yatātmānaḥ: with senses restrained. Delighting in the good of all beings, they harm none.

कामक्रोधवियुक्तानां यतीनां यतचेतसाम् । अभितो ब्रह्मनिर्वाणं वर्तते विदितात्मनाम् ॥ २६ ॥
kāmakrodhaviyuktānāṃ yatīnāṃ yatacetasām | abhito brahmanirvāṇaṃ vartate viditātmanām || 26 ||
Padaccheda
काम-क्रोध-वियुक्तानाम् यतीनाम् यत-चेतसाम् अभितः ब्रह्म-निर्वाणम् वर्तते विदित-आत्मनाम्
kāma-krodha-viyuktānām yatīnām yata-cetasām abhitaḥ brahma-nirvāṇam vartate vidita-ātmanām
Word by word
For the ascetics who are free from desire and anger, whose minds are controlled and who have known the Self, the bliss of Brahman is there on both sides.

Bhāṣya The ascetics are the renouncers. ‘On both sides’: whether they are living or dead, liberation is theirs.

स्पर्शान्कृत्वा बहिर्बाह्यांश्चक्षुश्चैवान्तरे भ्रुवोः । प्राणापानौ समौ कृत्वा नासाभ्यन्तरचारिणौ ॥ २७ ॥
sparśānkṛtvā bahirbāhyāṃścakṣuścaivāntare bhruvoḥ | prāṇāpānau samau kṛtvā nāsābhyantaracāriṇau || 27 ||
Padaccheda
स्पर्शान् कृत्वा बहिः बाह्यान् चक्षुः च एव अन्तरे भ्रुवोः प्राण-अपानौ समौ कृत्वा नासा-अभ्यन्तर-चारिणौ
sparśān kṛtvā bahiḥ bāhyān cakṣuḥ ca eva antare bhruvoḥ prāṇa-apānau samau kṛtvā nāsā-abhyantara-cāriṇau
Word by word
Having put the outward objects outside and fixed the gaze between the eyebrows, having made even the out-breath and the in-breath that move within the nose,

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara comments on verses 27 and 28 together. Liberation at once has been taught for the renouncers steadfast in right vision, and liberation by stages for the yoga of action. Now the yoga of meditation, the inner means to right vision, is to be taught at length (in the next adhyāya); these verses state it in brief, like aphorisms. The objects are outside by nature and come within through the senses; one who does not think of them has put them outside.

यतेन्द्रियमनोबुद्धिर्मुनिर्मोक्षपरायणः । विगतेच्छाभयक्रोधो यः सदा मुक्त एव सः ॥ २८ ॥
yatendriyamanobuddhirmunirmokṣaparāyaṇaḥ | vigatecchābhayakrodho yaḥ sadā mukta eva saḥ || 28 ||
Padaccheda
यत-इन्द्रिय-मनः-बुद्धिः मुनिः मोक्ष-परायणः विगत-इच्छा-भय-क्रोधः यः सदा मुक्तः एव सः
yata-indriya-manaḥ-buddhiḥ muniḥ mokṣa-parāyaṇaḥ vigata-icchā-bhaya-krodhaḥ yaḥ sadā muktaḥ eva saḥ
Word by word
the sage with senses, mind and understanding controlled, with liberation as his final goal, free from desire, fear and anger: he who is always so is indeed liberated.

Bhāṣya Muni, from reflecting; he is the renouncer. For one who always lives so there is no other liberation still to be won.

भोक्तारं यज्ञतपसां सर्वलोकमहेश्वरम् । सुहृदं सर्वभूतानां ज्ञात्वा मां शान्तिमृच्छति ॥ २९ ॥
bhoktāraṃ yajñatapasāṃ sarvalokamaheśvaram | suhṛdaṃ sarvabhūtānāṃ jñātvā māṃ śāntimṛcchati || 29 ||
Padaccheda
भोक्तारम् यज्ञ-तपसाम् सर्व-लोक-महा-ईश्वरम् सुहृदम् सर्व-भूतानाम् ज्ञात्वा माम् शान्तिम् ऋच्छति
bhoktāram yajña-tapasām sarva-loka-mahā-īśvaram suhṛdam sarva-bhūtānām jñātvā mām śāntim ṛcchati
Word by word
Knowing me as the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities, the great Lord of all the worlds, and the friend of all beings, he attains peace.

Bhāṣya This is what one whose mind is so collected has to know. The Lord is the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities both as their performer and as the deity. A friend does good without looking for a return. He is Nārāyaṇa, who dwells in the heart of all beings, oversees the fruits of all actions and witnesses all thoughts. Peace is the ceasing of the whole cycle of birth and death.
