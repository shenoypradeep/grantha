समाधिपादः
Samādhipādaḥ · Pāda 1 · On meditative absorption
अथ योगानुशासनम् ॥ १ ॥
atha yogānuśāsanam || 1 ||
Padaccheda
अथ योग-अनुशासनम्
atha yoga-anuśāsanam
Word by word
Now, the teaching of yoga.

Bhāṣya Atha marks the beginning of the subject: the treatise that teaches yoga is to be understood as begun. Yoga is meditative absorption (samādhi), and that is a property of the mind in all its states. The states of the mind are five: restless, dull, distracted, one-pointed and ceased. Absorption in a distracted mind, being subordinate to distraction, does not count as yoga. That which, in a one-pointed mind, lights up a thing as it really is, wears away the afflictions, loosens the bonds of action and turns the mind towards cessation is called yoga with cognition (saṃprajñāta); when all activities have ceased, the absorption is beyond cognition (asaṃprajñāta).

योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः ॥ २ ॥
yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ || 2 ||
Padaccheda
योगः चित्त-वृत्ति-निरोधः
yogaḥ citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ
Word by word
Yoga is the cessation of the activities of the mind.

Bhāṣya Since the sūtra does not say ‘all’, yoga with cognition is also called yoga. The mind has the three guṇas, since it tends to illumination, activity and inertia. When, with the last trace of rajas gone, it rests in its own nature and is nothing but the discernment that sattva and the Puruṣa are different, it turns to the meditation called ‘cloud of dharma’. The power of consciousness is unchanging, does not pass into objects, and is pure and endless; this discernment, being of the nature of sattva, is its opposite. So the mind, dispassionate towards that too, brings even that discernment to cessation and stays with latent impressions alone: that is seedless absorption, beyond cognition.

तदा द्रष्टुः स्वरूपेऽवस्थानम् ॥ ३ ॥
tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam || 3 ||
Padaccheda
तदा द्रष्टुः स्व-रूपे अवस्थानम्
tadā draṣṭuḥ sva-rūpe avasthānam
Word by word
Then the seer abides in his own nature.

Bhāṣya Then the power of consciousness is established in its own nature, as in soleness. When the mind is turned outwards it is so too, yet does not appear so.

Note kaivalya, ‘soleness’: the state of the Puruṣa alone (kevala), resting in its own nature, free of the guṇas (4.34).

वृत्तिसारूप्यमितरत्र ॥ ४ ॥
vṛttisārūpyam itaratra || 4 ||
Padaccheda
वृत्ति-सारूप्यम् इतरत्र
vṛtti-sārūpyam itaratra
Word by word
At other times he has the same form as the activities.

Bhāṣya When the mind is turned outwards, the Puruṣa's activity is not distinct from the activities of the mind. The mind is like a magnet: it serves merely by being near, and as what is seen it belongs to the Puruṣa, its owner. So the cause of the Puruṣa's awareness of the mind's activities is his beginningless connection with it.

वृत्तयः पञ्चतय्यः क्लिष्टाक्लिष्टाः ॥ ५ ॥
vṛttayaḥ pañcatayyaḥ kliṣṭākliṣṭāḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
वृत्तयः पञ्चतय्यः क्लिष्ट-अक्लिष्टाः
vṛttayaḥ pañcatayyaḥ kliṣṭa-akliṣṭāḥ
Word by word
The activities are of five kinds, afflicted and unafflicted.

Bhāṣya The afflicted are caused by the afflictions and are the field in which the store of action grows. The unafflicted have discernment for their object and oppose the working of the guṇas; they remain unafflicted even when they fall in a stream of the afflicted. Activities produce latent impressions of their own kind, and impressions produce activities: so the wheel of activities and impressions turns without rest.

प्रमाणविपर्ययविकल्पनिद्रास्मृतयः ॥ ६ ॥
pramāṇaviparyayavikalpanidrāsmṛtayaḥ || 6 ||
Padaccheda
प्रमाण-विपर्यय-विकल्प-निद्रा-स्मृतयः
pramāṇa-viparyaya-vikalpa-nidrā-smṛtayaḥ
Word by word
They are valid knowledge, error, verbal construction, sleep and memory.
प्रत्यक्षानुमानागमाः प्रमाणानि ॥ ७ ॥
pratyakṣānumānāgamāḥ pramāṇāni || 7 ||
Padaccheda
प्रत्यक्ष-अनुमान-आगमाः प्रमाणानि
pratyakṣa-anumāna-āgamāḥ pramāṇāni
Word by word
The kinds of valid knowledge are perception, inference and testimony.

Bhāṣya Perception is the activity of the mind, coloured by an outer thing through the channel of the senses, which chiefly determines the particular in an object that is both general and particular. Inference chiefly determines the general, through a relation present in things of the same class and absent from things of another: the moon and stars move, because they reach another place, like Caitra. Testimony is the hearer's knowledge of a thing from the words of a trustworthy person who has seen or inferred it.

विपर्ययो मिथ्याज्ञानमतद्रूपप्रतिष्ठम् ॥ ८ ॥
viparyayo mithyājñānam atadrūpapratiṣṭham || 8 ||
Padaccheda
विपर्ययः मिथ्या-ज्ञानम् अतत्-रूप-प्रतिष्ठम्
viparyayaḥ mithyā-jñānam atat-rūpa-pratiṣṭham
Word by word
Error is false knowledge, not resting on the form of the thing.

Bhāṣya It is not valid knowledge because valid knowledge, whose object is the thing as it is, cancels it, as the sight of one moon cancels the sight of two. This is the fivefold ignorance, the afflictions of 2.3, also named darkness, delusion, great delusion, gloom and blind gloom.

शब्दज्ञानानुपाती वस्तुशून्यो विकल्पः ॥ ९ ॥
śabdajñānānupātī vastuśūnyo vikalpaḥ || 9 ||
Padaccheda
शब्द-ज्ञान-अनुपाती वस्तु-शून्यः विकल्पः
śabda-jñāna-anupātī vastu-śūnyaḥ vikalpaḥ
Word by word
Verbal construction follows from knowledge of words and is empty of any real thing.

Bhāṣya It falls under neither valid knowledge nor error. Though there is no real thing, usage rests on the power of the knowledge of words: as in ‘consciousness is the nature of the Puruṣa’, where, since the Puruṣa is consciousness itself, nothing is predicated of anything; or ‘the Puruṣa is inactive’, ‘the arrow stands still’, where only the absence of a property is understood.

अभावप्रत्ययालम्बना वृत्तिर्निद्रा ॥ १० ॥
abhāvapratyayālambanā vṛttir nidrā || 10 ||
Padaccheda
अभाव-प्रत्यय-आलम्बना वृत्तिः निद्रा
abhāva-pratyaya-ālambanā vṛttiḥ nidrā
Word by word
Sleep is the activity that rests on the notion of absence.

Bhāṣya It is a particular cognition, because one reflects on it on waking: ‘I slept well, my mind is clear’; ‘I slept badly, my mind is dull’; ‘I slept in a deep stupor, my limbs are heavy’. That reflection, and the memories based on it, could not be without an experience. So sleep too is to be brought to cessation in absorption, like the other cognitions.

अनुभूतविषयासंप्रमोषः स्मृतिः ॥ ११ ॥
anubhūtaviṣayāsaṃpramoṣaḥ smṛtiḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
अनुभूत-विषय-असंप्रमोषः स्मृतिः
anubhūta-viṣaya-asaṃpramoṣaḥ smṛtiḥ
Word by word
Memory is the not letting slip of an object once experienced.

Bhāṣya A cognition, coloured by its object and showing the form of both the object and the grasping of it, sets up a latent impression of the same kind, and that impression produces a memory of both. Memory is of two kinds: of what is imagined, in dream, and of what is not imagined, in waking. All these activities are of the nature of pleasure, pain and delusion, and all are to be brought to cessation; then absorption follows, with cognition or beyond it.

अभ्यासवैराग्याभ्यां तन्निरोधः ॥ १२ ॥
abhyāsavairāgyābhyāṃ tannirodhaḥ || 12 ||
Padaccheda
अभ्यास-वैराग्याभ्याम् तत्-निरोधः
abhyāsa-vairāgyābhyām tat-nirodhaḥ
Word by word
Their cessation comes by practice and dispassion.

Bhāṣya The river of the mind flows both ways, towards good and towards evil. Flowing down the slope of discernment towards soleness it carries good; flowing down the slope of non-discernment towards the cycle of birth and death it carries evil. Dispassion dams the stream towards objects; the practice of discerning sight opens the stream of discernment. So cessation depends on both.

तत्र स्थितौ यत्नोऽभ्यासः ॥ १३ ॥
tatra sthitau yatno 'bhyāsaḥ || 13 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र स्थितौ यत्नः अभ्यासः
tatra sthitau yatnaḥ abhyāsaḥ
Word by word
Of these, practice is the effort for steadiness.

Bhāṣya Steadiness is the calm flow of a mind free of activities. Effort is energy, zeal; practice is carrying out the means with the wish to bring that about.

स तु दीर्घकालनैरन्तर्यसत्कारासेवितो दृढभूमिः ॥ १४ ॥
sa tu dīrghakālanairantaryasatkārāsevito dṛḍhabhūmiḥ || 14 ||
Padaccheda
सः तु दीर्घ-काल-नैरन्तर्य-सत्कार-आसेवितः दृढ-भूमिः
saḥ tu dīrgha-kāla-nairantarya-satkāra-āsevitaḥ dṛḍha-bhūmiḥ
Word by word
But that is firmly grounded when attended to for a long time, without break and with devotion.

Bhāṣya Carried out with austerity, continence, knowledge and faith, it is done with devotion and becomes firmly grounded: it is not suddenly overpowered by the impressions of the outward-turned mind.

दृष्टानुश्रविकविषयवितृष्णस्य वशीकारसंज्ञा वैराग्यम् ॥ १५ ॥
dṛṣṭānuśravikaviṣayavitṛṣṇasya vaśīkārasaṃjñā vairāgyam || 15 ||
Padaccheda
दृष्ट-आनुश्रविक-विषय-वितृष्णस्य वशीकार-संज्ञा वैराग्यम्
dṛṣṭa-ānuśravika-viṣaya-vitṛṣṇasya vaśīkāra-saṃjñā vairāgyam
Word by word
Dispassion is the consciousness of mastery in one without thirst for objects seen or heard of in scripture.

Bhāṣya Objects seen are women, food and drink, lordship; those heard of in scripture are heaven, the bodiless state and the state of being merged in Prakṛti. Even in contact with objects divine or not divine, the mind, seeing their faults by the strength of reflection, neither rejects nor takes them up: that freedom from enjoyment is the consciousness of mastery.

तत्परं पुरुषख्यातेर्गुणवैतृष्ण्यम् ॥ १६ ॥
tat paraṃ puruṣakhyāter guṇavaitṛṣṇyam || 16 ||
Padaccheda
तत् परम् पुरुष-ख्यातेः गुण-वैतृष्ण्यम्
tat param puruṣa-khyāteḥ guṇa-vaitṛṣṇyam
Word by word
The higher dispassion is freedom from thirst for the guṇas, from the discernment of the Puruṣa.

Bhāṣya One who is dispassionate through seeing the faults of objects seen and heard of, and whose understanding is filled by the practice of seeing the Puruṣa, becomes dispassionate towards the guṇas themselves, manifest and unmanifest. The second dispassion is only the clearness of knowledge. When it arises the yogin thinks: ‘what was to be gained is gained; the afflictions to be destroyed are destroyed; the chain of births is cut’. Dispassion is the highest point of knowledge, and soleness follows it without a gap.

वितर्कविचारानन्दास्मितारूपानुगमात्संप्रज्ञातः ॥ १७ ॥
vitarkavicārānandāsmitārūpānugamāt saṃprajñātaḥ || 17 ||
Padaccheda
वितर्क-विचार-आनन्द-अस्मिता-रूप-अनुगमात् संप्रज्ञातः
vitarka-vicāra-ānanda-asmitā-rūpa-anugamāt saṃprajñātaḥ
Word by word
(Meditative absorption is) with cognition when it is accompanied by the forms of gross thought, subtle thought, joy and the sense of ‘I am’.

Bhāṣya Vitarka is the mind's gross experience of its object, vicāra the subtle; ānanda is gladness; asmitā is the awareness of oneself as one. The first absorption has all four; the second lacks gross thought; the third lacks subtle thought too; the fourth is the sense of ‘I am’ alone. All of them have an object.

विरामप्रत्ययाभ्यासपूर्वः संस्कारशेषोऽन्यः ॥ १८ ॥
virāmapratyayābhyāsapūrvaḥ saṃskāraśeṣo 'nyaḥ || 18 ||
Padaccheda
विराम-प्रत्यय-अभ्यास-पूर्वः संस्कार-शेषः अन्यः
virāma-pratyaya-abhyāsa-pūrvaḥ saṃskāra-śeṣaḥ anyaḥ
Word by word
The other, preceded by the practice of the notion of cessation, is that in which latent impressions alone remain.

Bhāṣya This is absorption beyond cognition: the cessation of the mind when all activities have set and only impressions remain. Its means is the higher dispassion; a practice that has an object cannot bring it about, so the notion of cessation, which has no object, is made the support. The mind then, without support, is as if it had ceased to be: this is seedless absorption.

भवप्रत्ययो विदेहप्रकृतिलयानाम् ॥ १९ ॥
bhavapratyayo videhaprakṛtilayānām || 19 ||
Padaccheda
भव-प्रत्ययः विदेह-प्रकृतिलयानाम्
bhava-pratyayaḥ videha-prakṛtilayānām
Word by word
For the bodiless and for those merged in Prakṛti it is caused by birth.

Bhāṣya It is of two kinds, caused by a means and caused by birth; the first belongs to yogins. The bodiless are gods: with a mind that works by its own impressions alone they experience something like soleness, and live out the ripening of those impressions. Those merged in Prakṛti likewise experience something like soleness, until the mind, its task unfinished, returns.

श्रद्धावीर्यस्मृतिसमाधिप्रज्ञापूर्वक इतरेषाम् ॥ २० ॥
śraddhāvīryasmṛtisamādhiprajñāpūrvaka itareṣām || 20 ||
Padaccheda
श्रद्धा-वीर्य-स्मृति-समाधि-प्रज्ञा-पूर्वकः इतरेषाम्
śraddhā-vīrya-smṛti-samādhi-prajñā-pūrvakaḥ itareṣām
Word by word
For the others it is preceded by faith, energy, mindfulness, meditative absorption and wisdom.

Bhāṣya This is the one caused by a means, and belongs to yogins. Faith is the clearness of the mind; like a kind mother it protects the yogin. In one who has faith and seeks discernment, energy arises; then mindfulness; with mindfulness the mind, untroubled, becomes absorbed; and in the absorbed mind comes the discernment of wisdom, by which he knows a thing as it is. From the practice of that, and from dispassion, comes absorption beyond cognition.

तीव्रसंवेगानामासन्नः ॥ २१ ॥
tīvrasaṃvegānām āsannaḥ || 21 ||
Padaccheda
तीव्र-संवेगानाम् आसन्नः
tīvra-saṃvegānām āsannaḥ
Word by word
For those of keen ardour it is near.

Bhāṣya Yogins are of nine kinds, as their means are mild, middling or intense, and each of these as their ardour is mild, middling or keen. What is near is the gaining of absorption and its fruit.

मृदुमध्याधिमात्रत्वात्ततोऽपि विशेषः ॥ २२ ॥
mṛdumadhyādhimātratvāt tato 'pi viśeṣaḥ || 22 ||
Padaccheda
मृदु-मध्य-अधिमात्रत्वात् ततः अपि विशेषः
mṛdu-madhya-adhimātratvāt tataḥ api viśeṣaḥ
Word by word
Even among these there is a difference, as it is mild, middling or intense.

Bhāṣya Keen ardour is itself mildly, middlingly or intensely keen. For the mildly keen it is near, for the middlingly keen nearer, and for the intensely keen whose means are intense it is nearest.

ईश्वरप्रणिधानाद्वा ॥ २३ ॥
īśvarapraṇidhānād vā || 23 ||
Padaccheda
ईश्वर-प्रणिधानात् वा
īśvara-praṇidhānāt vā
Word by word
Or from devotion to the Lord.

Bhāṣya Praṇidhāna is a particular kind of devotion. Drawn by it, the Lord favours the yogin by his mere thought of him; and by that alone the gaining of absorption and its fruit comes nearest.

क्लेशकर्मविपाकाशयैरपरामृष्टः पुरुषविशेष ईश्वरः ॥ २४ ॥
kleśakarmavipākāśayair aparāmṛṣṭaḥ puruṣaviśeṣa īśvaraḥ || 24 ||
Padaccheda
क्लेश-कर्म-विपाक-आशयैः अपरामृष्टः पुरुष-विशेषः ईश्वरः
kleśa-karma-vipāka-āśayaiḥ aparāmṛṣṭaḥ puruṣa-viśeṣaḥ īśvaraḥ
Word by word
The Lord is a distinct Puruṣa, untouched by afflictions, actions, their ripening and their latent stores.

Bhāṣya The afflictions are ignorance and the rest; actions are good and bad; ripening is their fruit; the stores are the latent tendencies that accord with it. These exist in the mind and are ascribed to the Puruṣa as their experiencer, as victory or defeat among soldiers is ascribed to their master. He who is untouched by such experience is the Lord. Many have reached soleness after cutting the three bonds; but the Lord never had, and never will have, any tie with bondage: he is always free and always the Lord. His lordship has no equal and none higher, for of two equals wishing opposite things at once, one must fail.

तत्र निरतिशयं सर्वज्ञबीजम् ॥ २५ ॥
tatra niratiśayaṃ sarvajñabījam || 25 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र निरतिशयम् सर्वज्ञ-बीजम्
tatra niratiśayam sarvajña-bījam
Word by word
In him the seed of omniscience is unsurpassed.

Bhāṣya The grasping of things past, future and present, singly or together, beyond the senses, is small in one and greater in another: this is the seed of omniscience. Since it admits of more, it must reach a limit, as size does; he in whom it reaches its limit is the omniscient one. Inference shows only that there is such a one; his name and the like are to be learnt from scripture. He has nothing to gain for himself; his purpose is to favour beings, by teaching knowledge and dharma.

पूर्वेषामपि गुरुः कालेनानवच्छेदात् ॥ २६ ॥
pūrveṣām api guruḥ kālenānavacchedāt || 26 ||
Padaccheda
पूर्वेषाम् अपि गुरुः कालेन अनवच्छेदात्
pūrveṣām api guruḥ kālena anavacchedāt
Word by word
He is the teacher even of the earlier ones, because he is not limited by time.

Bhāṣya The earlier teachers are limited by time; he whom time does not reach as a limit is their teacher too. As he is there at the beginning of this creation, so he is to be understood at the beginnings of the creations that have passed.

तस्य वाचकः प्रणवः ॥ २७ ॥
tasya vācakaḥ praṇavaḥ || 27 ||
Padaccheda
तस्य वाचकः प्रणवः
tasya vācakaḥ praṇavaḥ
Word by word
The word that denotes him is the syllable Om.

Bhāṣya Is the relation of word and thing made by convention, or is it there already, like a lamp and its light? It is there already; the Lord's convention only makes known what exists, as the existing relation of father and son is shown by saying ‘this is his father’. In other creations too the convention is made in the same way.

तज्जपस्तदर्थभावनम् ॥ २८ ॥
tajjapas tadarthabhāvanam || 28 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-जपः तत्-अर्थ-भावनम्
tat-japaḥ tat-artha-bhāvanam
Word by word
The repetition of it and dwelling on its meaning (are to be practised).

Bhāṣya Repetition of Om, and dwelling on the Lord whom it denotes. The mind of the yogin who does both becomes one-pointed. As it is said: ‘From recitation let him pass to yoga, from yoga to recitation; by the perfection of both the supreme Self shines forth.’

ततः प्रत्यक्चेतनाधिगमोऽप्यन्तरायाभावश्च ॥ २९ ॥
tataḥ pratyakcetanādhigamo 'py antarāyābhāvaś ca || 29 ||
Padaccheda
ततः प्रत्यक्-चेतना-अधिगमः अपि अन्तराय-अभावः च
tataḥ pratyak-cetanā-adhigamaḥ api antarāya-abhāvaḥ ca
Word by word
From that come the attainment of the inward consciousness and also the absence of obstacles.

Bhāṣya The obstacles, sickness and the rest, do not arise, through devotion to the Lord; and he comes to see his own nature. As the Lord is a Puruṣa, pure, serene, alone, free of trouble, so he knows this Puruṣa, the one who is aware of the understanding.

व्याधिस्त्यानसंशयप्रमादालस्याविरतिभ्रान्तिदर्शनालब्धभूमिकत्वानवस्थितत्वानि चित्तविक्षेपास्तेऽन्तरायाः ॥ ३० ॥
vyādhistyānasaṃśayapramādālasyāviratibhrāntidarśanālabdhabhūmikatvānavasthitatvāni cittavikṣepās te 'ntarāyāḥ || 30 ||
Padaccheda
व्याधि-स्त्यान-संशय-प्रमाद-आलस्य-अविरति-भ्रान्तिदर्शन-अलब्धभूमिकत्व-अनवस्थितत्वानि चित्त-विक्षेपाः ते अन्तरायाः
vyādhi-styāna-saṃśaya-pramāda-ālasya-avirati-bhrāntidarśana-alabdhabhūmikatva-anavasthitatvāni citta-vikṣepāḥ te antarāyāḥ
Word by word
Sickness, dullness, doubt, negligence, laziness, craving, mistaken view, not gaining a stage and not staying in it are the distractions of the mind; they are the obstacles.

Bhāṣya Sickness is imbalance of the humours, secretions and organs. Dullness is the mind's unfitness for work. Doubt is a thought that touches both sides: ‘it may be so, it may not’. Negligence is not cultivating the means to absorption. Laziness is inactivity from heaviness of body and mind. Craving is the mind's greed for contact with objects. Mistaken view is knowledge by error. The stage is a stage of absorption; not staying is the mind's failure to hold a stage it has gained. These nine arise together with the activities of the mind.

दुःखदौर्मनस्याङ्गमेजयत्वश्वासप्रश्वासा विक्षेपसहभुवः ॥ ३१ ॥
duḥkhadaurmanasyāṅgamejayatvaśvāsapraśvāsā vikṣepasahabhuvaḥ || 31 ||
Padaccheda
दुःख-दौर्मनस्य-अङ्गमेजयत्व-श्वास-प्रश्वासाः विक्षेप-सहभुवः
duḥkha-daurmanasya-aṅgamejayatva-śvāsa-praśvāsāḥ vikṣepa-sahabhuvaḥ
Word by word
Pain, dejection, trembling of the limbs, in-breathing and out-breathing go with the distractions.

Bhāṣya Pain is of three kinds, from oneself, from other beings and from the gods; it is that which beings, when struck by it, strive to remove. Dejection is the mind's disturbance when a wish is thwarted. In-breathing is the breath drawing in the outer air; out-breathing is its sending out the air within. These belong to the distracted mind, and not to the absorbed mind.

तत्प्रतिषेधार्थमेकतत्त्वाभ्यासः ॥ ३२ ॥
tatpratiṣedhārtham ekatattvābhyāsaḥ || 32 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-प्रतिषेध-अर्थम् एक-तत्त्व-अभ्यासः
tat-pratiṣedha-artham eka-tattva-abhyāsaḥ
Word by word
To ward them off, the practice of one principle.

Bhāṣya To ward off the distractions one should train the mind to rest on a single principle. He then argues against those for whom the mind is a mere cognition, bound to one object and momentary. On that view every mind is one-pointed and none is distracted; yet the mind is said to become one- pointed when it is withdrawn from everything and settled on one object, so it is not bound to one object at a time. If one-pointedness is taken to be a stream of like cognitions, it cannot be a property of the stream-mind, for, being momentary, there is no one stream-mind; and if it is a property of each cognition in the stream, then every cognition, in a like or an unlike stream, is one-pointed, and a distracted mind is impossible.

Bhāṣya And if cognitions different in nature arose unconnected by one mind, how could one cognition remember what another saw, or experience the fruit of the store of action that another laid up? Whatever answer is contrived for this outdoes the reasoning that cow-dung is milk-rice. It also denies one's own experience: in ‘what I saw I now touch’ the notion ‘I’ stands undivided in the one who has the cognitions, though the cognitions differ. This is grasped by one's own experience; and the force of perception is not overcome by another means of knowledge, which itself works only by the strength of perception. So the mind is one, has many objects, and endures.

मैत्रीकरुणामुदितोपेक्षाणां सुखदुःखपुण्यापुण्यविषयाणां भावनातश्चित्तप्रसादनम् ॥ ३३ ॥
maitrīkaruṇāmuditopekṣāṇāṃ sukhaduḥkhapuṇyāpuṇyaviṣayāṇāṃ bhāvanātaś cittaprasādanam || 33 ||
Padaccheda
मैत्री-करुणा-मुदिता-उपेक्षाणाम् सुख-दुःख-पुण्य-अपुण्य-विषयाणाम् भावनातः चित्त-प्रसादनम्
maitrī-karuṇā-muditā-upekṣāṇām sukha-duḥkha-puṇya-apuṇya-viṣayāṇām bhāvanātaḥ citta-prasādanam
Word by word
The mind is made clear by cultivating friendliness towards the happy, compassion towards the suffering, gladness towards the virtuous and indifference towards the wicked.

Bhāṣya In one who cultivates these, bright dharma arises, and from it the mind becomes clear; clear, it becomes one-pointed and gains steadiness.

प्रच्छर्दनविधारणाभ्यां वा प्राणस्य ॥ ३४ ॥
pracchardanavidhāraṇābhyāṃ vā prāṇasya || 34 ||
Padaccheda
प्रच्छर्दन-विधारणाभ्याम् वा प्राणस्य
pracchardana-vidhāraṇābhyām vā prāṇasya
Word by word
Or by the expulsion and the retention of the breath.

Bhāṣya Expulsion is casting out the air of the chest through the nostrils with a particular effort; retention is the control of the breath. By these too one may bring about the steadiness of the mind.

विषयवती वा प्रवृत्तिरुत्पन्ना मनसः स्थितिनिबन्धनी ॥ ३५ ॥
viṣayavatī vā pravṛttir utpannā manasaḥ sthitinibandhanī || 35 ||
Padaccheda
विषयवती वा प्रवृत्तिः उत्पन्ना मनसः स्थिति-निबन्धनी
viṣayavatī vā pravṛttiḥ utpannā manasaḥ sthiti-nibandhanī
Word by word
Or a direct perception of a sense object, when it has arisen, holds the mind in steadiness.

Bhāṣya For one who fixes the mind on the tip of the nose there is an awareness of divine scent; on the tip of the tongue, of taste; on the palate, of colour; on the middle of the tongue, of touch; on its root, of sound. These hold the mind steady, blow away doubt and become a door to the wisdom of absorption. Though what is learnt from scripture, inference and a teacher is true, so long as no part of it is known by one's own faculties it all seems indirect and gives no firm conviction about subtle things such as liberation. When one part is directly seen, all the rest is believed; and then faith, energy, mindfulness and absorption come without hindrance.

विशोका वा ज्योतिष्मती ॥ ३६ ॥
viśokā vā jyotiṣmatī || 36 ||
Padaccheda
विशोका वा ज्योतिष्मती
viśokā vā jyotiṣmatī
Word by word
Or the sorrowless, luminous (perception).

Bhāṣya ‘a perception, when it has arisen, holds the mind in steadiness’ carries on. For one who fixes the mind in the lotus of the heart there is an awareness of the understanding, whose sattva is shining and like space; it takes the form of the light of sun, moon, planets and gems. Likewise the mind absorbed in the sense of ‘I am’ becomes calm and endless, like a great ocean without waves. These two are the sorrowless perception: the one with an object, and the one that is the sense of ‘I am’ alone.

वीतरागविषयं वा चित्तम् ॥ ३७ ॥
vītarāgaviṣayaṃ vā cittam || 37 ||
Padaccheda
वीत-राग-विषयम् वा चित्तम्
vīta-rāga-viṣayam vā cittam
Word by word
Or the mind that has for its object one free from passion.

Bhāṣya The yogin's mind, coloured by taking as its support the mind of one free from passion, gains steadiness.

स्वप्ननिद्राज्ञानालम्बनं वा ॥ ३८ ॥
svapnanidrājñānālambanaṃ vā || 38 ||
Padaccheda
स्वप्न-निद्रा-ज्ञान-आलम्बनम् वा
svapna-nidrā-jñāna-ālambanam vā
Word by word
Or (the mind) resting on the knowledge had in dream or in sleep.

Bhāṣya The yogin's mind, taking the form of the knowledge of dream or of sleep as its support, gains steadiness.

यथाभिमतध्यानाद्वा ॥ ३९ ॥
yathābhimatadhyānād vā || 39 ||
Padaccheda
यथा-अभिमत-ध्यानात् वा
yathā-abhimata-dhyānāt vā
Word by word
Or from meditation on whatever one wishes.

Bhāṣya Let him meditate on whatever he likes; a mind that has gained steadiness there gains it elsewhere too.

परमाणुपरममहत्त्वान्तोऽस्य वशीकारः ॥ ४० ॥
paramāṇuparamamahattvānto 'sya vaśīkāraḥ || 40 ||
Padaccheda
परमाणु-परममहत्त्व-अन्तः अस्य वशीकारः
paramāṇu-paramamahattva-antaḥ asya vaśīkāraḥ
Word by word
His mastery reaches from the smallest atom to the greatest magnitude.

Bhāṣya Entering the subtle, the mind gains steadiness down to the smallest atom; entering the gross, up to the greatest magnitude. That it is unhindered as it runs to both limits is the highest mastery. Filled with it, the yogin's mind needs no further training by practice.

क्षीणवृत्तेरभिजातस्येव मणेर्ग्रहीतृग्रहणग्राह्येषु तत्स्थतदञ्जनता समापत्तिः ॥ ४१ ॥
kṣīṇavṛtter abhijātasyeva maṇer grahītṛgrahaṇagrāhyeṣu tatsthatadañjanatā samāpattiḥ || 41 ||
Padaccheda
क्षीण-वृत्तेः अभिजातस्य इव मणेः ग्रहीतृ-ग्रहण-ग्राह्येषु तत्-स्थ-तत्-अञ्जनता समापत्तिः
kṣīṇa-vṛtteḥ abhijātasya iva maṇeḥ grahītṛ-grahaṇa-grāhyeṣu tat-stha-tat-añjanatā samāpattiḥ
Word by word
For the mind whose activities have dwindled, like a flawless gem, coalescence is its resting on the grasper, the grasping or the grasped and taking the colour of that.

Bhāṣya ‘whose activities have dwindled’: whose cognitions have set. As a crystal, coloured by whatever it rests on, shines with the form of that, so the mind coloured by the object grasped shines with the form of that object: a subtle element, a gross thing, or the variety of the universe. So too with the organs, the means of grasping; and with the Puruṣa as grasper, and the freed Puruṣa. The grasper, grasping and grasped are the Puruṣa, the senses and the elements; the mind's standing in them and taking their form is called coalescence.

तत्र शब्दार्थज्ञानविकल्पैः संकीर्णा सवितर्का समापत्तिः ॥ ४२ ॥
tatra śabdārthajñānavikalpaiḥ saṃkīrṇā savitarkā samāpattiḥ || 42 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र शब्द-अर्थ-ज्ञान-विकल्पैः संकीर्णा सवितर्का समापत्तिः
tatra śabda-artha-jñāna-vikalpaiḥ saṃkīrṇā savitarkā samāpattiḥ
Word by word
Of these, coalescence with gross thought is that mixed with the constructions of word, thing and knowledge.

Bhāṣya ‘cow’ the word, ‘cow’ the thing and ‘cow’ the knowledge are grasped as one though they are distinct; analysed, the properties of the word, of the thing and of the knowledge go separate ways. When the thing that has risen in the yogin's wisdom of absorption comes pierced by these constructions, the coalescence is mixed, and is called ‘with gross thought’.

स्मृतिपरिशुद्धौ स्वरूपशून्येवार्थमात्रनिर्भासा निर्वितर्का ॥ ४३ ॥
smṛtipariśuddhau svarūpaśūnyevārthamātranirbhāsā nirvitarkā || 43 ||
Padaccheda
स्मृति-परिशुद्धौ स्वरूप-शून्या इव अर्थ-मात्र-निर्भासा निर्वितर्का
smṛti-pariśuddhau svarūpa-śūnyā iva artha-mātra-nirbhāsā nirvitarkā
Word by word
When memory is purified, (the coalescence) that is as if empty of its own form and shines as the object alone is without gross thought.

Bhāṣya When memory is cleared of the conventions of words and of the constructions of knowledge from hearing and inference, the wisdom, coloured by the form of the object, as if gives up its own form as a grasping and becomes the object's form alone. That is the higher perception, the seed of scripture and inference. Its object is the whole (avayavin), such as a cow or a pot: a single thing, grasped by one cognition, that arises as a property common to the subtle elements. He argues against those who deny such a whole: without it nearly all knowledge would be false, and right knowledge would have no object.

एतयैव सविचारा निर्विचारा च सूक्ष्मविषया व्याख्याता ॥ ४४ ॥
etayaiva savicārā nirvicārā ca sūkṣmaviṣayā vyākhyātā || 44 ||
Padaccheda
एतया एव सविचारा निर्विचारा च सूक्ष्म-विषया व्याख्याता
etayā eva savicārā nirvicārā ca sūkṣma-viṣayā vyākhyātā
Word by word
By this same account are explained the coalescences with and without subtle thought, which have subtle objects.

Bhāṣya Coalescence in the subtle elements whose properties are manifest, limited by the experience of place, time and cause, is ‘with subtle thought’. That in them as unlimited by properties past, present or yet unnamed, and as the ground of all properties, is ‘without subtle thought’: the wisdom is then as if empty of its own form, the object alone. Those with and without gross thought have a gross object; these two a subtle one.

सूक्ष्मविषयत्वं चालिङ्गपर्यवसानम् ॥ ४५ ॥
sūkṣmaviṣayatvaṃ cāliṅgaparyavasānam || 45 ||
Padaccheda
सूक्ष्म-विषयत्वम् च अलिङ्ग-पर्यवसानम्
sūkṣma-viṣayatvam ca aliṅga-paryavasānam
Word by word
And the range of subtle objects ends in the unmarked.

Bhāṣya For the atom of earth the subtle object is the subtle element of smell; for water, of taste; for fire, of colour; for air, of touch; for space, of sound. Subtler than these is the sense of ‘I’; subtler than that, the mere mark (liṅga-mātra, mahat); subtler than that, the unmarked (aliṅga, Prakṛti). Nothing is subtler than the unmarked. The Puruṣa is subtle too, but not in this line: he is not the material cause of the mark, only its occasion.

ता एव सबीजः समाधिः ॥ ४६ ॥
tā eva sabījaḥ samādhiḥ || 46 ||
Padaccheda
ताः एव सबीजः समाधिः
tāḥ eva sabījaḥ samādhiḥ
Word by word
These are meditative absorption with seed.

Bhāṣya The four coalescences have an outer thing for their seed, so the absorption too is ‘with seed’: with and without gross thought on a gross object, with and without subtle thought on a subtle one.

निर्विचारवैशारद्येऽध्यात्मप्रसादः ॥ ४७ ॥
nirvicāravaiśāradye 'dhyātmaprasādaḥ || 47 ||
Padaccheda
निर्विचार-वैशारद्ये अध्यात्म-प्रसादः
nirvicāra-vaiśāradye adhyātma-prasādaḥ
Word by word
In the clearness of the absorption without subtle thought there is the inner clarity.

Bhāṣya Clearness is the pure, steady flow of the sattva of the understanding, freed of the dirt that covers it and not overpowered by rajas and tamas. Then the yogin has the inner clarity: the light of wisdom, plain, whose object is the thing as it is, and which does not follow a sequence. As it is said: ‘Having climbed to the clarity of wisdom, the wise man, himself beyond grief, looks on all grieving people as one on a hill looks on those on the plain.’

ऋतंभरा तत्र प्रज्ञा ॥ ४८ ॥
ṛtaṃbharā tatra prajñā || 48 ||
Padaccheda
ऋतंभरा तत्र प्रज्ञा
ṛtaṃbharā tatra prajñā
Word by word
There wisdom is truth-bearing.

Bhāṣya The wisdom born in the absorbed mind has this name, and it fits: it bears truth alone, with not a trace of error.

श्रुतानुमानप्रज्ञाभ्यामन्यविषया विशेषार्थत्वात् ॥ ४९ ॥
śrutānumānaprajñābhyām anyaviṣayā viśeṣārthatvāt || 49 ||
Padaccheda
श्रुत-अनुमान-प्रज्ञाभ्याम् अन्य-विषया विशेष-अर्थत्वात्
śruta-anumāna-prajñābhyām anya-viṣayā viśeṣa-arthatvāt
Word by word
Its object is different from that of the wisdom of scripture and of inference, because its object is the particular.

Bhāṣya Scripture has the general for its object, since a word is not tied by convention to a particular; inference too ends in the general. A subtle, hidden or distant thing is not grasped by ordinary perception either. Yet the particular exists; it is grasped only by the wisdom of absorption, whether it belongs to a subtle element or to the Puruṣa.

तज्जः संस्कारोऽन्यसंस्कारप्रतिबन्धी ॥ ५० ॥
tajjaḥ saṃskāro 'nyasaṃskārapratibandhī || 50 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-जः संस्कारः अन्य-संस्कार-प्रतिबन्धी
tat-jaḥ saṃskāraḥ anya-saṃskāra-pratibandhī
Word by word
The latent impression born of that obstructs other impressions.

Bhāṣya The impression born of the wisdom of absorption checks the store of impressions of the outward-turned mind; cognitions born of those then do not arise, and absorption sets in. From it comes wisdom, from wisdom new impressions, and so on. These do not give the mind a further task, for they cause the afflictions to waste away: the mind's striving ends in discernment.

तस्यापि निरोधे सर्वनिरोधान्निर्बीजः समाधिः ॥ ५१ ॥
tasyāpi nirodhe sarvanirodhān nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ || 51 ||
Padaccheda
तस्य अपि निरोधे सर्व-निरोधात् निर्बीजः समाधिः
tasya api nirodhe sarva-nirodhāt nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ
Word by word
On the cessation of that also, since all has ceased, there is seedless meditative absorption.

Bhāṣya The impression born of cessation checks even the impressions born of absorption. The mind, with the impressions that lead to soleness, dissolves into its own source, Prakṛti, its task ended. When it has ceased, the Puruṣa rests in his own nature alone, and so is called pure, alone (kevala) and free.

साधनपादः
Sādhanapādaḥ · Pāda 2 · On the means
तपःस्वाध्यायेश्वरप्रणिधानानि क्रियायोगः ॥ १ ॥
tapaḥsvādhyāyeśvarapraṇidhānāni kriyāyogaḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
तपः-स्वाध्याय-ईश्वरप्रणिधानानि क्रिया-योगः
tapaḥ-svādhyāya-īśvarapraṇidhānāni kriyā-yogaḥ
Word by word
Austerity, recitation and devotion to the Lord are the yoga of action.

Bhāṣya The first pāda taught yoga for one whose mind is absorbed; this begins how one whose mind is turned outwards may also come to yoga. Yoga does not succeed for one without austerity: the impurity, coloured by beginningless action and affliction, is not broken up without it; but it is to be practised so as not to disturb the clearness of the mind. Recitation is the repetition of Om and other purifying formulas, or the study of the scriptures of liberation. Devotion to the Lord is offering all actions to the supreme teacher, or renouncing their fruit.

समाधिभावनार्थः क्लेशतनूकरणार्थश्च ॥ २ ॥
samādhibhāvanārthaḥ kleśatanūkaraṇārthaś ca || 2 ||
Padaccheda
समाधि-भावना-अर्थः क्लेश-तनूकरण-अर्थः च
samādhi-bhāvanā-arthaḥ kleśa-tanūkaraṇa-arthaḥ ca
Word by word
It is for bringing about meditative absorption and for thinning the afflictions.

Bhāṣya The afflictions, once thinned, will be made barren like burnt seeds by the fire of reflection. Then the subtle wisdom, the discernment that sattva and the Puruṣa are different, untouched by the afflictions and its task finished, will be fit to return to its source.

अविद्यास्मितारागद्वेषाभिनिवेशाः क्लेशाः ॥ ३ ॥
avidyāsmitārāgadveṣābhiniveśāḥ kleśāḥ || 3 ||
Padaccheda
अविद्या-अस्मिता-राग-द्वेष-अभिनिवेशाः क्लेशाः
avidyā-asmitā-rāga-dveṣa-abhiniveśāḥ kleśāḥ
Word by word
The afflictions are ignorance, the sense of ‘I am’, attachment, aversion and clinging to life.

Bhāṣya The afflictions are the five errors. When active they strengthen the working of the guṇas, keep change going, swell the stream of cause and effect, and, helping one another, bring about the ripening of action.

अविद्या क्षेत्रमुत्तरेषां प्रसुप्ततनुविच्छिन्नोदाराणाम् ॥ ४ ॥
avidyā kṣetram uttareṣāṃ prasuptatanuvicchinnodārāṇām || 4 ||
Padaccheda
अविद्या क्षेत्रम् उत्तरेषाम् प्रसुप्त-तनु-विच्छिन्न-उदाराणाम्
avidyā kṣetram uttareṣām prasupta-tanu-vicchinna-udārāṇām
Word by word
Ignorance is the field of the others, whether they are dormant, thinned, interrupted or active.

Bhāṣya Ignorance is the field, the breeding ground, of the other four. Dormant: resting in the mind as a mere power, in the state of a seed; it wakes when its object comes before it. For one who has reflection the seed of the afflictions is burnt, and it does not wake even when the object is before him: he is called one whose afflictions are destroyed, skilled, in his last body. This burnt-seed state is a fifth state of the afflictions and is found in him alone. Thinned: weakened by cultivating the opposite. Interrupted: appearing again and again with breaks, as anger is not seen while attachment is at work; and Caitra, attached to one woman, is not thereby averse to the others: there his attachment is at work, elsewhere it is yet to come. Active: at work on its object.

Bhāṣya None of the four ceases to be an affliction; the names mark only the state each is in: turned back by cultivating the opposite, or brought out by what manifests it. All of them are kinds of ignorance, for ignorance runs through them all: they follow whatever ignorance gives shape to, they are found while the mistaken notion lasts, and they waste away as ignorance wastes away.

अनित्याशुचिदुःखानात्मसु नित्यशुचिसुखात्मख्यातिरविद्या ॥ ५ ॥
anityāśuciduḥkhānātmasu nityaśucisukhātmakhyātir avidyā || 5 ||
Padaccheda
अनित्य-अशुचि-दुःख-अनात्मसु नित्य-शुचि-सुख-आत्म-ख्यातिः अविद्या
anitya-aśuci-duḥkha-anātmasu nitya-śuci-sukha-ātma-khyātiḥ avidyā
Word by word
Ignorance is seeing the permanent, the pure, the pleasant and the self in what is impermanent, impure, painful and not the self.

Bhāṣya The permanent in the impermanent: ‘the earth is lasting, the gods are deathless’. The pure in the impure: the body, which the wise know to be impure from its place of origin, its seed, its sustenance, its discharges and its end; yet a girl is thought lovely as the new moon. The pleasant in the painful (2.15). The self in what is not the self: outer things, the body, or the mind. This fourfold ignorance is the root of the stream of afflictions and of the store of action. It is not the mere absence of knowledge but another knowledge, contrary to knowledge, as amitra is not ‘no friend’ but an enemy.

दृग्दर्शनशक्त्योरेकात्मतेवास्मिता ॥ ६ ॥
dṛgdarśanaśaktyor ekātmatevāsmitā || 6 ||
Padaccheda
दृक्-दर्शन-शक्त्योः एक-आत्मता इव अस्मिता
dṛk-darśana-śaktyoḥ eka-ātmatā iva asmitā
Word by word
The sense of ‘I am’ is the power of the seer and the power of seeing becoming, as it were, one self.

Bhāṣya The Puruṣa is the power that sees, the understanding the power by which seeing is done; their seeming to take one form is the affliction. Experience arises when the powers of the experiencer and the experienced, wholly distinct and unmixed, come as if to be undivided; when each is known in its own nature there is only soleness, and no experience.

सुखानुशयी रागः ॥ ७ ॥
sukhānuśayī rāgaḥ || 7 ||
Padaccheda
सुख-अनुशयी रागः
sukha-anuśayī rāgaḥ
Word by word
Attachment is that which follows on pleasure.

Bhāṣya The greed, thirst and craving for pleasure or its means, in one who has known pleasure and remembers it.

दुःखानुशयी द्वेषः ॥ ८ ॥
duḥkhānuśayī dveṣaḥ || 8 ||
Padaccheda
दुःख-अनुशयी द्वेषः
duḥkha-anuśayī dveṣaḥ
Word by word
Aversion is that which follows on pain.

Bhāṣya The resistance, anger and wish to strike at pain or its means, in one who has known pain and remembers it.

स्वरसवाही विदुषोऽपि तथा रूढोऽभिनिवेशः ॥ ९ ॥
svarasavāhī viduṣo 'pi tathā rūḍho 'bhiniveśaḥ || 9 ||
Padaccheda
स्व-रस-वाही विदुषः अपि तथा रूढः अभिनिवेशः
sva-rasa-vāhī viduṣaḥ api tathā rūḍhaḥ abhiniveśaḥ
Word by word
Clinging to life flows on by its own force, and is rooted likewise even in the learned.

Bhāṣya Every living being has this constant wish for itself: ‘may I not cease to be; may I be’. It could not arise in one who had not known death, so it shows the experience of earlier births. This fear of death is seen even in a worm just born, where perception, inference and scripture cannot account for it. As it is found in the wholly dull, so it is rooted in the learned who know the beginning and the end, for the latent trace of the pain of death is the same in both.

ते प्रतिप्रसवहेयाः सूक्ष्माः ॥ १० ॥
te pratiprasavaheyāḥ sūkṣmāḥ || 10 ||
Padaccheda
ते प्रतिप्रसव-हेयाः सूक्ष्माः
te pratiprasava-heyāḥ sūkṣmāḥ
Word by word
When subtle, they are to be given up by the return (of the mind) to its source.

Bhāṣya The five afflictions, become like burnt seeds, pass away together with the yogin's mind when, its task done, it dissolves.

ध्यानहेयास्तद्वृत्तयः ॥ ११ ॥
dhyānaheyās tadvṛttayaḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
ध्यान-हेयाः तत्-वृत्तयः
dhyāna-heyāḥ tat-vṛttayaḥ
Word by word
Their activities are to be given up by meditation.

Bhāṣya The gross activities of the afflictions, thinned by the yoga of action, are to be given up by the meditation of reflection until they are made subtle, like burnt seeds. As the gross dirt of a cloth is shaken off first and the fine dirt removed afterwards with effort, so the gross activities have a slight opponent and the subtle a great one.

क्लेशमूलः कर्माशयो दृष्टादृष्टजन्मवेदनीयः ॥ १२ ॥
kleśamūlaḥ karmāśayo dṛṣṭādṛṣṭajanmavedanīyaḥ || 12 ||
Padaccheda
क्लेश-मूलः कर्म-आशयः दृष्ट-अदृष्ट-जन्म-वेदनीयः
kleśa-mūlaḥ karma-āśayaḥ dṛṣṭa-adṛṣṭa-janma-vedanīyaḥ
Word by word
The store of action, rooted in the afflictions, is to be experienced in the present birth or in one unseen.

Bhāṣya The store of merit and demerit is born of desire, greed, delusion and anger. Merit gained with keen ardour by mantra, austerity and absorption, or by worship of the Lord, the gods and great seers, ripens at once; so does the evil of repeated harm done, under strong affliction, to the frightened, the sick and the wretched, or to those who trust one. Thus the boy Nandīśvara left the human state and became a god, and Nahuṣa, lord of the gods, became an animal.

सति मूले तद्विपाको जात्यायुर्भोगाः ॥ १३ ॥
sati mūle tadvipāko jātyāyurbhogāḥ || 13 ||
Padaccheda
सति मूले तत्-विपाकः जाति-आयुः-भोगाः
sati mūle tat-vipākaḥ jāti-āyuḥ-bhogāḥ
Word by word
While the root is there, its ripening is birth, span of life and experience.

Bhāṣya The store of action begins to ripen while the afflictions are there, not when that root is cut: rice grains sprout while they are in the husk and unburnt, not when husked or burnt. The ripening is of three kinds, birth, span of life and experience.

Bhāṣya Vyāsa asks whether one action causes one birth or many, and whether many actions cause many births or one. Not one action one birth: with the countless actions heaped up from time without beginning and those of the present, there would be no rule for the order of their fruits, and people would lose all confidence. Not one action many births: there would be no time left for the rest to ripen. Not many actions many births: the births cannot be together, and if they come in turn the first fault returns. So the varied store of merit and demerit made between birth and death, with some actions chief and others subordinate, is brought out by death, rolls into one, brings death about and makes one birth; that birth has its span of life from the same action, and in that span its experience. The store is therefore said to belong to one life and to have three ripenings.

Bhāṣya Action to be experienced in the present birth begins one ripening, experience, or two, span of life and experience, as with Nandīśvara and Nahuṣa. The latent tendencies are another matter: made by the experience of afflictions, action and ripening, they have filled the mind from time without beginning, as a fishing net is covered all over with knots; they come from many lives. They are the latent impressions that cause memory.

Bhāṣya The rule of one life holds for the store whose ripening is fixed and which is to be experienced in the next birth. Action to be experienced in an unseen birth, whose ripening is not fixed, has three courses. It may perish unripened, as dark action perishes here itself when bright action arises. It may be merged in a chief action: a slight admixture of evil does not suffice to take away the good, and makes only a small deduction even in heaven. Or it may stay for a long time, overpowered by a chief action whose ripening is fixed, until a like action that manifests it turns it towards ripening. Because the place, time and occasion of that ripening cannot be determined, the course of action is varied and hard to know. An exception does not set aside the general rule: the store of action belongs to one life.

ते ह्लादपरितापफलाः पुण्यापुण्यहेतुत्वात् ॥ १४ ॥
te hlādaparitāpaphalāḥ puṇyāpuṇyahetutvāt || 14 ||
Padaccheda
ते ह्लाद-परिताप-फलाः पुण्य-अपुण्य-हेतुत्वात्
te hlāda-paritāpa-phalāḥ puṇya-apuṇya-hetutvāt
Word by word
They have joy and distress as their fruit, because they are caused by merit and demerit.

Bhāṣya Birth, span of life and experience bear pleasure when caused by merit and pain when caused by demerit. And as this pain is disagreeable, so for the yogin there is pain, disagreeable, even at the time of pleasure from objects.

परिणामतापसंस्कारदुःखैर्गुणवृत्तिविरोधाच्च दुःखमेव सर्वं विवेकिनः ॥ १५ ॥
pariṇāmatāpasaṃskāraduḥkhair guṇavṛttivirodhāc ca duḥkham eva sarvaṃ vivekinaḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
परिणाम-ताप-संस्कार-दुःखैः गुण-वृत्ति-विरोधात् च दुःखम् एव सर्वम् विवेकिनः
pariṇāma-tāpa-saṃskāra-duḥkhaiḥ guṇa-vṛtti-virodhāt ca duḥkham eva sarvam vivekinaḥ
Word by word
Because of the pains of change, of distress and of latent impressions, and because the workings of the guṇas conflict, all is only pain for the discerning.

Bhāṣya The pain of change: pleasure is shot through with attachment, so it leaves a store of action; and the senses cannot be freed of thirst by enjoyment, which only makes craving and the senses' skill grow. He who seeks pleasure is like one who, fearing a scorpion's sting, is bitten by a snake. The pain of distress: distress is shot through with aversion; seeking the means of pleasure one acts with body, speech and mind, helps some and harms others, and heaps up dharma and adharma. The pain of impressions: from pleasure and pain come their latent impressions, and from experience a new store of action. This beginningless stream of pain troubles only the yogin, who is as sensitive as the eyeball, which a thread of wool hurts though it does not hurt other limbs.

Bhāṣya Pleasure in objects has been called ignorance. The stilling of the senses when they are satisfied is pleasure; their restlessness from greed is pain. The other man takes up again and again the pain his own action brings and drops it again and again; shot through on every side with ignorance by a mind varied with latent tendencies that have no beginning, following ‘I’ and ‘mine’ where they should be given up, he is flooded birth after birth by the three kinds of distress, from outer causes, inner causes and both. Seeing himself and all living beings carried along by this stream of pain, the yogin takes refuge in right seeing, the cause of the ending of all pain.

Bhāṣya The guṇas of the understanding, illumination, activity and inertia, help one another to produce a calm, a violent or a dull cognition; their working is unsteady. As medical science has four parts, disease, its cause, health and the remedy, so has this teaching: the cycle of birth and death, its cause, liberation, and the means to liberation. The cycle, full of pain, is what is to be avoided; the conjunction of Prakṛti and the Puruṣa is its cause; the final ceasing of that conjunction is the avoidance; and right seeing is the means.

Bhāṣya The guṇas in their excess of form and of working conflict with one another, while in their ordinary measure they work together; each, leaning on the others, gives rise to cognitions of pleasure, pain and delusion, so that all have all forms, the difference lying in which is chief. Hence all is pain for the discerning. The seed from which this great mass of pain grows is ignorance, and right seeing is the cause of its ceasing. The own nature of the one who avoids can be neither something to be taken up nor something to be avoided: if it were avoided, the doctrine of annihilation would follow; if taken up, the doctrine that it has a cause; with both set aside, the doctrine of the eternal. That is right seeing.

हेयं दुःखमनागतम् ॥ १६ ॥
heyaṃ duḥkham anāgatam || 16 ||
Padaccheda
हेयम् दुःखम् अनागतम्
heyam duḥkham anāgatam
Word by word
The pain that has not yet come is what is to be avoided.

Bhāṣya Past pain has been lived through and present pain is being experienced in its own moment; only pain that is yet to come can be avoided.

द्रष्टृदृश्ययोः संयोगो हेयहेतुः ॥ १७ ॥
draṣṭṛdṛśyayoḥ saṃyogo heyahetuḥ || 17 ||
Padaccheda
द्रष्टृ-दृश्ययोः संयोगः हेय-हेतुः
draṣṭṛ-dṛśyayoḥ saṃyogaḥ heya-hetuḥ
Word by word
The conjunction of the seer and the seen is the cause of what is to be avoided.

Bhāṣya The seer is the Puruṣa, who is aware of the understanding; the seen is all the properties that rest on the sattva of the understanding. The seen, like a magnet, serves merely by being near, and belongs to the Puruṣa as its owner. Their beginningless conjunction, made for a purpose, is the cause of pain. As one who knows the sole of the foot that can be pierced, the thorn that pierces, and the remedy of not stepping on it or wearing a shoe, escapes that pain, so here: rajas is what burns and sattva what is burnt, not the changeless knower of the field; but when sattva is burnt, the Puruṣa, following its form, seems to burn too.

प्रकाशक्रियास्थितिशीलं भूतेन्द्रियात्मकं भोगापवर्गार्थं दृश्यम् ॥ १८ ॥
prakāśakriyāsthitiśīlaṃ bhūtendriyātmakaṃ bhogāpavargārthaṃ dṛśyam || 18 ||
Padaccheda
प्रकाश-क्रिया-स्थिति-शीलम् भूत-इन्द्रिय-आत्मकम् भोग-अपवर्ग-अर्थम् दृश्यम्
prakāśa-kriyā-sthiti-śīlam bhūta-indriya-ātmakam bhoga-apavarga-artham dṛśyam
Word by word
The seen has illumination, activity and inertia as its nature, consists of the elements and the senses, and exists for experience and liberation.

Bhāṣya Sattva has illumination as its nature, rajas activity, tamas inertia. These guṇas colour one another, change, join and part, and take shape by leaning on one another, yet their powers stay unmixed; they serve by mere nearness, like a magnet, for the Puruṣa's purpose. They are what the word pradhāna means, and are called the seen. It changes into the elements, earth and the rest, subtle and gross, and into the senses, hearing and the rest, subtle and gross.

Bhāṣya Experience is the ascertaining of the guṇas as wished or unwished, without division (from the Puruṣa); liberation is the ascertaining of the experiencer's own nature. Both are made by the understanding and are in it alone; they are ascribed to the Puruṣa as victory or defeat, which is the soldiers', is ascribed to their lord, for he enjoys its fruit. Bondage is the understanding's not having finished the Puruṣa's purpose; release is the finishing of it.

Bhāṣya Apart from these two there is no other seeing. In the same way grasping, retaining, reasoning for and against, knowledge of the truth and resolve are in the understanding, and their existence is superimposed on the Puruṣa, for he enjoys their fruit.

विशेषाविशेषलिङ्गमात्रालिङ्गानि गुणपर्वाणि ॥ १९ ॥
viśeṣāviśeṣaliṅgamātrāliṅgāni guṇaparvāṇi || 19 ||
Padaccheda
विशेष-अविशेष-लिङ्गमात्र-अलिङ्गानि गुण-पर्वाणि
viśeṣa-aviśeṣa-liṅgamātra-aliṅgāni guṇa-parvāṇi
Word by word
The stages of the guṇas are the particular, the unparticular, the mere mark and the unmarked.

Bhāṣya The particulars are sixteen: the five elements, space, air, fire, water and earth; the five senses of knowledge; the five organs of action; and the mind, the eleventh, which serves all. The unparticulars are six: the five subtle elements of sound, touch, colour, taste and smell, and the bare sense of ‘I am’. These six are changes of the great one (mahat), which is bare existence, the mere mark. Beyond it is the unmarked, pradhāna, the unmanifest, neither existent nor non-existent, into which they return.

Bhāṣya The Puruṣa's purpose is not the cause of the unmarked state, so it is called eternal; of the other three states it is the cause, so they are called non-eternal. The guṇas themselves neither perish nor arise; they only seem to, through their manifestations, as Devadatta is said to grow poor because his cows die, not because he himself is lessened. The particulars change into no further principle.

Bhāṣya Sound and the other subtle elements have one, two, three, four and five characteristics in turn. The mere mark lies next to the unmarked; mixed in it, it is distinguished out of it, since the order of change is not overstepped; so the six unparticulars are distinguished out of the mere mark, and the elements and senses out of the unparticulars. The changes that the particulars do have, of property, temporal character and condition, are explained later (3.13).

द्रष्टा दृशिमात्रः शुद्धोऽपि प्रत्ययानुपश्यः ॥ २० ॥
draṣṭā dṛśimātraḥ śuddho 'pi pratyayānupaśyaḥ || 20 ||
Padaccheda
द्रष्टा दृशि-मात्रः शुद्धः अपि प्रत्यय-अनुपश्यः
draṣṭā dṛśi-mātraḥ śuddhaḥ api pratyaya-anupaśyaḥ
Word by word
The seer is seeing alone; though pure, he looks on by way of the cognition.

Bhāṣya ‘seeing alone’: the power of seeing itself, untouched by any qualification. This Puruṣa is aware of the understanding. He is not like it, and not wholly unlike it. Not like it: the understanding changes, since its object is now known and now unknown, while the Puruṣa's object is always known, which shows that he does not change; the understanding works by combining and so serves another, while the Puruṣa is for himself; it is of the three guṇas and so not conscious, while he is the onlooker of the guṇas. Not wholly unlike it: though pure, he looks on the cognition that belongs to the understanding, and so appears as if of its nature though he is not.

तदर्थ एव दृश्यस्यात्मा ॥ २१ ॥
tadartha eva dṛśyasyātmā || 21 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-अर्थः एव दृश्यस्य आत्मा
tat-arthaḥ eva dṛśyasya ātmā
Word by word
The being of the seen is only for his sake.

Bhāṣya The seen has become the object of the Puruṣa, whose form is seeing, so its being, that is, its own nature, is for his sake alone. When experience and liberation are accomplished it is no longer seen by the Puruṣa; having lost its nature it should perish, yet it does not (2.22).

कृतार्थं प्रति नष्टमप्यनष्टं तदन्यसाधारणत्वात् ॥ २२ ॥
kṛtārthaṃ prati naṣṭam apy anaṣṭaṃ tadanyasādhāraṇatvāt || 22 ||
Padaccheda
कृत-अर्थम् प्रति नष्टम् अपि अनष्टम् तत्-अन्य-साधारणत्वात्
kṛta-artham prati naṣṭam api anaṣṭam tat-anya-sādhāraṇatvāt
Word by word
Though it has ceased for one whose purpose is accomplished, it has not ceased, because it is common to others than him.

Bhāṣya The seen has ceased for the one skilled Puruṣa, but not for the unskilled, for whom its purpose is not accomplished. So, the powers of seer and seen being eternal, their conjunction is said to be without beginning.

स्वस्वामिशक्त्योः स्वरूपोपलब्धिहेतुः संयोगः ॥ २३ ॥
svasvāmiśaktyoḥ svarūpopalabdhihetuḥ saṃyogaḥ || 23 ||
Padaccheda
स्व-स्वामि-शक्त्योः स्वरूप-उपलब्धि-हेतुः संयोगः
sva-svāmi-śaktyoḥ svarūpa-upalabdhi-hetuḥ saṃyogaḥ
Word by word
The conjunction is the cause of the apprehension of the own nature of the two powers, the owned and the owner.

Bhāṣya The Puruṣa, the owner, is joined with the seen, his own, in order to see. The apprehension of the seen from that conjunction is experience; the apprehension of the seer's own nature is liberation. The conjunction lasts until seeing is done, so seeing is called the cause of disjunction, and non-seeing, its opposite, the cause of conjunction. Seeing is not here itself the cause of release: when non-seeing is absent, bondage is absent, and that is release.

Bhāṣya When seeing is there, non-seeing, the cause of bondage, is destroyed; in that sense seeing, that is knowledge, is called the cause of soleness. What then is this non-seeing? He sets out eight alternatives found in the teaching. (1) The guṇas' having a task still to do. (2) The non-arising of the chief mind, which shows objects to its owner, whose nature is seeing: the absence of seeing while his own seen is there. (3) The guṇas' having a purpose. (4) Ignorance, held back together with its own mind, as the seed of that mind's arising. (5) The coming out of the impression of motion when the impression of rest is spent: for pradhāna, if it only rested, would make no modification, and if it only moved, modification would be eternal; in neither case would it be pradhāna. (6) The power of seeing itself, since pradhāna acts in order to make itself known. (7) A property of both, the seen and the Puruṣa. (8) Seeing, knowledge itself, is the non-seeing, as some say.

Bhāṣya This multitude of alternatives concerns the conjunction of the guṇas with all Puruṣas in common. The conjunction of the inward consciousness with its own understanding is taken up in the next sūtra.

तस्य हेतुरविद्या ॥ २४ ॥
tasya hetur avidyā || 24 ||
Padaccheda
तस्य हेतुः अविद्या
tasya hetuḥ avidyā
Word by word
The cause of that is ignorance.

Bhāṣya ‘of that’: of the conjunction of the inner consciousness with its own understanding. Ignorance here is the latent tendency of false knowledge. An understanding steeped in it does not reach the end of its task, the seeing of the Puruṣa, and comes back with its task still to do; when it ends in the seeing of the Puruṣa, its task done and non-seeing gone, there is no cause of bondage and it does not come back.

तदभावात्संयोगाभावो हानं तद्दृशेः कैवल्यम् ॥ २५ ॥
tadabhāvāt saṃyogābhāvo hānaṃ taddṛśeḥ kaivalyam || 25 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-अभावात् संयोग-अभावः हानम् तत् दृशेः कैवल्यम्
tat-abhāvāt saṃyoga-abhāvaḥ hānam tat dṛśeḥ kaivalyam
Word by word
From the absence of that, the absence of conjunction is the avoidance; that is the soleness of the seer.

Bhāṣya When non-seeing is absent, the conjunction of the understanding and the Puruṣa is absent: the final ceasing of bondage. This is the avoidance. The soleness of the seer is the Puruṣa's being unmixed, never again joined with the guṇas. Then the Puruṣa rests in his own nature.

विवेकख्यातिरविप्लवा हानोपायः ॥ २६ ॥
vivekakhyātir aviplavā hānopāyaḥ || 26 ||
Padaccheda
विवेक-ख्यातिः अविप्लवा हान-उपायः
viveka-khyātiḥ aviplavā hāna-upāyaḥ
Word by word
The unwavering vision of discernment is the means of avoidance.

Bhāṣya The vision of discernment is the cognition that sattva and the Puruṣa are different. While false knowledge has not ceased it wavers. When false knowledge has become like a burnt seed, unable to sprout, the stream of discerning cognition runs clear in a sattva washed of the dust of the afflictions. This is the path to release.

तस्य सप्तधा प्रान्तभूमिः प्रज्ञा ॥ २७ ॥
tasya saptadhā prāntabhūmiḥ prajñā || 27 ||
Padaccheda
तस्य सप्तधा प्रान्त-भूमिः प्रज्ञा
tasya saptadhā prānta-bhūmiḥ prajñā
Word by word
His wisdom, at its final stage, is sevenfold.

Bhāṣya ‘his’: of the one in whom this vision has arisen. Four are the freedom of wisdom from its task: what is to be avoided is fully known, and nothing remains to be known; the causes of what is to be avoided have wasted away, and nothing remains to be wasted; the avoidance has been made present by the absorption of cessation; the means of avoidance, the vision of discernment, has been cultivated. Three are the freedom of the mind: the understanding has done its task; the guṇas, like stones fallen from a mountain peak, with nowhere to rest, go towards dissolution in their cause and set with it, not to arise again; and in that state the Puruṣa, beyond all tie with the guṇas, is the light of his own nature alone, stainless and sole.

योगाङ्गानुष्ठानादशुद्धिक्षये ज्ञानदीप्तिरा विवेकख्यातेः ॥ २८ ॥
yogāṅgānuṣṭhānād aśuddhikṣaye jñānadīptir ā vivekakhyāteḥ || 28 ||
Padaccheda
योग-अङ्ग-अनुष्ठानात् अशुद्धि-क्षये ज्ञान-दीप्तिः आ विवेक-ख्यातेः
yoga-aṅga-anuṣṭhānāt aśuddhi-kṣaye jñāna-dīptiḥ ā viveka-khyāteḥ
Word by word
From the practice of the limbs of yoga, as impurity wastes away, the light of knowledge grows, up to the vision of discernment.

Bhāṣya The impurity is the fivefold error. As the means are practised it thins, and as it wastes the light of knowledge grows by the same steps, up to the vision of discernment, that is, the knowing of the nature of the guṇas and the Puruṣa. The practice of the limbs is the cause of separation from impurity, as an axe is of what is cut, and the cause of attaining discernment, as dharma is of happiness. He then lists the nine kinds of cause known to the teaching: of arising, staying, manifestation, change, cognition, attainment, separation, difference and support.

यमनियमासनप्राणायामप्रत्याहारधारणाध्यानसमाधयोऽष्टावङ्गानि ॥ २९ ॥
yamaniyamāsanaprāṇāyāmapratyāhāradhāraṇādhyānasamādhayo 'ṣṭāv aṅgāni || 29 ||
Padaccheda
यम-नियम-आसन-प्राणायाम-प्रत्याहार-धारणा-ध्यान-समाधयः अष्टौ अङ्गानि
yama-niyama-āsana-prāṇāyāma-pratyāhāra-dhāraṇā-dhyāna-samādhayaḥ aṣṭau aṅgāni
Word by word
The eight limbs are the restraints, the observances, posture, control of the breath, withdrawal of the senses, fixing of the mind, meditation and meditative absorption.
अहिंसासत्यास्तेयब्रह्मचर्यापरिग्रहा यमाः ॥ ३० ॥
ahiṃsāsatyāsteyabrahmacaryāparigrahā yamāḥ || 30 ||
Padaccheda
अहिंसा-सत्य-अस्तेय-ब्रह्मचर्य-अपरिग्रहाः यमाः
ahiṃsā-satya-asteya-brahmacarya-aparigrahāḥ yamāḥ
Word by word
The restraints are non-harming, truthfulness, non-stealing, continence and non-possession.

Bhāṣya Non-harming is doing no injury to any being, in any way, at any time. The other restraints and observances are rooted in it and are taken up only to make it spotless. Truthfulness is speech and mind that agree with the fact, as seen, inferred or heard; speech is uttered to pass one's knowledge to another, and must not deceive, mislead or be empty. It is meant for the good of all beings: if what is said harms beings it is not truth but evil, so let one speak the truth after weighing the good of all beings. Stealing is taking others' goods against the teaching; non-stealing is its refusal, in the form of not coveting. Continence is the control of the hidden organ of generation. Non-possession is not taking objects to oneself, seeing the faults of getting, guarding, losing, clinging to and harming for them.

जातिदेशकालसमयानवच्छिन्नाः सार्वभौमा महाव्रतम् ॥ ३१ ॥
jātideśakālasamayānavacchinnāḥ sārvabhaumā mahāvratam || 31 ||
Padaccheda
जाति-देश-काल-समय-अनवच्छिन्नाः सार्वभौमाः महा-व्रतम्
jāti-deśa-kāla-samaya-anavacchinnāḥ sārvabhaumāḥ mahā-vratam
Word by word
Not limited by kind, place, time or occasion, and holding at every level, they are the great vow.

Bhāṣya Non-harming limited by kind: the fisherman harms fish only. By place: ‘I will not kill at a holy place’. By time: ‘not on the fourteenth day, not on a holy day’. By occasion: ‘I will kill only for the gods and brahmins’, or as warriors do harm only in battle. Kept without these limits, in every state and towards every object, with no exception, they are the great vow.

शौचसंतोषतपःस्वाध्यायेश्वरप्रणिधानानि नियमाः ॥ ३२ ॥
śaucasaṃtoṣatapaḥsvādhyāyeśvarapraṇidhānāni niyamāḥ || 32 ||
Padaccheda
शौच-संतोष-तपः-स्वाध्याय-ईश्वरप्रणिधानानि नियमाः
śauca-saṃtoṣa-tapaḥ-svādhyāya-īśvarapraṇidhānāni niyamāḥ
Word by word
The observances are purity, contentment, austerity, recitation and devotion to the Lord.

Bhāṣya Outer purity is that got by earth, water and the like, and by eating pure food; inner purity is the washing away of the stains of the mind. Contentment is not wishing for more than the means at hand. Austerity is bearing the pairs: hunger and thirst, cold and heat, standing and sitting, and the two kinds of silence; and the vows such as the kṛcchra and cāndrāyaṇa. Recitation is the study of the scriptures of liberation, or the repetition of Om. Devotion to the Lord is offering all actions to that supreme teacher.

वितर्कबाधने प्रतिपक्षभावनम् ॥ ३३ ॥
vitarkabādhane pratipakṣabhāvanam || 33 ||
Padaccheda
वितर्क-बाधने प्रतिपक्ष-भावनम्
vitarka-bādhane pratipakṣa-bhāvanam
Word by word
When contrary thoughts oppress, the opposite is to be cultivated.

Bhāṣya When thoughts of harming and the like arise, ‘I will kill him who wronged me, I will lie, I will take his goods’, let him reflect: ‘Burning on the fierce coals of the cycle of birth and death, I took refuge in the dharma of yoga by giving safety to all beings. If I take up again what I gave up, I am like a dog that licks its own vomit.’

वितर्का हिंसादयः कृतकारितानुमोदिता लोभक्रोधमोहपूर्वका मृदुमध्याधिमात्रा दुःखाज्ञानानन्तफला इति प्रतिपक्षभावनम् ॥ ३४ ॥
vitarkā hiṃsādayaḥ kṛtakāritānumoditā lobhakrodhamohapūrvakā mṛdumadhyādhimātrā duḥkhājñānānantaphalā iti pratipakṣabhāvanam || 34 ||
Padaccheda
वितर्काः हिंसा-आदयः कृत-कारित-अनुमोदिताः लोभ-क्रोध-मोह-पूर्वकाः मृदु-मध्य-अधिमात्राः दुःख-अज्ञान-अनन्त-फलाः इति प्रतिपक्ष-भावनम्
vitarkāḥ hiṃsā-ādayaḥ kṛta-kārita-anumoditāḥ lobha-krodha-moha-pūrvakāḥ mṛdu-madhya-adhimātrāḥ duḥkha-ajñāna-ananta-phalāḥ iti pratipakṣa-bhāvanam
Word by word
Contrary thoughts, harming and the rest, whether done, caused to be done or approved, preceded by greed, anger or delusion, mild, moderate or intense, have pain and ignorance as their endless fruit: such is the cultivation of the opposite.

Bhāṣya Harming is of three kinds, done, caused and approved; each from greed (for meat or hide), from anger (‘he wronged me’) or from delusion (‘it will be my dharma’); each of these mild, moderate or intense: twenty-seven kinds; and each of those again threefold: eighty-one. So too with falsehood and the rest. He who harms first takes away his victim's strength, then gives him pain with a weapon, then takes his life; for this his own means of living lose their strength, he suffers pain in hell and as an animal or a ghost, and lives wishing for death at every moment. Reflecting thus on the unwished ripening that follows such thoughts, let him not set his mind on them.

अहिंसाप्रतिष्ठायां तत्संनिधौ वैरत्यागः ॥ ३५ ॥
ahiṃsāpratiṣṭhāyāṃ tatsaṃnidhau vairatyāgaḥ || 35 ||
Padaccheda
अहिंसा-प्रतिष्ठायाम् तत्-संनिधौ वैर-त्यागः
ahiṃsā-pratiṣṭhāyām tat-saṃnidhau vaira-tyāgaḥ
Word by word
When non-harming is firmly established, enmity is given up in his presence.

Bhāṣya By all living beings.

सत्यप्रतिष्ठायां क्रियाफलाश्रयत्वम् ॥ ३६ ॥
satyapratiṣṭhāyāṃ kriyāphalāśrayatvam || 36 ||
Padaccheda
सत्य-प्रतिष्ठायाम् क्रिया-फल-आश्रयत्वम्
satya-pratiṣṭhāyām kriyā-phala-āśrayatvam
Word by word
When truthfulness is firmly established, action and its fruit rest on him.

Bhāṣya If he says ‘be a man of dharma’, the man becomes one; if he says ‘attain heaven’, he attains heaven. His speech is unfailing.

अस्तेयप्रतिष्ठायां सर्वरत्नोपस्थानम् ॥ ३७ ॥
asteyapratiṣṭhāyāṃ sarvaratnopasthānam || 37 ||
Padaccheda
अस्तेय-प्रतिष्ठायाम् सर्व-रत्न-उपस्थानम्
asteya-pratiṣṭhāyām sarva-ratna-upasthānam
Word by word
When non-stealing is firmly established, all jewels come to him.

Bhāṣya Jewels from every quarter come to him.

ब्रह्मचर्यप्रतिष्ठायां वीर्यलाभः ॥ ३८ ॥
brahmacaryapratiṣṭhāyāṃ vīryalābhaḥ || 38 ||
Padaccheda
ब्रह्मचर्य-प्रतिष्ठायाम् वीर्य-लाभः
brahmacarya-pratiṣṭhāyām vīrya-lābhaḥ
Word by word
When continence is firmly established, vigour is gained.

Bhāṣya By gaining it he heightens his powers unhindered; and, perfected, he is able to plant knowledge in his pupils.

अपरिग्रहस्थैर्ये जन्मकथंतासंबोधः ॥ ३९ ॥
aparigrahasthairye janmakathaṃtāsaṃbodhaḥ || 39 ||
Padaccheda
अपरिग्रह-स्थैर्ये जन्म-कथंता-संबोधः
aparigraha-sthairye janma-kathaṃtā-saṃbodhaḥ
Word by word
When non-possession is steady, there is full knowledge of the how of his births.

Bhāṣya ‘Who was I? How was I? What is this, and how? What shall we be, and how?’ The wish to know his own state in the past, the future and the present is fulfilled of itself. These are the perfections that come when the restraints are steady.

शौचात्स्वाङ्गजुगुप्सा परैरसंसर्गः ॥ ४० ॥
śaucāt svāṅgajugupsā parair asaṃsargaḥ || 40 ||
Padaccheda
शौचात् स्व-अङ्ग-जुगुप्सा परैः असंसर्गः
śaucāt sva-aṅga-jugupsā paraiḥ asaṃsargaḥ
Word by word
From purity come distaste for one's own body and non-contact with others.

Bhāṣya He who practises purity sees the faults of the body and does not cling to it. Seeing the body's nature, wishing to leave even his own, and finding it not clean though he washes it with earth and water, how should he join with the wholly unclean bodies of others?

सत्त्वशुद्धिसौमनस्यैकाग्र्येन्द्रियजयात्मदर्शनयोग्यत्वानि च ॥ ४१ ॥
sattvaśuddhisaumanasyaikāgryendriyajayātmadarśanayogyatvāni ca || 41 ||
Padaccheda
सत्त्वशुद्धि-सौमनस्य-ऐकाग्र्य-इन्द्रियजय-आत्मदर्शनयोग्यत्वानि च
sattvaśuddhi-saumanasya-aikāgrya-indriyajaya-ātmadarśanayogyatvāni ca
Word by word
And (there come) purity of sattva, gladness, one-pointedness, mastery of the senses and fitness to see the self.

Bhāṣya ‘there come’ completes the sentence. In the pure there is purity of sattva; from it gladness, from that one-pointedness, from that mastery of the senses, and from that the fitness of the sattva of the understanding to see the self.

संतोषादनुत्तमः सुखलाभः ॥ ४२ ॥
saṃtoṣād anuttamaḥ sukhalābhaḥ || 42 ||
Padaccheda
संतोषात् अनुत्तमः सुख-लाभः
saṃtoṣāt anuttamaḥ sukha-lābhaḥ
Word by word
From contentment, unsurpassed happiness is gained.

Bhāṣya As it is said: ‘The pleasure of desire in this world and the great pleasure of heaven are not worth a sixteenth part of the happiness of the wasting away of thirst.’

कायेन्द्रियसिद्धिरशुद्धिक्षयात्तपसः ॥ ४३ ॥
kāyendriyasiddhir aśuddhikṣayāt tapasaḥ || 43 ||
Padaccheda
काय-इन्द्रिय-सिद्धिः अशुद्धि-क्षयात् तपसः
kāya-indriya-siddhiḥ aśuddhi-kṣayāt tapasaḥ
Word by word
From austerity, through the wasting away of impurity, comes the perfection of the body and the senses.

Bhāṣya Austerity, as it is carried out, destroys the stain of impurity that covers. When that is gone there is perfection of the body, minuteness and the rest, and of the senses, hearing and seeing from afar and the like.

स्वाध्यायादिष्टदेवतासंप्रयोगः ॥ ४४ ॥
svādhyāyād iṣṭadevatāsaṃprayogaḥ || 44 ||
Padaccheda
स्वाध्यायात् इष्टदेवता-संप्रयोगः
svādhyāyāt iṣṭadevatā-saṃprayogaḥ
Word by word
From recitation comes communion with the chosen deity.

Bhāṣya Gods, seers and perfected ones come into the sight of one given to recitation, and serve his work.

समाधिसिद्धिरीश्वरप्रणिधानात् ॥ ४५ ॥
samādhisiddhir īśvarapraṇidhānāt || 45 ||
Padaccheda
समाधि-सिद्धिः ईश्वर-प्रणिधानात्
samādhi-siddhiḥ īśvara-praṇidhānāt
Word by word
From devotion to the Lord comes the perfection of absorption.

Bhāṣya For one who has offered his whole being to the Lord there is perfection of absorption, by which he knows all that he wishes to know, truly, in another place, another body and another time. Then his wisdom knows the thing as it is.

स्थिरसुखमासनम् ॥ ४६ ॥
sthirasukham āsanam || 46 ||
Padaccheda
स्थिर-सुखम् आसनम्
sthira-sukham āsanam
Word by word
Posture is steady and easy.

Bhāṣya Such as the lotus posture, the auspicious posture, the svastika, the staff posture, the one with a support, the couch, the seats of the curlew, the elephant and the camel, the even pose, the steady and easy, and the one as is comfortable.

प्रयत्नशैथिल्यानन्तसमापत्तिभ्याम् ॥ ४७ ॥
prayatnaśaithilyānantasamāpattibhyām || 47 ||
Padaccheda
प्रयत्न-शैथिल्य-अनन्त-समापत्तिभ्याम्
prayatna-śaithilya-ananta-samāpattibhyām
Word by word
(It comes) by the slackening of effort and by coalescence with the infinite.

Bhāṣya ‘it comes’ completes the sentence. Posture is perfected when effort ceases, so that the limbs do not tremble; or the mind, coalesced with the infinite, brings posture about.

ततो द्वन्द्वानभिघातः ॥ ४८ ॥
tato dvandvānabhighātaḥ || 48 ||
Padaccheda
ततः द्वन्द्व-अनभिघातः
tataḥ dvandva-anabhighātaḥ
Word by word
From that, one is not struck by the pairs of opposites.

Bhāṣya Having mastered posture, he is not overcome by pairs such as cold and heat.

तस्मिन्सति श्वासप्रश्वासयोर्गतिविच्छेदः प्राणायामः ॥ ४९ ॥
tasmin sati śvāsapraśvāsayor gativicchedaḥ prāṇāyāmaḥ || 49 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मिन् सति श्वास-प्रश्वासयोः गति-विच्छेदः प्राणायामः
tasmin sati śvāsa-praśvāsayoḥ gati-vicchedaḥ prāṇāyāmaḥ
Word by word
That being so, control of the breath is the cutting off of the movement of the in-breath and the out-breath.

Bhāṣya When posture is mastered. The in-breath is the drinking in of the outer air; the out-breath is the sending out of the air within. The cutting off of their movement is the absence of both.

बाह्याभ्यन्तरस्तम्भवृत्तिर्देशकालसंख्याभिः परिदृष्टो दीर्घसूक्ष्मः ॥ ५० ॥
bāhyābhyantarastambhavṛttir deśakālasaṃkhyābhiḥ paridṛṣṭo dīrghasūkṣmaḥ || 50 ||
Padaccheda
बाह्य-आभ्यन्तर-स्तम्भ-वृत्तिः देश-काल-संख्याभिः परिदृष्टः दीर्घ-सूक्ष्मः
bāhya-ābhyantara-stambha-vṛttiḥ deśa-kāla-saṃkhyābhiḥ paridṛṣṭaḥ dīrgha-sūkṣmaḥ
Word by word
Its working is outer, inner or arrested; measured by place, time and number, it becomes long and subtle.

Bhāṣya Outer: where the absence of movement follows the out-breath. Inner: where it follows the in-breath. The third, arrested: where both are absent by a single effort, as water dropped on a heated stone shrinks from every side. All three are measured by place (‘so far is its range’), by time (the number of moments), and by number (so many in-breaths and out-breaths to the first stroke, so many to the second, to the third; so mild, moderate, intense). Practised thus it becomes long and subtle.

बाह्याभ्यन्तरविषयाक्षेपी चतुर्थः ॥ ५१ ॥
bāhyābhyantaraviṣayākṣepī caturthaḥ || 51 ||
Padaccheda
बाह्य-आभ्यन्तर-विषय-आक्षेपी चतुर्थः
bāhya-ābhyantara-viṣaya-ākṣepī caturthaḥ
Word by word
The fourth passes beyond the outer and the inner range.

Bhāṣya The outer range, measured by place, time and number, is passed beyond; so too the inner. The absence of movement of both that follows this, coming by degrees as each stage is won, is the fourth. The third is an absence of movement begun at once, without regard to range; the fourth comes after the ranges of in-breath and out-breath are ascertained and passed beyond: that is the difference.

ततः क्षीयते प्रकाशावरणम् ॥ ५२ ॥
tataḥ kṣīyate prakāśāvaraṇam || 52 ||
Padaccheda
ततः क्षीयते प्रकाश-आवरणम्
tataḥ kṣīyate prakāśa-āvaraṇam
Word by word
From that, the covering of the light is worn away.

Bhāṣya For the yogin who practises control of the breath, the action that covers discerning knowledge wastes away. That action, which veils the light and binds to the cycle of birth and death, grows weak and wastes at every moment. As it is said: ‘There is no austerity higher than control of the breath; from it come the cleansing of stains and the light of knowledge.’

धारणासु च योग्यता मनसः ॥ ५३ ॥
dhāraṇāsu ca yogyatā manasaḥ || 53 ||
Padaccheda
धारणासु च योग्यता मनसः
dhāraṇāsu ca yogyatā manasaḥ
Word by word
And the mind becomes fit for the fixings of the mind.

Bhāṣya From the practice of control of the breath alone, by the saying ‘or by the breathing out and the holding of the breath’ (1.34).

स्वविषयासंप्रयोगे चित्तस्वरूपानुकार इवेन्द्रियाणां प्रत्याहारः ॥ ५४ ॥
svaviṣayāsaṃprayoge cittasvarūpānukāra ivendriyāṇāṃ pratyāhāraḥ || 54 ||
Padaccheda
स्व-विषय-असंप्रयोगे चित्त-स्वरूप-अनुकारः इव इन्द्रियाणाम् प्रत्याहारः

Word by word
Withdrawal is the senses' following, as it were, the mind's own nature when they are not joined to their own objects.

Bhāṣya When the mind has ceased, the senses have ceased like it; they need no other means, as the mastery of each sense otherwise would. As bees fly up after the king bee when he flies up and settle when he settles, so the senses cease when the mind ceases.

ततः परमा वश्यतेन्द्रियाणाम् ॥ ५५ ॥

Word by word
From that comes the highest mastery of the senses.

Bhāṣya Some say mastery of the senses is not being addicted to sound and the other objects; others, contact with them that is not forbidden, or at one's own will; others, knowing them without pleasure or pain, attachment and aversion being absent. Fixing of the mind is the binding of the mind to a place.

Bhāṣya In places such as the circle of the navel, the lotus of the heart, the light in the head, the tip of the nose or the tip of the tongue, or on an outer object; the binding is by the mind's activity alone.

Meditation is the single flow of cognition there.

The three together, on one object, are constraint.

Bhāṣya Fixing, meditation and absorption, the three means, when they have one object. ‘Constraint’ (saṃyama) is the technical name of this teaching for the three.

तज्जयात्प्रज्ञालोकः ॥ ५ ॥

From the mastery of that comes the light of wisdom.

Bhāṣya As constraint becomes firm-footed, the wisdom of absorption becomes clear.

तस्य भूमिषु विनियोगः ॥ ६ ॥

Padaccheda
तस्य भूमिषु विनियोगः
tasya bhūmiṣu viniyogaḥ
Word by word
It is applied to the stages.

Bhāṣya When one stage is mastered, it is applied to the next. One who has not mastered a lower stage cannot leap the next and gain constraint on the last stages; and without that, whence the light of wisdom? But one who by the Lord's grace has mastered a higher stage need not apply it to lower ones, such as knowing another's mind. As to which stage comes next, yoga itself is the teacher: ‘Yoga is to be known by yoga; yoga proceeds from yoga; he who is not careless in yoga delights long in yoga.’

The three are inner limbs compared with the earlier ones.

Bhāṣya Inner limbs of absorption with cognition, compared with the five means beginning with the restraints.

Even that is an outer limb of the seedless (absorption).

Bhāṣya Of the seedless yoga; because that comes about when these three are absent.

व्युत्थाननिरोधसंस्कारयोरभिभवप्रादुर्भावौ निरोधक्षणचित्तान्वयो निरोधपरिणामः ॥ ९ ॥
vyutthānanirodhasaṃskārayor abhibhavaprādurbhāvau nirodhakṣaṇacittānvayo nirodhapariṇāmaḥ || 9 ||
Padaccheda
व्युत्थान-निरोध-संस्कारयोः अभिभव-प्रादुर्भावौ निरोध-क्षण-चित्त-अन्वयः निरोध-परिणामः
vyutthāna-nirodha-saṃskārayoḥ abhibhava-prādurbhāvau nirodha-kṣaṇa-citta-anvayaḥ nirodha-pariṇāmaḥ
Word by word
The overpowering of the latent impressions of emergence and the appearance of those of cessation, the mind being joined to the moment of cessation, is the change of cessation.

Bhāṣya The impressions of emergence are properties of the mind; they are not cognitions, so they do not cease when cognition ceases. The impressions of cessation are properties of the mind too. The first are removed and the second laid down. This alteration of impressions in the one mind, moment by moment, is the change of cessation; the mind then has only impressions left.

तस्य प्रशान्तवाहिता संस्कारात् ॥ १० ॥
tasya praśāntavāhitā saṃskārāt || 10 ||
Padaccheda
तस्य प्रशान्त-वाहिता संस्कारात्
tasya praśānta-vāhitā saṃskārāt
Word by word
Its calm flow comes from the latent impression.

Bhāṣya The mind's calm flow depends on skill in the practice of the impression of cessation. When that impression is weak, it is overpowered by the impression of emergence.

सर्वार्थतैकाग्रतयोः क्षयोदयौ चित्तस्य समाधिपरिणामः ॥ ११ ॥
sarvārthataikāgratayoḥ kṣayodayau cittasya samādhipariṇāmaḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
सर्वार्थता-एकाग्रतयोः क्षय-उदयौ चित्तस्य समाधि-परिणामः
sarvārthatā-ekāgratayoḥ kṣaya-udayau cittasya samādhi-pariṇāmaḥ
Word by word
The dwindling of dispersal over all objects and the rise of one-pointedness is the mind's change of absorption.

Bhāṣya Both are properties of the mind. The dwindling of the one is its disappearance, the rise of the other its appearance; the mind runs through both as their bearer.

ततः पुनः शान्तोदितौ तुल्यप्रत्ययौ चित्तस्यैकाग्रतापरिणामः ॥ १२ ॥
tataḥ punaḥ śāntoditau tulyapratyayau cittasyaikāgratāpariṇāmaḥ || 12 ||
Padaccheda
ततः पुनः शान्त-उदितौ तुल्य-प्रत्ययौ चित्तस्य एकाग्रता-परिणामः
tataḥ punaḥ śānta-uditau tulya-pratyayau cittasya ekāgratā-pariṇāmaḥ
Word by word
Then again, when the cognition that has quieted and the one that has risen are alike, that is the mind's change of one-pointedness.

Bhāṣya In the absorbed mind the earlier cognition quiets and the later, like it, rises; the mind in absorption runs through both, and so on again until absorption breaks.

एतेन भूतेन्द्रियेषु धर्मलक्षणावस्थापरिणामा व्याख्याताः ॥ १३ ॥
etena bhūtendriyeṣu dharmalakṣaṇāvasthāpariṇāmā vyākhyātāḥ || 13 ||
Padaccheda
एतेन भूत-इन्द्रियेषु धर्म-लक्षण-अवस्था-परिणामाः व्याख्याताः
etena bhūta-indriyeṣu dharma-lakṣaṇa-avasthā-pariṇāmāḥ vyākhyātāḥ
Word by word
By this are explained the changes of property, of temporal character and of condition in the elements and the senses.

Bhāṣya In the mind, the overpowering of emergence and the appearance of cessation is change of property in the bearer. Change of temporal character: cessation has three characters, joined to the three paths of time; leaving the path not yet come, without ceasing to be a property, it takes the present character, where it is manifest in its own form, and is not cut off from the past and the future ones. Change of condition: in the moments of cessation its impressions are strong and those of emergence weak. So the bearer changes by properties, the properties by temporal characters, and these by conditions; the working of the guṇas does not stand for a moment without them.

Bhāṣya In truth there is one change only, for a property is nothing but the bearer's own nature, and it is the bearer's alteration that is set out by way of the property. Only the state alters, not the substance: when a gold vessel is broken and remade it is not the gold that becomes other. Another says that the bearer is nothing beyond the properties, for if it ran through them, passing unaltered through earlier and later states, it would be immovably eternal. That is no fault, because nothing absolute is held: the three worlds pass out of manifestation, since eternity is denied; having passed out they still exist, since destruction is denied; merged, they are subtle, and being subtle they are not perceived.

Note Vyāsa, on the objection that if every property has all three temporal characters the times are mixed: a property is present with the present character and is not cut off from the other two, as a man attached to one woman is not averse to the rest. It is not only at the present moment that a property is a property, or the mind would not have attachment as a property while anger is at work. The three characters cannot be manifest together in one manifestation; they come in turn, as what manifests each appears. The bearer does not have the three times; the properties do, and as they reach this or that condition they are spoken of as other because of the condition, not because of the substance: as one stroke is a hundred in the hundreds' place, ten in the tens' and one in the units', and as one woman is called mother, daughter and sister.

Note Vyāsa, on the objection that with change of condition everything would be immovably eternal, a property being future while it does not do its work, present while it does and past when it has done it: though the bearer of the qualities is eternal, the qualities crush one another in many ways. As a shape, which has a beginning and perishes, is a mere property of sound and the rest, which do not perish, so the mark (liṅga), which has a beginning and perishes, is a mere property of sattva and the other guṇas, which do not; ‘modification’ is the name for it. His example: clay, the bearer, passes from the form of a lump to that of a pot (property); the pot-form leaves the future character for the present (temporal character); the pot is new, then old, moment by moment (condition). So one change of the substance is shown in three ways. Change is the arising of another property in an abiding substance when an earlier property ceases.

शान्तोदिताव्यपदेश्यधर्मानुपाती धर्मी ॥ १४ ॥
śāntoditāvyapadeśyadharmānupātī dharmī || 14 ||
Padaccheda
शान्त-उदित-अव्यपदेश्य-धर्म-अनुपाती धर्मी
śānta-udita-avyapadeśya-dharma-anupātī dharmī
Word by word
The bearer of properties is that which runs through the properties quieted, risen and not yet named.

Bhāṣya A property is the bearer's own power, marked off by its fitness (for an effect). The quieted are those that have done their work and stopped (past); the risen are those at work (present); the unnamed are those to come, for everything has the nature of everything, though all do not show at once, being tied to place, time, form and cause. The bearer runs through these manifest and unmanifest properties. For one who holds that there are only properties, with nothing running through them, there could be no experience, since one cognition would not enjoy the action done by another, and no memory; and things are recognised as the same. So there is a lasting bearer.

क्रमान्यत्वं परिणामान्यत्वे हेतुः ॥ १५ ॥
kramānyatvaṃ pariṇāmānyatve hetuḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
क्रम-अन्यत्वम् परिणाम-अन्यत्वे हेतुः
krama-anyatvam pariṇāma-anyatve hetuḥ
Word by word
Difference of sequence is the cause of difference of change.

Bhāṣya Lest it be thought that one bearer has only one change. Powdered clay, clay as a lump, as a pot, as shards, as grains: that is a sequence; the property that comes next after a property is its sequence. There is such a sequence for property, for temporal character (future to present, present to past; the past has none), and for condition, as a new pot is seen to be old in the end. The mind's properties are of two kinds, seen (cognitions) and unseen, the latter seven, known by inference: cessation, dharma, latent impressions, change, life, movement and power.

परिणामत्रयसंयमादतीतानागतज्ञानम् ॥ १६ ॥
pariṇāmatrayasaṃyamād atītānāgatajñānam || 16 ||
Padaccheda
परिणाम-त्रय-संयमात् अतीत-अनागत-ज्ञानम्
pariṇāma-traya-saṃyamāt atīta-anāgata-jñānam
Word by word
From constraint on the three changes comes knowledge of the past and the future.

Bhāṣya On the changes of property, of temporal character and of condition. Seen directly by constraint, the three changes bring knowledge of what is past and to come in them.

शब्दार्थप्रत्ययानामितरेतराध्यासात्संकरस्तत्प्रविभागसंयमात्सर्वभूतरुतज्ञानम् ॥ १७ ॥
śabdārthapratyayānām itaretarādhyāsāt saṃkaras tatpravibhāgasaṃyamāt sarvabhūtarutajñānam || 17 ||
Padaccheda
शब्द-अर्थ-प्रत्ययानाम् इतरेतर-अध्यासात् संकरः तत्-प्रविभाग-संयमात् सर्व-भूत-रुत-ज्ञानम्
śabda-artha-pratyayānām itaretara-adhyāsāt saṃkaraḥ tat-pravibhāga-saṃyamāt sarva-bhūta-ruta-jñānam
Word by word
Word, thing and cognition are mixed because each is superimposed on the other; from constraint on their distinction comes knowledge of the cries of all beings.

Bhāṣya Speech has its purpose in the sounds of letters only, and hearing has for its object only a change of sound; the word is grasped by the understanding that gathers the sounds up at the end. The letters cannot exist together, and no one of them is the word; yet, limited by the convention of a meaning, ‘g’, ‘au’ and the visarga light up the thing that has a dewlap. The single appearance in the understanding of these, their sequence drawn together, is the word: one, without parts or sequence, mental. Convention is a superimposing of word and thing on each other, in the form of memory: ‘this word is this thing, this thing is this word’. So ‘cow’ the word, ‘cow’ the thing and ‘cow’ the knowledge are mixed. He who knows them apart knows all. The thing, changing by its own conditions, goes neither with the word nor with the cognition; so the word is one thing, the object another, the cognition another.

Bhāṣya Each letter by itself is not the word, yet each has the nature of a word and holds the power to express everything; taking on many forms as it is paired with other letters, the earlier is fixed to a particular sense by the later and the later by the earlier. The one word, the object of one cognition and thrown out by one effort, is brought up by the cognition of the last letter; hearers, whose understanding is steeped in the latent tendencies of the beginningless use of speech, take it as a settled thing.

Bhāṣya In every word there is the power of a sentence. When ‘tree’ is said, ‘is’ is understood, for a thing meant by a word never lacks existence; and there is no action without its means, so that ‘cooks’ implies all the factors, and naming agent, instrument and object (Caitra, fire, rice) only restricts them. A single word is also found in the sense of a sentence: śrotriya, ‘he recites the Veda’; jīvati, ‘he holds the breaths’. The sense of the word is manifest in the sentence, and the word is to be separated out and analysed as naming an action or a factor; otherwise forms such as bhavati, aśvaḥ and ajāpayaḥ, alike as noun and verb, could not be analysed. So in śvetate prāsādaḥ, ‘the palace shines white’, the word means an action, and in śvetaḥ prāsādaḥ, ‘the white palace’, a factor; the thing and the cognition have both in them, and the cognition has one form with the thing only through the tie ‘this is that’, which is the convention.

संस्कारसाक्षात्करणात्पूर्वजातिज्ञानम् ॥ १८ ॥
saṃskārasākṣātkaraṇāt pūrvajātijñānam || 18 ||
Padaccheda
संस्कार-साक्षात्करणात् पूर्व-जाति-ज्ञानम्
saṃskāra-sākṣātkaraṇāt pūrva-jāti-jñānam
Word by word
From the direct seeing of the latent impressions comes knowledge of former births.

Bhāṣya The impressions are of two kinds: latent tendencies, the causes of memory and of the afflictions; and dharma and adharma, the causes of ripening. Formed in earlier lives, they are unseen properties of the mind. They cannot be seen directly without the experience of their place, time and cause, so from seeing them comes knowledge of former births; and likewise of another's births.

Bhāṣya A story is told. The lord Jaigīṣavya, having seen his impressions, looked on the course of his births through ten great creations, and the knowledge born of discernment arose in him. Āvaṭya asked him which he had found the greater, pleasure or pain. He answered: ‘Whatever I experienced, born again and again among gods and men, I count it all as pain.’ ‘And this mastery over pradhāna, and the unsurpassed happiness of contentment?’ ‘It is unsurpassed beside the pleasure of objects; beside the happiness of soleness it is only pain.’

प्रत्ययस्य परचित्तज्ञानम् ॥ १९ ॥
pratyayasya paracittajñānam || 19 ||
Padaccheda
प्रत्ययस्य पर-चित्त-ज्ञानम्
pratyayasya para-citta-jñānam
Word by word
(From the direct seeing) of a cognition comes knowledge of another's mind.

Bhāṣya From constraint on a cognition, from seeing it directly.

न च तत्सालम्बनं तस्याविषयीभूतत्वात् ॥ २० ॥
na ca tat sālambanaṃ tasyāviṣayībhūtatvāt || 20 ||

But not that together with its object, because that has not been made the object (of the yogin's mind).

Bhāṣya He knows the cognition as coloured by attachment; he does not know on what object it is so coloured. The object of the other's cognition is not taken as object by the yogin's mind; only the other's cognition is.

कायरूपसंयमात्तद्ग्राह्यशक्तिस्तम्भे चक्षुःप्रकाशासंप्रयोगेऽन्तर्धानम् ॥ २१ ॥
kāyarūpasaṃyamāt tadgrāhyaśaktistambhe cakṣuḥprakāśāsaṃprayoge 'ntardhānam || 21 ||
Padaccheda
काय-रूप-संयमात् तत्-ग्राह्य-शक्ति-स्तम्भे चक्षुः-प्रकाश-असंप्रयोगे अन्तर्धानम्
kāya-rūpa-saṃyamāt tat-grāhya-śakti-stambhe cakṣuḥ-prakāśa-asaṃprayoge antardhānam
Word by word
From constraint on the form of the body, when its power of being grasped is arrested and it is not joined to the light of the eye, comes disappearance.

Bhāṣya By this the disappearance of sound and the rest is also to be understood.

सोपक्रमं निरुपक्रमं च कर्म तत्संयमादपरान्तज्ञानमरिष्टेभ्यो वा ॥ २२ ॥
sopakramaṃ nirupakramaṃ ca karma tatsaṃyamād aparāntajñānam ariṣṭebhyo vā || 22 ||
Padaccheda
सोपक्रमम् निरुपक्रमम् च कर्म तत्-संयमात् अपरान्त-ज्ञानम् अरिष्टेभ्यः वा
sopakramam nirupakramam ca karma tat-saṃyamāt aparānta-jñānam ariṣṭebhyaḥ vā
Word by word
Action is quick or slow in its course; from constraint on it, or from portents, comes knowledge of the end.

Bhāṣya The action that ripens as the span of life is of two kinds. As a wet cloth spread out dries quickly, or fire set to dry grass with wind on every side burns fast, so the quick; as the same cloth rolled up dries slowly, or fire put to a heap of grass part by part burns long, so the slow. From constraint on it comes knowledge of the end, that is, of death. Portents are of three kinds: of oneself (with ears closed he does not hear the sound in his body), of beings (he sees the men of Yama, or the departed ancestors), of the gods (he suddenly sees heaven or the perfected ones); or he sees everything reversed.

मैत्र्यादिषु बलानि ॥ २३ ॥
maitryādiṣu balāni || 23 ||
Padaccheda
मैत्री-आदिषु बलानि
maitrī-ādiṣu balāni
Word by word
(From constraint) on friendliness and the rest come strengths.

Bhāṣya Friendliness, compassion and gladness are the three cultivations (1.33). The absorption that comes from cultivating them is constraint, and from it come strengths of unfailing vigour. Towards the wicked there is indifference, which is not a cultivation; there is no absorption in it, and so no strength.

बलेषु हस्तिबलादीनि ॥ २४ ॥

Padaccheda
बलेषु हस्ति-बल-आदीनि
baleṣu hasti-bala-ādīni

These seven are all worlds of Brahmā.

The own nature: the general character of each: solidity is earth, moisture water, heat fire, motion air, going everywhere space. A substance is an aggregate of the general and the particular whose parts do not exist apart, as Patañjali says. The subtle: the subtle element, the cause of the element. The inherent: the guṇas, whose nature is illumination, activity and inertia. Purposefulness: their being for experience and liberation. He who has mastered the five forms masters the elements; their natures follow his intention as cows follow their calves.

Bhāṣya On what a substance is: of the general character the particulars, sound and the rest, are the differences; things of one class differ only by their properties. A group is of two kinds. In one the parts are not named apart: a body, a tree, a herd, a wood. In the other the word names the parts: ‘gods and men’, the gods one part of the group and men the other. It may be spoken of with or without the difference: ‘a wood of mangoes’ or ‘a mango-wood’. Again a group has parts that exist apart, as a wood or an assembly, or parts that do not, as a body, a tree or an atom; the second kind is a substance. The subtle form is the subtle element; one part of it is the atom, itself an aggregate of the general and the particular whose parts do not exist apart. The guṇas, which follow the nature of their effects, are the inherent; purposefulness inheres in the guṇas, and the guṇas in the subtle elements, the elements and what is made of them, so that everything has a purpose. From constraint on each form comes the sight of that form as it is, and mastery of it.

tadvairāgyād api doṣabījakṣaye kaivalyam || 50 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-वैराग्यात् अपि दोष-बीज-क्षये कैवल्यम्
tat-vairāgyāt api doṣa-bīja-kṣaye kaivalyam
Word by word
From dispassion even towards that, when the seeds of the faults waste away, there is soleness.

Bhāṣya When he sees that this cognition of discernment is a property of sattva, that sattva is among the things to be avoided, and that the Puruṣa is changeless, pure and other than sattva, he turns from it. The seeds of the afflictions, like burnt rice seeds, set together with the mind, and the Puruṣa no longer suffers the three kinds of distress. Soleness is the Puruṣa's final separation from the guṇas; then he is the power of consciousness alone, resting in his own nature.

स्थान्युपनिमन्त्रणे सङ्गस्मयाकरणं पुनरनिष्टप्रसङ्गात् ॥ ५१ ॥
sthānyupanimantraṇe saṅgasmayākaraṇaṃ punar aniṣṭaprasaṅgāt || 51 ||
Padaccheda
स्थानि-उपनिमन्त्रणे सङ्ग-स्मय-अकरणम् पुनः अनिष्ट-प्रसङ्गात्
sthāni-upanimantraṇe saṅga-smaya-akaraṇam punaḥ aniṣṭa-prasaṅgāt
Word by word
When invited by those in high places, he should not give way to attachment or pride, because the unwished would follow again.

Bhāṣya Yogins are of four kinds: the beginner, in whom the light has just begun; one at the honeyed stage, whose wisdom is truth-bearing; one of the light of wisdom, who has mastered the elements and the senses; and one gone beyond what is to be cultivated, for whom only the dissolving of the mind remains. The gods invite the one at the honeyed stage: ‘Sit here, enjoy here; here are pleasures, maidens, the elixir against age and death, a chariot of the sky, wish-granting trees.’ Let him reflect on the faults of attachment: ‘Burning on the coals of the cycle of birth and death, I have somehow found the lamp of yoga; these winds of sense objects are its enemies. How should I, having found the light, be cheated by this mirage?’ Nor should he feel pride that even the gods seek him; from pride he will think himself secure, carelessness will find its opening and rouse the afflictions.

Bhāṣya The third kind of yogin has made safe what he has already cultivated and has the means for what is still to be done; for the fourth, wisdom has seven final stages (2.27). The one who is invited should say, with his mind made up: ‘Fare you well, objects that are like dreams and are sought by pitiable men’, and cultivate absorption. Proud, he would not think of himself as seized by the hair by death. When he gives way to neither attachment nor pride, what he has cultivated grows firm and what is still to be cultivated comes before him.

क्षणतत्क्रमयोः संयमाद्विवेकजं ज्ञानम् ॥ ५२ ॥
kṣaṇatatkramayoḥ saṃyamād vivekajaṃ jñānam || 52 ||
Padaccheda
क्षण-तत्-क्रमयोः संयमात् विवेक-जम् ज्ञानम्
kṣaṇa-tat-kramayoḥ saṃyamāt viveka-jam jñānam
Word by word
From constraint on the moment and its sequence comes the knowledge born of discernment.

Bhāṣya As the atom is the smallest limit of substance, the moment is the smallest limit of time: the time in which a moving atom leaves one point and reaches the next. The unbroken flow of moments is their sequence. There is no real collection of moments; hours, days and nights are collections made by the understanding. Time, empty of reality, follows from the knowledge of words. Two moments do not exist together; only the present moment is. In that one moment the whole world undergoes change.

जातिलक्षणदेशैरन्यतानवच्छेदात्तुल्ययोस्ततः प्रतिपत्तिः ॥ ५३ ॥
jātilakṣaṇadeśair anyatānavacchedāt tulyayos tataḥ pratipattiḥ || 53 ||
Padaccheda
जाति-लक्षण-देशैः अन्यता-अनवच्छेदात् तुल्ययोः ततः प्रतिपत्तिः
jāti-lakṣaṇa-deśaiḥ anyatā-anavacchedāt tulyayoḥ tataḥ pratipattiḥ
Word by word
From that comes the ascertainment of two things that are alike, their difference not being determined by kind, mark or place.

Bhāṣya Difference is known by kind (a cow, a mare), by mark (the black-eyed cow, the lucky cow), or by place (two myrobalan fruits, one in front, one behind). But if, while the knower looks away, the one is put in the place of the other, they cannot be told apart by these. The knowledge born of discernment tells them apart: the place joined with the moment of the one differs from that of the other. So even of two atoms the yogin has the cognition of their difference.

तारकं सर्वविषयं सर्वथाविषयमक्रमं चेति विवेकजं ज्ञानम् ॥ ५४ ॥
tārakaṃ sarvaviṣayaṃ sarvathāviṣayam akramaṃ ceti vivekajaṃ jñānam || 54 ||
Padaccheda
तारकम् सर्व-विषयम् सर्वथा-विषयम् अक्रमम् च इति विवेक-जम् ज्ञानम्
tārakam sarva-viṣayam sarvathā-viṣayam akramam ca iti viveka-jam jñānam
Word by word
The knowledge born of discernment is the deliverer; it has all for its object, in every way, and is without sequence.

Bhāṣya ‘the deliverer’: it rises from one's own intuition, not from teaching. ‘All for its object’: nothing is outside it. ‘In every way’: it knows all, past, future and present, with all their aspects. ‘Without sequence’: it grasps all in a single moment.

सत्त्वपुरुषयोः शुद्धिसाम्ये कैवल्यमिति ॥ ५५ ॥
sattvapuruṣayoḥ śuddhisāmye kaivalyam iti || 55 ||
Padaccheda
सत्त्व-पुरुषयोः शुद्धि-साम्ये कैवल्यम् इति
sattva-puruṣayoḥ śuddhi-sāmye kaivalyam iti
Word by word
When the purity of sattva and of the Puruṣa is equal, there is soleness.

Bhāṣya When the sattva of the understanding, washed of rajas and tamas, its only task the cognition that the Puruṣa is other, the seeds of affliction burnt, has become as if like the Puruṣa in purity; and the Puruṣa's purity is the absence of the experience ascribed to him. In this state there is soleness, whether or not he has the powers and the knowledge born of discernment; for one whose seeds of affliction are burnt needs nothing further for knowledge. In truth non-seeing ceases through knowledge; then there are no afflictions, and so no ripening of action. The Puruṣa is then the light of his own nature alone, stainless and sole.

कैवल्यपादः
Kaivalyapādaḥ · Pāda 4 · On soleness
जन्मौषधिमन्त्रतपःसमाधिजाः सिद्धयः ॥ १ ॥
janmauṣadhimantratapaḥsamādhijāḥ siddhayaḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
जन्म-ओषधि-मन्त्र-तपः-समाधि-जाः सिद्धयः
janma-oṣadhi-mantra-tapaḥ-samādhi-jāḥ siddhayaḥ
Word by word
The perfections are born of birth, herbs, mantra, austerity or absorption.

Bhāṣya By birth: the perfection that belongs to another body. By herbs: by the elixir, in the dwellings of the asuras, and the like. By mantra: moving through space, minuteness and the rest. By austerity: the fulfilment of intention; he takes what form he wishes and goes where he wishes. Those born of absorption have been explained.

जात्यन्तरपरिणामः प्रकृत्यापूरात् ॥ २ ॥
jātyantarapariṇāmaḥ prakṛtyāpūrāt || 2 ||
Padaccheda
जाति-अन्तर-परिणामः प्रकृति-आपूरात्
jāti-antara-pariṇāmaḥ prakṛti-āpūrāt
Word by word
The change into another kind of birth comes from the filling in of the natures.

Bhāṣya When body and senses change into another kind, the earlier form passes and the later arises by the entering of parts not there before. The natures (prakṛti), the material causes of body and senses, favour each its own modification by filling in, depending on a cause such as dharma.

निमित्तमप्रयोजकं प्रकृतीनां वरणभेदस्तु ततः क्षेत्रिकवत् ॥ ३ ॥
nimittam aprayojakaṃ prakṛtīnāṃ varaṇabhedas tu tataḥ kṣetrikavat || 3 ||
Padaccheda
निमित्तम् अप्रयोजकम् प्रकृतीनाम् वरण-भेदः तु ततः क्षेत्रिक-वत्
nimittam aprayojakam prakṛtīnām varaṇa-bhedaḥ tu tataḥ kṣetrika-vat
Word by word
The occasioning cause does not set the natures in motion; from it comes only the breaking of the barrier, as with a farmer.

Bhāṣya A cause such as dharma does not move the natures, for a cause is not moved by its effect. As a farmer who wants to flood a field from one full of water does not carry the water with his hands but breaks the bank, and the water flows of itself; so dharma breaks adharma, the barrier of the natures, and they flow of themselves each into its own modification. Or as the farmer cannot push the sap into the roots of the rice but pulls out the weeds, and the sap enters of itself. So dharma is the cause only of the ceasing of adharma. Nandīśvara and others are examples; and in reverse, where adharma overcomes dharma, Nahuṣa the serpent and others.

निर्माणचित्तान्यस्मितामात्रात् ॥ ४ ॥
nirmāṇacittāny asmitāmātrāt || 4 ||
Padaccheda
निर्माण-चित्तानि अस्मिता-मात्रात्
nirmāṇa-cittāni asmitā-mātrāt
Word by word
Created minds come from the bare sense of ‘I am’.

Bhāṣya When the yogin creates many bodies, do they have one mind or many? Taking the bare sense of ‘I am’, the cause of mind, he makes created minds; so the bodies have minds.

प्रवृत्तिभेदे प्रयोजकं चित्तमेकमनेकेषाम् ॥ ५ ॥
pravṛttibhede prayojakaṃ cittam ekam anekeṣām || 5 ||
Padaccheda
प्रवृत्ति-भेदे प्रयोजकम् चित्तम् एकम् अनेकेषाम्
pravṛtti-bhede prayojakam cittam ekam anekeṣām
Word by word
In their different activities one mind is the mover of the many.

Bhāṣya How do many minds act according to the purpose of one? He creates one mind that moves them all.

तत्र ध्यानजमनाशयम् ॥ ६ ॥
tatra dhyānajam anāśayam || 6 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र ध्यान-जम् अनाशयम्
tatra dhyāna-jam anāśayam
Word by word
Of these, the one born of meditation is without a store.

Bhāṣya The created mind is of five kinds, as the perfections are. Only the mind born of meditation has no store, no activity of attachment and the like, and so no tie with merit and evil, for the yogin's afflictions have wasted away. The others have a store of action.

कर्माशुक्लाकृष्णं योगिनस्त्रिविधमितरेषाम् ॥ ७ ॥
karmāśuklākṛṣṇaṃ yoginas trividham itareṣām || 7 ||
Padaccheda
कर्म अशुक्ल-अकृष्णम् योगिनः त्रिविधम् इतरेषाम्
karma aśukla-akṛṣṇam yoginaḥ trividham itareṣām
Word by word
The yogin's action is neither white nor black; that of others is of three kinds.

Bhāṣya Action is of four classes. Black is that of the wicked. White-and-black is done by outer means, and its store is heaped up by hurting or helping others. White belongs to those given to austerity, recitation and meditation: it depends on the mind alone and hurts no one. Neither white nor black is that of renouncers whose afflictions have wasted away, in their last body: not white because the yogin renounces its fruit, not black because he does not take it up.

ततस्तद्विपाकानुगुणानामेवाभिव्यक्तिर्वासनानाम् ॥ ८ ॥
tatas tadvipākānuguṇānām evābhivyaktir vāsanānām || 8 ||
Padaccheda
ततः तत्-विपाक-अनुगुणानाम् एव अभिव्यक्तिः वासनानाम्
tataḥ tat-vipāka-anuguṇānām eva abhivyaktiḥ vāsanānām
Word by word
From that comes the manifestation of only those latent tendencies that accord with its ripening.

Bhāṣya From the threefold action. Action that ripens as a god's life cannot bring out the tendencies of a being in hell, an animal or a man, but only those that accord with a god's; and so for the others.

जातिदेशकालव्यवहितानामप्यानन्तर्यं स्मृतिसंस्कारयोरेकरूपत्वात् ॥ ९ ॥
jātideśakālavyavahitānām apy ānantaryaṃ smṛtisaṃskārayor ekarūpatvāt || 9 ||
Padaccheda
जाति-देश-काल-व्यवहितानाम् अपि आनन्तर्यम् स्मृति-संस्कारयोः एक-रूपत्वात्
jāti-deśa-kāla-vyavahitānām api ānantaryam smṛti-saṃskārayoḥ eka-rūpatvāt
Word by word
Even when they are separated by birth, place and time there is unbroken succession, because memory and latent impression are of one form.

Bhāṣya The ripening as a cat, if it rises again after a hundred births, a great distance or a hundred ages, rises at once with the tendencies formed by the earlier life as a cat, when like action brings it out. As the experiences, so the impressions; as the tendencies, so the memory. Since the relation of cause and effect is not cut, there is succession even across the gap.

तासामनादित्वं चाशिषो नित्यत्वात् ॥ १० ॥
tāsām anāditvaṃ cāśiṣo nityatvāt || 10 ||
Padaccheda
तासाम् अनादित्वम् च आशिषः नित्यत्वात्
tāsām anāditvam ca āśiṣaḥ nityatvāt
Word by word
And they are without beginning, because the wish is constant.

Bhāṣya The wish for oneself, ‘may I not cease to be; may I be’, is seen in all. It is not natural: how could a creature just born, which has not known death, fear it? So this mind, pierced by beginningless tendencies, takes up some of them as a cause prompts, for the Puruṣa's experience. Others hold that the mind shrinks and spreads to the size of the body, like a lamp in a pot or a palace; the teacher holds that the mind is all-pervading and only its activity shrinks and spreads. That depends on causes: outer, needing the body (praise, giving, salutation), and inner, of the mind alone (faith and the like). The mental is the stronger.

हेतुफलाश्रयालम्बनैः संगृहीतत्वादेषामभावे तदभावः ॥ ११ ॥
hetuphalāśrayālambanaiḥ saṃgṛhītatvād eṣām abhāve tadabhāvaḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
हेतु-फल-आश्रय-आलम्बनैः संगृहीतत्वात् एषाम् अभावे तत्-अभावः
hetu-phala-āśraya-ālambanaiḥ saṃgṛhītatvāt eṣām abhāve tat-abhāvaḥ
Word by word
Because they are held together by cause, fruit, substrate and support, in the absence of these they too are absent.

Bhāṣya The cause: from dharma pleasure, from adharma pain; from pleasure attachment, from pain aversion; from these effort, by which one helps or harms another with mind, speech or body; and from that again dharma and adharma. So turns the six-spoked wheel of the cycle of birth and death, and its driver is ignorance, the root of all afflictions. The fruit is that for the sake of which dharma and the like arise. The substrate is the mind that still has a task. The support is the thing which, when present, brings a tendency out.

अतीतानागतं स्वरूपतोऽस्त्यध्वभेदाद्धर्माणाम् ॥ १२ ॥
atītānāgataṃ svarūpato 'sty adhvabhedād dharmāṇām || 12 ||
Padaccheda
अतीत-अनागतम् स्वरूपतः अस्ति अध्व-भेदात् धर्माणाम्
atīta-anāgatam svarūpataḥ asti adhva-bhedāt dharmāṇām
Word by word
The past and the future exist in their own form; the properties differ by their path of time.

Bhāṣya He asks how the tendencies, existing as real, can cease, since nothing arises from the non-existent and nothing existent perishes. The future is what is yet to be manifest, the past what has been manifest, the present what is at work; all three are objects of knowledge, and knowledge without an object would not arise. A cause can make an existing fruit present; it cannot produce what was not. Past and future do not exist as the present does, as a manifest thing; the future exists in its own form to be manifested, the past in its own form as having been manifested.

ते व्यक्तसूक्ष्मा गुणात्मानः ॥ १३ ॥
te vyaktasūkṣmā guṇātmānaḥ || 13 ||
Padaccheda
ते व्यक्त-सूक्ष्माः गुण-आत्मानः
te vyakta-sūkṣmāḥ guṇa-ātmānaḥ
Word by word
They are manifest or subtle, and have the guṇas as their nature.

Bhāṣya The present ones are manifest; the past and future ones are subtle, of the form of the six unparticulars. All this is only a particular arrangement of the guṇas. So the teaching says: ‘The highest form of the guṇas does not come within sight; what comes within sight is like illusion, of little worth.’

परिणामैकत्वाद्वस्तुतत्त्वम् ॥ १४ ॥
pariṇāmaikatvād vastutattvam || 14 ||
Padaccheda
परिणाम-एकत्वात् वस्तु-तत्त्वम्
pariṇāma-ekatvāt vastu-tattvam
Word by word
The reality of a thing comes from the oneness of the change.

Bhāṣya If all is the guṇas, how is there one sound, one sense? The guṇas as means of grasping have one change, the sense of hearing; as what is grasped they have one change, sound; the subtle elements have one change, the atom of earth; and these one change, the earth, a cow, a tree. Those who say that there is no object apart from cognition, that a thing is only constructed by knowledge, like a dream object, deny the thing as it presents itself by its own power; how should they be believed?

वस्तुसाम्ये चित्तभेदात्तयोर्विभक्तः पन्थाः ॥ १५ ॥
vastusāmye cittabhedāt tayor vibhaktaḥ panthāḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
वस्तु-साम्ये चित्त-भेदात् तयोः विभक्तः पन्थाः
vastu-sāmye citta-bhedāt tayoḥ vibhaktaḥ panthāḥ
Word by word
The thing being the same and minds differing, the paths of the two are separate.

Bhāṣya One thing is the common object of many minds; it is not constructed by one mind or by many, but stands in itself. Of the same thing one mind has a pleasant cognition through dharma, another a painful one through adharma, another a deluded one through ignorance, another a neutral one through right seeing. So thing and knowledge, the grasped and the grasping, go separate ways.

न चैकचित्ततन्त्रं वस्तु तदप्रमाणकं तदा किं स्यात् ॥ १६ ॥
na caikacittatantraṃ vastu tadapramāṇakaṃ tadā kiṃ syāt || 16 ||
Padaccheda
न च एक-चित्त-तन्त्रम् वस्तु तत् अप्रमाणकम् तदा किम् स्यात्
na ca eka-citta-tantram vastu tat apramāṇakam tadā kim syāt
Word by word
And a thing does not depend on one mind; when not known by that mind, what would it then be?

Bhāṣya If a thing depended on one mind, then when that mind was distracted or had ceased, the thing, untouched by it and not the object of another, would be nothing at all; and whence would it arise again? The parts of it not present to the mind would not exist: there being no back, there would be no belly either. So the object is independent and common to all Puruṣas, and minds are independent, one for each Puruṣa. From the relation of the two comes perception, which is the Puruṣa's experience.

तदुपरागापेक्षित्वाच्चित्तस्य वस्तु ज्ञाताज्ञातम् ॥ १७ ॥
taduparāgāpekṣitvāc cittasya vastu jñātājñātam || 17 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-उपराग-अपेक्षित्वात् चित्तस्य वस्तु ज्ञात-अज्ञातम्
tat-uparāga-apekṣitvāt cittasya vastu jñāta-ajñātam
Word by word
A thing is known or unknown to the mind according as the mind is coloured by it.

Bhāṣya Objects are like magnets; the mind, like iron, is drawn and coloured by them. The object that colours it is known, another is unknown. Since its object is now known and now unknown, the mind is subject to change.

सदा ज्ञाताश्चित्तवृत्तयस्तत्प्रभोः पुरुषस्यापरिणामित्वात् ॥ १८ ॥
sadā jñātāś cittavṛttayas tatprabhoḥ puruṣasyāpariṇāmitvāt || 18 ||
Padaccheda
सदा ज्ञाताः चित्त-वृत्तयः तत्-प्रभोः पुरुषस्य अपरिणामित्वात्
sadā jñātāḥ citta-vṛttayaḥ tat-prabhoḥ puruṣasya apariṇāmitvāt
Word by word
The activities of the mind are always known, because its lord, the Puruṣa, does not change.

Bhāṣya If the Puruṣa, the lord, changed as the mind does, the mind's activities, his objects, would be now known and now unknown, like sound and other objects. That the mind is always known lets one infer that the Puruṣa does not change.

न तत्स्वाभासं दृश्यत्वात् ॥ १९ ॥
na tat svābhāsaṃ dṛśyatvāt || 19 ||
Padaccheda
न तत् स्व-आभासम् दृश्यत्वात्
na tat sva-ābhāsam dṛśyatvāt
Word by word
It is not self-illuminating, because it is something seen.

Bhāṣya Lest it be thought that the mind lights up both itself and its object, like fire. As the senses and sound and the rest are not self-illuminating, being seen, so the mind. Fire is no example: fire does not light up a self that was unlit, and light is seen where what lights and what is lit are joined; there is no such joining in a thing's own form alone. And beings are seen to be aware of the movement of their own understanding: ‘I am angry, I am afraid’.

एकसमये चोभयानवधारणम् ॥ २० ॥
ekasamaye cobhayānavadhāraṇam || 20 ||
Padaccheda
एक-समये च उभय-अनवधारणम्
eka-samaye ca ubhaya-anavadhāraṇam
Word by word
And both cannot be ascertained at one time.

Bhāṣya It is not possible to ascertain one's own form and another's in a single moment. For the holder of momentariness, a thing's coming to be is itself the act and the agent.

चित्तान्तरदृश्ये बुद्धिबुद्धेरतिप्रसङ्गः स्मृतिसंकरश्च ॥ २१ ॥
cittāntaradṛśye buddhibuddher atiprasaṅgaḥ smṛtisaṃkaraś ca || 21 ||
Padaccheda
चित्त-अन्तर-दृश्ये बुद्धि-बुद्धेः अतिप्रसङ्गः स्मृति-संकरः च
citta-antara-dṛśye buddhi-buddheḥ atiprasaṅgaḥ smṛti-saṃkaraḥ ca
Word by word
If it were seen by another mind, there would be an endless regress of cognition of cognition, and confusion of memories.

Bhāṣya If a mind were grasped by the next mind, by what is that cognition grasped? By another, and that by another. And there would be as many memories as cognitions of cognitions, so that no single memory could be ascertained. Those who deny the Puruṣa, who is aware of the understanding, have thrown everything into confusion. The Sāṃkhya, Yoga and like teachings mean by the word ‘own’ the Puruṣa, the owner and experiencer of the mind.

चितेरप्रतिसंक्रमायास्तदाकारापत्तौ स्वबुद्धिसंवेदनम् ॥ २२ ॥
citer apratisaṃkramāyās tadākārāpattau svabuddhisaṃvedanam || 22 ||
Padaccheda
चितेः अप्रतिसंक्रमायाः तत्-आकार-आपत्तौ स्व-बुद्धि-संवेदनम्
citeḥ apratisaṃkramāyāḥ tat-ākāra-āpattau sva-buddhi-saṃvedanam
Word by word
Consciousness, which does not pass over into objects, is aware of its own understanding when that takes on its form.

Bhāṣya The power of the experiencer does not change or pass over; it seems to have passed into the changing object and follows its activity. Because it merely imitates the activity of the understanding that has received the tinge of consciousness, the activity of knowing is said to be not distinct from that of the understanding.

द्रष्टृदृश्योपरक्तं चित्तं सर्वार्थम् ॥ २३ ॥
draṣṭṛdṛśyoparaktaṃ cittaṃ sarvārtham || 23 ||
Padaccheda
द्रष्टृ-दृश्य-उपरक्तम् चित्तम् सर्व-अर्थम्
draṣṭṛ-dṛśya-uparaktam cittam sarva-artham
Word by word
The mind, coloured by the seer and the seen, has all for its object.

Bhāṣya The mind is coloured by the object thought of, and, being itself an object, is tied to the Puruṣa, the subject. So it appears as object and subject, as conscious and unconscious, like a crystal. Misled by this likeness some call the mind itself conscious; others say that all this is mind only and there is no world of cows and pots. They are to be pitied. In the wisdom of absorption the object is reflected and is other than the wisdom; that by which the reflected object is ascertained in wisdom is the Puruṣa. Those who separate grasper, grasping and grasped as three kinds see rightly.

तदसंख्येयवासनाभिश्चित्रमपि परार्थं संहत्यकारित्वात् ॥ २४ ॥
tad asaṃkhyeyavāsanābhiś citram api parārthaṃ saṃhatyakāritvāt || 24 ||
Padaccheda
तत् असंख्येय-वासनाभिः चित्रम् अपि पर-अर्थम् संहत्य-कारित्वात्
tat asaṃkhyeya-vāsanābhiḥ citram api para-artham saṃhatya-kāritvāt
Word by word
Though variegated by countless latent tendencies, it exists for another, because it works by combining.

Bhāṣya For the experience and liberation of another, not for itself, like a house. A pleasant mind is not for the sake of pleasure, nor knowledge for the sake of knowledge. The other is the Puruṣa, who has experience and liberation as his purpose; he does not work by combining.

विशेषदर्शिन आत्मभावभावनानिवृत्तिः ॥ २५ ॥
viśeṣadarśina ātmabhāvabhāvanānivṛttiḥ || 25 ||
Padaccheda
विशेष-दर्शिनः आत्म-भाव-भावना-निवृत्तिः
viśeṣa-darśinaḥ ātma-bhāva-bhāvanā-nivṛttiḥ
Word by word
For one who sees the distinction, reflection on his own state ceases.

Bhāṣya As the sprouting of grass in the rains shows that its seed was there, so in one whose hair stands on end and whose tears fall on hearing of the path of release, a seed of seeing the distinction is inferred. In him the reflection arises naturally: ‘Who was I? How was I? What is this? What shall we be?’ It ceases for one who sees the distinction: this varied change belongs to the mind alone; the Puruṣa, when ignorance is gone, is pure and untouched by the mind's properties.

तदा विवेकनिम्नं कैवल्यप्राग्भारं चित्तम् ॥ २६ ॥
tadā vivekanimnaṃ kaivalyaprāgbhāraṃ cittam || 26 ||
Padaccheda
तदा विवेक-निम्नम् कैवल्य-प्राग्भारम् चित्तम्
tadā viveka-nimnam kaivalya-prāgbhāram cittam
Word by word
Then the mind slopes towards discernment and is borne on towards soleness.

Bhāṣya His mind, which was borne towards objects and sloped towards ignorance, becomes otherwise.

तच्छिद्रेषु प्रत्ययान्तराणि संस्कारेभ्यः ॥ २७ ॥
tacchidreṣu pratyayāntarāṇi saṃskārebhyaḥ || 27 ||
Padaccheda
तत्-छिद्रेषु प्रत्यय-अन्तराणि संस्कारेभ्यः
tat-chidreṣu pratyaya-antarāṇi saṃskārebhyaḥ
Word by word
In the gaps of that, other cognitions arise from latent impressions.

Bhāṣya In the mind that flows only with the vision of the difference of sattva and the Puruṣa, ‘I am’, ‘mine’, ‘I know’, ‘I do not know’ arise in the gaps, from earlier impressions whose seeds are wasting away.

हानमेषां क्लेशवदुक्तम् ॥ २८ ॥
hānam eṣāṃ kleśavad uktam || 28 ||
Padaccheda
हानम् एषाम् क्लेश-वत् उक्तम्
hānam eṣām kleśa-vat uktam
Word by word
Their avoidance is said to be like that of the afflictions.

Bhāṣya As the afflictions, become like burnt seeds, cannot sprout, so the earlier impression, burnt by the fire of knowledge, bears no cognition. The impressions of knowledge last until the mind's task is ended, so they need no thought.

प्रसंख्यानेऽप्यकुसीदस्य सर्वथा विवेकख्यातेर्धर्ममेघः समाधिः ॥ २९ ॥
prasaṃkhyāne 'py akusīdasya sarvathā vivekakhyāter dharmameghaḥ samādhiḥ || 29 ||
Padaccheda
प्रसंख्याने अपि अकुसीदस्य सर्वथा विवेक-ख्यातेः धर्म-मेघः समाधिः
prasaṃkhyāne api akusīdasya sarvathā viveka-khyāteḥ dharma-meghaḥ samādhiḥ
Word by word
For one who seeks no gain even in reflection, from the vision of discernment in every way there comes the absorption called the ‘cloud of dharma’.

Bhāṣya When he wants nothing even from reflection and is dispassionate towards that too, there is only the vision of discernment at all times; the seeds of impressions having wasted away, no other cognitions arise.

ततः क्लेशकर्मनिवृत्तिः ॥ ३० ॥
tataḥ kleśakarmanivṛttiḥ || 30 ||
Padaccheda
ततः क्लेश-कर्म-निवृत्तिः
tataḥ kleśa-karma-nivṛttiḥ
Word by word
From that, afflictions and actions cease.

Bhāṣya The afflictions, ignorance and the rest, are torn up by the root, and the stores of good and bad action are destroyed with their root. When these have ceased the knower is liberated while still living; for error is the cause of birth, and no one whose error has wasted away is seen to be born.

तदा सर्वावरणमलापेतस्य ज्ञानस्यानन्त्याज्ज्ञेयमल्पम् ॥ ३१ ॥
tadā sarvāvaraṇamalāpetasya jñānasyānantyāj jñeyam alpam || 31 ||
Padaccheda
तदा सर्व-आवरण-मल-अपेतस्य ज्ञानस्य आनन्त्यात् ज्ञेयम् अल्पम्
tadā sarva-āvaraṇa-mala-apetasya jñānasya ānantyāt jñeyam alpam
Word by word
Then, because knowledge freed from all the stains that cover it is infinite, what is to be known is little.

Bhāṣya The sattva of knowledge is infinite; covered by tamas, it is uncovered only here and there by rajas. Freed of all covering it is infinite, and what is to be known becomes little, like a firefly in the sky.

ततः कृतार्थानां परिणामक्रमसमाप्तिर्गुणानाम् ॥ ३२ ॥
tataḥ kṛtārthānāṃ pariṇāmakramasamāptir guṇānām || 32 ||
Padaccheda
ततः कृत-अर्थानाम् परिणाम-क्रम-समाप्तिः गुणानाम्
tataḥ kṛta-arthānām pariṇāma-krama-samāptiḥ guṇānām
Word by word
From that, the sequence of changes of the guṇas, their purpose accomplished, comes to an end.

Bhāṣya From the rise of the cloud of dharma. Having brought about experience and liberation, they cannot stay even a moment.

क्षणप्रतियोगी परिणामापरान्तनिर्ग्राह्यः क्रमः ॥ ३३ ॥
kṣaṇapratiyogī pariṇāmāparāntanirgrāhyaḥ kramaḥ || 33 ||
Padaccheda
क्षण-प्रतियोगी परिणाम-अपरान्त-निर्ग्राह्यः क्रमः
kṣaṇa-pratiyogī pariṇāma-aparānta-nirgrāhyaḥ kramaḥ
Word by word
Sequence is correlated with the moments and is grasped at the end of a change.

Bhāṣya A cloth is not old at the end without having passed through the sequence of moments. Sequence is seen in eternal things too. Eternity is of two kinds: the unchanging eternity of the Puruṣa and the changing eternity of the guṇas; that is eternal whose essence is not destroyed while it changes. In the properties of the guṇas, the understanding and the rest, the sequence has an end; in the guṇas themselves it has none.

Bhāṣya Does the sequence of the cycle of birth and death end or not? This cannot be answered outright. ‘Will all who are born die?’ can be answered so: yes. ‘Will all who die be born?’ needs a division: the skilled one, in whom the vision has arisen and thirst has wasted away, will not; another will. So here: for the skilled one the cycle ends, for another it does not.

पुरुषार्थशून्यानां गुणानां प्रतिप्रसवः कैवल्यं स्वरूपप्रतिष्ठा वा चितिशक्तिरिति ॥ ३४ ॥
puruṣārthaśūnyānāṃ guṇānāṃ pratiprasavaḥ kaivalyaṃ svarūpapratiṣṭhā vā citiśaktir iti || 34 ||
Padaccheda
पुरुष-अर्थ-शून्यानाम् गुणानाम् प्रतिप्रसवः कैवल्यम् स्वरूप-प्रतिष्ठा वा चिति-शक्तिः इति
puruṣa-artha-śūnyānām guṇānām pratiprasavaḥ kaivalyam svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā vā citi-śaktiḥ iti
Word by word
Soleness is the return to their source of the guṇas, empty of any purpose for the Puruṣa; or it is the power of consciousness resting in its own nature.

Bhāṣya The return to their source of the guṇas, as effects and causes, when experience and liberation are accomplished. Or: the Puruṣa's power of consciousness, alone, no longer tied to the sattva of the understanding; its abiding so for ever is soleness.
