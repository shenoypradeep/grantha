अष्टादशोऽध्यायः
Aṣṭādaśo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 18 · Mokṣasaṃnyāsayoga
अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
संन्यासस्य महाबाहो तत्त्वमिच्छामि वेदितुम् । त्यागस्य च हृषीकेश पृथक्केशिनिषूदन ॥ १ ॥
saṃnyāsasya mahābāho tattvamicchāmi veditum | tyāgasya ca hṛṣīkeśa pṛthakkeśiniṣūdana || 1 ||
Padaccheda
संन्यासस्य महा-बाहो तत्त्वम् इच्छामि वेदितुम् त्यागस्य च हृषीकेश पृथक् केशि-निषूदन
saṃnyāsasya mahā-bāho tattvam icchāmi veditum tyāgasya ca hṛṣīkeśa pṛthak keśi-niṣūdana
Word by word
Arjuna said: I wish to know the truth of renunciation, O mighty-armed, and of abandonment, O Hṛṣīkeśa, separately, O slayer of Keśin.

Note Śaṅkara: this chapter sums up the meaning of the whole Gītā and of the whole Veda; what was taught in all the earlier chapters is found in it. Arjuna asks only about the meanings of the two words saṃnyāsa and tyāga, as distinct from each other. Keśin was a demon in the form of a horse, slain by Vāsudeva.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
काम्यानां कर्मणां न्यासं संन्यासं कवयो विदुः । सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं प्राहुस्त्यागं विचक्षणाः ॥ २ ॥
kāmyānāṃ karmaṇāṃ nyāsaṃ saṃnyāsaṃ kavayo viduḥ | sarvakarmaphalatyāgaṃ prāhustyāgaṃ vicakṣaṇāḥ || 2 ||
Padaccheda
काम्यानाम् कर्मणाम् न्यासम् संन्यासम् कवयः विदुः सर्व-कर्म-फल-त्यागम् प्राहुः त्यागम् विचक्षणाः
kāmyānām karmaṇām nyāsam saṃnyāsam kavayaḥ viduḥ sarva-karma-phala-tyāgam prāhuḥ tyāgam vicakṣaṇāḥ
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: The learned know renunciation as the giving up of actions prompted by desire; the wise declare abandonment to be the abandonment of the fruit of all actions.

Note Śaṅkara: actions prompted by desire are the Aśvamedha and the like. ‘All actions’ are the obligatory and occasional ones that are being performed; giving up the fruit that would come to oneself from them is tyāga. Either way the two words mean simply ‘giving up’ and do not differ as ‘pot’ and ‘cloth’ do. (Objection) the obligatory actions have no fruit, so how can it be given up? (Reply) the Lord holds that they do bear fruit, as 18.12 shows.

त्याज्यं दोषवदित्येके कर्म प्राहुर्मनीषिणः । यज्ञदानतपःकर्म न त्याज्यमिति चापरे ॥ ३ ॥
tyājyaṃ doṣavadityeke karma prāhurmanīṣiṇaḥ | yajñadānatapaḥkarma na tyājyamiti cāpare || 3 ||
Padaccheda
त्याज्यम् दोषवत् इति एके कर्म प्राहुः मनीषिणः यज्ञ-दान-तपः-कर्म न त्याज्यम् इति च अपरे
tyājyam doṣavat iti eke karma prāhuḥ manīṣiṇaḥ yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma na tyājyam iti ca apare
Word by word
Some thinkers declare that action is to be abandoned as an evil; and others, that the action of sacrifice, giving and austerity is not to be abandoned.

Note Śaṅkara: doṣavat: all action is faulty because it binds; or, it is to be abandoned as a fault such as attachment is. The first view follows the Sāṃkhya outlook.

Note Śaṅkara: these alternatives concern only those entitled to action. The renouncers established in knowledge are not in question: they see neither bodily pain nor action in the Self, so abandonment from fear of pain or from delusion (18.7–8) cannot apply to them. What is praised here as abandonment of sattva is the giving up of the fruit of action by men of action who do not know the Self; it is called renunciation by way of praise and is not the renunciation of all action in the primary sense. ‘It is not possible for an embodied being’ (18.11) is likewise said in praise. About 160 characters of this long passage were not read.

निश्चयं शृणु मे तत्र त्यागे भरतसत्तम । त्यागो हि पुरुषव्याघ्र त्रिविधः संप्रकीर्तितः ॥ ४ ॥
niścayaṃ śṛṇu me tatra tyāge bharatasattama | tyāgo hi puruṣavyāghra trividhaḥ saṃprakīrtitaḥ || 4 ||
Padaccheda
निश्चयम् शृणु मे तत्र त्यागे भरत-सत्तम त्यागः हि पुरुष-व्याघ्र त्रिविधः संप्रकीर्तितः
niścayam śṛṇu me tatra tyāge bharata-sattama tyāgaḥ hi puruṣa-vyāghra trividhaḥ saṃprakīrtitaḥ
Word by word
Hear my conclusion about that abandonment, O best of the Bharatas. For abandonment, O tiger among men, is declared to be of three kinds.

Note Śaṅkara: he says ‘abandonment’ alone, meaning that what the two words tyāga and saṃnyāsa denote is one. It is of three kinds, of tamas and the rest, for the man of action who does not know the Self, and not for one who sees the highest truth. This is hard to know, so no one else can state the truth of it.

यज्ञदानतपःकर्म न त्याज्यं कार्यमेव तत् । यज्ञो दानं तपश्चैव पावनानि मनीषिणाम् ॥ ५ ॥
yajñadānatapaḥkarma na tyājyaṃ kāryameva tat | yajño dānaṃ tapaścaiva pāvanāni manīṣiṇām || 5 ||
Padaccheda
यज्ञ-दान-तपः-कर्म न त्याज्यम् कार्यम् एव तत् यज्ञः दानम् तपः च एव पावनानि मनीषिणाम्
yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma na tyājyam kāryam eva tat yajñaḥ dānam tapaḥ ca eva pāvanāni manīṣiṇām
Word by word
The action of sacrifice, giving and austerity is not to be abandoned; it is indeed to be done. Sacrifice, giving and austerity are purifiers of the wise.

Note Śaṅkara: of the wise who do not aim at the fruit.

एतान्यपि तु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा फलानि च । कर्तव्यानीति मे पार्थ निश्चितं मतमुत्तमम् ॥ ६ ॥
etānyapi tu karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā phalāni ca | kartavyānīti me pārtha niścitaṃ matamuttamam || 6 ||
Padaccheda
एतानि अपि तु कर्माणि सङ्गम् त्यक्त्वा फलानि च कर्तव्यानि इति मे पार्थ निश्चितम् मतम् उत्तमम्
etāni api tu karmāṇi saṅgam tyaktvā phalāni ca kartavyāni iti me pārtha niścitam matam uttamam
Word by word
But even these actions are to be done abandoning attachment and the fruits: this, O Pārtha, is my settled and highest view.

Note Śaṅkara: this concludes what was promised in 18.4. ‘Even these’: even these actions, which bind one who is attached and seeks their fruit, are to be done by the seeker of liberation. Others take ‘even these’ to mean actions prompted by desire, on the ground that obligatory actions have no fruit. That is wrong: their fruit has been shown (18.5), and one who wishes to give up even obligatory actions for fear of bondage has nothing to do with actions prompted by desire.

नियतस्य तु संन्यासः कर्मणो नोपपद्यते । मोहात्तस्य परित्यागस्तामसः परिकीर्तितः ॥ ७ ॥
niyatasya tu saṃnyāsaḥ karmaṇo nopapadyate | mohāttasya parityāgastāmasaḥ parikīrtitaḥ || 7 ||
Padaccheda
नियतस्य तु संन्यासः कर्मणः न उपपद्यते मोहात् तस्य परित्यागः तामसः परिकीर्तितः
niyatasya tu saṃnyāsaḥ karmaṇaḥ na upapadyate mohāt tasya parityāgaḥ tāmasaḥ parikīrtitaḥ
Word by word
But the renunciation of obligatory action is not proper; its abandonment from delusion is declared to be of tamas.

Note Śaṅkara: for the seeker of liberation who does not know the Self and is entitled to action, since for him it is a purifier. ‘Obligatory’ means that it must be done, and yet it is abandoned: that is a contradiction, so the abandonment comes from delusion, and delusion is tamas.

दुःखमित्येव यत्कर्म कायक्लेशभयात्त्यजेत् । स कृत्वा राजसं त्यागं नैव त्यागफलं लभेत् ॥ ८ ॥
duḥkhamityeva yatkarma kāyakleśabhayāttyajet | sa kṛtvā rājasaṃ tyāgaṃ naiva tyāgaphalaṃ labhet || 8 ||
Padaccheda
दुःखम् इति एव यत् कर्म काय-क्लेश-भयात् त्यजेत् सः कृत्वा राजसम् त्यागम् न एव त्याग-फलम् लभेत्
duḥkham iti eva yat karma kāya-kleśa-bhayāt tyajet saḥ kṛtvā rājasam tyāgam na eva tyāga-phalam labhet
Word by word
He who abandons an action merely because it is painful, from fear of bodily hardship, makes an abandonment of rajas and would not obtain the fruit of abandonment.

Note Śaṅkara: the fruit is liberation, the fruit of the abandonment of all action that is preceded by knowledge.

कार्यमित्येव यत्कर्म नियतं क्रियतेऽर्जुन । सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा फलं चैव स त्यागः सात्त्विको मतः ॥ ९ ॥
kāryamityeva yatkarma niyataṃ kriyate 'rjuna | saṅgaṃ tyaktvā phalaṃ caiva sa tyāgaḥ sāttviko mataḥ || 9 ||
Padaccheda
कार्यम् इति एव यत् कर्म नियतम् क्रियते अर्जुन सङ्गम् त्यक्त्वा फलम् च एव सः त्यागः सात्त्विकः मतः
kāryam iti eva yat karma niyatam kriyate arjuna saṅgam tyaktvā phalam ca eva saḥ tyāgaḥ sāttvikaḥ mataḥ
Word by word
When an obligatory action is done, O Arjuna, simply because it ought to be done, abandoning attachment and the fruit, that abandonment is held to be of sattva.

Note Śaṅkara: or, though no fruit of an obligatory action is stated, the man who does not know imagines that it purifies him or wards off the fault of omission; ‘abandoning the fruit’ rules out that fancy too. (Objection) the topic is the threefold giving up of action; how is giving up attachment and fruit the third kind? (Reply) both are alike in being abandonment; by blaming the giving up of action as of rajas and tamas, the giving up of the aim at its fruit is praised as of sattva.

न द्वेष्ट्यकुशलं कर्म कुशले नानुषज्जते । त्यागी सत्त्वसमाविष्टो मेधावी छिन्नसंशयः ॥ १० ॥
na dveṣṭyakuśalaṃ karma kuśale nānuṣajjate | tyāgī sattvasamāviṣṭo medhāvī chinnasaṃśayaḥ || 10 ||
Padaccheda
न द्वेष्टि अकुशलम् कर्म कुशले न अनुषज्जते त्यागी सत्त्व-समाविष्टः मेधावी छिन्न-संशयः
na dveṣṭi akuśalam karma kuśale na anuṣajjate tyāgī sattva-samāviṣṭaḥ medhāvī chinna-saṃśayaḥ
Word by word
The abandoner, filled with sattva, wise, his doubts cut, does not hate a disagreeable action, nor is he attached to an agreeable one.

Note Śaṅkara: the inner organ of one who does obligatory action without attachment and without aim at its fruit grows pure and fit to reflect on the Self; this verse tells how he comes by degrees to be established in knowledge. akuśala is action prompted by desire, a cause of the cycle of birth and death; kuśala is obligatory action, which he does not cling to as ‘the cause of liberation’. sattva is the cause of discerning the Self from what is not the Self; medhā is the wisdom that is knowledge of the Self; the doubt cut is that made by ignorance: he is sure that abiding in the Self's own nature alone is the means to the highest good. Such a man, mentally renouncing all action, reaches steadfastness in knowledge: this is the purpose of the yoga of action.

न हि देहभृता शक्यं त्यक्तुं कर्माण्यशेषतः । यस्तु कर्मफलत्यागी स त्यागीत्यभिधीयते ॥ ११ ॥
na hi dehabhṛtā śakyaṃ tyaktuṃ karmāṇyaśeṣataḥ | yastu karmaphalatyāgī sa tyāgītyabhidhīyate || 11 ||
Padaccheda
न हि देहभृता शक्यम् त्यक्तुम् कर्माणि अशेषतः यः तु कर्म-फल-त्यागी सः त्यागी इति अभिधीयते
na hi dehabhṛtā śakyam tyaktum karmāṇi aśeṣataḥ yaḥ tu karma-phala-tyāgī saḥ tyāgī iti abhidhīyate
Word by word
For it is not possible for an embodied being to abandon actions entirely; but he who abandons the fruit of action is called an abandoner.

Note Śaṅkara: dehabhṛt here is one who takes the body to be the Self, firm in the thought ‘I am the doer’, and not the man of discernment. He cannot give up all action, and is entitled only to do the enjoined actions while abandoning their fruit; he is called an abandoner, though a man of action, by way of praise. So the renunciation of all action is possible only for one who sees the highest truth and does not take the body to be the Self.

अनिष्टमिष्टं मिश्रं च त्रिविधं कर्मणः फलम् । भवत्यत्यागिनां प्रेत्य न तु संन्यासिनां क्वचित् ॥ १२ ॥
aniṣṭamiṣṭaṃ miśraṃ ca trividhaṃ karmaṇaḥ phalam | bhavatyatyāgināṃ pretya na tu saṃnyāsināṃ kvacit || 12 ||
Padaccheda
अनिष्टम् इष्टम् मिश्रम् च त्रिविधम् कर्मणः फलम् भवति अत्यागिनाम् प्रेत्य न तु संन्यासिनाम् क्वचित्
aniṣṭam iṣṭam miśram ca trividham karmaṇaḥ phalam bhavati atyāginām pretya na tu saṃnyāsinām kvacit
Word by word
The threefold fruit of action, unwished-for, wished-for and mixed, comes after death to those who have not abandoned, but never to renouncers.

Note Śaṅkara: unwished-for: hell, animal birth and the like; wished-for: the state of gods and the like; mixed: human birth. Action is dharma and adharma. Its fruit, made by ignorance, is like a magician's show; phala is so called because, being worthless (phalgu), it vanishes (laya). Those who have not abandoned are men of action, not renouncers in the true sense. The renouncers are the wandering ascetics established in knowledge alone, for right seeing uproots ignorance and the other seeds of the cycle of birth and death.

पञ्चैतानि महाबाहो कारणानि निबोध मे । सांख्ये कृतान्ते प्रोक्तानि सिद्धये सर्वकर्मणाम् ॥ १३ ॥
pañcaitāni mahābāho kāraṇāni nibodha me | sāṃkhye kṛtānte proktāni siddhaye sarvakarmaṇām || 13 ||
Padaccheda
पञ्च एतानि महा-बाहो कारणानि निबोध मे सांख्ये कृत-अन्ते प्रोक्तानि सिद्धये सर्व-कर्मणाम्
pañca etāni mahā-bāho kāraṇāni nibodha me sāṃkhye kṛta-ante proktāni siddhaye sarva-karmaṇām
Word by word
Learn from me, O mighty-armed, these five causes for the accomplishment of all actions, taught in the Sāṃkhya, in which action ends.

Note Śaṅkara: the renunciation of all action is possible only for one who sees the highest truth, since action, its factors and its fruit are superimposed on the Self by ignorance; the following verses show this. sāṃkhya is the śāstra in which the things to be known are enumerated, that is, the Vedānta. kṛtānta qualifies it: kṛta is action, and it is where action comes to its end, for when knowledge of the Self arises all action ceases (2.46, 4.33).

अधिष्ठानं तथा कर्ता करणं च पृथग्विधम् । विविधाश्च पृथक्चेष्टा दैवं चैवात्र पञ्चमम् ॥ १४ ॥
adhiṣṭhānaṃ tathā kartā karaṇaṃ ca pṛthagvidham | vividhāśca pṛthakceṣṭā daivaṃ caivātra pañcamam || 14 ||
Padaccheda
अधिष्ठानम् तथा कर्ता करणम् च पृथग्विधम् विविधाः च पृथक् चेष्टाः दैवम् च एव अत्र पञ्चमम्
adhiṣṭhānam tathā kartā karaṇam ca pṛthagvidham vividhāḥ ca pṛthak ceṣṭāḥ daivam ca eva atra pañcamam
Word by word
The seat, the agent, the instruments of various kinds, the manifold distinct motions, and the divine, the fifth among these.

Note Śaṅkara: the seat is the body, the ground in which desire, aversion, pleasure, pain and knowledge show themselves. The agent is the experiencer marked by his limiting adjuncts. The instruments are hearing and the rest, twelve in number. The motions are those of the breath, the out-breath, in-breath and the rest. The divine is the sun and the other gods who favour the eye and the other organs.

शरीरवाङ्मनोभिर्यत्कर्म प्रारभते नरः । न्याय्यं वा विपरीतं वा पञ्चैते तस्य हेतवः ॥ १५ ॥
śarīravāṅmanobhiryatkarma prārabhate naraḥ | nyāyyaṃ vā viparītaṃ vā pañcaite tasya hetavaḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
शरीर-वाक्-मनोभिः यत् कर्म प्रारभते नरः न्याय्यम् वा विपरीतम् वा पञ्च एते तस्य हेतवः
śarīra-vāk-manobhiḥ yat karma prārabhate naraḥ nyāyyam vā viparītam vā pañca ete tasya hetavaḥ
Word by word
Whatever action a man undertakes with body, speech and mind, whether right or the contrary, these five are its causes.

Note Śaṅkara: right: in accord with dharma and scripture; the contrary: against them. Acts that merely keep one alive, such as blinking, are effects of earlier dharma and adharma and so are included. All action enjoined or forbidden has body, speech or mind as chief, and seeing, hearing and the like are their parts; so it is grouped under these three.

तत्रैवं सति कर्तारमात्मानं केवलं तु यः । पश्यत्यकृतबुद्धित्वान्न स पश्यति दुर्मतिः ॥ १६ ॥
tatraivaṃ sati kartāramātmānaṃ kevalaṃ tu yaḥ | paśyatyakṛtabuddhitvānna sa paśyati durmatiḥ || 16 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र एवम् सति कर्तारम् आत्मानम् केवलम् तु यः पश्यति अकृत-बुद्धित्वात् न सः पश्यति दुर्मतिः
tatra evam sati kartāram ātmānam kevalam tu yaḥ paśyati akṛta-buddhitvāt na saḥ paśyati durmatiḥ
Word by word
This being so, he who, because his understanding is untrained, sees the Self, which is alone, as the agent, that man of perverse mind does not see.

Note Śaṅkara: though action is carried out by these five, imagined through ignorance to be the Self, he thinks ‘I alone am the doer’; kevala is ‘pure’. His understanding is not trained by the Vedānta, a teacher's instruction and reasoning. Even one who holds that the Self is other than the body, yet sees that Self alone as the doer, is of untrained understanding. His mind is perverse, the cause of endless birth and death. He sees and yet does not see, as one with an eye disease sees many moons, or as one thinks the moon runs when the clouds run.

यस्य नाहंकृतो भावो बुद्धिर्यस्य न लिप्यते । हत्वापि स इमांल्लोकान्न हन्ति न निबध्यते ॥ १७ ॥
yasya nāhaṃkṛto bhāvo buddhiryasya na lipyate | hatvāpi sa imāṃllokānna hanti na nibadhyate || 17 ||
Padaccheda
यस्य न अहंकृतः भावः बुद्धिः यस्य न लिप्यते हत्वा अपि सः इमान् लोकान् न हन्ति न निबध्यते
yasya na ahaṃkṛtaḥ bhāvaḥ buddhiḥ yasya na lipyate hatvā api saḥ imān lokān na hanti na nibadhyate
Word by word
He who has not the notion ‘I am the doer’, whose understanding is not tainted, even though he slays these creatures, does not slay, nor is he bound.

Note Śaṅkara: he sees: ‘these five, imagined in the Self through ignorance, are the doers of all actions, not I; I am the witness of their working, alone and changeless’. His understanding does not regret: ‘I did this, and by it I shall go to hell’. lokān: all these living beings. ‘Though he slays’ is said from the worldly view, ‘he does not slay’ from the view of the highest truth.

Note Śaṅkara: nor does the Self act in combination with the five: what is changeless cannot combine with anything, so ‘alone’ (18.16) only restates its nature. That the Self is changeless is taught in the Gītā (2.25, 3.27, 13.31), in śruti and by reasoning. What ignorance brings to it does not belong to it, as silver does not belong to the shell. This closes what was begun at 2.19–21: the knower has no duty of action, and the threefold fruit does not come to renouncers. This is the meaning of the Gītā and the essence of the whole Veda. Padaccheda
ज्ञानम् ज्ञेयम् परिज्ञाता त्रिविधा कर्म-चोदना करणम् कर्म कर्ता इति त्रिविधः कर्म-संग्रहः
jñānam jñeyam parijñātā trividhā karma-codanā karaṇam karma kartā iti trividhaḥ karma-saṃgrahaḥ
Word by word
Knowledge, what is to be known and the knower are the threefold prompting to action; the instrument, the object and the agent are the threefold compass of action.

Note Śaṅkara: knowledge here is knowledge of any object, and what is to be known is any object; the knower is the experiencer imagined through ignorance. When the three meet, action begins, to avoid or to obtain something. The instrument is outer, hearing and the rest, and inner, the understanding and the rest. karma here is what the agent most wants to reach by his act. Action inheres in these three, so they are its compass.

ज्ञानं कर्म च कर्ता च त्रिधैव गुणभेदतः । प्रोच्यते गुणसंख्याने यथावच्छृणु तान्यपि ॥ १९ ॥

Padaccheda

Knowledge, action and the agent are declared in the enumeration of the guṇas to be of three kinds only, according to the difference of the guṇas. Hear of them also, duly.

Note Śaṅkara: karma here is the act, and not the technical ‘object’ of 18.18. ‘Only’: there is no class apart from the guṇas. guṇa-saṃkhyāna is the śāstra of Kapila; though it conflicts with the oneness of Brahman, the highest truth, its followers are expert in the working of the guṇas, and it is cited in support of what follows.

सर्वभूतेषु येनैकं भावमव्ययमीक्षते । अविभक्तं विभक्तेषु तज्ज्ञानं विद्धि सात्त्विकम् ॥ २० ॥

Padaccheda
सर्व-भूतेषु येन एकम् भावम् अव्ययम् ईक्षते अविभक्तम् विभक्तेषु तत् ज्ञानम् विद्धि सात्त्विकम्
sarva-bhūteṣu yena ekam bhāvam avyayam īkṣate avibhaktam vibhakteṣu tat jñānam viddhi sāttvikam
Word by word
That knowledge by which one sees one undecaying reality in all beings, undivided in the divided: know that to be of sattva.

Note Śaṅkara: beings from the unmanifest down to the unmoving. bhāva means a thing: the one reality that is the Self, which does not decay either in itself or in its properties. It is not divided in the different bodies, being continuous like space. This is right seeing, whose object is the non-dual Self.

पृथक्त्वेन तु यज्ज्ञानं नानाभावान्पृथग्विधान् । वेत्ति सर्वेषु भूतेषु तज्ज्ञानं विद्धि राजसम् ॥ २१ ॥
pṛthaktvena tu yajjñānaṃ nānābhāvānpṛthagvidhān | vetti sarveṣu bhūteṣu tajjñānaṃ viddhi rājasam || 21 ||
Padaccheda

Word by word
But that knowledge which knows in all beings different entities of various kinds, as separate: know that knowledge to be of rajas.

Note Śaṅkara: the views of duality are not right views; being of rajas and tamas, they do not directly end the cycle of birth and death. This one knows selves as different in each body. Since knowledge cannot be an agent, ‘which knows’ means ‘by which one knows’.

यत्तु कृत्स्नवदेकस्मिन्कार्ये सक्तमहैतुकम् । अतत्त्वार्थवदल्पं च तत्तामसमुदाहृतम् ॥ २२ ॥

Padaccheda
यत् तु कृत्स्न-वत् एकस्मिन् कार्ये सक्तम् अहैतुकम् अतत्त्व-अर्थवत् अल्पम् च तत् तामसम् उदाहृतम्
yat tu kṛtsna-vat ekasmin kārye saktam ahaitukam atattva-arthavat alpam ca tat tāmasam udāhṛtam
Word by word
But that which clings to one effect as if it were the whole, without reason, not having the truth for its object, and petty, is declared to be of tamas.

Note Śaṅkara: the one effect is the body, or an outer thing such as an image: ‘the Self, or the Lord, is just this much and nothing beyond’; as the naked mendicants hold the jīva to be within the body and of its size, or as some take the Lord to be mere stone or wood. It is petty because its object, or its fruit, is small.

नियतं सङ्गरहितमरागद्वेषतः कृतम् । अफलप्रेप्सुना कर्म यत्तत्सात्त्विकमुच्यते ॥ २३ ॥

Word by word
The action that is obligatory, free from attachment, done without love or hate by one who does not seek its fruit, is called of sattva.

Padaccheda
यत् तु काम-ईप्सुना कर्म स-अहंकारेण वा पुनः क्रियते बहुल-आयासम् तत् राजसम् उदाहृतम्

But the action that is done by one who seeks what he desires, or by one with the sense of ‘I’, with much toil, is declared to be of rajas.

Note Śaṅkara: kāmepsu: seeking the fruit of the action. ‘With the sense of ‘I’’ is said in contrast with a learned man of the world who is called free of it, and not in contrast with the knower of the truth; for even the doer of an action of sattva, if he does not know the Self, has the sense of ‘I’. punaḥ only fills the line.

Note Śaṅkara: loss of strength or of wealth; injury to living beings; ability: ‘I am able to finish this action’.

The agent who is free from attachment, not given to speaking of ‘I’, endowed with firmness and zeal, unchanged in success and failure, is called of sattva.

Note Śaṅkara: unchanged: moved to act only by the authority of scripture, and not by desire for the fruit and the like.

The agent who is passionate, seeking the fruit of action, greedy, given to injury, impure, subject to joy and grief, is declared to be of rajas.

Note Śaṅkara: greedy: thirsting for what belongs to others, or not parting with his own at holy places and the like. Joy at gaining the wished-for, grief at meeting the unwished-for or losing the wished-for; or at the success and failure of that very action.

Padaccheda

The agent who is uncollected, uncultivated, unbending, deceitful, malicious, lazy, despondent and procrastinating is called of tamas.

Note Śaṅkara: prākṛta: of wholly untrained understanding, like a child. Unbending: like a staff, he bows to no one. śaṭha: he hides what he can do. naikṛtika: intent on setting others at odds. dīrghasūtrin: what should be done today or tomorrow he does not do even in a month.

Word by word
Hear the threefold division of understanding and of firmness according to the guṇas, declared in full and severally, O winner of wealth.

Note Śaṅkara: Arjuna is Dhanaṃjaya because in his conquest of the quarters he won much wealth, human and divine.

The understanding that knows the path of action and the path of renunciation, what is and is not to be done, fear and freedom from fear, bondage and liberation, O Pārtha, is of sattva.

having given up the sense of ‘I’, force, arrogance, desire, anger and possessions, without the sense of ‘mine’ and at peace, he is fit for becoming Brahman.

Note Śaṅkara: the sense of ‘I’ in the body and the rest. Force is power joined with desire and passion, and not the natural strength of the body, which cannot be given up. Arrogance follows on elation and leads one to overstep dharma. Even after the faults of senses and mind are given up, outer possessions may come for keeping the body alive or for the practice of dharma; these too he gives up and becomes a wandering renouncer, without ‘mine’ even towards the bare life of the body.

ब्रह्मभूतः प्रसन्नात्मा न शोचति न काङ्क्षति । समः सर्वेषु भूतेषु मद्भक्तिं लभते पराम् ॥ ५४ ॥
brahmabhūtaḥ prasannātmā na śocati na kāṅkṣati | samaḥ sarveṣu bhūteṣu madbhaktiṃ labhate parām || 54 ||
Padaccheda
ब्रह्म-भूतः प्रसन्न-आत्मा न शोचति न काङ्क्षति समः सर्वेषु भूतेषु मत्-भक्तिम् लभते पराम्
brahma-bhūtaḥ prasanna-ātmā na śocati na kāṅkṣati samaḥ sarveṣu bhūteṣu mat-bhaktim labhate parām
Word by word
Having become Brahman, serene in mind, he neither grieves nor longs. The same towards all beings, he attains supreme devotion to me.

Note Śaṅkara: he does not grieve over any lack or defect in himself, and a knower of Brahman cannot long for what he has not; this states his nature. Another reading is na hṛṣyati, ‘he does not exult’. ‘The same’: he sees the pleasure and pain of all beings as like his own; the seeing of the Self as the same is not meant here, as it comes in the next verse. The supreme devotion is the fourth, which is knowledge (7.16–18).

भक्त्या मामभिजानाति यावान्यश्चास्मि तत्त्वतः । ततो मां तत्त्वतो ज्ञात्वा विशते तदनन्तरम् ॥ ५५ ॥
bhaktyā māmabhijānāti yāvānyaścāsmi tattvataḥ | tato māṃ tattvato jñātvā viśate tadanantaram || 55 ||
Padaccheda
भक्त्या माम् अभिजानाति यावान् यः च अस्मि तत्त्वतः ततः माम् तत्त्वतः ज्ञात्वा विशते तत्-अनन्तरम्
bhaktyā mām abhijānāti yāvān yaḥ ca asmi tattvataḥ tataḥ mām tattvataḥ jñātvā viśate tat-anantaram
Word by word
By devotion he knows me in truth, how great I am and who I am. Then, having known me in truth, he enters into me immediately after that.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘how great’: with the differences of extent made by limiting adjuncts; ‘who’: the supreme Puruṣa free of all such differences, like space, non-dual, pure consciousness. Knowing and entering are not two acts; there is no fruit other than the knowledge itself (13.2).

Note Śaṅkara: (objection) one knows a thing the moment knowledge of it arises, so how is he said to know by the culmination of knowledge? (Reply) niṣṭhā is knowledge ending in the sure experience of the Self, once its aids are present and what opposes it is absent: the knowledge that the knower of the field and the supreme Self are one, born of scripture and teacher, together with the renunciation of all action. This is the fourth, supreme devotion. All the scriptures that enjoin withdrawal have their purpose here. Steadfastness in knowledge cannot go together with action, as one going to the eastern sea cannot share a road with one going to the western; so it is to be practised only with the renunciation of all action.

सर्वकर्माण्यपि सदा कुर्वाणो मद्व्यपाश्रयः । मत्प्रसादादवाप्नोति शाश्वतं पदमव्ययम् ॥ ५६ ॥
sarvakarmāṇyapi sadā kurvāṇo madvyapāśrayaḥ | matprasādādavāpnoti śāśvataṃ padamavyayam || 56 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-कर्माणि अपि सदा कुर्वाणः मत्-व्यपाश्रयः मत्-प्रसादात् अवाप्नोति शाश्वतम् पदम् अव्ययम्
sarva-karmāṇi api sadā kurvāṇaḥ mat-vyapāśrayaḥ mat-prasādāt avāpnoti śāśvatam padam avyayam
Word by word
Even though always doing all actions, one whose refuge I am attains by my grace the eternal, undecaying abode.

Note Śaṅkara: the yoga of devotion, the worship of the Lord by one's own action, is now praised; its fruit is the fitness for steadfastness in knowledge, which ends in liberation. ‘All actions’: even forbidden ones. The abode is that of Viṣṇu.

चेतसा सर्वकर्माणि मयि संन्यस्य मत्परः । बुद्धियोगमुपाश्रित्य मच्चित्तः सततं भव ॥ ५७ ॥
cetasā sarvakarmāṇi mayi saṃnyasya matparaḥ | buddhiyogamupāśritya maccittaḥ satataṃ bhava || 57 ||
Padaccheda
चेतसा सर्व-कर्माणि मयि संन्यस्य मत्-परः बुद्धि-योगम् उपाश्रित्य मत्-चित्तः सततम् भव
cetasā sarva-karmāṇi mayi saṃnyasya mat-paraḥ buddhi-yogam upāśritya mat-cittaḥ satatam bhava
Word by word
Giving over all actions to me with the mind, holding me as the supreme, and resorting to the yoga of understanding, always have your mind on me.

Note Śaṅkara: with the mind: with a discerning understanding; actions with seen and unseen ends are given over to the Lord in the way taught at 9.27. buddhi-yoga is a collected understanding. He reads apāśritya: taking it as one's only refuge.

मच्चित्तः सर्वदुर्गाणि मत्प्रसादात्तरिष्यसि । अथ चेत्त्वमहंकारान्न श्रोष्यसि विनङ्क्ष्यसि ॥ ५८ ॥
maccittaḥ sarvadurgāṇi matprasādāttariṣyasi | atha cettvamahaṃkārānna śroṣyasi vinaṅkṣyasi || 58 ||
Padaccheda
मत्-चित्तः सर्व-दुर्गाणि मत्-प्रसादात् तरिष्यसि अथ चेत् त्वम् अहंकारात् न श्रोष्यसि विनङ्क्ष्यसि
mat-cittaḥ sarva-durgāṇi mat-prasādāt tariṣyasi atha cet tvam ahaṃkārāt na śroṣyasi vinaṅkṣyasi
Word by word
With your mind on me, you will cross all difficulties by my grace. But if, from the sense of ‘I’, you will not listen, you will perish.

Note Śaṅkara: the difficulties are the causes of the cycle of birth and death, hard to cross. The sense of ‘I’ here: ‘I am learned’.

यदहंकारमाश्रित्य न योत्स्य इति मन्यसे । मिथ्यैष व्यवसायस्ते प्रकृतिस्त्वां नियोक्ष्यति ॥ ५९ ॥
yadahaṃkāramāśritya na yotsya iti manyase | mithyaiṣa vyavasāyaste prakṛtistvāṃ niyokṣyati || 59 ||
Padaccheda
यत् अहंकारम् आश्रित्य न योत्स्ये इति मन्यसे मिथ्या एषः व्यवसायः ते प्रकृतिः त्वाम् नियोक्ष्यति
yat ahaṃkāram āśritya na yotsye iti manyase mithyā eṣaḥ vyavasāyaḥ te prakṛtiḥ tvām niyokṣyati
Word by word
If, resorting to the sense of ‘I’, you think ‘I will not fight’, this resolve of yours is vain: your nature will compel you.

Note Śaṅkara: do not think ‘I am independent; why should I do what another says?’ prakṛti here is the nature of a warrior. He reads yady ahaṃkāram, ‘if’; the source has yad ahaṃkāram.

स्वभावजेन कौन्तेय निबद्धः स्वेन कर्मणा । कर्तुं नेच्छसि यन्मोहात्करिष्यस्यवशोऽपि तत् ॥ ६० ॥
svabhāvajena kaunteya nibaddhaḥ svena karmaṇā | kartuṃ necchasi yanmohātkariṣyasyavaśo 'pi tat || 60 ||
Padaccheda
स्वभाव-जेन कौन्तेय निबद्धः स्वेन कर्मणा कर्तुम् न इच्छसि यत् मोहात् करिष्यसि अवशः अपि तत्
svabhāva-jena kaunteya nibaddhaḥ svena karmaṇā kartum na icchasi yat mohāt kariṣyasi avaśaḥ api tat
Word by word
Bound by your own action, born of your nature, O son of Kuntī, what from delusion you do not wish to do, that you will do even helplessly.

Note Śaṅkara: the action born of his nature is valour and the rest (18.43).

ईश्वरः सर्वभूतानां हृद्देशेऽर्जुन तिष्ठति । भ्रामयन्सर्वभूतानि यन्त्रारूढानि मायया ॥ ६१ ॥
īśvaraḥ sarvabhūtānāṃ hṛddeśe 'rjuna tiṣṭhati | bhrāmayansarvabhūtāni yantrārūḍhāni māyayā || 61 ||
Padaccheda
ईश्वरः सर्व-भूतानाम् हृत्-देशे अर्जुन तिष्ठति भ्रामयन् सर्व-भूतानि यन्त्र-आरूढानि मायया
īśvaraḥ sarva-bhūtānām hṛt-deśe arjuna tiṣṭhati bhrāmayan sarva-bhūtāni yantra-ārūḍhāni māyayā
Word by word
The Lord dwells in the region of the heart of all beings, O Arjuna, causing all beings to revolve by illusion, as if mounted on a machine.

Note Śaṅkara: the Lord is Nārāyaṇa, whose nature it is to rule. ‘Arjuna’ means of a white, that is, pure inner nature. ‘As if’ is to be supplied: like wooden figures mounted on a machine. māyā here is deception.

तमेव शरणं गच्छ सर्वभावेन भारत । तत्प्रसादात्परां शान्तिं स्थानं प्राप्स्यसि शाश्वतम् ॥ ६२ ॥
tameva śaraṇaṃ gaccha sarvabhāvena bhārata | tatprasādātparāṃ śāntiṃ sthānaṃ prāpsyasi śāśvatam || 62 ||
Padaccheda
तम् एव शरणम् गच्छ सर्व-भावेन भारत तत्-प्रसादात् पराम् शान्तिम् स्थानम् प्राप्स्यसि शाश्वतम्
tam eva śaraṇam gaccha sarva-bhāvena bhārata tat-prasādāt parām śāntim sthānam prāpsyasi śāśvatam
Word by word
Take refuge in him alone with your whole being, O descendant of Bharata. By his grace you will attain supreme peace and the eternal abode.

Note Śaṅkara: refuge, to remove the distress of the cycle of birth and death. Peace is cessation; the abode is the supreme abode of Viṣṇu.

इति ते ज्ञानमाख्यातं गुह्याद्गुह्यतरं मया । विमृश्यैतदशेषेण यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु ॥ ६३ ॥
iti te jñānamākhyātaṃ guhyādguhyataraṃ mayā | vimṛśyaitadaśeṣeṇa yathecchasi tathā kuru || 63 ||
Padaccheda
इति ते ज्ञानम् आख्यातम् गुह्यात् गुह्यतरम् मया विमृश्य एतत् अशेषेण यथा इच्छसि तथा कुरु
iti te jñānam ākhyātam guhyāt guhyataram mayā vimṛśya etat aśeṣeṇa yathā icchasi tathā kuru
Word by word
Thus knowledge more secret than the secret has been declared to you by me. Reflect on it fully, and then do as you wish.

Note Śaṅkara: declared by me, the all-knowing Lord. ‘This’ is the teaching as given, and all that it has said.

सर्वगुह्यतमं भूयः शृणु मे परमं वचः । इष्टोऽसि मे दृढमिति ततो वक्ष्यामि ते हितम् ॥ ६४ ॥
sarvaguhyatamaṃ bhūyaḥ śṛṇu me paramaṃ vacaḥ | iṣṭo 'si me dṛḍhamiti tato vakṣyāmi te hitam || 64 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-गुह्यतमम् भूयः शृणु मे परमम् वचः इष्टः असि मे दृढम् इति ततः वक्ष्यामि ते हितम्
sarva-guhyatamam bhūyaḥ śṛṇu me paramam vacaḥ iṣṭaḥ asi me dṛḍham iti tataḥ vakṣyāmi te hitam
Word by word
Hear again my supreme word, the most secret of all. You are firmly dear to me; therefore I shall tell you what is for your good.

Note Śaṅkara: though said more than once. ‘Not from fear nor for gain do I speak, but because you are dear to me.’ The good is the means of gaining knowledge, the best of all goods.

मन्मना भव मद्भक्तो मद्याजी मां नमस्कुरु । मामेवैष्यसि सत्यं ते प्रतिजाने प्रियोऽसि मे ॥ ६५ ॥
manmanā bhava madbhakto madyājī māṃ namaskuru | māmevaiṣyasi satyaṃ te pratijāne priyo 'si me || 65 ||
Padaccheda
मत्-मनाः भव मत्-भक्तः मत्-याजी माम् नमस्कुरु माम् एव एष्यसि सत्यम् ते प्रतिजाने प्रियः असि मे
mat-manāḥ bhava mat-bhaktaḥ mat-yājī mām namaskuru mām eva eṣyasi satyam te pratijāne priyaḥ asi me
Word by word
Have your mind on me, be devoted to me, sacrifice to me, bow down to me. You will come to me alone; truly I promise you, for you are dear to me.

Note Śaṅkara: living so, with end, means and purpose all given over to Vāsudeva. Knowing that the Lord's promise is true and that liberation is the sure fruit of devotion to him, one should have him as one's only refuge: this is the meaning of the sentence.

सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज । अहं त्वा सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ॥ ६६ ॥
sarvadharmānparityajya māmekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja | ahaṃ tvā sarvapāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ || 66 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-धर्मान् परित्यज्य माम् एकम् शरणम् व्रज अहम् त्वा सर्व-पापेभ्यः मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः
sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekam śaraṇam vraja aham tvā sarva-pāpebhyaḥ mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ
Word by word
Giving up all dharmas, come to me alone as refuge. I shall free you from all sins; do not grieve.

Note Śaṅkara: having summed up refuge in the Lord, the highest secret of the path of the yoga of action, he now states its fruit, right seeing, taught as the essence of all the Upaniṣads. ‘Dharma’ here includes adharma too, since actionlessness is meant: giving up all dharmas is renouncing all actions. ‘Me alone’: the Self of all, the same in all beings, the Lord, free from birth, old age and death; know that ‘I am he’ and that there is nothing other than me. The sins are all the bonds of dharma and adharma; he frees by making one's own nature as the Self manifest (10.11).

Note Śaṅkara then examines at length whether in the Gītā the means to the highest good is knowledge, action, or both together. His conclusion: knowledge of the Self alone, since it removes the notion of difference, of act, agent and fruit, which ignorance has set up in the Self from time without beginning; liberation, being eternal, is not something produced by action, nor by knowledge and action combined. The passage ends: the Self does not truly act or experience through the working of body and organs; the cycle of birth and death is an error resting on a false notion, as in a dream, and it ceases wholly through right seeing. Of this passage of some 15,000 characters only the opening and the close were read; about 12,700 characters of the argument in between were not.

इदं ते नातपस्काय नाभक्ताय कदाचन । न चाशुश्रूषवे वाच्यं न च मां योऽभ्यसूयति ॥ ६७ ॥
idaṃ te nātapaskāya nābhaktāya kadācana | na cāśuśrūṣave vācyaṃ na ca māṃ yo 'bhyasūyati || 67 ||
Padaccheda
इदम् ते न अतपस्काय न अभक्ताय कदाचन न च अशुश्रूषवे वाच्यम् न च माम् यः अभ्यसूयति
idam te na atapaskāya na abhaktāya kadācana na ca aśuśrūṣave vācyam na ca mām yaḥ abhyasūyati
Word by word
This is never to be told by you to one without austerity, nor to one without devotion, nor to one who does not wish to hear, nor to one who finds fault with me.

Note Śaṅkara: having summed up the teaching, he gives the rule for handing it on. He takes te as ‘for your good’: this teaching was told for your good. ‘Without devotion’: to teacher and God. Finding fault with me: taking Vāsudeva for an ordinary man and not bearing his lordship. It is to be told to one who serves and is devoted and has either austerity or intelligence.

य इदं परमं गुह्यं मद्भक्तेष्वभिधास्यति । भक्तिं मयि परां कृत्वा मामेवैष्यत्यसंशयः ॥ ६८ ॥
ya idaṃ paramaṃ guhyaṃ madbhakteṣvabhidhāsyati | bhaktiṃ mayi parāṃ kṛtvā māmevaiṣyatyasaṃśayaḥ || 68 ||
Padaccheda
यः इदम् परमम् गुह्यम् मत्-भक्तेषु अभिधास्यति भक्तिम् मयि पराम् कृत्वा माम् एव एष्यति असंशयः
yaḥ idam paramam guhyam mat-bhakteṣu abhidhāsyati bhaktim mayi parām kṛtvā mām eva eṣyati asaṃśayaḥ
Word by word
He who will teach this supreme secret among my devotees, having shown supreme devotion to me, will come to me alone, without doubt.

Note Śaṅkara: this is the fruit for the one who hands the teaching on. The secret is this work, the dialogue of Keśava and Arjuna; to teach it is to establish it in text and meaning, as the Lord did in Arjuna. Devotion is named again to show that by devotion alone one becomes fit to receive it. He teaches thinking ‘I am serving the Lord, the supreme teacher’. He reads imam for idam.

न च तस्मान्मनुष्येषु कश्चिन्मे प्रियकृत्तमः । भविता न च मे तस्मादन्यः प्रियतरो भुवि ॥ ६९ ॥
na ca tasmānmanuṣyeṣu kaścinme priyakṛttamaḥ | bhavitā na ca me tasmādanyaḥ priyataro bhuvi || 69 ||
Padaccheda
न च तस्मात् मनुष्येषु कश्चित् मे प्रिय-कृत्तमः भविता न च मे तस्मात् अन्यः प्रियतरः भुवि
na ca tasmāt manuṣyeṣu kaścit me priya-kṛttamaḥ bhavitā na ca me tasmāt anyaḥ priyataraḥ bhuvi
Word by word
There is no one among men who does what is dearer to me than he; nor will there be another on earth dearer to me than he.

Note Śaṅkara: than the one who hands on the teaching; none among those now living, and none in time to come.

अध्येष्यते च य इमं धर्म्यं संवादमावयोः । ज्ञानयज्ञेन तेनाहमिष्टः स्यामिति मे मतिः ॥ ७० ॥
adhyeṣyate ca ya imaṃ dharmyaṃ saṃvādamāvayoḥ | jñānayajñena tenāhamiṣṭaḥ syāmiti me matiḥ || 70 ||
Padaccheda
अध्येष्यते च यः इमम् धर्म्यम् संवादम् आवयोः ज्ञान-यज्ञेन तेन अहम् इष्टः स्याम् इति मे मतिः
adhyeṣyate ca yaḥ imam dharmyam saṃvādam āvayoḥ jñāna-yajñena tena aham iṣṭaḥ syām iti me matiḥ
Word by word
And he who will study this dialogue of ours, which accords with dharma, by him I would be worshipped with the sacrifice of knowledge: such is my conviction.

Note Śaṅkara: of the sacrifices, the ritual, the muttered, the whispered and the mental, the sacrifice of knowledge is the best because it is mental; so this praises the study of the Gītā. Or it states the fruit: it equals that of a sacrifice of knowledge directed to a deity.

श्रद्धावाननसूयश्च शृणुयादपि यो नरः । सोऽपि मुक्तः शुभांल्लोकान्प्राप्नुयात्पुण्यकर्मणाम् ॥ ७१ ॥
śraddhāvān anasūyaś ca śṛṇuyādapi yo naraḥ | so 'pi muktaḥ śubhāṃllokānprāpnuyātpuṇyakarmaṇām || 71 ||
Padaccheda
श्रद्धावान् अनसूयः च शृणुयात् अपि यः नरः सः अपि मुक्तः शुभान् लोकान् प्राप्नुयात् पुण्य-कर्मणाम्
śraddhāvān anasūyaḥ ca śṛṇuyāt api yaḥ naraḥ saḥ api muktaḥ śubhān lokān prāpnuyāt puṇya-karmaṇām
Word by word
And the man who, full of faith and free from cavil, merely hears it, he too, freed, would attain the happy worlds of those of meritorious deeds.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘merely’: how much more one who knows its meaning. Freed: from sin. Those of meritorious deeds are performers of the Agnihotra and the like.

कच्चिदेतच्छ्रुतं पार्थ त्वयैकाग्रेण चेतसा । कच्चिदज्ञानसंमोहः प्रनष्टस्ते धनंजय ॥ ७२ ॥
kaccidetacchrutaṃ pārtha tvayaikāgreṇa cetasā | kaccidajñānasaṃmohaḥ pranaṣṭaste dhanaṃjaya || 72 ||
Padaccheda
कच्चित् एतत् श्रुतम् पार्थ त्वया एक-अग्रेण चेतसा कच्चित् अज्ञान-संमोहः प्रनष्टः ते धनंजय
kaccit etat śrutam pārtha tvayā eka-agreṇa cetasā kaccit ajñāna-saṃmohaḥ pranaṣṭaḥ te dhanaṃjaya
Word by word
Has this been heard by you, O Pārtha, with a one-pointed mind? Has your delusion born of ignorance been destroyed, O winner of wealth?

Note Śaṅkara: he asks in order to know whether the pupil has grasped the teaching, meaning to teach it again by other means if he has not. This shows the dharma of a teacher: to take further pains until the pupil has reached his end.

अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा त्वत्प्रसादान्मयाच्युत । स्थितोऽस्मि गतसंदेहः करिष्ये वचनं तव ॥ ७३ ॥
naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtirlabdhā tvatprasādānmayācyuta | sthito 'smi gatasaṃdehaḥ kariṣye vacanaṃ tava || 73 ||
Padaccheda
नष्टः मोहः स्मृतिः लब्धा त्वत्-प्रसादात् मया अच्युत स्थितः अस्मि गत-संदेहः करिष्ये वचनम् तव
naṣṭaḥ mohaḥ smṛtiḥ labdhā tvat-prasādāt mayā acyuta sthitaḥ asmi gata-saṃdehaḥ kariṣye vacanam tava
Word by word
Arjuna said: My delusion is destroyed and memory has been gained by me through your grace, O Acyuta. I stand firm, with doubts gone; I shall do your word.

Note Śaṅkara: delusion, born of ignorance, is the cause of all the evil of the cycle of birth and death, hard to cross like the sea. The memory is of the truth of the Self; on gaining it all the knots of the heart are loosed. This answer shows that the fruit of knowing the whole teaching is just this: the end of delusion and the gaining of the memory of the Self. ‘I stand in your command; by your grace I have reached my end; nothing remains for me to do.’

संजय उवाचsaṃjaya uvāca
इत्यहं वासुदेवस्य पार्थस्य च महात्मनः । संवादमिममश्रौषमद्भुतं रोमहर्षणम् ॥ ७४ ॥
ityahaṃ vāsudevasya pārthasya ca mahātmanaḥ | saṃvādamimamaśrauṣamadbhutaṃ romaharṣaṇam || 74 ||
Padaccheda
इति अहम् वासुदेवस्य पार्थस्य च महा-आत्मनः संवादम् इमम् अश्रौषम् अद्भुतम् रोम-हर्षणम्
iti aham vāsudevasya pārthasya ca mahā-ātmanaḥ saṃvādam imam aśrauṣam adbhutam roma-harṣaṇam
Word by word
Saṃjaya said: Thus I heard this wonderful dialogue of Vāsudeva and the great-souled Pārtha, which makes the hair stand on end.

Note Śaṅkara: the teaching is complete; Saṃjaya now speaks to show its link with the story.

व्यासप्रसादाच्छ्रुतवानेतद्गुह्यमहं परम् । योगं योगेश्वरात्कृष्णात्साक्षात्कथयतः स्वयम् ॥ ७५ ॥
vyāsaprasādācchrutavānetadguhyamahaṃ param | yogaṃ yogeśvarātkṛṣṇātsākṣātkathayataḥ svayam || 75 ||
Padaccheda
व्यास-प्रसादात् श्रुतवान् एतत् गुह्यम् अहम् परम् योगम् योग-ईश्वरात् कृष्णात् साक्षात् कथयतः स्वयम्
vyāsa-prasādāt śrutavān etat guhyam aham param yogam yoga-īśvarāt kṛṣṇāt sākṣāt kathayataḥ svayam
Word by word
By the grace of Vyāsa I heard this supreme secret, this yoga, directly from Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of yoga, as he himself declared it.

Note Śaṅkara: by Vyāsa's grace he had gained divine sight. The work is called yoga because yoga is its purpose; or the dialogue is itself yoga. ‘Directly’: not by hearsay. He reads imaṃ guhyatamaṃ param, ‘this most secret, supreme’.

राजन्संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य संवादमिममद्भुतम् । केशवार्जुनयोः पुण्यं हृष्यामि च मुहुर्मुहुः ॥ ७६ ॥
rājansaṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya saṃvādamimamadbhutam | keśavārjunayoḥ puṇyaṃ hṛṣyāmi ca muhurmuhuḥ || 76 ||
Padaccheda
राजन् संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य संवादम् इमम् अद्भुतम् केशव-अर्जुनयोः पुण्यम् हृष्यामि च मुहुः मुहुः
rājan saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya saṃvādam imam adbhutam keśava-arjunayoḥ puṇyam hṛṣyāmi ca muhuḥ muhuḥ
Word by word
O king, remembering again and again this wonderful and holy dialogue of Keśava and Arjuna, I rejoice again and again.

Note Śaṅkara: the king is Dhṛtarāṣṭra. Holy: it removes sin even by being heard.

तच्च संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य रूपमत्यद्भुतं हरेः । विस्मयो मे महान्राजन्हृष्यामि च पुनः पुनः ॥ ७७ ॥
tacca saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya rūpamatyadbhutaṃ hareḥ | vismayo me mahānrājan hṛṣyāmi ca punaḥ punaḥ || 77 ||
Padaccheda
तत् च संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य रूपम् अति-अद्भुतम् हरेः विस्मयः मे महान् राजन् हृष्यामि च पुनः पुनः
tat ca saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya rūpam ati-adbhutam hareḥ vismayaḥ me mahān rājan hṛṣyāmi ca punaḥ punaḥ
Word by word
And remembering again and again that most wonderful form of Hari, great is my wonder, O king, and I rejoice again and again.

Note Śaṅkara: the form is the form of the universe (adhyāya 11).

यत्र योगेश्वरः कृष्णो यत्र पार्थो धनुर्धरः । तत्र श्रीर्विजयो भूतिर्ध्रुवा नीतिर्मतिर्मम ॥ ७८ ॥
yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇo yatra pārtho dhanurdharaḥ | tatra śrīrvijayo bhūtirdhruvā nītirmatirmama || 78 ||
Padaccheda
यत्र योग-ईश्वरः कृष्णः यत्र पार्थः धनुः-धरः तत्र श्रीः विजयः भूतिः ध्रुवा नीतिः मतिः मम
yatra yoga-īśvaraḥ kṛṣṇaḥ yatra pārthaḥ dhanuḥ-dharaḥ tatra śrīḥ vijayaḥ bhūtiḥ dhruvā nītiḥ matiḥ mama
Word by word
Where Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of yoga, is, and where Pārtha, the bearer of the bow, is, there are fortune, victory, prosperity and unfailing right conduct: such is my conviction.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘where’: on whichever side. He is the Lord of all yogas, since the seed of all yoga springs from him. The bow is Gāṇḍīva. bhūti is the further growth of fortune.
