oṃ saha nāvavatu | saha nau bhunaktu | saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai | tejasvi nāvadhītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai || oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
Padaccheda
ॐ सह नौ अवतु सह नौ भुनक्तु सह वीर्यम् करवावहै तेजस्वि नौ अधीतम् अस्तु मा विद्विषावहै ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः
oṃ saha nau avatu saha nau bhunaktu saha vīryam karavāvahai tejasvi nau adhītam astu mā vidviṣāvahai oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ
Word by word
Om. May He protect us both together. May He nourish us both together. May we work together with vigour. May what we study be full of light. May we never bear ill will towards each other. Om. Peace, peace, peace.

Note The peace invocation of teacher and student, recited before and after the study of this Upaniṣad. It is not part of the DCS text; the wording is that of the edition of Śaṅkara's bhāṣya, which prints it at the opening and again after the last verse.

Note Śaṅkara's Kaṭha bhāṣya does not comment on this mantra.

प्रथमा वल्ली
Prathamā vallī · Adhyāya 1 · First vallī
उशन्ह वै वाजश्रवसः सर्ववेदसं ददौ । तस्य ह नचिकेता नाम पुत्र आस ॥ १ ॥
uśan ha vai vājaśravasaḥ sarvavedasaṃ dadau | tasya ha naciketā nāma putra āsa || 1 ||
Padaccheda
उशन् ह वै वाजश्रवसः सर्ववेदसम् ददौ तस्य ह नचिकेताः नाम पुत्रः आस
uśan ha vai vājaśravasaḥ sarvavedasam dadau tasya ha naciketāḥ nāma putraḥ āsa
Word by word
Desiring its reward, Vājaśravasa, it is told, gave away all that he owned. He had a son named Naciketas.

Note Śaṅkara: uśan is ‘desiring’ the fruit of the Viśvajit sacrifice, in which all one's wealth is given away. vājaśravasaḥ is taken as a nominative, ‘the descendant of Vājaśravas’, a man whose fame (śravas) came from gifts of food (vāja). ha and vai recall an event of the past.

तं ह कुमारं सन्तं दक्षिणासु नीयमानासु श्रद्धाविवेश । सोऽमन्यत ॥ २ ॥
taṃ ha kumāraṃ santaṃ dakṣiṇāsu nīyamānāsu śraddhāviveśa | so 'manyata || 2 ||
Padaccheda
तम् ह कुमारम् सन्तम् दक्षिणासु नीयमानासु श्रद्धा आविवेश सः अमन्यत
tam ha kumāram santam dakṣiṇāsu nīyamānāsu śraddhā āviveśa saḥ amanyata
Word by word
Though he was still a boy, faith entered him as the cows were being led away as gifts to the priests, and he thought:

Note Śaṅkara: śraddhā is āstikya-buddhi, the conviction that what the scriptures teach is true, roused by the wish for his father's good. The dakṣiṇās are the cows being brought, in their portions, for the priests.

पीतोदका जग्धतृणा दुग्धदोहा निरिन्द्रियाः । अनन्दा नाम ते लोकास्तान्स गच्छति ता ददत् ॥ ३ ॥
pītodakā jagdhatṛṇā dugdhadohā nirindriyāḥ | anandā nāma te lokās tān sa gacchati tā dadat || 3 ||
Padaccheda
पीत-उदकाः जग्ध-तृणाः दुग्ध-दोहाः निरिन्द्रियाः अनन्दाः नाम ते लोकाः तान् सः गच्छति ताः ददत्
pīta-udakāḥ jagdha-tṛṇāḥ dugdha-dohāḥ nirindriyāḥ anandāḥ nāma te lokāḥ tān saḥ gacchati tāḥ dadat
Word by word
“Their water drunk, their grass eaten, their milk milked, their strength gone: joyless indeed are the worlds to which he goes who gives such cows.”

Note Śaṅkara: nirindriyāḥ means no longer able to bear calves: old, barren cows.

स होवाच पितरं तत कस्मै मां दास्यसीति । द्वितीयं तृतीयम् । तं होवाच मृत्यवे त्वा ददामीति ॥ ४ ॥
sa hovāca pitaraṃ tata kasmai māṃ dāsyasīti | dvitīyaṃ tṛtīyam | taṃ hovāca mṛtyave tvā dadāmīti || 4 ||
Padaccheda
सः ह उवाच पितरम् तत कस्मै माम् दास्यसि इति द्वितीयम् तृतीयम् तम् ह उवाच मृत्यवे त्वा ददामि इति
saḥ ha uvāca pitaram tata kasmai mām dāsyasi iti dvitīyam tṛtīyam tam ha uvāca mṛtyave tvā dadāmi iti
Word by word
He said to his father, “Father, to whom will you give me?” He asked a second time and a third. The father said to him, “To Death I give you.”

Note Śaṅkara: the boy offers himself so that the sacrifice may not fall short and bring harm to his father. Ignored at first, he asks again and again, and the father answers in anger.

बहूनामेमि प्रथमो बहूनामेमि मध्यमः । किं स्विद्यमस्य कर्तव्यं यन्मयाद्य करिष्यति ॥ ५ ॥
bahūnām emi prathamo bahūnām emi madhyamaḥ | kiṃ svid yamasya kartavyaṃ yan mayādya kariṣyati || 5 ||
Padaccheda
बहूनाम् एमि प्रथमः बहूनाम् एमि मध्यमः किम् स्वित् यमस्य कर्तव्यम् यत् मया अद्य करिष्यति
bahūnām emi prathamaḥ bahūnām emi madhyamaḥ kim svit yamasya kartavyam yat mayā adya kariṣyati
Word by word
“Among many I go as the first, and among many as the middlemost. What purpose of Yama's is there that my father will accomplish through me today?”

Note Śaṅkara: Naciketas reflects by himself. Among many sons or pupils his conduct is that of the best, or at least of the middling, never of the worst. His father spoke in anger and with no purpose in view; even so, his father's word must not prove false.

अनुपश्य यथा पूर्वे प्रतिपश्य तथापरे । सस्यमिव मर्त्यः पच्यते सस्यमिवाजायते पुनः ॥ ६ ॥
anupaśya yathā pūrve pratipaśya tathāpare | sasyam iva martyaḥ pacyate sasyam ivājāyate punaḥ || 6 ||
Padaccheda
अनुपश्य यथा पूर्वे प्रतिपश्य तथा अपरे सस्यम् इव मर्त्यः पच्यते सस्यम् इव आजायते पुनः
anupaśya yathā pūrve pratipaśya tathā apare sasyam iva martyaḥ pacyate sasyam iva ājāyate punaḥ
Word by word
“Consider how those before us acted, and see how others act now. Like corn a mortal ripens, and like corn he is born again.”

Note Śaṅkara: Naciketas says this to his father. Neither the forefathers nor good men now living have gone back on their word. Since man ripens and dies and is born again like corn, nothing in this passing world is gained by a falsehood: keep your word and send me to Yama.

वैश्वानरः प्रविशति अतिथिर्ब्राह्मणो गृहान् । तस्यैतां शान्तिं कुर्वन्ति हर वैवस्वतोदकम् ॥ ७ ॥
vaiśvānaraḥ praviśati atithir brāhmaṇo gṛhān | tasyaitāṃ śāntiṃ kurvanti hara vaivasvatodakam || 7 ||
Padaccheda
वैश्वानरः प्रविशति अतिथिः ब्राह्मणः गृहान् तस्य एताम् शान्तिम् कुर्वन्ति हर वैवस्वत उदकम्
vaiśvānaraḥ praviśati atithiḥ brāhmaṇaḥ gṛhān tasya etām śāntim kurvanti hara vaivasvata udakam
Word by word
“A brahmin guest enters a house as fire itself, and people make this offering to appease him. Bring water, O son of Vivasvat!”

Note Śaṅkara: sent by his father, Naciketas went to Yama's abode and stayed three nights while Yama was away. When Yama returned, his ministers or his wife said this to him. The appeasement is the offering of water for the feet, a seat and the like, which calms the guest as water calms a fire.

आशाप्रतीक्षे संगतं सूनृतां च इष्टापूर्ते पुत्रपशूंश्च सर्वान् । एतद्वृङ्क्ते पुरुषस्याल्पमेधसो यस्यानश्नन्वसति ब्राह्मणो गृहे ॥ ८ ॥
āśāpratīkṣe saṃgataṃ sūnṛtāṃ ca iṣṭāpūrte putrapaśūṃś ca sarvān | etad vṛṅkte puruṣasyālpamedhaso yasyānaśnan vasati brāhmaṇo gṛhe || 8 ||
Padaccheda
आशा-प्रतीक्षे संगतम् सूनृताम् च इष्टापूर्ते पुत्र-पशून् च सर्वान् एतत् वृङ्क्ते पुरुषस्य अल्पमेधसः यस्य अन्-अश्नन् वसति ब्राह्मणः गृहे
āśā-pratīkṣe saṃgatam sūnṛtām ca iṣṭāpūrte putra-paśūn ca sarvān etat vṛṅkte puruṣasya alpamedhasaḥ yasya an-aśnan vasati brāhmaṇaḥ gṛhe
Word by word
“Hope and expectation, the fruit of good company and of kindly speech, of sacrifices and of works for the public good, and all his sons and cattle: all this is destroyed for the man of little understanding in whose house a brahmin stays without food.”

Note Śaṅkara: āśā is the wish for a desired thing not yet known to be within reach; pratīkṣā is the awaiting of one that is. saṃgata, sūnṛtā and iṣṭāpūrta stand for their fruits: what comes of keeping good company, of pleasant speech, of sacrifice, and of works such as the laying out of gardens.

तिस्रो रात्रीर्यदवात्सीर्गृहे मे अनश्नन्ब्रह्मन्नतिथिर्नमस्यः । नमस्तेऽस्तु ब्रह्मन्स्वस्ति मेऽस्तु तस्मात्प्रति त्रीन्वरान्वृणीष्व ॥ ९ ॥
tisro rātrīr yad avātsīr gṛhe me anaśnan brahmann atithir namasyaḥ | namas te 'stu brahman svasti me 'stu tasmāt prati trīn varān vṛṇīṣva || 9 ||
Padaccheda
तिस्रः रात्रीः यत् अवात्सीः गृहे मे अन्-अश्नन् ब्रह्मन् अतिथिः नमस्यः नमः ते अस्तु ब्रह्मन् स्वस्ति मे अस्तु तस्मात् प्रति त्रीन् वरान् वृणीष्व
tisraḥ rātrīḥ yat avātsīḥ gṛhe me an-aśnan brahman atithiḥ namasyaḥ namaḥ te astu brahman svasti me astu tasmāt prati trīn varān vṛṇīṣva
Word by word
“Since you have stayed three nights in my house without food, O brahmin, a guest worthy of reverence, homage to you, O brahmin, and may it be well with me. Therefore choose three boons, one for each night.”

Note Śaṅkara: Yama says this, approaching Naciketas with honour. prati: a boon in return for each night spent without food.

शान्तसङ्कल्पः सुमना यथा स्याद्वीतमन्युर्गौतमो माभि मृत्यो । त्वत्प्रसृष्टं माभिवदेत्प्रतीत एतत्त्रयाणां प्रथमं वरं वृणे ॥ १० ॥
śāntasaṅkalpaḥ sumanā yathā syād vītamanyur gautamo mābhi mṛtyo | tvatprasṛṣṭaṃ mābhivadet pratīta etat trayāṇāṃ prathamaṃ varaṃ vṛṇe || 10 ||
Padaccheda
शान्त-सङ्कल्पः सु-मनाः यथा स्यात् वीत-मन्युः गौतमः मा अभि मृत्यो त्वद्-प्रसृष्टम् मा अभिवदेत् प्रतीतः एतत् त्रयाणाम् प्रथमम् वरम् वृणे
śānta-saṅkalpaḥ su-manāḥ yathā syāt vīta-manyuḥ gautamaḥ mā abhi mṛtyo tvad-prasṛṣṭam mā abhivadet pratītaḥ etat trayāṇām prathamam varam vṛṇe
Word by word
“O Death, may Gautama, my father, be free of anxiety, glad at heart and without anger towards me; and when you send me back, may he recognise me and greet me. This I choose as the first of the three boons.”

Note Śaṅkara: śāntasaṃkalpa: his anxious thought, ‘what will become of my son, gone to Yama?’, laid to rest. pratīta: with memory restored, recognising ‘this is my own son, come back’.

यथा पुरस्ताद्भविता प्रतीत औद्दालकिरारुणिर्मत्प्रसृष्टः । सुखं रात्रीः शयिता वीतमन्युस्त्वां ददृशिवान्मृत्युमुखात्प्रमुक्तम् ॥ ११ ॥
yathā purastād bhavitā pratīta auddālakir āruṇir matprasṛṣṭaḥ | sukhaṃ rātrīḥ śayitā vītamanyus tvāṃ dadṛśivān mṛtyumukhāt pramuktam || 11 ||
Padaccheda
यथा पुरस्तात् भविता प्रतीतः औद्दालकिः आरुणिः मद्-प्रसृष्टः सुखम् रात्रीः शयिता वीत-मन्युः त्वाम् ददृशिवान् मृत्यु-मुखात् प्रमुक्तम्
yathā purastāt bhavitā pratītaḥ auddālakiḥ āruṇiḥ mad-prasṛṣṭaḥ sukham rātrīḥ śayitā vīta-manyuḥ tvām dadṛśivān mṛtyu-mukhāt pramuktam
Word by word
“Auddālaki Āruṇi will recognise you and be towards you as he was before, by my leave. Free from anger, he will sleep happily through the nights, having seen you released from the jaws of Death.”

Note Śaṅkara: Yama speaks. Auddālaki is Uddālaka himself, and Āruṇi ‘the son of Aruṇa’. matprasṛṣṭaḥ: ‘permitted by me’. yathā purastāt: with the same affection he had for you before.

स्वर्गे लोके न भयं किंचनास्ति न तत्र त्वं न जरया बिभेति । उभे तीर्त्वा अशनायापिपासे शोकातिगो मोदते स्वर्गलोके ॥ १२ ॥
svarge loke na bhayaṃ kiṃcanāsti na tatra tvaṃ na jarayā bibheti | ubhe tīrtvā aśanāyāpipāse śokātigo modate svargaloke || 12 ||
Padaccheda
स्वर्गे लोके न भयम् किंचन अस्ति न तत्र त्वम् न जरया बिभेति उभे तीर्त्वा अशनाया-पिपासे शोक-अतिगः मोदते स्वर्ग-लोके
svarge loke na bhayam kiṃcana asti na tatra tvam na jarayā bibheti ubhe tīrtvā aśanāyā-pipāse śoka-atigaḥ modate svarga-loke
Word by word
“In the world of heaven there is no fear at all. You are not there, and no one fears old age. Having crossed beyond both hunger and thirst, and gone beyond sorrow, one rejoices in the world of heaven.”

Note Śaṅkara: Naciketas speaks. ‘You are not there’: Death does not suddenly prevail there, so no one, touched by old age, fears him as people do here. śoka is sorrow of the mind.

स त्वमग्निं स्वर्ग्यमध्येषि मृत्यो प्रब्रूहि तं श्रद्दधानाय मह्यम् । स्वर्गलोका अमृतत्वं भजन्त एतद्द्वितीयेन वृणे वरेण ॥ १३ ॥
sa tvam agniṃ svargyam adhyeṣi mṛtyo prabrūhi taṃ śraddadhānāya mahyam | svargalokā amṛtatvaṃ bhajanta etad dvitīyena vṛṇe vareṇa || 13 ||
Padaccheda
सः त्वम् अग्निम् स्वर्ग्यम् अध्येषि मृत्यो प्रब्रूहि तम् श्रद्दधानाय मह्यम् स्वर्ग-लोकाः अमृत-त्वम् भजन्ते एतत् द्वितीयेन वृणे वरेण
saḥ tvam agnim svargyam adhyeṣi mṛtyo prabrūhi tam śraddadhānāya mahyam svarga-lokāḥ amṛta-tvam bhajante etat dvitīyena vṛṇe vareṇa
Word by word
“You, O Death, know the fire that leads to heaven. Explain it to me, who have faith. Those whose world is heaven attain immortality. This I choose as my second boon.”

Note Śaṅkara: adhyeṣi: ‘you remember, you know’. svargalokāḥ are the sacrificers who win heaven by building this fire. The immortality meant here is the deathlessness of the gods (devatva).

प्र ते ब्रवीमि तदु मे निबोध स्वर्ग्यमग्निं नचिकेतः प्रजानन् । अनन्तलोकाप्तिमथो प्रतिष्ठां विद्धि त्वमेतं निहितं गुहायाम् ॥ १४ ॥
pra te bravīmi tad u me nibodha svargyam agniṃ naciketaḥ prajānan | anantalokāptim atho pratiṣṭhāṃ viddhi tvam etaṃ nihitaṃ guhāyām || 14 ||
Padaccheda
प्र ते ब्रवीमि तत् उ मे निबोध स्वर्ग्यम् अग्निम् नचिकेतः प्रजानन् अनन्त-लोक-आप्तिम् अथ उ प्रतिष्ठाम् विद्धि त्वम् एतम् निहितम् गुहायाम्
pra te bravīmi tat u me nibodha svargyam agnim naciketaḥ prajānan ananta-loka-āptim atha u pratiṣṭhām viddhi tvam etam nihitam guhāyām
Word by word
“I will explain it to you; learn it from me, O Naciketas, for I know well the fire that leads to heaven. Know this fire to be the means of attaining the endless world, and its support, lodged in the cave.”

Note pra … bravīmi is one verb (prabravīmi), its parts separated in the verse.

Note Śaṅkara: Yama asks for attention so as to steady the pupil's mind, and then praises the fire. anantalokāpti: the means of gaining heaven. pratiṣṭhā: the support of the world, in its form as Virāṭ. ‘Lodged in the cave’: seated in the understanding of those who know.

लोकादिमग्निं तमुवाच तस्मै या इष्टका यावतीर्वा यथा वा । स चापि तत्प्रत्यवदद्यथोक्तमथास्य मृत्युः पुनराह तुष्टः ॥ १५ ॥
lokādim agniṃ tam uvāca tasmai yā iṣṭakā yāvatīr vā yathā vā | sa cāpi tat pratyavadad yathoktam athāsya mṛtyuḥ punar āha tuṣṭaḥ || 15 ||
Padaccheda
लोक-आदिम् अग्निम् तम् उवाच तस्मै याः इष्टकाः यावतीः वा यथा वा सः च अपि तत् प्रत्यवदत् यथा उक्तम् अथ अस्य मृत्युः पुनः आह तुष्टः
loka-ādim agnim tam uvāca tasmai yāḥ iṣṭakāḥ yāvatīḥ vā yathā vā saḥ ca api tat pratyavadat yathā uktam atha asya mṛtyuḥ punaḥ āha tuṣṭaḥ
Word by word
Death told him of that fire, the beginning of the worlds: which bricks, how many, and how they are laid. And Naciketas repeated it just as it had been told. Then Death, pleased with him, spoke again.

Note Śaṅkara: this verse is the Upaniṣad's own narration. lokādi: the first of the worlds, being the first to take a body. Pleased by the exact repetition, Death wishes to give a boon beyond the three.

तमब्रवीत्प्रीयमाणो महात्मा वरं तवेहाद्य ददामि भूयः । तवैव नाम्ना भवितायमग्निः सृङ्कां चेमामनेकरूपां गृहाण ॥ १६ ॥
tam abravīt prīyamāṇo mahātmā varaṃ tavehādya dadāmi bhūyaḥ | tavaiva nāmnā bhavitāyam agniḥ sṛṅkāṃ cemām anekarūpāṃ gṛhāṇa || 16 ||
Padaccheda
तम् अब्रवीत् प्रीयमाणः महात्मा वरम् तव इह अद्य ददामि भूयः तव एव नाम्ना भविता अयम् अग्निः सृङ्काम् च इमाम् अनेक-रूपाम् गृहाण
tam abravīt prīyamāṇaḥ mahātmā varam tava iha adya dadāmi bhūyaḥ tava eva nāmnā bhavitā ayam agniḥ sṛṅkām ca imām aneka-rūpām gṛhāṇa
Word by word
Pleased, the great-souled one said to him: “Here and now I give you one boon more. This fire will be known by your name alone. Accept, too, this chain of many forms.”

Note Śaṅkara: this is a fourth boon, given out of delight at the pupil's fitness. sṛṅkā: a tinkling necklace of jewels; or else a course of ritual action that is not to be despised, that is, further knowledge of rites, which yields many results.

त्रिणाचिकेतस्त्रिभिरेत्य सन्धिं त्रिकर्मकृत्तरति जन्ममृत्यू । ब्रह्मजज्ञं देवमीड्यं विदित्वा निचाय्येमां शान्तिमत्यन्तमेति ॥ १७ ॥
triṇāciketas tribhir etya sandhiṃ trikarmakṛt tarati janmamṛtyū | brahmajajñaṃ devam īḍyaṃ viditvā nicāyyemāṃ śāntim atyantam eti || 17 ||
Padaccheda
त्रिणाचिकेतः त्रिभिः एत्य सन्धिम् त्रि-कर्म-कृत् तरति जन्म-मृत्यू ब्रह्म-ज-ज्ञम् देवम् ईड्यम् विदित्वा निचाय्य इमाम् शान्तिम् अत्यन्तम् एति
triṇāciketaḥ tribhiḥ etya sandhim tri-karma-kṛt tarati janma-mṛtyū brahma-ja-jñam devam īḍyam viditvā nicāyya imām śāntim atyantam eti
Word by word
“One who has thrice built the Nāciketa fire, who has attained union with the three and performs the three duties, crosses beyond birth and death. Knowing that shining one, all-knowing and born of Brahmā, worthy of praise, and realising him, one attains this peace completely.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘the three’ are mother, father and teacher, whose instruction he has duly received; or else the Veda, the smṛti and the conduct of the good; or perception, inference and scripture. The three duties are sacrifice, study and giving. brahmajajña is the all-knowing one born of Hiraṇyagarbha, the fire as Virāṭ. By knowledge and rites together he reaches the state of Virāṭ.

Note The DCS has the word brahmajajñam in its text but gives no analysis for it; the analysis here is ours.

त्रिणाचिकेतस्त्रयमेतद्विदित्वा य एवं विद्वांश्चिनुते नाचिकेतम् । स मृत्युपाशान्पुरतः प्रणोद्य शोकातिगो मोदते स्वर्गलोके ॥ १८ ॥
triṇāciketas trayam etad viditvā ya evaṃ vidvāṃś cinute nāciketam | sa mṛtyupāśān purataḥ praṇodya śokātigo modate svargaloke || 18 ||
Padaccheda
त्रिणाचिकेतः त्रयम् एतत् विदित्वा यः एवम् विद्वान् चिनुते नाचिकेतम् सः मृत्यु-पाशान् पुरतः प्रणोद्य शोक-अतिगः मोदते स्वर्ग-लोके
triṇāciketaḥ trayam etat viditvā yaḥ evam vidvān cinute nāciketam saḥ mṛtyu-pāśān purataḥ praṇodya śoka-atigaḥ modate svarga-loke
Word by word
“He who has thrice built the Nāciketa fire, having known this triad, and who, knowing thus, builds the Nāciketa fire, casts off the snares of death beforehand and, gone beyond sorrow, rejoices in the world of heaven.”

Note Śaṅkara: the triad is the bricks, their number and the manner of laying them (verse 15). The snares of death are unrighteousness, ignorance, attachment, aversion and the like. purataḥ: even before the body falls. The heaven meant is the state of Virāṭ.

एष तेऽग्निर्नचिकेतः स्वर्ग्यो यमवृणीथा द्वितीयेन वरेण । एतमग्निं तवैव प्रवक्ष्यन्ति जनासस्तृतीयं वरं नचिकेतो वृणीष्व ॥ १९ ॥
eṣa te 'gnir naciketaḥ svargyo yam avṛṇīthā dvitīyena vareṇa | etam agniṃ tavaiva pravakṣyanti janāsas tṛtīyaṃ varaṃ naciketo vṛṇīṣva || 19 ||
Padaccheda
एषः ते अग्निः नचिकेतः स्वर्ग्यः यम् अवृणीथाः द्वितीयेन वरेण एतम् अग्निम् तव एव प्रवक्ष्यन्ति जनासः तृतीयम् वरम् नचिकेतः वृणीष्व
eṣaḥ te agniḥ naciketaḥ svargyaḥ yam avṛṇīthāḥ dvitīyena vareṇa etam agnim tava eva pravakṣyanti janāsaḥ tṛtīyam varam naciketaḥ vṛṇīṣva
Word by word
“This, O Naciketas, is the fire that leads to heaven, which you chose as your second boon. People will speak of this fire as yours alone. Choose the third boon, O Naciketas.”

Note janāsaḥ is the Vedic nominative plural of jana.

Note Śaṅkara: this sums up what was given. Until the third boon is given, Yama remains in debt.

येयं प्रेते विचिकित्सा मनुष्ये अस्तीत्येके नायमस्तीति चैके । एतद्विद्यामनुशिष्टस्त्वयाहं वराणामेष वरस्तृतीयः ॥ २० ॥
yeyaṃ prete vicikitsā manuṣye astīty eke nāyam astīti caike | etad vidyām anuśiṣṭas tvayāhaṃ varāṇām eṣa varas tṛtīyaḥ || 20 ||
Padaccheda
या इयम् प्रेते विचिकित्सा मनुष्ये अस्ति इति एके न अयम् अस्ति इति च एके एतत् विद्याम् अनुशिष्टः त्वया अहम् वराणाम् एषः वरः तृतीयः
yā iyam prete vicikitsā manuṣye asti iti eke na ayam asti iti ca eke etat vidyām anuśiṣṭaḥ tvayā aham varāṇām eṣaḥ varaḥ tṛtīyaḥ
Word by word
“There is this doubt about a man who has departed: some say ‘he exists’, and others say ‘he does not’. Taught by you, may I come to know this. Of the boons, this is the third.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘he exists’: some hold that there is a Self apart from body, senses, mind and intellect, which passes on to another body; others deny it. Neither perception nor inference can settle the matter, and the highest human end depends on knowing it.

देवैरत्रापि विचिकित्सितं पुरा न हि सुज्ञेयमणुरेष धर्मः । अन्यं वरं नचिकेतो वृणीष्व मा मोपरोत्सीरति मा सृजैनम् ॥ २१ ॥
devair atrāpi vicikitsitaṃ purā na hi sujñeyam aṇur eṣa dharmaḥ | anyaṃ varaṃ naciketo vṛṇīṣva mā moparotsīr ati mā sṛjainam || 21 ||
Padaccheda
देवैः अत्र अपि विचिकित्सितम् पुरा न हि सु-ज्ञेयम् अणुः एषः धर्मः अन्यम् वरम् नचिकेतः वृणीष्व मा मा उपरोत्सीः अति मा सृज एनम्
devaiḥ atra api vicikitsitam purā na hi su-jñeyam aṇuḥ eṣaḥ dharmaḥ anyam varam naciketaḥ vṛṇīṣva mā mā uparotsīḥ ati mā sṛja enam
Word by word
“Even the gods had doubts about this in ancient times, for it is not easy to understand; subtle is this matter. Choose another boon, O Naciketas. Do not press me; release me from this one.”

Note ati … sṛja is one verb (atisṛja), its parts separated in the verse.

Note Śaṅkara: the dharma spoken of is the thing called the Self. ‘Do not press me’: as a creditor presses his debtor.

देवैरत्रापि विचिकित्सितं किल त्वं च मृत्यो यन्न सुज्ञेयमात्थ । वक्ता चास्य त्वादृगन्यो न लभ्यो नान्यो वरस्तुल्य एतस्य कश्चित् ॥ २२ ॥
devair atrāpi vicikitsitaṃ kila tvaṃ ca mṛtyo yan na sujñeyam āttha | vaktā cāsya tvādṛg anyo na labhyo nānyo varas tulya etasya kaścit || 22 ||
Padaccheda
देवैः अत्र अपि विचिकित्सितम् किल त्वम् च मृत्यो यत् न सु-ज्ञेयम् आत्थ वक्ता च अस्य त्वादृक् अन्यः न लभ्यः न अन्यः वरः तुल्यः एतस्य कश्चित्
devaiḥ atra api vicikitsitam kila tvam ca mṛtyo yat na su-jñeyam āttha vaktā ca asya tvādṛk anyaḥ na labhyaḥ na anyaḥ varaḥ tulyaḥ etasya kaścit
Word by word
“Even the gods, indeed, had doubts about this, and you yourself, O Death, say that it is not easy to understand. No other teacher of it like you can be found, and no other boon at all is equal to this.”

Note Śaṅkara: this boon leads to the highest good, while the fruit of every other is passing.

शतायुषः पुत्रपौत्रान्वृणीष्व बहून्पशून्हस्तिहिरण्यमश्वान् । भूमेर्महदायतनं वृणीष्व स्वयं च जीव शरदो यावदिच्छसि ॥ २३ ॥
śatāyuṣaḥ putrapautrān vṛṇīṣva bahūn paśūn hastihiraṇyam aśvān | bhūmer mahad āyatanaṃ vṛṇīṣva svayaṃ ca jīva śarado yāvad icchasi || 23 ||
Padaccheda
शत-आयुषः पुत्र-पौत्रान् वृणीष्व बहून् पशून् हस्ति-हिरण्यम् अश्वान् भूमेः महत् आयतनम् वृणीष्व स्वयम् च जीव शरदः यावत् इच्छसि
śata-āyuṣaḥ putra-pautrān vṛṇīṣva bahūn paśūn hasti-hiraṇyam aśvān bhūmeḥ mahat āyatanam vṛṇīṣva svayam ca jīva śaradaḥ yāvat icchasi
Word by word
“Choose sons and grandsons who will live a hundred years, many cattle, elephants and gold, and horses. Choose a vast domain of land, and yourself live as many years as you wish.”

Note Śaṅkara: only one who has turned away from the impermanent sphere of rites and their results is fit for knowledge of the Self. Hence these offers, which tempt Naciketas with sons and the rest.

एतत्तुल्यं यदि मन्यसे वरं वृणीष्व वित्तं चिरजीविकां च । महाभूमौ नचिकेतस्त्वमेधि कामानां त्वा कामभाजं करोमि ॥ २४ ॥
etat tulyaṃ yadi manyase varaṃ vṛṇīṣva vittaṃ cirajīvikāṃ ca | mahābhūmau naciketas tvam edhi kāmānāṃ tvā kāmabhājaṃ karomi || 24 ||
Padaccheda
एतत् तुल्यम् यदि मन्यसे वरम् वृणीष्व वित्तम् चिर-जीविकाम् च महा-भूमौ नचिकेतः त्वम् एधि कामानाम् त्वा काम-भाजम् करोमि
etat tulyam yadi manyase varam vṛṇīṣva vittam cira-jīvikām ca mahā-bhūmau naciketaḥ tvam edhi kāmānām tvā kāma-bhājam karomi
Word by word
“If you think of any boon equal to this, choose it; choose wealth and long life as well. Be king, O Naciketas, over a great land. I will make you one who enjoys every desire.”

Note Śaṅkara: edhi, ‘be’, means ‘be king’. The desires are both divine and human; Yama can grant them because his will does not fail.

ये ये कामा दुर्लभा मर्त्यलोके सर्वान्कामांश्छन्दतः प्रार्थयस्व । इमा रामाः सरथाः सतूर्या न हीदृशा लम्भनीया मनुष्यैः । आभिर्मत्प्रत्ताभिः परिचारयस्व नचिकेतो मरणं मानुप्राक्षीः ॥ २५ ॥
ye ye kāmā durlabhā martyaloke sarvān kāmāṃś chandataḥ prārthayasva | imā rāmāḥ sarathāḥ satūryā na hīdṛśā lambhanīyā manuṣyaiḥ | ābhir matprattābhiḥ paricārayasva naciketo maraṇaṃ mānuprākṣīḥ || 25 ||
Padaccheda
ये ये कामाः दुर्लभाः मर्त्य-लोके सर्वान् कामान् छन्दतः प्रार्थयस्व इमाः रामाः स-रथाः स-तूर्याः न हि ईदृशाः लम्भनीयाः मनुष्यैः आभिः मद्-प्रत्ताभिः परिचारयस्व नचिकेतः मरणम् मा अनुप्राक्षीः
ye ye kāmāḥ durlabhāḥ martya-loke sarvān kāmān chandataḥ prārthayasva imāḥ rāmāḥ sa-rathāḥ sa-tūryāḥ na hi īdṛśāḥ lambhanīyāḥ manuṣyaiḥ ābhiḥ mad-prattābhiḥ paricārayasva naciketaḥ maraṇam mā anuprākṣīḥ
Word by word
“Whatever desires are hard to obtain in the world of mortals, ask for them all as you please. Here are delightful women with chariots and musical instruments; such as these are not to be obtained by men. Have yourself served by them, given by me. But, O Naciketas, do not ask about death.”

Note Śaṅkara: rāmāḥ are heavenly nymphs, so called because they delight (ramayanti) men. The question about death is whether or not one exists after dying.

श्वोभावा मर्त्यस्य यदन्तकैतत्सर्वेन्द्रियाणां जरयन्ति तेजः । अपि सर्वं जीवितमल्पमेव तवैव वाहास्तव नृत्यगीते ॥ २६ ॥
śvobhāvā martyasya yad antakaitat sarvendriyāṇāṃ jarayanti tejaḥ | api sarvaṃ jīvitam alpam eva tavaiva vāhās tava nṛtyagīte || 26 ||
Padaccheda
श्वोभावाः मर्त्यस्य यत् अन्तक एतत् सर्व-इन्द्रियाणाम् जरयन्ति तेजः अपि सर्वम् जीवितम् अल्पम् एव तव एव वाहाः तव नृत्य-गीते
śvobhāvāḥ martyasya yat antaka etat sarva-indriyāṇām jarayanti tejaḥ api sarvam jīvitam alpam eva tava eva vāhāḥ tava nṛtya-gīte
Word by word
“These things last only till tomorrow, O Death, and they wear away the vigour of all a mortal's senses. Even a whole life is short indeed. Let the chariots be yours alone, and yours the dance and song.”

Note Śaṅkara: śvobhāvāḥ: enjoyments of which it is doubtful whether they will be there tomorrow. They waste virtue, strength, understanding, vigour and good name. ‘A whole life’: even the lifespan of Brahmā is short.

न वित्तेन तर्पणीयो मनुष्यो लप्स्यामहे वित्तमद्राक्ष्म चेत्त्वा । जीविष्यामो यावदीशिष्यसि त्वं वरस्तु मे वरणीयः स एव ॥ २७ ॥
na vittena tarpaṇīyo manuṣyo lapsyāmahe vittam adrākṣma cet tvā | jīviṣyāmo yāvad īśiṣyasi tvaṃ varas tu me varaṇīyaḥ sa eva || 27 ||
Padaccheda
न वित्तेन तर्पणीयः मनुष्यः लप्स्यामहे वित्तम् अद्राक्ष्म चेत् त्वा जीविष्यामः यावत् ईशिष्यसि त्वम् वरः तु मे वरणीयः सः एव
na vittena tarpaṇīyaḥ manuṣyaḥ lapsyāmahe vittam adrākṣma cet tvā jīviṣyāmaḥ yāvat īśiṣyasi tvam varaḥ tu me varaṇīyaḥ saḥ eva
Word by word
“A man is not to be satisfied by wealth. If we want wealth, we shall have it, now that we have seen you; and we shall live as long as you rule. But the boon I must choose is that one alone.”

Note Śaṅkara: no gain of wealth has ever been seen to content anyone. ‘That one’: the knowledge of the Self.

अजीर्यताममृतानामुपेत्य जीर्यन्मर्त्यः क्वधःस्थः प्रजानन् । अभिध्यायन्वर्णरतिप्रमोदानतिदीर्घे जीविते को रमेत ॥ २८ ॥
ajīryatām amṛtānām upetya jīryan martyaḥ kvadhaḥsthaḥ prajānan | abhidhyāyan varṇaratipramodān atidīrghe jīvite ko rameta || 28 ||
Padaccheda
अजीर्यताम् अमृतानाम् उपेत्य जीर्यन् मर्त्यः कु-अधःस्थः प्रजानन् अभिध्यायन् वर्ण-रति-प्रमोदान् अति-दीर्घे जीविते कः रमेत
ajīryatām amṛtānām upetya jīryan martyaḥ ku-adhaḥsthaḥ prajānan abhidhyāyan varṇa-rati-pramodān ati-dīrghe jīvite kaḥ rameta
Word by word
“Having come near the undecaying immortals, what decaying mortal who dwells on the earth below, and who knows of a higher goal, would delight in an over-long life, once he has reflected on the pleasures of beauty and love?”

Note Śaṅkara: kvadhaḥsthaḥ: ku is the earth, which lies below the sky and the other worlds. He also records another reading, kva tadāsthaḥ, ‘how could he set his heart on such things?’. prajānan: knowing that a higher end is to be had from the immortals. The pleasures are seen, on reflection, to be unlasting.

यस्मिन्निदं विचिकित्सन्ति मृत्यो यत्साम्पराये महति ब्रूहि नस्तत् । योऽयं वरो गूढमनुप्रविष्टो नान्यं तस्मान्नचिकेता वृणीते ॥ २९ ॥
yasminn idaṃ vicikitsanti mṛtyo yat sāmparāye mahati brūhi nas tat | yo 'yaṃ varo gūḍham anupraviṣṭo nānyaṃ tasmān naciketā vṛṇīte || 29 ||
Padaccheda
यस्मिन् इदम् विचिकित्सन्ति मृत्यो यत् साम्पराये महति ब्रूहि नः तत् यः अयम् वरः गूढम् अनुप्रविष्टः न अन्यम् तस्मात् नचिकेताः वृणीते
yasmin idam vicikitsanti mṛtyo yat sāmparāye mahati brūhi naḥ tat yaḥ ayam varaḥ gūḍham anupraviṣṭaḥ na anyam tasmāt naciketāḥ vṛṇīte
Word by word
“That about which they are in doubt, O Death, in the great matter of the passing beyond: tell us that.” This boon, which reaches into what is hidden: no other than this does Naciketas choose.

Note Śaṅkara: sāmparāya is the world beyond; ‘great’, because a great end depends on it. gūḍham anupraviṣṭaḥ: the boon concerning the Self is deep and hard to discern. The last statement is the Upaniṣad's own.

अन्यच्छ्रेयोऽन्यदुतैव प्रेयस्ते उभे नानार्थे पुरुषं सिनीतः । तयोः श्रेय आददानस्य साधु भवति हीयतेऽर्थाद्य उ प्रेयो वृणीते ॥ १ ॥
anyacchreyo 'nyad utaiva preyas te ubhe nānārthe puruṣaṃ sinītaḥ | tayoḥ śreya ādadānasya sādhu bhavati hīyate 'rthād ya u preyo vṛṇīte || 1 ||
Padaccheda
अन्यत् श्रेयः अन्यत् उत एव प्रेयः ते उभे नाना-अर्थे पुरुषम् सिनीतः तयोः श्रेयः आददानस्य साधु भवति हीयते अर्थात् यः उ प्रेयः वृणीते
anyat śreyaḥ anyat uta eva preyaḥ te ubhe nānā-arthe puruṣam sinītaḥ tayoḥ śreyaḥ ādadānasya sādhu bhavati hīyate arthāt yaḥ u preyaḥ vṛṇīte
Word by word
“The good is one thing, and the pleasant quite another. Both, having different ends, bind a person. Of the two, it goes well with him who takes up the good; he who chooses the pleasant falls away from the goal.”

Note Śaṅkara: Yama speaks. śreyas is the highest good (niḥśreyasa), preyas what is more pleasing. Being of the nature of knowledge and of ignorance, the two cannot be followed together by one person. The ‘goal’ is the true and lasting end of man.

श्रेयश्च प्रेयश्च मनुष्यमेतस्तौ सम्परीत्य विविनक्ति धीरः । श्रेयो हि धीरोऽभि प्रेयसो वृणीते प्रेयो मन्दो योगक्षेमाद्वृणीते ॥ २ ॥
śreyaś ca preyaś ca manuṣyam etas tau samparītya vivinakti dhīraḥ | śreyo hi dhīro 'bhi preyaso vṛṇīte preyo mando yogakṣemād vṛṇīte || 2 ||
Padaccheda
श्रेयः च प्रेयः च मनुष्यम् एतः तौ सम्परीत्य विविनक्ति धीरः श्रेयः हि धीरः अभि प्रेयसः वृणीते प्रेयः मन्दः योगक्षेमात् वृणीते
śreyaḥ ca preyaḥ ca manuṣyam etaḥ tau samparītya vivinakti dhīraḥ śreyaḥ hi dhīraḥ abhi preyasaḥ vṛṇīte preyaḥ mandaḥ yogakṣemāt vṛṇīte
Word by word
“Both the good and the pleasant come to a man. The wise one examines them well and tells them apart. The wise chooses the good over the pleasant; the dull chooses the pleasant, for the sake of getting and keeping.”

Note abhi … vṛṇīte is one verb (abhivṛṇīte), its parts separated in the verse.

Note Śaṅkara: the two come as though mixed together, hard for a dull mind to separate; the wise separates them as a swan separates milk from water. yogakṣema: the increase and protection of the body and the like. The pleasant is cattle, sons and so on.

स त्वं प्रियान्प्रियरूपांश्च कामानभिध्यायन्नचिकेतोऽत्यस्राक्षीः । नैतां सृङ्कां वित्तमयीमवाप्तो यस्यां मज्जन्ति बहवो मनुष्याः ॥ ३ ॥
sa tvaṃ priyān priyarūpāṃś ca kāmān abhidhyāyan naciketo 'tyasrākṣīḥ | naitāṃ sṛṅkāṃ vittamayīm avāpto yasyāṃ majjanti bahavo manuṣyāḥ || 3 ||
Padaccheda
सः त्वम् प्रियान् प्रिय-रूपान् च कामान् अभिध्यायन् नचिकेतः अत्यस्राक्षीः न एताम् सृङ्काम् वित्त-मयीम् अवाप्तः यस्याम् मज्जन्ति बहवः मनुष्याः
saḥ tvam priyān priya-rūpān ca kāmān abhidhyāyan naciketaḥ atyasrākṣīḥ na etām sṛṅkām vitta-mayīm avāptaḥ yasyām majjanti bahavaḥ manuṣyāḥ
Word by word
“You, O Naciketas, have reflected on the things men desire, the dear and the pleasing in form, and have let them go. You have not taken this path of wealth, in which many men sink.”

Note Śaṅkara: the dear things are sons and the like, the pleasing ones the nymphs and the rest; Naciketas let them go on seeing that they pass away and have no substance. sṛṅkā here is a base path, trodden by the foolish. (At 1.1.16 he explained the same word as a necklace.)

दूरमेते विपरीते विषूची अविद्या या च विद्येति ज्ञाता । विद्याभीप्सिनं नचिकेतसं मन्ये न त्वा कामा बहवोऽलोलुपन्त ॥ ४ ॥
dūram ete viparīte viṣūcī avidyā yā ca vidyeti jñātā | vidyābhīpsinaṃ naciketasaṃ manye na tvā kāmā bahavo 'lolupanta || 4 ||
Padaccheda
दूरम् एते विपरीते विषूची अविद्या या च विद्या इति ज्ञाता विद्या-अभीप्सिनम् नचिकेतसम् मन्ये न त्वा कामाः बहवः अलोलुपन्त
dūram ete viparīte viṣūcī avidyā yā ca vidyā iti jñātā vidyā-abhīpsinam naciketasam manye na tvā kāmāḥ bahavaḥ alolupanta
Word by word
“Far apart, opposed, and leading different ways are these two: ignorance, and what is known as knowledge. I consider Naciketas one who longs for knowledge, for the many desires did not tear you away.”

Note Śaṅkara: they differ as darkness and light; one leads to the cycle of birth and death, the other to liberation. Ignorance has the pleasant for its object, knowledge the good. alolupanta: the desires did not cut Naciketas off from the path of the good.

अविद्यायामन्तरे वर्तमानाः स्वयं धीराः पण्डितं मन्यमानाः । दन्द्रम्यमाणाः परियन्ति मूढा अन्धेनैव नीयमाना यथान्धाः ॥ ५ ॥
avidyāyām antare vartamānāḥ svayaṃ dhīrāḥ paṇḍitaṃ manyamānāḥ | dandramyamāṇāḥ pariyanti mūḍhā andhenaiva nīyamānā yathāndhāḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
अविद्यायाम् अन्तरे वर्तमानाः स्वयम् धीराः पण्डितम् मन्यमानाः दन्द्रम्यमाणाः परियन्ति मूढाः अन्धेन एव नीयमानाः यथा अन्धाः
avidyāyām antare vartamānāḥ svayam dhīrāḥ paṇḍitam manyamānāḥ dandramyamāṇāḥ pariyanti mūḍhāḥ andhena eva nīyamānāḥ yathā andhāḥ
Word by word
“Living in the midst of ignorance, yet thinking themselves wise and learned, the deluded go round and round, staggering to and fro, like the blind led by one who is himself blind.”

Note Śaṅkara: wrapped in hundreds of snares of craving for sons, cattle and the like, they follow a crooked course through the sufferings of old age, death and disease.

Note The DCS has the word dandramyamāṇāḥ in its text but gives no analysis for it; the analysis here is ours.

न साम्परायः प्रतिभाति बालं प्रमाद्यन्तं वित्तमोहेन मूढम् । अयं लोको नास्ति पर इति मानी पुनः पुनर्वशमापद्यते मे ॥ ६ ॥
na sāmparāyaḥ pratibhāti bālaṃ pramādyantaṃ vittamohena mūḍham | ayaṃ loko nāsti para iti mānī punaḥ punar vaśam āpadyate me || 6 ||
Padaccheda
न साम्परायः प्रतिभाति बालम् प्रमाद्यन्तम् वित्त-मोहेन मूढम् अयम् लोकः न अस्ति परः इति मानी पुनः पुनः वशम् आपद्यते मे
na sāmparāyaḥ pratibhāti bālam pramādyantam vitta-mohena mūḍham ayam lokaḥ na asti paraḥ iti mānī punaḥ punaḥ vaśam āpadyate me
Word by word
“The passing beyond does not show itself to the childish one, heedless and deluded by the delusion of wealth. Thinking ‘there is this world and no other’, he falls under my sway again and again.”

Note Śaṅkara: sāmparāya is the other world, and the scriptural means of reaching it. Such a man is born again and again and remains caught in the unbroken run of suffering that is birth and death. Most people are like this.

श्रवणायापि बहुभिर्यो न लभ्यः शृण्वन्तोऽपि बहवो यं न विद्युः । आश्चर्यो वक्ता कुशलोऽस्य लब्धा आश्चर्यो ज्ञाता कुशलानुशिष्टः ॥ ७ ॥
śravaṇāyāpi bahubhir yo na labhyaḥ śṛṇvanto 'pi bahavo yaṃ na vidyuḥ | āścaryo vaktā kuśalo 'sya labdhā āścaryo jñātā kuśalānuśiṣṭaḥ || 7 ||
Padaccheda
श्रवणाय अपि बहुभिः यः न लभ्यः शृण्वन्तः अपि बहवः यम् न विद्युः आश्चर्यः वक्ता कुशलः अस्य लब्धा आश्चर्यः ज्ञाता कुशल-अनुशिष्टः
śravaṇāya api bahubhiḥ yaḥ na labhyaḥ śṛṇvantaḥ api bahavaḥ yam na vidyuḥ āścaryaḥ vaktā kuśalaḥ asya labdhā āścaryaḥ jñātā kuśala-anuśiṣṭaḥ
Word by word
“He of whom many do not get even to hear, and whom many do not know even when they hear: a wonder is the one who teaches him, and skilful the one who attains him; a wonder is the one who knows him, taught by a skilful teacher.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘he’ is the Self. Among thousands, only one such as Naciketas seeks the good and comes to know the Self.

न नरेणावरेण प्रोक्त एष सुविज्ञेयो बहुधा चिन्त्यमानः । अनन्यप्रोक्ते गतिरत्र नास्त्यणीयान्ह्यतर्क्यमणुप्रमाणात् ॥ ८ ॥
na nareṇāvareṇa prokta eṣa suvijñeyo bahudhā cintyamānaḥ | ananyaprokte gatir atra nāsty aṇīyān hy atarkyam aṇupramāṇāt || 8 ||
Padaccheda
न नरेण अवरेण प्रोक्तः एषः सु-विज्ञेयः बहुधा चिन्त्यमानः अन्-अन्य-प्रोक्ते गतिः अत्र न अस्ति अणीयान् हि अ-तर्क्यम् अणु-प्रमाणात्
na nareṇa avareṇa proktaḥ eṣaḥ su-vijñeyaḥ bahudhā cintyamānaḥ an-anya-prokte gatiḥ atra na asti aṇīyān hi a-tarkyam aṇu-pramāṇāt
Word by word
“Taught by an inferior man, this Self is not easy to understand well, for it is thought of in many ways. When it is taught by one who is not other than it, there is no more wavering about it; for it is subtler than the subtlest and beyond the reach of reasoning.”

Note Śaṅkara: an inferior man is one of ordinary understanding. Disputants think of the Self variously: as existing or not, as agent or not, as pure or impure. ananyaprokte: taught by a teacher who sees no difference, who has become the Brahman he teaches.

Note Śaṅkara gives several senses for gatir atra nāsti: no more doubt of ‘is it or is it not’; nothing further left to be known; no more passage through birth and death; or, dividing the words as agatiḥ, no failure to understand.

नैषा तर्केण मतिरापनेया प्रोक्तान्येनैव सुज्ञानाय प्रेष्ठ । यां त्वमापः सत्यधृतिर्बतासि त्वादृङ्नो भूयान्नचिकेतः प्रष्टा ॥ ९ ॥
naiṣā tarkeṇa matir āpaneyā proktānyenaiva sujñānāya preṣṭha | yāṃ tvam āpaḥ satyadhṛtir batāsi tvādṛṅ no bhūyān naciketaḥ praṣṭā || 9 ||
Padaccheda
न एषा तर्केण मतिः आपनेया प्रोक्ता अन्येन एव सु-ज्ञानाय प्रेष्ठ याम् त्वम् आपः सत्य-धृतिः बत असि त्वादृक् नः भूयात् नचिकेतः प्रष्टा
na eṣā tarkeṇa matiḥ āpaneyā proktā anyena eva su-jñānāya preṣṭha yām tvam āpaḥ satya-dhṛtiḥ bata asi tvādṛk naḥ bhūyāt naciketaḥ praṣṭā
Word by word
“This understanding is not to be reached by reasoning. Taught by another, dearest one, it leads to sound knowledge. You have attained it. Ah, you are of true resolve! May we have a questioner like you, O Naciketas.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘another’ is a teacher versed in the scriptures, as distinct from a mere reasoner. āpaneyā: ‘to be reached’; or else ‘to be set aside’, in which case the sense is that this understanding is not to be given up for reasoning.

Note bhūyāt is a benedictive (āśīrliṅ); it is labelled here as an optative.

जानाम्यहं शेवधिरित्यनित्यं न ह्यध्रुवैः प्राप्यते हि ध्रुवं तत् । ततो मया नाचिकेतश्चितोऽग्निरनित्यैर्द्रव्यैः प्राप्तवानस्मि नित्यम् ॥ १० ॥
jānāmy ahaṃ śevadhir ity anityaṃ na hy adhruvaiḥ prāpyate hi dhruvaṃ tat | tato mayā nāciketaś cito 'gnir anityair dravyaiḥ prāptavān asmi nityam || 10 ||
Padaccheda
जानामि अहम् शेवधिः इति अनित्यम् न हि अध्रुवैः प्राप्यते हि ध्रुवम् तत् ततः मया नाचिकेतः चितः अग्निः अनित्यैः द्रव्यैः प्राप्तवान् अस्मि नित्यम्
jānāmi aham śevadhiḥ iti anityam na hi adhruvaiḥ prāpyate hi dhruvam tat tataḥ mayā nāciketaḥ citaḥ agniḥ anityaiḥ dravyaiḥ prāptavān asmi nityam
Word by word
“I know that what is called treasure is impermanent, for what is stable is not attained by things that are unstable. Therefore I built the Nāciketa fire with impermanent things, and I have attained what is lasting.”

Note Śaṅkara: Yama, pleased, speaks again. The treasure is the fruit of rites. The ‘lasting’ that he gained is the station of Yama, called heaven, which lasts only in a relative sense; the truly stable treasure, the supreme Self, is not reached by such means.

कामस्य आप्तिं जगतः प्रतिष्ठां क्रतोरनन्त्यमभयस्य पारम् । स्तोममहदुरुगायं प्रतिष्ठां दृष्ट्वा धृत्या धीरो नचिकेतोऽत्यस्राक्षीः ॥ ११ ॥
kāmasya āptiṃ jagataḥ pratiṣṭhāṃ krator anantyam abhayasya pāram | stomamahad urugāyaṃ pratiṣṭhāṃ dṛṣṭvā dhṛtyā dhīro naciketo 'tyasrākṣīḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
कामस्य आप्तिम् जगतः प्रतिष्ठाम् क्रतोः अनन्त्यम् अभयस्य पारम् स्तोम-महत् उरु-गायम् प्रतिष्ठाम् दृष्ट्वा धृत्या धीरः नचिकेतः अत्यस्राक्षीः
kāmasya āptim jagataḥ pratiṣṭhām kratoḥ anantyam abhayasya pāram stoma-mahat uru-gāyam pratiṣṭhām dṛṣṭvā dhṛtyā dhīraḥ naciketaḥ atyasrākṣīḥ
Word by word
“The fulfilment of desire, the support of the world, the endless fruit of meditation, the farther shore of fearlessness, great and worthy of praise, far-reaching, a firm standing: having seen all this, O Naciketas, you, being wise, have with firmness let it go.”

Note Śaṅkara: all of this describes the state of Hiraṇyagarbha, the fruit of meditation (kratu here is upāsanā). Naciketas gave up even that, desiring only the highest.

तं दुर्दर्शं गूढमनुप्रविष्टं गुहाहितं गह्वरेष्ठं पुराणम् । अध्यात्मयोगाधिगमेन देवं मत्वा धीरो हर्षशोकौ जहाति ॥ १२ ॥
taṃ durdarśaṃ gūḍham anupraviṣṭaṃ guhāhitaṃ gahvareṣṭhaṃ purāṇam | adhyātmayogādhigamena devaṃ matvā dhīro harṣaśokau jahāti || 12 ||
Padaccheda
तम् दुर्दर्शम् गूढम् अनुप्रविष्टम् गुहा-हितम् गह्वरेष्ठम् पुराणम् अध्यात्म-योग-अधिगमेन देवम् मत्वा धीरः हर्ष-शोकौ जहाति
tam durdarśam gūḍham anupraviṣṭam guhā-hitam gahvareṣṭham purāṇam adhyātma-yoga-adhigamena devam matvā dhīraḥ harṣa-śokau jahāti
Word by word
“Him who is hard to see, who has entered into what is hidden, who is set in the cave and dwells in the deep, the ancient one: knowing that shining one by fixing the mind on the Self, the wise man leaves joy and sorrow behind.”

Note Śaṅkara: this is the Self that Naciketas wishes to know, hidden by the perceptions of ordinary objects. The cave is the intellect, where it is perceived; the ‘deep’ is a hard place crowded with many evils. adhyātmayoga: withdrawing the mind from objects and settling it on the Self. deva is the Self.

एतच्छ्रुत्वा सम्परिगृह्य मर्त्यः प्रवृह्य धर्म्यमणुमेतमाप्य । स मोदते मोदनीयं हि लब्ध्वा विवृतं सद्म नचिकेतसं मन्ये ॥ १३ ॥
etacchrutvā samparigṛhya martyaḥ pravṛhya dharmyam aṇum etam āpya | sa modate modanīyaṃ hi labdhvā vivṛtaṃ sadma naciketasaṃ manye || 13 ||
Padaccheda
एतत् श्रुत्वा सम्परिगृह्य मर्त्यः प्रवृह्य धर्म्यम् अणुम् एतम् आप्य सः मोदते मोदनीयम् हि लब्ध्वा विवृतम् सद्म नचिकेतसम् मन्ये
etat śrutvā samparigṛhya martyaḥ pravṛhya dharmyam aṇum etam āpya saḥ modate modanīyam hi labdhvā vivṛtam sadma naciketasam manye
Word by word
“Having heard this and grasped it fully, having drawn out this subtle Self, which accords with dharma, and attained it, the mortal rejoices, for he has gained what is worth rejoicing in. The abode, I think, stands open for Naciketas.”

Note Śaṅkara: pravṛhya: setting the Self apart from the body and the rest. The abode is Brahman, its door open to Naciketas: ‘I consider you fit for liberation.’

अन्यत्र धर्मादन्यत्राधर्मादन्यत्रास्मात्कृताकृतात् । अन्यत्र भूताच्च भव्याच्च यत्तत्पश्यसि तद्वद ॥ १४ ॥
anyatra dharmād anyatrādharmād anyatrāsmāt kṛtākṛtāt | anyatra bhūtāc ca bhavyāc ca yat tat paśyasi tad vada || 14 ||
Padaccheda
अन्यत्र धर्मात् अन्यत्र अधर्मात् अन्यत्र अस्मात् कृत-अकृतात् अन्यत्र भूतात् च भव्यात् च यत् तत् पश्यसि तत् वद
anyatra dharmāt anyatra adharmāt anyatra asmāt kṛta-akṛtāt anyatra bhūtāt ca bhavyāt ca yat tat paśyasi tat vada
Word by word
“That which you see as apart from righteousness and apart from unrighteousness, apart from this effect and cause, apart from what has been and what is to be: tell me that.”

Note Śaṅkara: Naciketas speaks. dharma is the doing of what scripture enjoins, with its results and its means; kṛta is the effect and akṛta the cause. With past and future the present too is meant: that which the three times do not limit.

सर्वे वेदा यत्पदमामनन्ति तपांसि सर्वाणि च यद्वदन्ति । यदिच्छन्तो ब्रह्मचर्यं चरन्ति तत्ते पदं संग्रहेण ब्रवीमि ओमित्येतत् ॥ १५ ॥
sarve vedā yat padam āmananti tapāṃsi sarvāṇi ca yad vadanti | yad icchanto brahmacaryaṃ caranti tat te padaṃ saṃgraheṇa bravīmi om ity etat || 15 ||
Padaccheda
सर्वे वेदाः यत् पदम् आमनन्ति तपांसि सर्वाणि च यत् वदन्ति यत् इच्छन्तः ब्रह्मचर्यम् चरन्ति तत् ते पदम् संग्रहेण ब्रवीमि ॐ इति एतत्
sarve vedāḥ yat padam āmananti tapāṃsi sarvāṇi ca yat vadanti yat icchantaḥ brahmacaryam caranti tat te padam saṃgraheṇa bravīmi om iti etat
Word by word
“The goal that all the Vedas declare, that all austerities speak of, and desiring which people live the life of a religious student: that goal I tell you in brief. It is this: Om.”

Note The DCS sets “om ity etat” apart as a verse of its own and leaves bravīmi unjoined. Śaṅkara's text and the common editions keep the words in this verse, reading bravīmy om ity etat.

Note Śaṅkara: Death speaks. pada is ‘that which is to be reached’. Austerities ‘speak of’ it in that they are means of attaining it. Om is both the word that denotes it and its symbol.

एतद्ध्येवाक्षरं ब्रह्म एतद्ध्येवाक्षरं परम् । एतद्ध्येवाक्षरं ज्ञात्वा यो यदिच्छति तस्य तत् ॥ १६ ॥
etaddhy evākṣaraṃ brahma etaddhyevākṣaraṃ param | etaddhyevākṣaraṃ jñātvā yo yad icchati tasya tat || 16 ||
Padaccheda
एतत् हि एव अक्षरम् ब्रह्म एतत् हि एव अक्षरम् परम् एतत् हि एव अक्षरम् ज्ञात्वा यः यत् इच्छति तस्य तत्
etat hi eva akṣaram brahma etat hi eva akṣaram param etat hi eva akṣaram jñātvā yaḥ yat icchati tasya tat
Word by word
“This syllable indeed is Brahman; this syllable indeed is the supreme. Knowing this very syllable, whatever one desires is his.”

Note Śaṅkara: the first ‘Brahman’ is the lower Brahman and ‘the supreme’ the higher; the syllable is the symbol of both. One who meditates on it as Brahman gains whichever he seeks: the higher is to be known, the lower to be attained.

एतदालम्बनं श्रेष्ठमेतदालम्बनं परम् । एतदालम्बनं ज्ञात्वा ब्रह्मलोके महीयते ॥ १७ ॥
etad ālambanaṃ śreṣṭham etad ālambanaṃ param | etad ālambanaṃ jñātvā brahmaloke mahīyate || 17 ||
Padaccheda
एतत् आलम्बनम् श्रेष्ठम् एतत् आलम्बनम् परम् एतत् आलम्बनम् ज्ञात्वा ब्रह्म-लोके महीयते
etat ālambanam śreṣṭham etat ālambanam param etat ālambanam jñātvā brahma-loke mahīyate
Word by word
“This support is the best; this support is the highest. Knowing this support, one is honoured in the world of Brahman.”

Note Śaṅkara: it is the best of the supports for attaining Brahman, and relates to both the higher and the lower Brahman. ‘Honoured in the world of Brahman’: having become Brahman, he is to be revered as Brahman.

न जायते म्रियते वा विपश्चिन्नायं कुतश्चिन्न बभूव कश्चित् । अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥ १८ ॥
na jāyate mriyate vā vipaścin nāyaṃ kutaścin na babhūva kaścit | ajo nityaḥ śāśvato 'yaṃ purāṇo na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre || 18 ||
Padaccheda
न जायते म्रियते वा विपश्चित् न अयम् कुतश्चित् न बभूव कश्चित् अजः नित्यः शाश्वतः अयम् पुराणः न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे
na jāyate mriyate vā vipaścit na ayam kutaścit na babhūva kaścit ajaḥ nityaḥ śāśvataḥ ayam purāṇaḥ na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre
Word by word
“The knowing Self is not born, nor does it die. It did not come from anything, nor did anything come from it. Unborn, eternal, everlasting and ancient, it is not slain when the body is slain.”

Note Śaṅkara: vipaścit, ‘intelligent’, because its nature as consciousness is never lost. Birth and death, the first and last of the changes that belong to things that arise, are denied so as to deny all change. purāṇa: ‘new even of old’, that is, without growth. It is in the body as space is.

हन्ता चेन्मन्यते हन्तुं हतश्चेन्मन्यते हतम् । उभौ तौ न विजानीतो नायं हन्ति न हन्यते ॥ १९ ॥
hantā cen manyate hantuṃ hataś cen manyate hatam | ubhau tau na vijānīto nāyaṃ hanti na hanyate || 19 ||
Padaccheda
हन्ता चेत् मन्यते हन्तुम् हतः चेत् मन्यते हतम् उभौ तौ न विजानीतः न अयम् हन्ति न हन्यते
hantā cet manyate hantum hataḥ cet manyate hatam ubhau tau na vijānītaḥ na ayam hanti na hanyate
Word by word
“If the slayer thinks that he slays, and if the slain thinks himself slain, neither of the two knows. This Self does not slay, nor is it slain.”

Note Śaṅkara: both take the body alone to be the Self. The cycle of birth and death, with its merit and demerit, belongs only to one who does not know the Self.

अणोरणीयान्महतो महीयानात्मास्य जन्तोर्निहितो गुहायाम् । तमक्रतुः पश्यति वीतशोको धातुप्रसादान्महिमानमात्मनः ॥ २० ॥
aṇor aṇīyān mahato mahīyān ātmāsya jantor nihito guhāyām | tam akratuḥ paśyati vītaśoko dhātuprasādān mahimānam ātmanaḥ || 20 ||
Padaccheda
अणोः अणीयान् महतः महीयान् आत्मा अस्य जन्तोः निहितः गुहायाम् तम् अक्रतुः पश्यति वीत-शोकः धातु-प्रसादात् महिमानम् आत्मनः
aṇoḥ aṇīyān mahataḥ mahīyān ātmā asya jantoḥ nihitaḥ guhāyām tam akratuḥ paśyati vīta-śokaḥ dhātu-prasādāt mahimānam ātmanaḥ
Word by word
“Subtler than the subtle, greater than the great, the Self is set in the cave of the heart of this creature. One who is free from desire sees that greatness of the Self through the serenity of the mind and senses, and becomes free from sorrow.”

Note Śaṅkara: the cave is the heart, of every creature from Brahmā down to a tuft of grass. akratu: without desire, the mind withdrawn from outer objects, seen and unseen. The dhātus are the mind and the other organs, so called because they sustain the body. The greatness of the Self neither grows nor declines through action; he sees it directly, as ‘I am this’.

आसीनो दूरं व्रजति शयानो याति सर्वतः । कस्तं मदामदं देवं मदन्यो ज्ञातुमर्हति ॥ २१ ॥
āsīno dūraṃ vrajati śayāno yāti sarvataḥ | kas taṃ madāmadaṃ devaṃ mad anyo jñātum arhati || 21 ||
Padaccheda
आसीनः दूरम् व्रजति शयानः याति सर्वतः कः तम् मदामदम् देवम् मत् अन्यः ज्ञातुम् अर्हति
āsīnaḥ dūram vrajati śayānaḥ yāti sarvataḥ kaḥ tam madāmadam devam mat anyaḥ jñātum arhati
Word by word
“Sitting, it travels far; lying down, it goes everywhere. Who other than I can know that shining one, who both rejoices and does not rejoice?”

Note Śaṅkara: because of its limiting adjuncts the Self seems to have opposed qualities, rest and movement, joy and its absence, as a jewel shows many colours. So it is hard to know, except for one of subtle understanding such as Yama. ‘Lying down’: when the organs are at rest and particular cognition has ceased.

अशरीरं शरीरेषु अनवस्थेष्ववस्थितम् । महान्तं विभुमात्मानं मत्वा धीरो न शोचति ॥ २२ ॥
aśarīraṃ śarīreṣu anavastheṣv avasthitam | mahāntaṃ vibhum ātmānaṃ matvā dhīro na śocati || 22 ||
Padaccheda
अशरीरम् शरीरेषु अनवस्थेषु अवस्थितम् महान्तम् विभुम् आत्मानम् मत्वा धीरः न शोचति
aśarīram śarīreṣu anavastheṣu avasthitam mahāntam vibhum ātmānam matvā dhīraḥ na śocati
Word by word
“Knowing the Self as bodiless within bodies, abiding within what does not abide, great and all-pervading, the wise man does not grieve.”

Note Śaṅkara: the bodies are those of gods, ancestors, men and the rest, all impermanent. The word ātman shows that it is not other than oneself: he knows it as ‘I am this’.

नायमात्मा प्रवचनेन लभ्यो न मेधया न बहुना श्रुतेन । यमेवैष वृणुते तेन लभ्यस्तस्यैष आत्मा विवृणुते तनूं स्वाम् ॥ २३ ॥
nāyam ātmā pravacanena labhyo na medhayā na bahunā śrutena | yam evaiṣa vṛṇute tena labhyas tasyaiṣa ātmā vivṛṇute tanūṃ svām || 23 ||
Padaccheda
न अयम् आत्मा प्रवचनेन लभ्यः न मेधया न बहुना श्रुतेन यम् एव एषः वृणुते तेन लभ्यः तस्य एषः आत्मा विवृणुते तनूम् स्वाम्
na ayam ātmā pravacanena labhyaḥ na medhayā na bahunā śrutena yam eva eṣaḥ vṛṇute tena labhyaḥ tasya eṣaḥ ātmā vivṛṇute tanūm svām
Word by word
“This Self is not attained by study of the Vedas, nor by intellect, nor by much learning. It is attained through the very Self that the seeker longs for; to him this Self reveals its own nature.”

Note Śaṅkara: pravacana: mastering many Vedas; medhā: the power to retain the meaning of texts. The Self that this seeker prays for is attained through that same Self: one who, free from desire, seeks the Self alone, gains the Self by the Self. tanū: its true nature.

नाविरतो दुश्चरितान्नाशान्तो नासमाहितः । नाशान्तमानसो वापि प्रज्ञानेनैनमाप्नुयात् ॥ २४ ॥
nāvirato duścaritān nāśānto nāsamāhitaḥ | nāśāntamānaso vāpi prajñānenainam āpnuyāt || 24 ||
Padaccheda
न अ-विरतः दुश्चरितात् न अ-शान्तः न अ-समाहितः न अ-शान्त-मानसः वा अपि प्रज्ञानेन एनम् आप्नुयात्
na a-virataḥ duścaritāt na a-śāntaḥ na a-samāhitaḥ na a-śānta-mānasaḥ vā api prajñānena enam āpnuyāt
Word by word
“One who has not turned away from bad conduct, who is not calm, who is not concentrated, or whose mind is not at peace, cannot attain this Self by knowledge.”

Note Śaṅkara: aśānta: not withdrawn from the restlessness of the senses; asamāhita: with a scattered mind; aśāntamānasa: one whose mind, though concentrated, is still busy seeking the fruits of concentration. One free of these, who has a teacher, attains the Self through the knowledge of Brahman.

यस्य ब्रह्म च क्षत्रं च उभे भवत ओदनः । मृत्युर्यस्योपसेचनं क इत्था वेद यत्र सः ॥ २५ ॥
yasya brahma ca kṣatraṃ ca ubhe bhavata odanaḥ | mṛtyur yasyopasecanaṃ ka itthā veda yatra saḥ || 25 ||
Padaccheda
यस्य ब्रह्म च क्षत्रम् च उभे भवतः ओदनः मृत्युः यस्य उपसेचनम् कः इत्था वेद यत्र सः
yasya brahma ca kṣatram ca ubhe bhavataḥ odanaḥ mṛtyuḥ yasya upasecanam kaḥ itthā veda yatra saḥ
Word by word
“He for whom the brahmin and the warrior orders are both food, and death the sauce upon it: who thus knows where he is?”

Note Śaṅkara: a man of ordinary understanding, without the means just described, cannot know the Self as one who has them does. Brahmin and warrior, upholders of all order and protectors of all, are mere food to it, and Death, who carries off everything, does not even suffice as its sauce.

तृतीया वल्ली

ऋतं पिबन्तौ सुकृतस्य लोके गुहां प्रविष्टौ परमे परार्धे । छायातपौ ब्रह्मविदो वदन्ति पञ्चाग्नयो ये च त्रिणाचिकेताः ॥ १ ॥
ṛtaṃ pibantau sukṛtasya loke guhāṃ praviṣṭau parame parārdhe | chāyātapau brahmavido vadanti pañcāgnayo ye ca triṇāciketāḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
ऋतम् पिबन्तौ सुकृतस्य लोके गुहाम् प्रविष्टौ परमे परार्धे छाया-आतपौ ब्रह्म-विदः वदन्ति पञ्च-अग्नयः ये च त्रिणाचिकेताः
ṛtam pibantau sukṛtasya loke guhām praviṣṭau parame parārdhe chāyā-ātapau brahma-vidaḥ vadanti pañca-agnayaḥ ye ca triṇāciketāḥ
Word by word
“There are two who drink the sure fruit of their own deeds in this world, who have entered the cave, in the supreme abode of the highest. The knowers of Brahman call them shade and light; so do those who keep the five fires and those who have thrice built the Nāciketa fire.”

Note Śaṅkara: the two are the individual self and the supreme Self. Only one of them tastes the fruit of action, but both are said to ‘drink’, as a whole company is called ‘the people with umbrellas’ when only some carry them. ṛta is ‘truth’, the fruit of action, because it is certain to come. loke: in this body. The cave is the intellect; parārdha, ‘the place of the highest Brahman’, is the space within the heart, where Brahman is perceived. Shade and light: unlike each other, as one is bound to the cycle of birth and death and the other is not. Those of the five fires are householders.

यः सेतुरीजानानामक्षरं ब्रह्म यत्परम् । अभयं तितीर्षतां पारं नाचिकेतं शकेमहि ॥ २ ॥
yaḥ setur ījānānām akṣaraṃ brahma yat param | abhayaṃ titīrṣatāṃ pāraṃ nāciketaṃ śakemahi || 2 ||
Padaccheda
यः सेतुः ईजानानाम् अक्षरम् ब्रह्म यत् परम् अभयम् तितीर्षताम् पारम् नाचिकेतम् शकेमहि
yaḥ setuḥ ījānānām akṣaram brahma yat param abhayam titīrṣatām pāram nāciketam śakemahi
Word by word
“May we be able to know the Nāciketa fire, which is the bridge for those who sacrifice; and also the imperishable, supreme Brahman, fearless, the farther shore for those who wish to cross.”

Note Śaṅkara: the fire is a bridge because it carries those devoted to rites across sorrow; ‘may we be able’ to know it and to build it. The supreme Brahman, called the Self, is the refuge of the knowers of Brahman who wish to cross the cycle of birth and death. Both the lower and the higher Brahman are to be known: these are the two spoken of in the first verse.

Note titīrṣatām is a desiderative participle; it is labelled here as a present participle.

आत्मानं रथिनं विद्धि शरीरं रथमेव तु । बुद्धिं तु सारथिं विद्धि मनः प्रग्रहमेव च ॥ ३ ॥
ātmānaṃ rathinaṃ viddhi śarīraṃ ratham eva tu | buddhiṃ tu sārathiṃ viddhi manaḥ pragraham eva ca || 3 ||
Padaccheda
आत्मानम् रथिनम् विद्धि शरीरम् रथम् एव तु बुद्धिम् तु सारथिम् विद्धि मनः प्रग्रहम् एव च
ātmānam rathinam viddhi śarīram ratham eva tu buddhim tu sārathim viddhi manaḥ pragraham eva ca
Word by word
“Know the self as the master of the chariot, and the body as the chariot. Know the intellect as the charioteer, and the mind as the reins.”

Note Śaṅkara: the self meant here is the one bound to the cycle of birth and death, the ‘drinker’ of the first verse, who may travel either to liberation or onward in that cycle. The intellect is what determines; the mind is what forms intentions and doubts, and it holds the senses as reins hold horses.

इन्द्रियाणि हयानाहुर्विषयांस्तेषु गोचरान् । आत्मेन्द्रियमनोयुक्तं भोक्तेत्याहुर्मनीषिणः ॥ ४ ॥
indriyāṇi hayān āhur viṣayāṃsteṣu gocarān | ātmendriyamanoyuktaṃ bhoktety āhur manīṣiṇaḥ || 4 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियाणि हयान् आहुः विषयान् तेषु गोचरान् आत्म-इन्द्रिय-मनः-युक्तम् भोक्ता इति आहुः मनीषिणः
indriyāṇi hayān āhuḥ viṣayān teṣu gocarān ātma-indriya-manaḥ-yuktam bhoktā iti āhuḥ manīṣiṇaḥ
Word by word
“The senses, they say, are the horses, and the objects of the senses are the roads they travel. The self joined with body, senses and mind is what the wise call the enjoyer.”

Note Śaṅkara: ātman in the compound means the body. The Self by itself is no enjoyer; it is an enjoyer only through the adjuncts of intellect and the rest. Only so can it be understood, further on, that the state of Viṣṇu is one's own Self.

यस्त्वविज्ञानवान्भवत्ययुक्तेन मनसा सदा । तस्येन्द्रियाण्यवश्यानि दुष्टाश्वा इव सारथेः ॥ ५ ॥
yas tv avijñānavān bhavaty ayuktena manasā sadā | tasyendriyāṇy avaśyāni duṣṭāśvā iva sāratheḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
यः तु अ-विज्ञानवान् भवति अयुक्तेन मनसा सदा तस्य इन्द्रियाणि अवश्यानि दुष्ट-अश्वाः इव सारथेः
yaḥ tu a-vijñānavān bhavati ayuktena manasā sadā tasya indriyāṇi avaśyāni duṣṭa-aśvāḥ iva sāratheḥ
Word by word
“But he who is without discernment, his mind always unrestrained: his senses are out of control, like the bad horses of a charioteer.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘without discernment’ is said of the charioteer, the intellect, when it is unskilled in what to pursue and what to avoid. The unrestrained mind is the reins not held.

यस्तु विज्ञानवान्भवति युक्तेन मनसा सदा । तस्येन्द्रियाणि वश्यानि सदश्वा इव सारथेः ॥ ६ ॥
yas tu vijñānavān bhavati yuktena manasā sadā | tasyendriyāṇi vaśyāni sadaśvā iva sāratheḥ || 6 ||
Padaccheda
यः तु विज्ञानवान् भवति युक्तेन मनसा सदा तस्य इन्द्रियाणि वश्यानि सत्-अश्वाः इव सारथेः
yaḥ tu vijñānavān bhavati yuktena manasā sadā tasya indriyāṇi vaśyāni sat-aśvāḥ iva sāratheḥ
Word by word
“But he who has discernment, his mind always restrained: his senses are under control, like the good horses of a charioteer.”

Note Śaṅkara: such senses can be set to work or drawn back at will, like well-trained horses.

यस्त्वविज्ञानवान्भवत्यमनस्कः सदाशुचिः । न स तत्पदमाप्नोति संसारं चाधिगच्छति ॥ ७ ॥
yas tv avijñānavān bhavaty amanaskaḥ sadāśuciḥ | na sa tat padam āpnoti saṃsāraṃ cādhigacchati || 7 ||
Padaccheda
यः तु अ-विज्ञानवान् भवति अमनस्कः सदा अशुचिः न सः तत् पदम् आप्नोति संसारम् च अधिगच्छति
yaḥ tu a-vijñānavān bhavati amanaskaḥ sadā aśuciḥ na saḥ tat padam āpnoti saṃsāram ca adhigacchati
Word by word
“But he who is without discernment, his mind unrestrained and always impure, does not attain that goal; he goes on in the cycle of birth and death.”

Note Śaṅkara: amanaska: one whose mind is not held in check, and who is for that reason always impure. ‘That goal’ is the imperishable supreme spoken of above.

यस्तु विज्ञानवान्भवति समनस्कः सदा शुचिः । स तु तत्पदमाप्नोति यस्माद्भूयो न जायते ॥ ८ ॥
yas tu vijñānavān bhavati samanaskaḥ sadā śuciḥ | sa tu tat padam āpnoti yasmād bhūyo na jāyate || 8 ||
Padaccheda
यः तु विज्ञानवान् भवति समनस्कः सदा शुचिः सः तु तत् पदम् आप्नोति यस्मात् भूयः न जायते
yaḥ tu vijñānavān bhavati samanaskaḥ sadā śuciḥ saḥ tu tat padam āpnoti yasmāt bhūyaḥ na jāyate
Word by word
“But he who has discernment, his mind restrained and always pure, attains that goal from which he is not born again.”

Note Śaṅkara: having reached that goal he does not fall from it, and so is not born again into the cycle of birth and death.

विज्ञानसारथिर्यस्तु मनःप्रग्रहवान्नरः । सोऽध्वनः पारमाप्नोति तद्विष्णोः परमं पदम् ॥ ९ ॥
vijñānasārathir yas tu manaḥpragrahavān naraḥ | so 'dhvanaḥ pāram āpnoti tad viṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padam || 9 ||
Padaccheda
विज्ञान-सारथिः यः तु मनः-प्रग्रहवान् नरः सः अध्वनः पारम् आप्नोति तत् विष्णोः परमम् पदम्
vijñāna-sārathiḥ yaḥ tu manaḥ-pragrahavān naraḥ saḥ adhvanaḥ pāram āpnoti tat viṣṇoḥ paramam padam
Word by word
“The man whose charioteer is discernment, and who holds the reins of the mind, reaches the end of the road: that highest state of Viṣṇu.”

Note Śaṅkara: the road is the course of birth and death; reaching its end, he is freed from every bond. Viṣṇu is the all-pervading Brahman, the supreme Self, called Vāsudeva; his highest state is his true nature.

इन्द्रियेभ्यः परा ह्यर्था अर्थेभ्यश्च परं मनः । मनसस्तु परा बुद्धिर्बुद्धेरात्मा महान्परः ॥ १० ॥
indriyebhyaḥ parā hy arthā arthebhyaś ca paraṃ manaḥ | manasas tu parā buddhir buddher ātmā mahān paraḥ || 10 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियेभ्यः पराः हि अर्थाः अर्थेभ्यः च परम् मनः मनसः तु परा बुद्धिः बुद्धेः आत्मा महान् परः
indriyebhyaḥ parāḥ hi arthāḥ arthebhyaḥ ca param manaḥ manasaḥ tu parā buddhiḥ buddheḥ ātmā mahān paraḥ
Word by word
“Higher than the senses are their objects, and higher than the objects is the mind. Higher than the mind is the intellect, and higher than the intellect is the great self.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘higher’ means subtler, greater and more inward. The objects are the subtle elements from which the senses are formed. Mind and intellect here stand for the subtle elements that give rise to them. The great self is the principle of Hiraṇyagarbha, the first-born of the unmanifest, the inner self of the intellects of all beings.

महतः परमव्यक्तमव्यक्तात्पुरुषः परः । पुरुषान्न परं किंचित्सा काष्ठा सा परा गतिः ॥ ११ ॥
mahataḥ param avyaktam avyaktāt puruṣaḥ paraḥ | puruṣān na paraṃ kiṃcit sā kāṣṭhā sā parā gatiḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
महतः परम् अव्यक्तम् अव्यक्तात् पुरुषः परः पुरुषात् न परम् किंचित् सा काष्ठा सा परा गतिः
mahataḥ param avyaktam avyaktāt puruṣaḥ paraḥ puruṣāt na param kiṃcit sā kāṣṭhā sā parā gatiḥ
Word by word
“Higher than the great is the unmanifest, and higher than the unmanifest is the Person. Higher than the Person there is nothing: that is the limit, that is the highest goal.”

Note Śaṅkara: the unmanifest is the seed of the whole world, with name and form not yet unfolded, resting in the supreme Self as the power of a banyan tree rests in its seed. The Person is so called because he fills all (pūraṇāt). He is the limit at which subtlety, greatness and inwardness end, and the highest goal of all who travel in the cycle of birth and death.

एष सर्वेषु भूतेषु गूढोऽत्मा न प्रकाशते । दृश्यते त्वग्र्यया बुद्ध्या सूक्ष्मया सूक्ष्मदर्शिभिः ॥ १२ ॥
eṣa sarveṣu bhūteṣu gūḍho 'tmā na prakāśate | dṛśyate tv agryayā buddhyā sūkṣmayā sūkṣmadarśibhiḥ || 12 ||
Padaccheda
एषः सर्वेषु भूतेषु गूढः आत्मा न प्रकाशते दृश्यते तु अग्र्यया बुद्ध्या सूक्ष्मया सूक्ष्म-दर्शिभिः
eṣaḥ sarveṣu bhūteṣu gūḍhaḥ ātmā na prakāśate dṛśyate tu agryayā buddhyā sūkṣmayā sūkṣma-darśibhiḥ
Word by word
“This Self, hidden in all beings, does not shine forth. But it is seen by those who see what is subtle, through a pointed and subtle intellect.”

Note Śaṅkara: it is hidden in all beings, from Brahmā down to a tuft of grass, covered by ignorance and illusion in the form of seeing, hearing and other acts; so it does not show itself to anyone as the Self. Reaching it is called a ‘going’ only by a figure of speech: since it is the inmost Self of all, knowing it is the reaching. agryā: one-pointed, like the tip of a thing.

यच्छेद्वाङ्मनसी प्राज्ञस्तद्यच्छेज्ज्ञान आत्मनि । ज्ञानमात्मनि महति नियच्छेत्तद्यच्छेच्छान्त आत्मनि ॥ १३ ॥
yacched vāṅmanasī prājñas tad yacchej jñāna ātmani | jñānam ātmani mahati niyacchet tad yacchecchānta ātmani || 13 ||
Padaccheda
यच्छेत् वाक् मनसी प्राज्ञः तत् यच्छेत् ज्ञाने आत्मनि ज्ञानम् आत्मनि महति नियच्छेत् तत् यच्छेत् शान्ते आत्मनि
yacchet vāk manasī prājñaḥ tat yacchet jñāne ātmani jñānam ātmani mahati niyacchet tat yacchet śānte ātmani
Word by word
“The wise one should merge speech in the mind, and the mind in the knowing self, the intellect. He should merge the intellect in the great self, and that in the peaceful Self.”

Note Śaṅkara: speech stands for all the senses. vāk is the accusative vācam with its ending dropped, and manasī is manasi with a Vedic long vowel. The ‘knowing self’ is the intellect, luminous by nature. The great self is the first-born. The peaceful Self is the principal Self, in which every distinction has ceased: changeless, inmost in all, witness of every thought.

Note The DCS takes vāṅmanasī as a compound, ‘speech and mind’; the analysis here follows Śaṅkara.

उत्तिष्ठत जाग्रत प्राप्य वरान्निबोधत । क्षुरस्य धारा निशिता दुरत्यया दुर्गं पथस्तत्कवयो वदन्ति ॥ १४ ॥
uttiṣṭhata jāgrata prāpya varān nibodhata | kṣurasya dhārā niśitā duratyayā durgaṃ pathas tat kavayo vadanti || 14 ||
Padaccheda
उत्तिष्ठत जाग्रत प्राप्य वरान् निबोधत क्षुरस्य धारा निशिता दुरत्यया दुर्गम् पथः तत् कवयः वदन्ति
uttiṣṭhata jāgrata prāpya varān nibodhata kṣurasya dhārā niśitā duratyayā durgam pathaḥ tat kavayaḥ vadanti
Word by word
“Arise! Awake! Approach the best of teachers and understand. Like the sharpened edge of a razor, hard to pass over: so hard to travel, the sages declare, is that path.”

Note Śaṅkara: the Upaniṣad speaks out of compassion, like a mother: awake from the dreadful sleep of ignorance, the seed of every evil. ‘Understand’: know the inmost Self taught by them as ‘I am this’. pathaḥ is to be taken as panthānam, ‘the path’ of knowledge, which is hard to gain because what is to be known is so subtle.

Note The DCS reads varāt, an ablative; the text and Śaṅkara have varān, ‘the best’, which is followed here.

अशब्दमस्पर्शमरूपमव्ययं तथारसं नित्यमगन्धवच्च यत् । अनाद्यनन्तं महतः परं ध्रुवं निचाय्य तन्मृत्युमुखात्प्रमुच्यते ॥ १५ ॥
aśabdam asparśam arūpam avyayaṃ tathārasaṃ nityam agandhavacca yat | anādy anantaṃ mahataḥ paraṃ dhruvaṃ nicāyya tan mṛtyumukhāt pramucyate || 15 ||
Padaccheda
अशब्दम् अस्पर्शम् अरूपम् अव्ययम् तथा अरसम् नित्यम् अ-गन्धवत् च यत् अनादि अनन्तम् महतः परम् ध्रुवम् निचाय्य तत् मृत्यु-मुखात् प्रमुच्यते
aśabdam asparśam arūpam avyayam tathā arasam nityam a-gandhavat ca yat anādi anantam mahataḥ param dhruvam nicāyya tat mṛtyu-mukhāt pramucyate
Word by word
“That which is without sound, touch, form, taste or smell, undecaying and eternal, without beginning or end, higher than the great, and unchanging: realising that, one is released from the jaws of death.”

Note Śaṅkara: whatever has sound and the other qualities decays; Brahman, having none, does not. Without beginning: it has no cause into which it could dissolve; without end: it has no effect by producing which it would perish. ‘The great’ here is the principle called intellect. The jaws of death are ignorance, desire and action.

नाचिकेतमुपाख्यानं मृत्युप्रोक्तं सनातनम् । उक्त्वा श्रुत्वा च मेधावी ब्रह्मलोके महीयते ॥ १६ ॥
nāciketam upākhyānaṃ mṛtyuproktaṃ sanātanam | uktvā śrutvā ca medhāvī brahmaloke mahīyate || 16 ||
Padaccheda
नाचिकेतम् उपाख्यानम् मृत्यु-प्रोक्तम् सनातनम् उक्त्वा श्रुत्वा च मेधावी ब्रह्म-लोके महीयते
nāciketam upākhyānam mṛtyu-proktam sanātanam uktvā śrutvā ca medhāvī brahma-loke mahīyate
Word by word
A wise person who tells, and who hears, this ancient story of Naciketas, told by Death, is honoured in the world that is Brahman.

Note Śaṅkara: the Upaniṣad says this in praise of the teaching. The story is the three vallīs; it is ‘ancient’ because it is Vedic. It is told to brahmins and heard from teachers. brahmaloka: the world which is Brahman itself.

य इमं परमं गुह्यं श्रावयेद्ब्रह्मसंसदि । प्रयतः श्राद्धकाले वा तदानन्त्याय कल्पते । तदानन्त्याय कल्पत इति ॥ १७ ॥
ya imaṃ paramaṃ guhyaṃ śrāvayed brahmasaṃsadi | prayataḥ śrāddhakāle vā tad ānantyāya kalpate | tad ānantyāya kalpata iti || 17 ||
Padaccheda
यः इमम् परमम् गुह्यम् श्रावयेत् ब्रह्म-संसदि प्रयतः श्राद्ध-काले वा तत् आनन्त्याय कल्पते तत् आनन्त्याय कल्पते इति
yaḥ imam paramam guhyam śrāvayet brahma-saṃsadi prayataḥ śrāddha-kāle vā tat ānantyāya kalpate tat ānantyāya kalpate iti
Word by word
Whoever, having purified himself, recites this supreme secret in an assembly of brahmins, or at the time of the rite for the ancestors: that leads to an endless reward, that leads to an endless reward.

Note Śaṅkara: recited both in its words and in its meaning. The repetition marks the end of the adhyāya.

पराञ्चि खानि व्यतृणत्स्वयंभूस्तस्मात्पराङ्पश्यति नान्तरात्मन् । कश्चिद्धीरः प्रत्यगात्मानमैक्षदावृत्तचक्षुरमृतत्वमिच्छन् ॥ १ ॥
parāñci khāni vyatṛṇat svayaṃbhūs tasmāt parāṅ paśyati nāntarātman | kaścid dhīraḥ pratyag ātmānam aikṣad āvṛttacakṣur amṛtatvam icchan || 1 ||
Padaccheda
पराञ्चि खानि व्यतृणत् स्वयंभूः तस्मात् पराङ् पश्यति न अन्तरात्मन् कश्चित् धीरः प्रत्यक् आत्मानम् ऐक्षत् आवृत्त-चक्षुः अमृत-त्वम् इच्छन्
parāñci khāni vyatṛṇat svayaṃbhūḥ tasmāt parāṅ paśyati na antarātman kaścit dhīraḥ pratyak ātmānam aikṣat āvṛtta-cakṣuḥ amṛta-tvam icchan
Word by word
“The Self-existent pierced the senses so that they turn outward; therefore one sees what is outside and not the Self within. Some wise person, desiring immortality, turned his eyes back and saw the inward Self.”

Note Śaṅkara: khāni, ‘openings’, stands for the ear and the other senses. ‘Pierced’ means ‘injured’: the Lord made them outward-going by nature. The wise one turns them back as one would turn a river against its current. antarātman stands for antarātmānam. aikṣat, a past form, has the sense ‘sees’, tense being free in the Veda. āvṛttacakṣuḥ: with eye, ear and all the senses withdrawn from every object.

पराचः कामाननुयन्ति बालास्ते मृत्योर्यन्ति विततस्य पाशम् । अथ धीरा अमृतत्वं विदित्वा ध्रुवमध्रुवेष्विह न प्रार्थयन्ते ॥ २ ॥
parācaḥ kāmān anuyanti bālās te mṛtyor yanti vitatasya pāśam | atha dhīrā amṛtatvaṃ viditvā dhruvam adhruveṣv iha na prārthayante || 2 ||
Padaccheda
पराचः कामान् अनुयन्ति बालाः ते मृत्योः यन्ति विततस्य पाशम् अथ धीराः अमृत-त्वम् विदित्वा ध्रुवम् अध्रुवेषु इह न प्रार्थयन्ते
parācaḥ kāmān anuyanti bālāḥ te mṛtyoḥ yanti vitatasya pāśam atha dhīrāḥ amṛta-tvam viditvā dhruvam adhruveṣu iha na prārthayante
Word by word
“The childish follow outward objects of desire, and they walk into the widespread snare of death. The wise, therefore, having known immortality as the one stable thing, ask for nothing here among things that are unstable.”

Note Śaṅkara: death here is the whole of ignorance, desire and action; its snare is union with body and senses, that is, the unbroken run of birth, death, old age and disease. The stable immortality is abiding in one's nature as the inward Self. The wise rise above the desire for sons, wealth and worlds.

येन रूपं रसं गन्धं शब्दान्स्पर्शांश्च मैथुनान् । एतेनैव विजानाति किमत्र परिशिष्यते । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ३ ॥
yena rūpaṃ rasaṃ gandhaṃ śabdān sparśāṃśca maithunān | etenaiva vijānāti kim atra pariśiṣyate | etad vai tat || 3 ||
Padaccheda
येन रूपम् रसम् गन्धम् शब्दान् स्पर्शान् च मैथुनान् एतेन एव विजानाति किम् अत्र परिशिष्यते एतत् वै तत्
yena rūpam rasam gandham śabdān sparśān ca maithunān etena eva vijānāti kim atra pariśiṣyate etat vai tat
Word by word
“That by which one knows form, taste, smell, sounds, touches and the pleasures of union: by this alone one knows. What remains here unknown to it? This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: it is the Self, whose nature is consciousness. The body and the rest, being themselves made of form and the like and being objects of knowledge, cannot be the knower; one knows them through the Self that is distinct from them, as that by which iron burns is fire. Nothing is left that the Self does not know: it is all-knowing.

Note Śaṅkara: etad vai tat: this is that which Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, which is other than righteousness and the rest: the highest state of Viṣṇu, beyond which there is nothing.

स्वप्नान्तं जागरितान्तं चोभौ येनानुपश्यति । महान्तं विभुमात्मानं मत्वा धीरो न शोचति ॥ ४ ॥
svapnāntaṃ jāgaritāntaṃ cobhau yenānupaśyati | mahāntaṃ vibhum ātmānaṃ matvā dhīro na śocati || 4 ||
Padaccheda
स्वप्न-अन्तम् जागरित-अन्तम् च उभौ येन अनुपश्यति महान्तम् विभुम् आत्मानम् मत्वा धीरः न शोचति
svapna-antam jāgarita-antam ca ubhau yena anupaśyati mahāntam vibhum ātmānam matvā dhīraḥ na śocati
Word by word
“That by which one perceives both what is seen in dream and what is seen in waking: knowing that great, all-pervading Self, the wise man does not grieve.”

Note Śaṅkara: because the Self is so subtle and hard to know, the same thing is said again and again. svapnānta: what lies within dream, the objects known in dream. He knows it directly as ‘I am the supreme Self’.

य इमं मध्वदं वेद आत्मानं जीवमन्तिकात् । ईशानं भूतभव्यस्य न ततो विजुगुप्सते । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ५ ॥
ya imaṃ madhvadaṃ veda ātmānaṃ jīvam antikāt | īśānaṃ bhūtabhavyasya na tato vijugupsate | etad vai tat || 5 ||
Padaccheda
यः इमम् मध्वदम् वेद आत्मानम् जीवम् अन्तिकात् ईशानम् भूत-भव्यस्य न ततः विजुगुप्सते एतत् वै तत्
yaḥ imam madhvadam veda ātmānam jīvam antikāt īśānam bhūta-bhavyasya na tataḥ vijugupsate etat vai tat
Word by word
“He who knows this Self, the eater of honey, the living one, close at hand, as the lord of what has been and what is to be, no longer seeks to protect himself. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: madhvada: the one who tastes the fruits of action; jīva: the sustainer of the life-breaths. ‘Lord of past and future’ covers the three times. As long as a man thinks himself impermanent and exposed to fear, he wishes to guard himself; once he knows the Self as eternal and without a second, who would guard what, and from what?

Note vijugupsate is a desiderative form; it is labelled here as a present.

यः पूर्वं तपसो जातमद्भ्यः पूर्वमजायत । गुहां प्रविश्य तिष्ठन्तं यो भूतेभिर्व्यपश्यत । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ६ ॥
yaḥ pūrvaṃ tapaso jātam adbhyaḥ pūrvam ajāyata | guhāṃ praviśya tiṣṭhantaṃ yo bhūtebhir vyapaśyata | etad vai tat || 6 ||
Padaccheda
यः पूर्वम् तपसः जातम् अद्भ्यः पूर्वम् अजायत गुहाम् प्रविश्य तिष्ठन्तम् यः भूतेभिः व्यपश्यत एतत् वै तत्
yaḥ pūrvam tapasaḥ jātam adbhyaḥ pūrvam ajāyata guhām praviśya tiṣṭhantam yaḥ bhūtebhiḥ vyapaśyata etat vai tat
Word by word
“He who sees the first-born, born of old from austerity, born before the waters, who has entered the cave and abides there with the elements: he sees this very Brahman. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: the inward Self, already shown to be the Lord, is now shown to be the Self of all. The first-born is Hiraṇyagarbha, born from Brahman, whose austerity is knowledge. ‘Before the waters’ means before all five elements, water included. The cave is the space in the heart of every creature; the bhūtas are the body and organs. bhūtebhiḥ is the Vedic form of bhūtaiḥ. vyapaśyata has the sense ‘sees’.

Note Śaṅkara: etad vai tat: this is that which Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, which is other than righteousness and the rest: the highest state of Viṣṇu, beyond which there is nothing.

या प्राणेन सम्भवति अदितिर्देवतामयी । गुहां प्रविश्य तिष्ठन्तीं या भूतेभिर्व्यजायत । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ७ ॥
yā prāṇena sambhavati aditir devatāmayī | guhāṃ praviśya tiṣṭhantīṃ yā bhūtebhir vyajāyata | etad vai tat || 7 ||
Padaccheda
या प्राणेन सम्भवति अदितिः देवता-मयी गुहाम् प्रविश्य तिष्ठन्तीम् या भूतेभिः व्यजायत एतत् वै तत्
yā prāṇena sambhavati aditiḥ devatā-mayī guhām praviśya tiṣṭhantīm yā bhūtebhiḥ vyajāyata etat vai tat
Word by word
“She who arises as the life-breath, Aditi, made of all the gods, who has entered the cave and abides there, who was born together with the elements: this indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: she arises from the supreme Brahman in the form of Hiraṇyagarbha, the life-breath. She is called Aditi because she ‘eats’ (adana) sound and the other objects.

Note Śaṅkara: etad vai tat: this is that which Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, which is other than righteousness and the rest: the highest state of Viṣṇu, beyond which there is nothing.

अरण्योर्निहितो जातवेदा गर्भ इव सुभृतो गर्भिणीभिः । दिवे दिव ईड्यो जागृवद्भिर्हविष्मद्भिर्मनुष्येभिरग्निः । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ८ ॥
araṇyor nihito jātavedā garbha iva subhṛto garbhiṇībhiḥ | dive diva īḍyo jāgṛvadbhir haviṣmadbhir manuṣyebhir agniḥ | etad vai tat || 8 ||
Padaccheda
अरण्योः निहितः जातवेदाः गर्भः इव सु-भृतः गर्भिणीभिः दिवे दिवे ईड्यः जागृवद्भिः हविष्मद्भिः मनुष्येभिः अग्निः एतत् वै तत्
araṇyoḥ nihitaḥ jātavedāḥ garbhaḥ iva su-bhṛtaḥ garbhiṇībhiḥ dive dive īḍyaḥ jāgṛvadbhiḥ haviṣmadbhiḥ manuṣyebhiḥ agniḥ etat vai tat
Word by word
“The fire lodged in the two fire-sticks, well cared for as an unborn child is by pregnant women, and to be praised day after day by wakeful men who bring offerings: this indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: in the sacrifice it is the fire kept in the upper and lower fire-sticks, the consumer of all offerings; within the person it is tended in the heart by yogīs. ‘Wakeful’: vigilant; the offerings are clarified butter and the like for the ritualist, and meditation for the yogī. manuṣyebhiḥ is the Vedic form of manuṣyaiḥ.

Note Śaṅkara: etad vai tat: this is that which Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, which is other than righteousness and the rest: the highest state of Viṣṇu, beyond which there is nothing.

यतश्चोदेति सूर्यो अस्तं यत्र च गच्छति । तं देवाः सर्वे अर्पितास्तदु नात्येति कश्चन । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ९ ॥
yataś codeti sūryo astaṃ yatra ca gacchati | taṃ devāḥ sarve arpitās tad u nātyeti kaścana | etad vai tat || 9 ||
Padaccheda
यतः च उदेति सूर्यः अस्तम् यत्र च गच्छति तम् देवाः सर्वे अर्पिताः तत् उ न अत्येति कश्चन एतत् वै तत्
yataḥ ca udeti sūryaḥ astam yatra ca gacchati tam devāḥ sarve arpitāḥ tat u na atyeti kaścana etat vai tat
Word by word
“That from which the sun rises and in which it sets: in that all the gods are fixed, and no one goes beyond it. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘that’ is the life-breath, the Self. The gods are fire and the rest in the cosmos, and speech and the rest in the person; they are fixed in it like spokes in the hub of a wheel. It too is Brahman, the Self of all. No one, ceasing to be of its nature, becomes other than it.

Note Śaṅkara: etad vai tat: this is that which Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, which is other than righteousness and the rest: the highest state of Viṣṇu, beyond which there is nothing.

यदेवेह तदमुत्र यदमुत्र तदन्विह । मृत्योः स मृत्युमाप्नोति य इह नानेव पश्यति ॥ १० ॥
yad eveha tad amutra yad amutra tad anv iha | mṛtyoḥ sa mṛtyum āpnoti ya iha nāneva paśyati || 10 ||
Padaccheda
यत् एव इह तत् अमुत्र यत् अमुत्र तत् अनु इह मृत्योः सः मृत्युम् आप्नोति यः इह नाना इव पश्यति
yat eva iha tat amutra yat amutra tat anu iha mṛtyoḥ saḥ mṛtyum āpnoti yaḥ iha nānā iva paśyati
Word by word
“What is here, that is there; what is there, that again is here. He goes from death to death who sees here any seeming difference.”

Note Śaṅkara: this removes the doubt that the self in beings, from Brahmā down to plants, is other than the supreme Brahman. ‘Here’: joined to the adjuncts of body and organs, and appearing to the undiscerning to have the properties of the cycle of birth and death. ‘There’: established in itself, by nature unbroken consciousness. One who thinks ‘I am other than the supreme, and Brahman is other than I’ comes to birth and death again and again.

मनसैवेदमाप्तव्यं नेह नानास्ति किंचन । मृत्योः स मृत्युं गच्छति य इह नानेव पश्यति ॥ ११ ॥
manasaivedam āptavyaṃ neha nānāsti kiṃcana | mṛtyoḥ sa mṛtyuṃ gacchati ya iha nāneva paśyati || 11 ||
Padaccheda
मनसा एव इदम् आप्तव्यम् न इह नाना अस्ति किंचन मृत्योः सः मृत्युम् गच्छति यः इह नाना इव पश्यति
manasā eva idam āptavyam na iha nānā asti kiṃcana mṛtyoḥ saḥ mṛtyum gacchati yaḥ iha nānā iva paśyati
Word by word
“By the mind alone is this to be attained: there is no difference here at all. He goes from death to death who sees here any seeming difference.”

Note Śaṅkara: by a mind made ready by teacher and scripture. Once it is attained, the ignorance that sets up difference is gone, and not the least trace of difference remains in Brahman.

अङ्गुष्ठमात्रः पुरुषो मध्य आत्मनि तिष्ठति । ईशानो भूतभव्यस्य न ततो विजुगुप्सते । एतद्वै तत् ॥ १२ ॥
aṅguṣṭhamātraḥ puruṣo madhya ātmani tiṣṭhati | īśāno bhūtabhavyasya na tato vijugupsate | etad vai tat || 12 ||
Padaccheda
अङ्गुष्ठ-मात्रः पुरुषः मध्ये आत्मनि तिष्ठति ईशानः भूत-भव्यस्य न ततः विजुगुप्सते एतत् वै तत्
aṅguṣṭha-mātraḥ puruṣaḥ madhye ātmani tiṣṭhati īśānaḥ bhūta-bhavyasya na tataḥ vijugupsate etat vai tat
Word by word
“The Person, the size of a thumb, dwells in the middle of the body, the lord of what has been and what is to be. Knowing him, one no longer seeks to protect oneself. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: the lotus of the heart is the size of a thumb; limited by the inner organ that lies in its hollow, the Self is called ‘the size of a thumb’, like the space inside a thumb-length joint of bamboo. puruṣa: by him all this is filled. ātmani here means ‘in the body’.

Note Śaṅkara: etad vai tat: this is that which Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, which is other than righteousness and the rest: the highest state of Viṣṇu, beyond which there is nothing.

अङ्गुष्ठमात्रः पुरुषो ज्योतिरिव अधूमकः । ईशानो भूतभव्यस्य स एवाद्य स उ श्वः । एतद्वै तत् ॥ १३ ॥
aṅguṣṭhamātraḥ puruṣo jyotir iva adhūmakaḥ | īśāno bhūtabhavyasya sa evādya sa u śvaḥ | etad vai tat || 13 ||
Padaccheda
अङ्गुष्ठ-मात्रः पुरुषः ज्योतिः इव अधूमकः ईशानः भूत-भव्यस्य सः एव अद्य सः उ श्वः एतत् वै तत्
aṅguṣṭha-mātraḥ puruṣaḥ jyotiḥ iva adhūmakaḥ īśānaḥ bhūta-bhavyasya saḥ eva adya saḥ u śvaḥ etat vai tat
Word by word
“The Person, the size of a thumb, is like a flame without smoke, the lord of what has been and what is to be. He alone is today, and he will be tomorrow. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: the one thus perceived in the heart by yogīs is eternal and unchanging: present in beings now, he will be so tomorrow, and no other like him will be born. In its own words the Upaniṣad here sets aside the view ‘he does not exist’ (1.1.20), and also the doctrine that everything is momentary.

Note Śaṅkara: etad vai tat: this is that which Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, which is other than righteousness and the rest: the highest state of Viṣṇu, beyond which there is nothing.

यथोदकं दुर्गे वृष्टं पर्वतेषु विधावति । एवं धर्मान्पृथक्पश्यंस्तानेवानु विधावति ॥ १४ ॥
yathodakaṃ durge vṛṣṭaṃ parvateṣu vidhāvati | evaṃ dharmān pṛthak paśyaṃs tān evānu vidhāvati || 14 ||
Padaccheda
यथा उदकम् दुर्गे वृष्टम् पर्वतेषु विधावति एवम् धर्मान् पृथक् पश्यन् तान् एव अनु विधावति
yathā udakam durge vṛṣṭam parvateṣu vidhāvati evam dharmān pṛthak paśyan tān eva anu vidhāvati
Word by word
“As water rained on a height runs off among the hills and is lost, so one who sees the selves as separate runs after them alone.”

Note anu … vidhāvati is one verb (anuvidhāvati), its parts separated in the verse.

Note Śaṅkara: the dharmas here are selves, which are not different from the Self; one who sees them as separate in each body keeps taking on one separate body after another. The water, scattered over the low ground, is lost.

यथोदकं शुद्धे शुद्धमासिक्तं तादृगेव भवति । एवं मुनेर्विजानत आत्मा भवति गौतम ॥ १५ ॥
yathodakaṃ śuddhe śuddham āsiktaṃ tādṛg eva bhavati | evaṃ muner vijānata ātmā bhavati gautama || 15 ||
Padaccheda
यथा उदकम् शुद्धे शुद्धम् आसिक्तम् तादृक् एव भवति एवम् मुनेः विजानतः आत्मा भवति गौतम
yathā udakam śuddhe śuddham āsiktam tādṛk eva bhavati evam muneḥ vijānataḥ ātmā bhavati gautama
Word by word
“As pure water poured into pure water becomes just the same, so becomes the Self of the sage who knows, O Gautama.”

Note Śaṅkara: for the sage, the reflective one, who has cast off the seeing of difference made by adjuncts and knows the oneness of the Self, the Self is of one nature only. Therefore the vision of oneness taught by the Veda, a better friend than a thousand mothers and fathers, is to be honoured.

पुरमेकादशद्वारमजस्यावक्रचेतसः । अनुष्ठाय न शोचति विमुक्तश्च विमुच्यते । एतद्वै तत् ॥ १ ॥
puram ekādaśadvāram ajasyāvakracetasaḥ | anuṣṭhāya na śocati vimuktaś ca vimucyate | etad vai tat || 1 ||
Padaccheda
पुरम् एकादश-द्वारम् अजस्य अ-वक्र-चेतसः अनुष्ठाय न शोचति विमुक्तः च विमुच्यते एतत् वै तत्
puram ekādaśa-dvāram ajasya a-vakra-cetasaḥ anuṣṭhāya na śocati vimuktaḥ ca vimucyate etat vai tat
Word by word
“There is a city of eleven gates, belonging to the unborn one whose consciousness never bends. Meditating on its lord, one does not grieve; and, already freed, one is set free. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: the city is the body, which like a city has gatekeepers, rulers and much equipment, and exists for a master who is no part of it. The eleven gates are the seven in the head, three below including the navel, and one at the crown. avakracetas: whose consciousness, like the light of the sun, is always the same. anuṣṭhāya: having meditated on the supreme Lord, the master of the city. ‘Freed’ here and now from the bonds of desire and action made by ignorance, he is ‘set free’: he takes no further body.

हंसः शुचिषद्वसुरन्तरिक्षसद्धोता वेदिषदतिथिर्दुरोणसत् । नृषद्वरसदृतसद्व्योमसदब्जा गोजा ऋतजा अद्रिजा ऋतं बृहत् ॥ २ ॥
haṃsaḥ śuciṣad vasur antarikṣasad dhotā vediṣad atithir duroṇasat | nṛṣad varasad ṛtasad vyomasad abjā gojā ṛtajā adrijā ṛtaṃ bṛhat || 2 ||
Padaccheda
हंसः शुचिषत् वसुः अन्तरिक्ष-सत् होता वेदिषत् अतिथिः दुरोण-सत् नृषत् वर-सत् ऋत-सत् व्योम-सत् अप्-जाः गो-जाः ऋत-जाः अद्रि-जाः ऋतम् बृहत्
haṃsaḥ śuciṣat vasuḥ antarikṣa-sat hotā vediṣat atithiḥ duroṇa-sat nṛṣat vara-sat ṛta-sat vyoma-sat ap-jāḥ go-jāḥ ṛta-jāḥ adri-jāḥ ṛtam bṛhat
Word by word
“He is the sun dwelling in the bright sky, the air dwelling in the mid-region, the fire dwelling on the altar of the earth, the guest dwelling in the house. He dwells in men, in the gods, in truth, in space. He is born in the waters, on the earth, in the sacrifice, from the mountains. He is the true, the great.”

Note Śaṅkara: the Self dwells not in the city of one body only but in all. haṃsa, ‘the one who goes’: the sun; vasu, ‘he who gives all a dwelling’: air; hotṛ: fire; the altar is the earth. atithi: Soma in the jar, or a brahmin guest in houses. vara: the gods. abjā: as conch, pearl-oyster and sea creatures; gojā: as rice and barley; ṛtajā: as the parts of the sacrifice; adrijā: as rivers. Though the Self of all, it is ṛta, of unfailing nature, and great as the cause of all. The one all-pervading Self is the Self of the world: there is no division in it.

ऊर्ध्वं प्राणमुन्नयति अपानं प्रत्यगस्यति । मध्ये वामनमासीनं विश्वे देवा उपासते ॥ ३ ॥
ūrdhvaṃ prāṇam unnayati apānaṃ pratyag asyati | madhye vāmanam āsīnaṃ viśve devā upāsate || 3 ||
Padaccheda
ऊर्ध्वम् प्राणम् उन्नयति अपानम् प्रत्यक् अस्यति मध्ये वामनम् आसीनम् विश्वे देवाः उपासते
ūrdhvam prāṇam unnayati apānam pratyak asyati madhye vāmanam āsīnam viśve devāḥ upāsate
Word by word
“He leads the out-breath upward and casts the in-breath downward. Him, the adorable one seated in the middle, all the gods wait upon.”

Note Śaṅkara: this gives a mark by which the Self may be known. ‘In the middle’: in the space of the lotus of the heart. vāmana: the one to be adored (vananīya). The gods are the eye and the other senses, which bring him the perceptions of form and the rest as subjects bring tribute to a king. He for whose sake all the workings of breath and senses go on is thereby shown to be other than they.

अस्य विस्रंसमानस्य शरीरस्थस्य देहिनः । देहाद्विमुच्यमानस्य किमत्र परिशिष्यते । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ४ ॥
asya visraṃsamānasya śarīrasthasya dehinaḥ | dehād vimucyamānasya kim atra pariśiṣyate | etad vai tat || 4 ||
Padaccheda
अस्य विस्रंसमानस्य शरीर-स्थस्य देहिनः देहात् विमुच्यमानस्य किम् अत्र परिशिष्यते एतत् वै तत्
asya visraṃsamānasya śarīra-sthasya dehinaḥ dehāt vimucyamānasya kim atra pariśiṣyate etat vai tat
Word by word
“When this embodied one who dwells in the body slips away and is released from the body, what remains here? This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: nothing remains of the whole group of breaths and organs; all of it loses its strength in a moment, as the townspeople do when the master of the city has gone. That whose departure brings this about is the Self, shown to be other than they.

न प्राणेन नापानेन मर्त्यो जीवति कश्चन । इतरेण तु जीवन्ति यस्मिन्नेतावुपाश्रितौ ॥ ५ ॥
na prāṇena nāpānena martyo jīvati kaścana | itareṇa tu jīvanti yasminn etāv upāśritau || 5 ||
Padaccheda
न प्राणेन न अपानेन मर्त्यः जीवति कश्चन इतरेण तु जीवन्ति यस्मिन् एतौ उपाश्रितौ
na prāṇena na apānena martyaḥ jīvati kaścana itareṇa tu jīvanti yasmin etau upāśritau
Word by word
“No mortal lives by the out-breath or by the in-breath. They live by another, on which these two depend.”

Note Śaṅkara: things that work together in combination exist for the sake of something else, as a house does; so the breaths cannot themselves be the cause of life. All live by that which is other than the combination, the Self.

हन्त त इदं प्रवक्ष्यामि गुह्यं ब्रह्म सनातनम् । यथा च मरणं प्राप्य आत्मा भवति गौतम ॥ ६ ॥
hanta ta idaṃ pravakṣyāmi guhyaṃ brahma sanātanam | yathā ca maraṇaṃ prāpya ātmā bhavati gautama || 6 ||
Padaccheda
हन्त ते इदम् प्रवक्ष्यामि गुह्यम् ब्रह्म सनातनम् यथा च मरणम् प्राप्य आत्मा भवति गौतम
hanta te idam pravakṣyāmi guhyam brahma sanātanam yathā ca maraṇam prāpya ātmā bhavati gautama
Word by word
“Come, I shall declare to you this secret, eternal Brahman, and also how the self fares on meeting death, O Gautama.”

Note Śaṅkara: by knowing it the whole cycle of birth and death comes to an end; and hear how the self of one who does not know it passes on after death.

योनिमन्ये प्रपद्यन्ते शरीरत्वाय देहिनः । स्थाणुमन्येऽनुसंयन्ति यथाकर्म यथाश्रुतम् ॥ ७ ॥
yonim anye prapadyante śarīratvāya dehinaḥ | sthāṇum anye 'nusaṃyanti yathākarma yathāśrutam || 7 ||
Padaccheda
योनिम् अन्ये प्रपद्यन्ते शरीर-त्वाय देहिनः स्थाणुम् अन्ये अनुसंयन्ति यथाकर्म यथाश्रुतम्
yonim anye prapadyante śarīra-tvāya dehinaḥ sthāṇum anye anusaṃyanti yathākarma yathāśrutam
Word by word
“Some embodied ones enter a womb to take a body; others pass into the motionless state of plants: each according to his action, each according to his knowledge.”

Note Śaṅkara: these are the ignorant. sthāṇu: the state of trees and other unmoving things, to which the very lowest go. Each takes a body in keeping with the action done in this life and the knowledge gained.

य एष सुप्तेषु जागर्ति कामं कामं पुरुषो निर्मिमाणः । तदेव शुक्रं तद्ब्रह्म तदेवामृतमुच्यते । तस्मिंल्लोकाः श्रिताः सर्वे तदु नात्येति कश्चन । एतद्वै तत् ॥ ८ ॥
ya eṣa supteṣu jāgarti kāmaṃ kāmaṃ puruṣo nirmimāṇaḥ | tad eva śukraṃ tad brahma tad evāmṛtam ucyate | tasmiṃl lokāḥ śritāḥ sarve tad u nātyeti kaścana | etad vai tat || 8 ||
Padaccheda
यः एषः सुप्तेषु जागर्ति कामम् कामम् पुरुषः निर्मिमाणः तत् एव शुक्रम् तत् ब्रह्म तत् एव अमृतम् उच्यते तस्मिन् लोकाः श्रिताः सर्वे तत् उ न अत्येति कश्चन एतत् वै तत्
yaḥ eṣaḥ supteṣu jāgarti kāmam kāmam puruṣaḥ nirmimāṇaḥ tat eva śukram tat brahma tat eva amṛtam ucyate tasmin lokāḥ śritāḥ sarve tat u na atyeti kaścana etat vai tat
Word by word
“The Person who stays awake while they sleep, shaping desire after desire: that alone is the pure, that is Brahman, that alone is called the immortal. In it all the worlds rest, and no one goes beyond it. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: this is the secret Brahman that was promised. ‘They’ are the breaths and the senses. Out of ignorance he brings forth one desired object after another. There is no other secret Brahman than this.

अग्निर्यथैको भुवनं प्रविष्टो रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बभूव । एकस्तथा सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बहिश्च ॥ ९ ॥
agnir yathaiko bhuvanaṃ praviṣṭo rūpaṃ rūpaṃ pratirūpo babhūva | ekas tathā sarvabhūtāntarātmā rūpaṃ rūpaṃ pratirūpo bahiś ca || 9 ||
Padaccheda
अग्निः यथा एकः भुवनम् प्रविष्टः रूपम् रूपम् प्रतिरूपः बभूव एकः तथा सर्व-भूत-अन्तरात्मा रूपम् रूपम् प्रतिरूपः बहिः च
agniḥ yathā ekaḥ bhuvanam praviṣṭaḥ rūpam rūpam pratirūpaḥ babhūva ekaḥ tathā sarva-bhūta-antarātmā rūpam rūpam pratirūpaḥ bahiḥ ca
Word by word
“As the one fire, having entered the world, takes a form to match each form it meets, so the one inner Self of all beings takes a form to match each form; and it is outside them too.”

Note Śaṅkara: the Upaniṣad says this again and again, since minds unsettled by the arguments of many disputants do not take in the oneness of the Self. Fire takes the shape of whatever it burns, wood and the like. ‘Outside’: in its own unchanged nature, like space.

वायुर्यथैको भुवनं प्रविष्टो रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बभूव । एकस्तथा सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बहिश्च ॥ १० ॥
vāyur yathaiko bhuvanaṃ praviṣṭo rūpaṃ rūpaṃ pratirūpo babhūva | ekas tathā sarvabhūtāntarātmā rūpaṃ rūpaṃ pratirūpo bahiś ca || 10 ||
Padaccheda
वायुः यथा एकः भुवनम् प्रविष्टः रूपम् रूपम् प्रतिरूपः बभूव एकः तथा सर्व-भूत-अन्तरात्मा रूपम् रूपम् प्रतिरूपः बहिः च
vāyuḥ yathā ekaḥ bhuvanam praviṣṭaḥ rūpam rūpam pratirūpaḥ babhūva ekaḥ tathā sarva-bhūta-antarātmā rūpam rūpam pratirūpaḥ bahiḥ ca
Word by word
“As the one air, having entered the world, takes a form to match each form it meets, so the one inner Self of all beings takes a form to match each form; and it is outside them too.”

Note Śaṅkara: a second illustration: air enters bodies as the life-breath.

सूर्यो यथा सर्वलोकस्य चक्षुर्न लिप्यते चाक्षुषैर्बाह्यदोषैः । एकस्तथा सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा न लिप्यते लोकदुःखेन बाह्यः ॥ ११ ॥
sūryo yathā sarvalokasya cakṣur na lipyate cākṣuṣair bāhyadoṣaiḥ | ekas tathā sarvabhūtāntarātmā na lipyate lokaduḥkhena bāhyaḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
सूर्यः यथा सर्व-लोकस्य चक्षुः न लिप्यते चाक्षुषैः बाह्य-दोषैः एकः तथा सर्व-भूत-अन्तरात्मा न लिप्यते लोक-दुःखेन बाह्यः
sūryaḥ yathā sarva-lokasya cakṣuḥ na lipyate cākṣuṣaiḥ bāhya-doṣaiḥ ekaḥ tathā sarva-bhūta-antarātmā na lipyate loka-duḥkhena bāhyaḥ
Word by word
“As the sun, the eye of the whole world, is not stained by the outward faults that the eye sees, so the one inner Self of all beings is not stained by the sorrow of the world, for it stands outside it.”

Note Śaṅkara: this answers the objection that, if the one Self is the Self of all, the sorrow of worldly life must belong to the supreme itself. The sun lights up unclean things and is untouched by them. People suffer through ignorance laid upon the Self; that ignorance is not truly in it, just as the snake is not in the rope nor silver in mother-of-pearl.

एको वशी सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा एकं रूपं बहुधा यः करोति । तमात्मस्थं येऽनुपश्यन्ति धीरास्तेषां सुखं शाश्वतं नेतरेषाम् ॥ १२ ॥
eko vaśī sarvabhūtāntarātmā ekaṃ rūpaṃ bahudhā yaḥ karoti | tam ātmasthaṃ ye 'nupaśyanti dhīrās teṣāṃ sukhaṃ śāśvataṃ netareṣām || 12 ||
Padaccheda
एकः वशी सर्व-भूत-अन्तरात्मा एकम् रूपम् बहुधा यः करोति तम् आत्म-स्थम् ये अनुपश्यन्ति धीराः तेषाम् सुखम् शाश्वतम् न इतरेषाम्
ekaḥ vaśī sarva-bhūta-antarātmā ekam rūpam bahudhā yaḥ karoti tam ātma-stham ye anupaśyanti dhīrāḥ teṣām sukham śāśvatam na itareṣām
Word by word
“The one ruler, the inner Self of all beings, who makes his one form manifold: the wise who perceive him dwelling in themselves have everlasting happiness, and no others.”

Note Śaṅkara: he is the supreme Lord, free, with none equal or greater; the whole world is under his sway. By his mere being, through powers beyond thought, he makes the one pure consciousness many through the limiting adjuncts of name and form. ātmastha: shown as consciousness in the intellect, in the space of the heart; the body is not really his container, any more than a mirror contains the face. anu: after the teaching of teacher and scripture. The happiness is the bliss of the Self.

नित्योऽनित्यानां चेतनश्चेतनानामेको बहूनां यो विदधाति कामान् । तमात्मस्थं येऽनुपश्यन्ति धीरास्तेषां शान्तिः शाश्वती नेतरेषाम् ॥ १३ ॥
nityo 'nityānāṃ cetanaś cetanānām eko bahūnāṃ yo vidadhāti kāmān | tam ātmasthaṃ ye 'nupaśyanti dhīrās teṣāṃ śāntiḥ śāśvatī netareṣām || 13 ||
Padaccheda
नित्यः अनित्यानाम् चेतनः चेतनानाम् एकः बहूनाम् यः विदधाति कामान् तम् आत्म-स्थम् ये अनुपश्यन्ति धीराः तेषाम् शान्तिः शाश्वती न इतरेषाम्
nityaḥ anityānām cetanaḥ cetanānām ekaḥ bahūnām yaḥ vidadhāti kāmān tam ātma-stham ye anupaśyanti dhīrāḥ teṣām śāntiḥ śāśvatī na itareṣām
Word by word
“The eternal among the non-eternal, the conscious among the conscious, the one who grants the desires of the many: the wise who perceive him dwelling in themselves have everlasting peace, and no others.”

Note Śaṅkara: other beings, from Brahmā down, are conscious only through the consciousness of the Self, as water burns only through fire. All-knowing and Lord of all, he gives those who desire the fruits of their actions, each according to his action. The peace is cessation, and it is their very Self.

तदेतदिति मन्यन्तेऽनिर्देश्यं परमं सुखम् । कथं नु तद्विजानीयां किमु भाति विभाति वा ॥ १४ ॥
tad etad iti manyante 'nirdeśyaṃ paramaṃ sukham | kathaṃ nu tad vijānīyāṃ kim u bhāti vibhāti vā || 14 ||
Padaccheda
तत् एतत् इति मन्यन्ते अ-निर्देश्यम् परमम् सुखम् कथम् नु तत् विजानीयाम् किम् उ भाति विभाति वा
tat etat iti manyante a-nirdeśyam paramam sukham katham nu tat vijānīyām kim u bhāti vibhāti vā
Word by word
“‘That is this’: so they hold the indescribable, supreme happiness to be. How, then, may I know it? Does it shine of itself, and is it clearly seen, or not?”

Note Śaṅkara: the happiness of knowing the Self cannot be pointed out and lies beyond the speech and mind of ordinary men; yet those who have laid down all desires hold it to be directly present, ‘this’. The question is how one may make it an object of one's own understanding as they do: is it luminous, so that it is plainly seen by our understanding, or not?

न तत्र सूर्यो भाति न चन्द्रतारकं नेमा विद्युतो भान्ति कुतोऽयमग्निः । तमेव भान्तमनु भाति सर्वं तस्य भासा सर्वमिदं विभाति ॥ १५ ॥
na tatra sūryo bhāti na candratārakaṃ nemā vidyuto bhānti kuto 'yam agniḥ | tam eva bhāntam anu bhāti sarvaṃ tasya bhāsā sarvam idaṃ vibhāti || 15 ||
Padaccheda
न तत्र सूर्यः भाति न चन्द्र-तारकम् न इमाः विद्युतः भान्ति कुतः अयम् अग्निः तम् एव भान्तम् अनु भाति सर्वम् तस्य भासा सर्वम् इदम् विभाति
na tatra sūryaḥ bhāti na candra-tārakam na imāḥ vidyutaḥ bhānti kutaḥ ayam agniḥ tam eva bhāntam anu bhāti sarvam tasya bhāsā sarvam idam vibhāti
Word by word
“There the sun does not shine, nor the moon and stars, nor do these lightnings shine; how then this fire? Everything shines only after him, as he shines; by his light all this shines.”

Note Śaṅkara: this is the answer: it both shines and is clearly seen. The sun, which lights up everything, does not light up Brahman, one's own Self. All else shines after it, as water or a firebrand burns only through contact with fire and not of itself. From the varied light found in its effects, the luminous nature of Brahman is known to be its own.

ऊर्ध्वमूलो अवाक्शाख एषोऽश्वत्थः सनातनः । तदेव शुक्रं तद्ब्रह्म तदेवामृतमुच्यते । तस्मिंल्लोकाः श्रिताः सर्वे तदु नात्येति कश्चन । एतद्वै तत् ॥ १ ॥
ūrdhvamūlo avākśākha eṣo 'śvatthaḥ sanātanaḥ | tad eva śukraṃ tad brahma tad evāmṛtam ucyate | tasmiṃllokāḥ śritāḥ sarve tad u nātyeti kaścana | etad vai tat || 1 ||
Padaccheda
ऊर्ध्व-मूलः अवाक्-शाखः एषः अश्वत्थः सनातनः तत् एव शुक्रम् तत् ब्रह्म तत् एव अमृतम् उच्यते तस्मिन् लोकाः श्रिताः सर्वे तत् उ न अत्येति कश्चन एतत् वै तत्
ūrdhva-mūlaḥ avāk-śākhaḥ eṣaḥ aśvatthaḥ sanātanaḥ tat eva śukram tat brahma tat eva amṛtam ucyate tasmin lokāḥ śritāḥ sarve tat u na atyeti kaścana etat vai tat
Word by word
“This is the ancient fig tree, with its root above and its branches below. That root alone is the pure, that is Brahman, that alone is called the immortal. In it all the worlds rest, and no one goes beyond it. This indeed is that.”

Note Śaṅkara: the tree is the tree of worldly existence, reaching from the unmanifest down to unmoving things. Its root, above, is the highest state of Viṣṇu. It is called a tree (vṛkṣa) because it can be cut down, and aśvattha because, like that tree, it is always trembling, shaken by the wind of desire and action. Its branches are heaven, hell, and the states of animals and departed spirits. It is ‘ancient’ because it has no beginning. It is felled by the weapon of non-attachment that comes from seeing Brahman as the Self.

यदिदं किंच जगत्सर्वं प्राण एजति निःसृतम् । महद्भयं वज्रमुद्यतं य एतद्विदुरमृतास्ते भवन्ति ॥ २ ॥
yad idaṃ kiṃca jagat sarvaṃ prāṇa ejati niḥsṛtam | mahad bhayaṃ vajram udyataṃ ya etad vidur amṛtās te bhavanti || 2 ||
Padaccheda
यत् इदम् किंच जगत् सर्वम् प्राणे एजति निःसृतम् महत् भयम् वज्रम् उद्यतम् ये एतत् विदुः अमृताः ते भवन्ति
yat idam kiṃca jagat sarvam prāṇe ejati niḥsṛtam mahat bhayam vajram udyatam ye etat viduḥ amṛtāḥ te bhavanti
Word by word
“This whole world, whatever there is, has come forth from Brahman, the life-breath, and moves in it. It is a great terror, a thunderbolt raised. Those who know this become immortal.”

Note Śaṅkara: this answers the view that the world arose from nothing. prāṇa is the supreme Brahman. As servants keep to their master's command on seeing him before them with thunderbolt raised, so this world of sun, moon, planets and stars keeps to its order without a moment's rest.

भयादस्याग्निस्तपति भयात्तपति सूर्यः । भयादिन्द्रश्च वायुश्च मृत्युर्धावति पञ्चमः ॥ ३ ॥
bhayād asyāgnis tapati bhayāt tapati sūryaḥ | bhayād indraś ca vāyuś ca mṛtyur dhāvati pañcamaḥ || 3 ||
Padaccheda
भयात् अस्य अग्निः तपति भयात् तपति सूर्यः भयात् इन्द्रः च वायुः च मृत्युः धावति पञ्चमः
bhayāt asya agniḥ tapati bhayāt tapati sūryaḥ bhayāt indraḥ ca vāyuḥ ca mṛtyuḥ dhāvati pañcamaḥ
Word by word
“From fear of him fire burns; from fear of him the sun gives heat; from fear of him Indra, the wind, and Death, the fifth, run to their tasks.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘him’ is the supreme Lord. Were there no ruler over these mighty guardians of the world, their regular activity, like that of servants in fear of their master, could not be explained.

इह चेदशकद्बोद्धुं प्राक्शरीरस्य विस्रसः । ततः सर्गेषु लोकेषु शरीरत्वाय कल्पते ॥ ४ ॥
iha ced aśakad boddhuṃ prākśarīrasya visrasaḥ | tataḥ sargeṣu lokeṣu śarīratvāya kalpate || 4 ||
Padaccheda
इह चेत् अशकत् बोद्धुम् प्राक् शरीरस्य विस्रसः ततः सर्गेषु लोकेषु शरीर-त्वाय कल्पते
iha cet aśakat boddhum prāk śarīrasya visrasaḥ tataḥ sargeṣu lokeṣu śarīra-tvāya kalpate
Word by word
“If one has been able to know it here, before the body falls away, one is freed; if not, one becomes fit to take a body in the worlds of creation.”

Note Śaṅkara supplies what is left unsaid: one who knows Brahman, the cause of that fear, while still living is released from the bondage of worldly existence; one who has not is embodied again. So the effort to know the Self must be made before the body falls, for only here is the Self seen clearly, like a face in a mirror.

यथादर्शे तथात्मनि यथा स्वप्ने तथा पितृलोके । यथाप्सु परीव ददृशे तथा गन्धर्वलोके छायातपयोरिव ब्रह्मलोके ॥ ५ ॥
yathādarśe tathātmani yathā svapne tathā pitṛloke | yathāpsu parīva dadṛśe tathā gandharvaloke chāyātapayor iva brahmaloke || 5 ||
Padaccheda
यथा आदर्शे तथा आत्मनि यथा स्वप्ने तथा पितृ-लोके यथा अप्सु परि इव ददृशे तथा गन्धर्व-लोके छाया-आतपयोः इव ब्रह्म-लोके
yathā ādarśe tathā ātmani yathā svapne tathā pitṛ-loke yathā apsu pari iva dadṛśe tathā gandharva-loke chāyā-ātapayoḥ iva brahma-loke
Word by word
“As in a mirror, so it is seen in oneself; as in a dream, so in the world of the ancestors; as it is seen, so to speak, in water, so in the world of the gandharvas; as shade and light, so in the world of Brahmā.”

Note pari … dadṛśe is one verb (paridadṛśe), its parts separated in the verse.

Note Śaṅkara: in one's own intellect, when it is clear as a mirror, the Self is seen quite distinctly. In the world of the ancestors the sight is blurred, as in a dream, and likewise in the world of the gandharvas, as a reflection in water. Only in the world of Brahmā is it as distinct as shade from light, and that world is hard to reach. Therefore the effort must be made here.

इन्द्रियाणां पृथग्भावमुदयास्तमयौ च यत् । पृथगुत्पद्यमानानां मत्वा धीरो न शोचति ॥ ६ ॥
indriyāṇāṃ pṛthagbhāvam udayāstamayau ca yat | pṛthag utpadyamānānāṃ matvā dhīro na śocati || 6 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियाणाम् पृथग्भावम् उदय-अस्तमयौ च यत् पृथक् उत्पद्यमानानाम् मत्वा धीरः न शोचति
indriyāṇām pṛthagbhāvam udaya-astamayau ca yat pṛthak utpadyamānānām matvā dhīraḥ na śocati
Word by word
“Knowing that the senses, which arise separately, are different in nature, and that the rising and setting are theirs, the wise man does not grieve.”

Note Śaṅkara: the senses arise separately from their causes, space and the rest, each to grasp its own object, and are wholly unlike the Self, which is pure consciousness alone. Their rising and setting, in waking and sleep, do not belong to the Self.

इन्द्रियेभ्यः परं मनो मनसः सत्त्वमुत्तमम् । सत्त्वादधि महानात्मा महतोऽव्यक्तमुत्तमम् ॥ ७ ॥
indriyebhyaḥ paraṃ mano manasaḥ sattvam uttamam | sattvād adhi mahānātmā mahato 'vyaktam uttamam || 7 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियेभ्यः परम् मनः मनसः सत्त्वम् उत्तमम् सत्त्वात् अधि महान् आत्मा महतः अव्यक्तम् उत्तमम्
indriyebhyaḥ param manaḥ manasaḥ sattvam uttamam sattvāt adhi mahān ātmā mahataḥ avyaktam uttamam
Word by word
“Higher than the senses is the mind; higher than the mind is the intellect; above the intellect is the great self; higher than the great is the unmanifest.”

Note Śaṅkara: the Self is not to be sought outside, for it is the inmost of all; this is how. The objects are here included with the senses, being of the same kind. sattva means the intellect. The rest is as at 1.3.10–11.

अव्यक्तात्तु परः पुरुषो व्यापकोऽलिङ्ग एव च । यं ज्ञात्वा मुच्यते जन्तुरमृतत्वं च गच्छति ॥ ८ ॥
avyaktāt tu paraḥ puruṣo vyāpako 'liṅga eva ca | yaṃ jñātvā mucyate jantur amṛtatvaṃ ca gacchati || 8 ||
Padaccheda
अव्यक्तात् तु परः पुरुषः व्यापकः अलिङ्गः एव च यम् ज्ञात्वा मुच्यते जन्तुः अमृत-त्वम् च गच्छति
avyaktāt tu paraḥ puruṣaḥ vyāpakaḥ aliṅgaḥ eva ca yam jñātvā mucyate jantuḥ amṛta-tvam ca gacchati
Word by word
“But higher than the unmanifest is the Person, all-pervading and without any mark. Knowing him, a creature is freed and attains immortality.”

Note Śaṅkara: a mark (liṅga) is that by which a thing is inferred, such as the intellect; he has none, being free of every property of worldly existence. Knowing him from teacher and scripture, one is freed while still living from the knots of the heart, such as ignorance, and attains immortality when the body falls.

न संदृशे तिष्ठति रूपमस्य न चक्षुषा पश्यति कश्चनैनम् । हृदा मनीषा मनसाभिकॢप्तो य एतद्विदुरमृतास्ते भवन्ति ॥ ९ ॥
na saṃdṛśe tiṣṭhati rūpam asya na cakṣuṣā paśyati kaścanainam | hṛdā manīṣā manasābhikḷpto ya etad vidur amṛtās te bhavanti || 9 ||
Padaccheda
न संदृशे तिष्ठति रूपम् अस्य न चक्षुषा पश्यति कश्चन एनम् हृदा मनीषा मनसा अभिकॢप्तः ये एतत् विदुः अमृताः ते भवन्ति
na saṃdṛśe tiṣṭhati rūpam asya na cakṣuṣā paśyati kaścana enam hṛdā manīṣā manasā abhikḷptaḥ ye etat viduḥ amṛtāḥ te bhavanti
Word by word
“His form does not stand within sight; no one sees him with the eye. He is revealed by the intellect that dwells in the heart and rules the mind, through reflection. Those who know this become immortal.”

Note Śaṅkara: the eye stands for all the senses. hṛdā: by the intellect seated in the heart. manīṣā is taken as the instrumental of manīṣ, ‘that which rules (īṣṭe) the mind’, again the intellect, free of wavering. manasā: by right seeing in the form of reflection. abhikḷpta: made clear; ‘the Self can be known’ is to be supplied.

Note The DCS data has a damaged character where ḷ should stand in abhikḷpto; it is corrected here.

यदा पञ्चावतिष्ठन्ते ज्ञानानि मनसा सह । बुद्धिश्च न विचेष्टति तामाहुः परमां गतिम् ॥ १० ॥
yadā pañcāvatiṣṭhante jñānāni manasā saha | buddhiś ca na viceṣṭati tām āhuḥ paramāṃ gatim || 10 ||
Padaccheda
यदा पञ्च अवतिष्ठन्ते ज्ञानानि मनसा सह बुद्धिः च न विचेष्टति ताम् आहुः परमाम् गतिम्
yadā pañca avatiṣṭhante jñānāni manasā saha buddhiḥ ca na viceṣṭati tām āhuḥ paramām gatim
Word by word
“When the five senses of knowledge, together with the mind, stand still, and the intellect does not stir, that, they say, is the highest state.”

Note Śaṅkara: this teaches the yoga by which that intellect is gained. The senses are called ‘knowledges’ because they serve knowledge; they are withdrawn from their objects and rest in the Self, together with the mind turned back from intention and the like.

तां योगमिति मन्यन्ते स्थिरामिन्द्रियधारणाम् । अप्रमत्तस्तदा भवति योगो हि प्रभवाप्ययौ ॥ ११ ॥
tāṃ yogam iti manyante sthirām indriyadhāraṇām | apramattas tadā bhavati yogo hi prabhavāpyayau || 11 ||
Padaccheda
ताम् योगम् इति मन्यन्ते स्थिराम् इन्द्रिय-धारणाम् अप्रमत्तः तदा भवति योगः हि प्रभव-अप्ययौ
tām yogam iti manyante sthirām indriya-dhāraṇām apramattaḥ tadā bhavati yogaḥ hi prabhava-apyayau
Word by word
“That steady holding of the senses they regard as yoga. One must then be vigilant, for yoga comes and goes.”

Note Śaṅkara: though called yoga, ‘union’, this state is really disunion, a separation from contact with every evil; in it the Self stands in its own nature, free of what ignorance lays upon it. Vigilance is enjoined from the time one begins yoga, for yoga is subject to arising and passing away.

नैव वाचा न मनसा प्राप्तुं शक्यो न चक्षुषा । अस्तीति ब्रुवतोऽन्यत्र कथं तदुपलभ्यते ॥ १२ ॥
naiva vācā na manasā prāptuṃ śakyo na cakṣuṣā | astīti bruvato 'nyatra kathaṃ tad upalabhyate || 12 ||
Padaccheda
न एव वाचा न मनसा प्राप्तुम् शक्यः न चक्षुषा अस्ति इति ब्रुवतः अन्यत्र कथम् तत् उपलभ्यते
na eva vācā na manasā prāptum śakyaḥ na cakṣuṣā asti iti bruvataḥ anyatra katham tat upalabhyate
Word by word
“It cannot be reached by speech, nor by the mind, nor by the eye. How can it be perceived by anyone other than one who says ‘it is’?”

Note Śaṅkara: this meets the objection that what no organ grasps does not exist. Though free of every distinction, the Self certainly exists, being known as the root of the world: an effect traced back ends in being, as a pot is always found joined to clay. One who says ‘it is’ is the believer who follows scripture; the denier, for whom the world ends in nothing, cannot perceive Brahman at all.

अस्तीत्येवोपलब्धव्यस्तत्त्वभावेन चोभयोः । अस्तीत्येवोपलब्धस्य तत्त्वभावः प्रसीदति ॥ १३ ॥
astīty evopalabdhavyas tattvabhāvena cobhayoḥ | astīty evopalabdhasya tattvabhāvaḥ prasīdati || 13 ||
Padaccheda
अस्ति इति एव उपलब्धव्यः तत्त्व-भावेन च उभयोः अस्ति इति एव उपलब्धस्य तत्त्व-भावः प्रसीदति
asti iti eva upalabdhavyaḥ tattva-bhāvena ca ubhayoḥ asti iti eva upalabdhasya tattva-bhāvaḥ prasīdati
Word by word
“It is to be perceived first as ‘it is’, and then in its true nature. Of these two, when it has been perceived as ‘it is’, its true nature becomes clear.”

Note Śaṅkara: first the Self is to be perceived as existing, through its adjuncts, the intellect and other effects that are real. Its true nature is the Self without adjuncts, beyond the ideas of being and non-being, described as ‘not this, not this’. ubhayoḥ is a genitive of selection, ‘of these two’. The true nature turns towards one who has first perceived the Self as existing.

यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामा येऽस्य हृदि श्रिताः । अथ मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्यत्र ब्रह्म समश्नुते ॥ १४ ॥
yadā sarve pramucyante kāmā ye 'sya hṛdi śritāḥ | atha martyo 'mṛto bhavaty atra brahma samaśnute || 14 ||
Padaccheda
यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामाः ये अस्य हृदि श्रिताः अथ मर्त्यः अमृतः भवति अत्र ब्रह्म समश्नुते
yadā sarve pramucyante kāmāḥ ye asya hṛdi śritāḥ atha martyaḥ amṛtaḥ bhavati atra brahma samaśnute
Word by word
“When all the desires lodged in his heart fall away, then the mortal becomes immortal; here he attains Brahman.”

Note Śaṅkara: for one who sees the true Self there is nothing else left to desire. The heart is the intellect, which, and not the Self, is the seat of desires. With death in the form of ignorance, desire and action destroyed, there is no going anywhere: here itself, like a lamp gone out, every bond is stilled and he becomes Brahman.

यदा सर्वे प्रभिद्यन्ते हृदयस्येह ग्रन्थयः । अथ मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्येतावद्ध्यनुशासनम् ॥ १५ ॥
yadā sarve prabhidyante hṛdayasyeha granthayaḥ | atha martyo 'mṛto bhavaty etāvaddhyanuśāsanam || 15 ||
Padaccheda
यदा सर्वे प्रभिद्यन्ते हृदयस्य इह ग्रन्थयः अथ मर्त्यः अमृतः भवति एतावत् हि अनुशासनम्
yadā sarve prabhidyante hṛdayasya iha granthayaḥ atha martyaḥ amṛtaḥ bhavati etāvat hi anuśāsanam
Word by word
“When all the knots of the heart are cut through here, then the mortal becomes immortal. This much alone is the teaching.”

Note Śaṅkara: the knots are the notions born of ignorance that bind fast, such as ‘I am this body’, ‘this wealth is mine’, ‘I am happy, I am unhappy’. They are destroyed, while one still lives, by the opposite knowledge, ‘I am Brahman and am not bound to birth and death’; the desires that spring from them then perish at the root. ‘This much’: this and no more is the teaching of all the Upaniṣads.

शतं चैका च हृदयस्य नाड्यस्तासां मूर्धानमभिनिःसृतैका । तयोर्ध्वमायन्नमृतत्वमेति विष्वङ्ङन्या उत्क्रमणे भवति ॥ १६ ॥
śataṃ caikā ca hṛdayasya nāḍyas tāsāṃ mūrdhānam abhiniḥsṛtaikā | tayordhvam āyann amṛtatvam eti viṣvaṅṅ anyā utkramaṇe bhavati || 16 ||
Padaccheda
शतम् च एका च हृदयस्य नाड्यः तासाम् मूर्धानम् अभिनिःसृता एका तया ऊर्ध्वम् आयन् अमृत-त्वम् एति विष्वङ् अन्याः उत्क्रमणे भवति
śatam ca ekā ca hṛdayasya nāḍyaḥ tāsām mūrdhānam abhiniḥsṛtā ekā tayā ūrdhvam āyan amṛta-tvam eti viṣvaṅ anyāḥ utkramaṇe bhavati
Word by word
“There are a hundred and one channels of the heart. One of them goes out to the crown of the head; going upward by it, one reaches immortality. The others, leading in every direction, serve for departing.”

Note Śaṅkara: for the knower who has become Brahman in this life there is no going at all. This verse is about those who know Brahman less perfectly, or follow other meditations, and go to the world of Brahmā; and it tells the fruit of the fire-teaching asked for earlier. The one channel is the suṣumnā. The immortality reached by it is relative, lasting to the dissolution of the elements, or it is the final immortality gained in time together with Brahmā. The other channels lead back to birth and death.

Note The text reads bhavati, a singular; Śaṅkara glosses it with the plural bhavanti, which the sense requires with anyāḥ.

अङ्गुष्ठमात्रः पुरुषोऽन्तरात्मा सदा जनानां हृदये संनिविष्टः । तं स्वाच्छरीरात्प्रवृहेन्मुञ्जादिवेषीकां धैर्येण । तं विद्याच्छुक्रममृतं तं विद्याच्छुक्रममृतमिति ॥ १७ ॥
aṅguṣṭhamātraḥ puruṣo 'ntarātmā sadā janānāṃ hṛdaye saṃniviṣṭaḥ | taṃ svāccharīrāt pravṛhen muñjād iveṣīkāṃ dhairyeṇa | taṃ vidyācchukram amṛtaṃ taṃ vidyācchukram amṛtam iti || 17 ||
Padaccheda
अङ्गुष्ठ-मात्रः पुरुषः अन्तरात्मा सदा जनानाम् हृदये संनिविष्टः तम् स्वात् शरीरात् प्रवृहेत् मुञ्जात् इव इषीकाम् धैर्येण तम् विद्यात् शुक्रम् अमृतम् तम् विद्यात् शुक्रम् अमृतम् इति
aṅguṣṭha-mātraḥ puruṣaḥ antarātmā sadā janānām hṛdaye saṃniviṣṭaḥ tam svāt śarīrāt pravṛhet muñjāt iva iṣīkām dhairyeṇa tam vidyāt śukram amṛtam tam vidyāt śukram amṛtam iti
Word by word
“The Person, the size of a thumb, the inner Self, is always seated in the hearts of people. One should draw him out from one's own body with steadiness, like the inner stalk from muñja grass. One should know him as the pure, the immortal; one should know him as the pure, the immortal.”

Note Śaṅkara: this sums up the meaning of all the vallīs. To ‘draw out’ is to set the Self apart from the body, as pure consciousness alone. The repetition, and the word iti, mark the end of the Upaniṣad.

मृत्युप्रोक्तां नचिकेतोऽथ लब्ध्वा विद्यामेतां योगविधिं च कृत्स्नम् । ब्रह्मप्राप्तो विरजोऽभूद्विमृत्युरन्योऽप्येवं यो विदध्यात्ममेव ॥ १८ ॥
mṛtyuproktāṃ naciketo 'tha labdhvā vidyām etāṃ yogavidhiṃ ca kṛtsnam | brahmaprāpto virajo 'bhūd vimṛtyur anyo 'py evaṃ yo vid adhyātmam eva || 18 ||
Padaccheda
मृत्यु-प्रोक्ताम् नचिकेतः अथ लब्ध्वा विद्याम् एताम् योग-विधिम् च कृत्स्नम् ब्रह्म-प्राप्तः विरजः अभूत् विमृत्युः अन्यः अपि एवम् यः वित् अध्यात्मम् एव
mṛtyu-proktām naciketaḥ atha labdhvā vidyām etām yoga-vidhim ca kṛtsnam brahma-prāptaḥ virajaḥ abhūt vimṛtyuḥ anyaḥ api evam yaḥ vit adhyātmam eva
Word by word
Then Naciketas, having received this knowledge taught by Death, and the whole method of yoga, attained Brahman and became free from stain and free from death. So too will anyone else who thus knows the inner Self.

Note Śaṅkara: this closes the story, in praise of the knowledge. ‘The whole’: with its means and its fruit. viraja: free of merit and demerit; vimṛtyu: free of desire and ignorance. evaṃvit: one who knows the inner Self in the way described; ‘he too becomes free from death’ is to be supplied.

Note naciketaḥ here is a nominative (naciketā in some editions), the subject of abhūt.

oṃ saha nāvavatu | saha nau bhunaktu | saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai | tejasvi nāvadhītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai || oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
