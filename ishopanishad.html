ॐ पूर्णमदः पूर्णमिदं पूर्णात्पूर्णमुदच्यते । पूर्णस्य पूर्णमादाय पूर्णमेवावशिष्यते ॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥
oṃ pūrṇamadaḥ pūrṇamidaṃ pūrṇātpūrṇamudacyate | pūrṇasya pūrṇamādāya pūrṇamevāvaśiṣyate || oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
Padaccheda
ॐ पूर्णम् अदः पूर्णम् इदम् पूर्णात् पूर्णम् उदच्यते पूर्णस्य पूर्णम् आदाय पूर्णम् एव अवशिष्यते ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः
oṃ pūrṇam adaḥ pūrṇam idam pūrṇāt pūrṇam udacyate pūrṇasya pūrṇam ādāya pūrṇam eva avaśiṣyate oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ
Word by word
Om. That is full; this is full. From the full the full comes forth. When the full is taken from the full, the full alone remains. Om. Peace, peace, peace.

Note The peace invocation recited before and after the study of this Upaniṣad. The edition of the bhāṣya prints it at the opening. Śaṅkara's Īśa bhāṣya does not comment on it; the translation gives the plain sense of the words.

ईशावास्योपनिषत्
Īśāvāsyopaniṣat · Eighteen mantras
ईशा वास्यमिदं सर्वं यत्किञ्च जगत्यां जगत् । तेन त्यक्तेन भुञ्जीथा मा गृधः कस्य स्विद्धनम् ॥ १ ॥
īśā vāsyam idaṃ sarvaṃ yat kiñca jagatyāṃ jagat | tena tyaktena bhuñjīthā mā gṛdhaḥ kasya sviddhanam || 1 ||
Padaccheda
ईशा वास्यम् इदम् सर्वम् यत् किम् च जगत्याम् जगत् तेन त्यक्तेन भुञ्जीथाः मा गृधः कस्य स्वित् धनम्
īśā vāsyam idam sarvam yat kim ca jagatyām jagat tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ mā gṛdhaḥ kasya svit dhanam
Word by word
All this, whatever moves on the earth, is to be covered by the Lord. By that renunciation protect (the Self); do not covet the wealth of anyone.

Introduction Introducing the Upaniṣad: these mantras are not employed in rites, because they reveal the true nature of the Self, and the Self is not subordinate to action. Its true nature, pure, untouched by evil, one, eternal, bodiless, all-pervading, conflicts with action: it is not something to be produced, changed, reached or refined, nor is it a doer or an experiencer. All the Upaniṣads, the Gītā and the Mokṣadharma end in determining it. Actions are enjoined by taking for granted what ordinary understanding holds of the Self, that it is many, a doer, an experiencer; he is entitled to them who wants their fruit, seen or unseen, and thinks ‘I am a twice-born, free of the defects that disqualify’. These mantras, by revealing the true nature of the Self, remove the natural ignorance about it and give rise to the knowledge of its oneness, the means of cutting off grief, delusion and the other marks of saṃsāra.

Bhāṣya Īśā, ‘by the one who rules’, the supreme Lord, the supreme Self, who is the inner Self of all creatures. vāsyam: to be covered. All this, moving and unmoving, which is unreal, is to be covered by one's own Self, the Lord, seen as the ultimately real: ‘I alone am all this’. So the bad smell that sandalwood or aloe takes on from damp is covered by its own true fragrance when the wood is rubbed. ‘On the earth’ stands for everything: the whole mass of modifications called name, form and action is given up by contemplating the Self as the ultimately real. One who is so engaged is entitled only to the renunciation of the three desires, for a son and the rest, not to action.

Bhāṣya Tyaktena means ‘by renunciation’ (tyāgena). What is given up, like a dead son or a servant dismissed, does not protect one, since it is no longer one's own; so the sense is ‘by renunciation’. bhuñjīthāḥ: ‘you should protect’ (pālayethāḥ). ‘Do not covet’: do not long for wealth, another's or your own; svid is a particle without meaning. Or: do not covet, for whose is wealth? It is no one's: all this is the Self's and the Self is all, so do not long for what is false.

कुर्वन्नेवेह कर्माणि जिजीविषेच्छतं समाः । एवं त्वयि नान्यथेतोऽस्ति न कर्म लिप्यते नरे ॥ २ ॥
kurvann eveha karmāṇi jijīviṣecchataṃ samāḥ | evaṃ tvayi nānyatheto 'sti na karma lipyate nare || 2 ||
Padaccheda
कुर्वन् एव इह कर्माणि जिजीविषेत् शतम् समाः एवम् त्वयि न अन्यथा इतः अस्ति न कर्म लिप्यते नरे
kurvan eva iha karmāṇi jijīviṣet śatam samāḥ evam tvayi na anyathā itaḥ asti na karma lipyate nare
Word by word
Only doing actions here should one wish to live a hundred years. For you, a man, (wishing to live) thus, there is no way other than this by which action does not cling.

Introduction The first mantra says that the knower of the Self protects the Self by renouncing the three desires and abiding in knowledge. This one teaches the other, who does not know the Self and cannot grasp it.

Bhāṣya Actions are the agnihotra and the rest; a hundred years is the full life of a man. That one wishes to live is taken as given; what is enjoined is that it be while doing actions. ‘A man’: one who thinks of himself as no more than a man. For such a one there is no other way by which evil action does not cling. jijīviṣet is a desiderative.

Bhāṣya How is it known that the first mantra speaks of the renouncer's steadfastness in knowledge and the second of steadfastness in action for one who cannot have the first? The opposition of knowledge and action, unshakable as a mountain, has been stated; and here too the one is told ‘to be covered by the Lord … by that renunciation … do not covet’ and the other to live doing actions. The fruits of the two will be shown to differ. These are the two paths, that of action first and that of renunciation after, of which renunciation excels (so the Taittirīyaka), as Vyāsa told his son after reflection: ‘there are these two paths on which the Vedas rest’.

असुर्या नाम ते लोका अन्धेन तमसावृताः । तांस्ते प्रेत्याभिगच्छन्ति ये के चात्महनो जनाः ॥ ३ ॥
asuryā nāma te lokā andhena tamasāvṛtāḥ | tāṃste pretyābhigacchanti ye ke cātmahano janāḥ || 3 ||
Padaccheda
असुर्याः नाम ते लोकाः अन्धेन तमसा आवृताः तान् ते प्रेत्य अभिगच्छन्ति ये के च आत्म-हनः जनाः
asuryāḥ nāma te lokāḥ andhena tamasā āvṛtāḥ tān te pretya abhigacchanti ye ke ca ātma-hanaḥ janāḥ
Word by word
Those worlds are the asuras', covered by blinding darkness. To them go, after departing, whatever people are slayers of the Self.

Introduction It is begun in order to blame the one who does not know.

Bhāṣya Measured against the non-dual state of the supreme Self even the gods are asuras, and the worlds that are theirs are ‘of the asuras’; nāma is a particle without meaning. The worlds are the fruits of action, births, so called because they are seen and experienced. The blinding darkness is ignorance, whose nature is not seeing. They go to these, down to the state of unmoving things, according to their action and what they have learnt.

Bhāṣya The slayers of the Self are those who do not know. They hide the Self, which is there, by the fault of ignorance; its effect, the awareness of being ageless and deathless, is hidden as if the Self had been slain. By that fault they pass from birth to birth.

अनेजदेकं मनसो जवीयो नैनद्देवा आप्नुवन्पूर्वमर्षत् । तद्धावतोऽन्यानत्येति तिष्ठत्तस्मिन्नपो मातरिश्वा दधाति ॥ ४ ॥
anejad ekaṃ manaso javīyo nainaddevā āpnuvanpūrvamarṣat | taddhāvato 'nyānatyeti tiṣṭhat tasminn apo mātariśvā dadhāti || 4 ||
Padaccheda
अन्-एजत् एकम् मनसः जवीयः न एनत् देवाः आप्नुवन् पूर्वम् अर्षत् तत् धावतः अन्यान् अत्येति तिष्ठत् तस्मिन् अपः मातरिश्वा दधाति
an-ejat ekam manasaḥ javīyaḥ na enat devāḥ āpnuvan pūrvam arṣat tat dhāvataḥ anyān atyeti tiṣṭhat tasmin apaḥ mātariśvā dadhāti
Word by word
Unmoving, one, swifter than the mind: the senses did not reach it, for it had gone ahead. Standing still, it passes beyond the others as they run. It being there, the wind apportions the activities (of all).

Introduction By slaying the Self those who do not know pass from birth to birth, and those who know are freed. What is the nature of that Self?

Bhāṣya Anejat: not trembling, never falling from its own state, always of one form. ‘Swifter than the mind’ does not contradict this: it is unmoving and one in its own form, without adjuncts, and swifter than the mind as it follows its adjunct, the mind, whose mark is intending and doubting. The mind, in the body, goes in an instant by a thought as far as the world of Brahmā; and when it arrives, the light of the Self's consciousness is found to be there already.

Bhāṣya The devas are the senses, eye and the rest, so called because they light up their objects. The mind is swifter than they, and its working comes between: not even a reflection of the Self becomes their object. ‘It had gone ahead’: being all-pervading like space. Free of every mark of saṃsāra and changeless, it seems to the undiscerning to undergo the changes its adjuncts make, and to be many, one in each body. It only seems to pass the others, mind, speech and senses, which are unlike the Self: ‘standing still’ shows this.

Bhāṣya Mātariśvan is the wind, the bearer of all breath, whose nature is activity, on which bodies and organs rest and in which all is woven; it is called the thread and upholds the whole world. apaḥ: the actions of living things, their movements, and the burning, shining and raining of fire, sun and cloud. dadhāti: apportions, or upholds (Taittirīya 2.8.1). All change of bodies and organs takes place only while the Self, whose nature is eternal consciousness, is there as the ground of all.

तदेजति तन्नैजति तद्दूरे तद्वन्तिके । तदन्तरस्य सर्वस्य तदु सर्वस्यास्य बाह्यतः ॥ ५ ॥
tad ejati tan naijati tad dūre tad v antike | tad antar asya sarvasya tad u sarvasyāsya bāhyataḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
तत् एजति तत् न एजति तत् दूरे तत् उ अन्तिके तत् अन्तर् अस्य सर्वस्य तत् उ सर्वस्य अस्य बाह्यतः
tat ejati tat na ejati tat dūre tat u antike tat antar asya sarvasya tat u sarvasya asya bāhyataḥ
Word by word
It moves, and it does not move. It is far, and it is near. It is within all this, and it is outside all this.

Introduction Mantras do not tire of repeating; it states again what the last mantra said.

Bhāṣya It moves, and that same does not move: of itself motionless, it seems to move. It is far, as it were, because those who do not know do not reach it in hundreds of crores of years; it is very near to those who know, being their Self. It is within all this world of name, form and action (‘the Self that is within all’, Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.4.1) and outside it, pervading like space; being subtle beyond measure it is within, and, as ‘a mass of consciousness only’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.5.13), without a gap.

यस्तु सर्वाणि भूतान्यात्मन्येवानुपश्यति । सर्वभूतेषु चात्मानं ततो न विजुगुप्सते ॥ ६ ॥
yas tu sarvāṇi bhūtāny ātmany evānupaśyati | sarvabhūteṣu cātmānaṃ tato na vijugupsate || 6 ||
Padaccheda
यः तु सर्वाणि भूतानि आत्मनि एव अनुपश्यति सर्व-भूतेषु च आत्मानम् ततः न विजुगुप्सते
yaḥ tu sarvāṇi bhūtāni ātmani eva anupaśyati sarva-bhūteṣu ca ātmānam tataḥ na vijugupsate
Word by word
But he who sees all beings in the Self alone, and the Self in all beings, feels no revulsion because of that.

Bhāṣya ‘he who’: the wandering renouncer who seeks liberation. He sees all beings, from the unmanifest down to unmoving things, in the Self alone, that is, as not other than the Self; and he sees his own Self as the Self of those beings too: ‘as I am the Self of this aggregate of body and organs, the witness of all its cognitions, the one who makes it conscious, alone and without qualities, so in that same form I am the Self of all, from the unmanifest down to unmoving things’.

Bhāṣya Vijugupsate: feels revulsion (ghṛṇā). This only restates what already follows: all revulsion comes to one who sees something bad that is other than himself; for one who sees only the wholly pure Self, without a break, there is no other thing to cause it.

यस्मिन्सर्वाणि भूतान्यात्मैवाभूद्विजानतः । तत्र को मोहः कः शोक एकत्वमनुपश्यतः ॥ ७ ॥
yasmin sarvāṇi bhūtāny ātmaivābhūd vijānataḥ | tatra ko mohaḥ kaḥ śoka ekatvam anupaśyataḥ || 7 ||
Padaccheda
यस्मिन् सर्वाणि भूतानि आत्मा एव अभूत् विजानतः तत्र कः मोहः कः शोकः एकत्वम् अनुपश्यतः
yasmin sarvāṇi bhūtāni ātmā eva abhūt vijānataḥ tatra kaḥ mohaḥ kaḥ śokaḥ ekatvam anupaśyataḥ
Word by word
When, for one who knows, all beings have become the Self alone, what delusion is there then, what grief, for him who sees oneness?

Introduction Another mantra states the same thing.

Bhāṣya Yasmin: ‘at the time when’, or ‘in the Self in which’. Those same beings have all become the Self through the seeing of the ultimately real Self. Grief and delusion, the seed of desire and action, come to one who does not know, not to one who sees the oneness of the Self, pure and like the sky. By denying grief and delusion, the effects of ignorance, the mantra shows the complete uprooting of saṃsāra together with its cause.

स पर्यगाच्छुक्रमकायमव्रणमस्नाविरं शुद्धमपापविद्धम् । कविर्मनीषी परिभूः स्वयम्भूर्याथातथ्यतोऽर्थान्व्यदधाच्छाश्वतीभ्यः समाभ्यः ॥ ८ ॥
sa paryagāc chukram akāyam avraṇam asnāviraṃ śuddham apāpaviddham | kavir manīṣī paribhūḥ svayambhūr yāthātathyato 'rthān vyadadhāc chāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ || 8 ||
Padaccheda
सः पर्यगात् शुक्रम् अ-कायम् अ-व्रणम् अ-स्नाविरम् शुद्धम् अ-पाप-विद्धम् कविः मनीषी परिभूः स्वयम्भूः याथातथ्यतः अर्थान् व्यदधात् शाश्वतीभ्यः समाभ्यः
saḥ paryagāt śukram a-kāyam a-vraṇam a-snāviram śuddham a-pāpa-viddham kaviḥ manīṣī paribhūḥ svayambhūḥ yāthātathyataḥ arthān vyadadhāt śāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ
Word by word
He is all-pervading, bright, bodiless, without wound, without sinews, pure, untouched by evil. The seer, the lord of the mind, above all, self-existent, he has allotted their duties as is fitting to the eternal years (the Prajāpatis).

Introduction What is the Self spoken of in the mantras so far like in its own form?

Bhāṣya Paryagāt: has gone all around, that is, pervades like space. śukra: pure, luminous. akāya: without the subtle body. avraṇa and asnāvira, ‘unwounded’ and ‘without sinews’, deny the gross body. śuddha: free of the stain of ignorance; this denies the causal body. apāpaviddha: free of dharma, adharma and other evil. The words from śukram on are to be turned into the masculine, since the mantra begins with saḥ and ends with kaviḥ, manīṣī.

Bhāṣya Kavi: seeing beyond, seeing all (‘there is no seer other than this’, Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.8.11). manīṣin: the ruler of the mind, the omniscient Lord. paribhū: he is above all. svayambhū: he is of himself; those he is above, and he who is above, are all he himself. That ever-free Lord, being omniscient, has allotted the things to be done, in keeping with action, its fruit and its means, to the Prajāpatis called the years, who are eternal.

Reading The e-text reads yāthātathyator 'thān; it is set right here to yāthātathyato 'rthān, the reading of the bhāṣya in the same file.

अन्धं तमः प्रविशन्ति येऽविद्यामुपासते । ततो भूय इव ते तमो य उ विद्यायां रताः ॥ ९ ॥
andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti ye 'vidyām upāsate | tato bhūya iva te tamo ya u vidyāyāṃ ratāḥ || 9 ||
Padaccheda
अन्धम् तमः प्रविशन्ति ये अविद्याम् उपासते ततः भूयः इव ते तमः ये उ विद्यायाम् रताः
andham tamaḥ praviśanti ye avidyām upāsate tataḥ bhūyaḥ iva te tamaḥ ye u vidyāyām ratāḥ
Word by word
They enter blinding darkness who are devoted to ignorance (rites alone); and into darkness greater than that, as it were, they who delight in knowledge (of the deities alone).

Introduction The first mantra taught steadfastness in knowledge through the giving up of all desires, the first sense of the Veda; the second taught steadfastness in action for the ignorant who wish to live, the second sense of the Veda. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka shows the same division: ‘he desired: may I have a wife’ (1.4.17) for the ignorant man of desire, whose fruit is the creation of the seven kinds of food; and for the knowers of the Self, who renounce the three desires, ‘what shall we do with offspring, we whose world is this Self’ (4.4.22). To the renouncers steadfast in knowledge the true nature of the Self was taught in mantras 3 to 8; they are the ones entitled here, not the men of desire (so also Śvetāśvatara 6.21). What follows is said to those steadfast in action, who wish to live doing actions.

Bhāṣya How is it known that it is not for all? The knowledge of the oneness of the Self (mantra 7) crushes the difference of end and means, and no one who is not deluded wishes to combine it with any action or with another knowledge. Here the one who does not know is blamed with a view to combination, and what is named is that which can be combined, by reasoning and by scripture: the ‘wealth of the gods’, the knowledge that concerns a deity, which is set beside action; not the knowledge of the supreme Self. Each has its own fruit: ‘by knowledge the world of the gods’, ‘by action the world of the fathers’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.16). The practice of either alone is blamed in order that the two be combined, not merely to blame, since nothing that scripture enjoins is something not to be done.

Bhāṣya Avidyā is what is other than vidyā, that is, action, since action is opposed to knowledge: those who perform the agnihotra and the rest alone, intent on that, enter the darkness whose nature is not seeing. vidyā is the knowledge of the deities: those who, leaving action, delight in that alone enter a darkness greater still.

Reading The e-text reads ya uvidyāyāṃ; the words are divided here as ya u vidyāyāṃ, as the bhāṣya takes them.

अन्यदेवाहुर्विद्ययान्यदाहुरविद्यया । इति शुश्रुम धीराणां ये नस्तद्विचचक्षिरे ॥ १० ॥
anyad evāhur vidyayānyad āhur avidyayā | iti śuśruma dhīrāṇāṃ ye nas tad vicacakṣire || 10 ||
Padaccheda
अन्यत् एव आहुः विद्यया अन्यत् आहुः अविद्यया इति शुश्रुम धीराणाम् ये नः तत् विचचक्षिरे
anyat eva āhuḥ vidyayā anyat āhuḥ avidyayā iti śuśruma dhīrāṇām ye naḥ tat vicacakṣire
Word by word
One (fruit), they say, comes by knowledge; another, they say, by rites. So we have heard from the wise, who explained that to us.

Bhāṣya A separate fruit is produced by knowledge, the world of the gods, and another by action, the world of the fathers (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.16). ‘We have heard the word of the wise’: the teachers who explained action and knowledge to us; this is their teaching, handed down in succession. The difference of the lesser fruits is given as the reason for combining the two; otherwise, of two things set side by side, the one without a fruit would only be subordinate to the one that has it.

Reading The e-text reads vidyayān yad āhur; the words are divided here as vidyayānyad āhur (vidyayā anyat).

विद्यां चाविद्यां च यस्तद्वेदोभयं सह । अविद्यया मृत्युं तीर्त्वा विद्ययामृतमश्नुते ॥ ११ ॥
vidyāṃ cāvidyāṃ ca yas tad vedobhayaṃ saha | avidyayā mṛtyuṃ tīrtvā vidyayāmṛtam aśnute || 11 ||
Padaccheda
विद्याम् च अविद्याम् च यः तत् वेद उभयम् सह अविद्यया मृत्युम् तीर्त्वा विद्यया अमृतम् अश्नुते
vidyām ca avidyām ca yaḥ tat veda ubhayam saha avidyayā mṛtyum tīrtvā vidyayā amṛtam aśnute
Word by word
He who knows both together, knowledge (of the deities) and rites, crosses death by rites and attains immortality by that knowledge.

Bhāṣya Vidyā and avidyā are the knowledge of the deities and action. He who knows that both are to be practised together by one person: for him who so combines them the two are joined, in turn, to the one end of man. Death here is natural action and natural knowledge, both of which the word means; crossing them by rites such as the agnihotra, he attains by the knowledge of the deities immortality, which is becoming one with the deity.

अन्धं तमः प्रविशन्ति येऽसम्भूतिमुपासते । ततो भूय इव ते तमो य उ सम्भूत्यां रताः ॥ १२ ॥
andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti ye 'sambhūtim upāsate | tato bhūya iva te tamo ya u sambhūtyāṃ ratāḥ || 12 ||
Padaccheda
अन्धम् तमः प्रविशन्ति ये असम्भूतिम् उपासते ततः भूयः इव ते तमः ये उ सम्भूत्याम् रताः
andham tamaḥ praviśanti ye asambhūtim upāsate tataḥ bhūyaḥ iva te tamaḥ ye u sambhūtyām ratāḥ
Word by word
They enter blinding darkness who worship what does not originate (Prakṛti, the unmanifest); and into darkness greater than that, as it were, they who delight in what originates (Hiraṇyagarbha).

Introduction Now the worship of the manifest and of the unmanifest are each blamed, with a view to combining them.

Bhāṣya Sambhūti is origination, and so the effect that has it; asambhūti is what is other than that: Prakṛti, the cause, the ignorance called the unmanifest, the seed of desire and action, whose nature is not seeing. Those who worship it enter a darkness of the same kind. sambhūti is Brahman as effect, called Hiraṇyagarbha.

अन्यदेवाहुः संभवादन्यदाहुरसंभवात् । इति शुश्रुम धीराणां ये नस्तद्विचचक्षिरे ॥ १३ ॥
anyad evāhuḥ saṃbhavād anyad āhur asaṃbhavāt | iti śuśruma dhīrāṇāṃ ye nas tad vicacakṣire || 13 ||
Padaccheda
अन्यत् एव आहुः सम्भवात् अन्यत् आहुः असम्भवात् इति शुश्रुम धीराणाम् ये नः तत् विचचक्षिरे
anyat eva āhuḥ sambhavāt anyat āhuḥ asambhavāt iti śuśruma dhīrāṇām ye naḥ tat vicacakṣire
Word by word
One (fruit), they say, comes from (the worship of) what originates; another, they say, from what does not originate. So we have heard from the wise, who explained that to us.

Introduction It gives the difference of their partial fruits as the reason for combining the two kinds of worship.

Bhāṣya From the worship of Brahman as effect comes a fruit of its own, the powers beginning with minuteness; from the worship of the unmanifest another, that which was called entering blinding darkness, and which the Paurāṇikas call merging in Prakṛti.

संभूतिं च विनाशं च यस्तद्वेदोभयं सह । विनाशेन मृत्युं तीर्त्वा संभूत्यामृतमश्नुते ॥ १४ ॥
saṃbhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ ca yas tad vedobhayaṃ saha | vināśena mṛtyuṃ tīrtvā saṃbhūtyāmṛtam aśnute || 14 ||
Padaccheda
सम्भूतिम् च विनाशम् च यः तत् वेद उभयम् सह विनाशेन मृत्युम् तीर्त्वा सम्भूत्या अमृतम् अश्नुते
sambhūtim ca vināśam ca yaḥ tat veda ubhayam saha vināśena mṛtyum tīrtvā sambhūtyā amṛtam aśnute
Word by word
He who knows both together, what does not originate (Prakṛti) and the perishable (Hiraṇyagarbha), crosses death by (the worship of) the perishable and attains immortality by (that of) what does not originate.

Introduction Since their fruits differ, and since both serve one end of man, it is right to combine the worship of what originates and of what does not.

Bhāṣya Vināśa, ‘destruction’, stands for the effect whose property it is, the property being named for its bearer: that is, Hiraṇyagarbha. By its worship one crosses death, which is want of lordly power, adharma, desire and other such faults, for the fruit of that worship is the gaining of the powers beginning with minuteness; and by the worship of the unmanifest one attains immortality, which is merging in Prakṛti.

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara reads the words sambhūtiṃ ca and sambhūtyā here as asambhūtim and asambhūtyā, with the letter ‘a’ elided, in keeping with the fruit that scripture gives, merging in Prakṛti. The translation follows him; the word-by-word meaning gives the words as they stand.

हिरण्मयेन पात्रेण सत्यस्यापिहितं मुखम् । तत्त्वं पूषन्नपावृणु सत्यधर्माय दृष्टये ॥ १५ ॥
hiraṇmayena pātreṇa satyasyāpihitaṃ mukham | tat tvaṃ pūṣann apāvṛṇu satyadharmāya dṛṣṭaye || 15 ||
Padaccheda
हिरण्मयेन पात्रेण सत्यस्य अपिहितम् मुखम् तत् त्वम् पूषन् अपावृणु सत्य-धर्माय दृष्टये
hiraṇmayena pātreṇa satyasya apihitam mukham tat tvam pūṣan apāvṛṇu satya-dharmāya dṛṣṭaye
Word by word
The door of the True is covered by a golden vessel. Uncover it, O Pūṣan, for me whose dharma is the True, that I may see.

Introduction The fruit that scripture teaches as won by human and divine wealth ends in merging in Prakṛti; so far goes the course of saṃsāra. Beyond it is the state of being the Self of all (mantra 7), the fruit of steadfastness in knowledge with the renunciation of all desires. So the two senses of the Veda, engagement and withdrawal, are shown here: for the first the Brāhmaṇa up to the Pravargya serves, for the second the Bṛhadāraṇyaka that follows it.

Bhāṣya He who wishes to live doing the rites from conception to the cremation ground, together with the knowledge of the lower Brahman, was told in mantra 11 that he attains immortality. By what path? ‘That which is the True is the sun yonder: the Puruṣa in that orb and the Puruṣa in the right eye’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.5.2). He who has worshipped Brahman as the True and done the rites as taught asks, when his end has come, the one whose Self is the True for the door by which to reach him.

Bhāṣya ‘golden’: as if of gold, made of light. By it, as by a lid, the mukha, the door, of Brahman as the True, which is in the orb of the sun, is covered. satyadharmāya: for me, whose dharma is the True because I have worshipped the True; or, for one who practises dharma as it truly is. dṛṣṭaye: that you, whose Self is the True, may be reached.

पूषन्नेकर्षे यम सूर्य प्राजापत्य व्यूह रश्मीन्समूह तेजः । यत्ते रूपं कल्याणतमं तत्ते पश्यामि योऽसावसौ पुरुषः सोऽहमस्मि ॥ १६ ॥
pūṣann ekarṣe yama sūrya prājāpatya vyūha raśmīn samūha tejaḥ | yat te rūpaṃ kalyāṇatamaṃ tat te paśyāmi yo 'sāv asau puruṣaḥ so 'ham asmi || 16 ||
Padaccheda
पूषन् एकर्षे यम सूर्य प्राजापत्य व्यूह रश्मीन् समूह तेजः यत् ते रूपम् कल्याणतमम् तत् ते पश्यामि यः असौ असौ पुरुषः सः अहम् अस्मि
pūṣan ekarṣe yama sūrya prājāpatya vyūha raśmīn samūha tejaḥ yat te rūpam kalyāṇatamam tat te paśyāmi yaḥ asau asau puruṣaḥ saḥ aham asmi
Word by word
O Pūṣan, sole traveller, Yama, Sūrya, son of Prajāpati: draw away your rays, gather in your burning light. That form of yours which is most auspicious, that I see by your grace. The Puruṣa who is yonder, yonder: I am he.

Bhāṣya Pūṣan, the sun, because he nourishes the world; ekarṣi, because he travels alone; Yama, because he restrains all; Sūrya, because he takes up rays, breaths and fluids; Prājāpatya, the offspring of Prajāpati. vyūha: remove your rays. samūha: draw together, withdraw, your tejas, the light that burns. ‘That I see’: by the grace of you who are the Self.

Bhāṣya ‘and I do not beg of you as a servant’: the Puruṣa in the orb of the sun, whose limbs are the vyāhṛtis, I am he. He is called Puruṣa because he has the shape of a person; or because by him, as breath and understanding, the whole world is filled; or because he lies in the city (the body).

वायुरनिलममृतमथेदं भस्मान्तं शरीरम् । ॐ क्रतो स्मर कृतं स्मर क्रतो स्मर कृतं स्मर ॥ १७ ॥
vāyur anilam amṛtam athedaṃ bhasmāntaṃ śarīram | oṃ krato smara kṛtaṃ smara krato smara kṛtaṃ smara || 17 ||
Padaccheda
वायुः अनिलम् अमृतम् अथ इदम् भस्म-अन्तम् शरीरम् ॐ क्रतो स्मर कृतम् स्मर क्रतो स्मर कृतम् स्मर
vāyuḥ anilam amṛtam atha idam bhasma-antam śarīram om krato smara kṛtam smara krato smara kṛtam smara
Word by word
(May my) breath (go to) the immortal air; then may this body end in ashes. Om. O will, remember; remember what has been done. O will, remember; remember what has been done.

Bhāṣya ‘now that I am about to die, may my breath leave its limit in the body and reach the air, its divine form, the Self of all, the immortal, the thread-Self’: this is to be supplied. It is also to be understood that the subtle body, refined by knowledge and rites, is to go up, since a path is being asked for. Then may this body, offered in the fire, end in ashes.

Bhāṣya Om: in keeping with the worship, the Brahman called Agni, whose nature is the True, is spoken of as not different from Om, its symbol. krato: you whose nature is intention (saṃkalpa). ‘Remember what I have to remember; its time has come. Remember, O Agni, what has been cultivated all this while, and the rites I have performed from childhood.’ The words are repeated out of earnestness.

अग्ने नय सुपथा राये अस्मान्विश्वानि देव वयुनानि विद्वान् । युयोध्यस्मज्जुहुराणमेनो भूयिष्ठां ते नम उक्तिं विधेम ॥ १८ ॥
agne naya supathā rāye asmān viśvāni deva vayunāni vidvān | yuyodhy asmaj juhurāṇam eno bhūyiṣṭhāṃ te nama uktiṃ vidhema || 18 ||
Padaccheda
अग्ने नय सुपथा राये अस्मान् विश्वानि देव वयुनानि विद्वान् युयोधि अस्मत् जुहुराणम् एनः भूयिष्ठाम् ते नमः-उक्तिम् विधेम
agne naya supathā rāye asmān viśvāni deva vayunāni vidvān yuyodhi asmat juhurāṇam enaḥ bhūyiṣṭhām te namaḥ-uktim vidhema
Word by word
O Agni, lead us by the good path to wealth (the fruit of our actions), O god who know all our deeds. Keep the crooked sin away from us. We offer you the fullest words of homage.

Introduction With another mantra he asks again for the path.

Bhāṣya ‘the good path’ is said to exclude the southern path: ‘I am weary of the southern path, with its going and returning; lead me by the good path, on which there is no going and coming back’. rāye: for wealth, that is, for the enjoyment of the fruit of action. vayunāni: actions, or knowledges. yuyodhi: separate, destroy. juhurāṇam: crooked, deceiving. ‘Then, made pure, we shall reach what we wish. But we cannot now serve you: we serve you with many words of homage.’

Bhāṣya Closing: some are in doubt on hearing mantras 11 and 14. Why should vidyā not be taken in its primary sense, the knowledge of the supreme Self, and immortality likewise? If it is said that this knowledge and action are opposed and cannot be combined, the objector answers that opposition and its absence are known from scripture alone, and that as ‘one should not harm any being’ is set aside by ‘one should slay the animal in the sacrifice’, so here scripture may enjoin the combination. Not so: ‘far apart, opposed and leading different ways are ignorance and what is known as knowledge’ (Kaṭha 1.2.4); they are opposed in cause, nature and fruit, and the two cannot exist together.

Bhāṣya Nor can they be in one person in turn: when knowledge has arisen, ignorance is destroyed and cannot be in that same person again. When the knowledge has arisen that fire is hot and bright, the ignorance that fire is cold or without light does not arise in the same person, nor doubt, nor not-knowing; and mantra 7 says that grief and delusion are impossible for one who sees oneness. Since ignorance is impossible, so is action, which rests on it. The immortality of mantra 11 is relative. If vidyā there were the knowledge of the supreme Self, the asking for a door and a path in mantras 15 to 18 would have no place. So the combination is of rites with worship, not with the knowledge of the supreme Self, and the meaning of the mantras is as explained.

Śānti mantra
ॐ पूर्णमदः पूर्णमिदं पूर्णात्पूर्णमुदच्यते । पूर्णस्य पूर्णमादाय पूर्णमेवावशिष्यते ॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥
oṃ pūrṇamadaḥ pūrṇamidaṃ pūrṇātpūrṇamudacyate | pūrṇasya pūrṇamādāya pūrṇamevāvaśiṣyate || oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
Padaccheda
ॐ पूर्णम् अदः पूर्णम् इदम् पूर्णात् पूर्णम् उदच्यते पूर्णस्य पूर्णम् आदाय पूर्णम् एव अवशिष्यते ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः
oṃ pūrṇam adaḥ pūrṇam idam pūrṇāt pūrṇam udacyate pūrṇasya pūrṇam ādāya pūrṇam eva avaśiṣyate oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ
Word by word
Om. That is full; this is full. From the full the full comes forth. When the full is taken from the full, the full alone remains. Om. Peace, peace, peace.

Note The peace invocation recited before and after the study of this Upaniṣad. The edition of the bhāṣya prints it at the opening. Śaṅkara's Īśa bhāṣya does not comment on it; the translation gives the plain sense of the words.
