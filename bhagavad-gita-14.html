With gratitudeBuilt on Pioneering Shoulders
चतुर्दशोऽध्यायः
Caturdaśo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 14 · Guṇatrayavibhāgayoga
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
परं भूयः प्रवक्ष्यामि ज्ञानानां ज्ञानमुत्तमम् । यज्ज्ञात्वा मुनयः सर्वे परां सिद्धिमितो गताः ॥ १ ॥
paraṃ bhūyaḥ pravakṣyāmi jñānānāṃ jñānamuttamam | yajjñātvā munayaḥ sarve parāṃ siddhimito gatāḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
परम् भूयः प्रवक्ष्यामि ज्ञानानाम् ज्ञानम् उत्तमम् यत् ज्ञात्वा मुनयः सर्वे पराम् सिद्धिम् इतः गताः
param bhūyaḥ pravakṣyāmi jñānānām jñānam uttamam yat jñātvā munayaḥ sarve parām siddhim itaḥ gatāḥ
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: I shall declare again the supreme knowledge, the best of knowledges, knowing which all the sages have gone from here to supreme perfection.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘again’, though taught more than once in the earlier chapters. It is supreme because its object is the supreme, and best because its fruit is the best. ‘Of knowledges’ does not mean absence of pride and the rest (13.7–11) but knowledge of things such as sacrifice; those do not lead to liberation, this does. The sages are renouncers given to reflection; perfection is liberation; ‘from here’ is from this bondage of the body.

इदं ज्ञानमुपाश्रित्य मम साधर्म्यमागताः । सर्गेऽपि नोपजायन्ते प्रलये न व्यथन्ति च ॥ २ ॥
idaṃ jñānamupāśritya mama sādharmyamāgatāḥ | sarge 'pi nopajāyante pralaye na vyathanti ca || 2 ||
Padaccheda
इदम् ज्ञानम् उपाश्रित्य मम साधर्म्यम् आगताः सर्गे अपि न उपजायन्ते प्रलये न व्यथन्ति च
idam jñānam upāśritya mama sādharmyam āgatāḥ sarge api na upajāyante pralaye na vyathanti ca
Word by word
Those who have resorted to this knowledge and come to be of my own nature are not born even at creation, and are not troubled at dissolution.

Note Śaṅkara: resorting to it is practising the means to knowledge. sādharmya here is having my very nature, and not merely having the same qualities, for the teaching of the Gītā admits no difference between the knower of the field and the Lord. The fruit is stated as praise. Dissolution is the time when even Brahmā perishes; they do not fall.

मम योनिर्महद्ब्रह्म तस्मिन्गर्भं दधाम्यहम् । संभवः सर्वभूतानां ततो भवति भारत ॥ ३ ॥
mama yonirmahadbrahma tasmingarbhaṃ dadhāmyaham | saṃbhavaḥ sarvabhūtānāṃ tato bhavati bhārata || 3 ||
Padaccheda
मम योनिः महत् ब्रह्म तस्मिन् गर्भम् दधामि अहम् संभवः सर्व-भूतानाम् ततः भवति भारत
mama yoniḥ mahat brahma tasmin garbham dadhāmi aham saṃbhavaḥ sarva-bhūtānām tataḥ bhavati bhārata
Word by word
The great Brahman is my womb; in that I place the seed. From that comes the birth of all beings, O descendant of Bharata.

Note Śaṅkara: the womb is my own illusion, Prakṛti made of the three guṇas, the cause of all beings. It is called great because it is greater than all its effects, and Brahman because it makes its modifications grow. The seed is the seed of the birth of Hiraṇyagarbha, the cause of the birth of all beings: I, the Lord, who have the two Prakṛtis as my powers, join the knower of the field, who takes on the limiting adjuncts of ignorance, desire and action, with the field.

सर्वयोनिषु कौन्तेय मूर्तयः सम्भवन्ति याः । तासां ब्रह्म महद्योनिरहं बीजप्रदः पिता ॥ ४ ॥
sarvayoniṣu kaunteya mūrtayaḥ sambhavanti yāḥ | tāsāṃ brahma mahadyonir ahaṃ bījapradaḥ pitā || 4 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-योनिषु कौन्तेय मूर्तयः सम्भवन्ति याः तासाम् ब्रह्म महत् योनिः अहम् बीज-प्रदः पिता
sarva-yoniṣu kaunteya mūrtayaḥ sambhavanti yāḥ tāsām brahma mahat yoniḥ aham bīja-pradaḥ pitā
Word by word
Whatever forms are born in all wombs, O son of Kuntī, the great Brahman is their womb, and I am the father who gives the seed.

Note Śaṅkara: the wombs are those of gods, ancestors, men, cattle, wild animals and the rest; the forms are bodies with their limbs set.

सत्त्वं रजस्तम इति गुणाः प्रकृतिसंभवाः । निबध्नन्ति महाबाहो देहे देहिनमव्ययम् ॥ ५ ॥
sattvaṃ rajastama iti guṇāḥ prakṛtisaṃbhavāḥ | nibadhnanti mahābāho dehe dehinamavyayam || 5 ||
Padaccheda
सत्त्वम् रजः तमः इति गुणाः प्रकृति-संभवाः निबध्नन्ति महा-बाहो देहे देहिनम् अव्ययम्
sattvam rajaḥ tamaḥ iti guṇāḥ prakṛti-saṃbhavāḥ nibadhnanti mahā-bāho dehe dehinam avyayam
Word by word
Sattva, rajas and tamas: these guṇas, born of Prakṛti, bind the undecaying embodied one in the body, O mighty-armed.

Note Śaṅkara: guṇa is a technical term here, and does not mean a quality resting in a substance, like colour. Like qualities they always depend on another, the knower of the field; being of the nature of ignorance they bind him, as it were: they come to be by taking him as their ground. It was said that the embodied one is not tainted (13.31); so ‘bind’ means ‘bind, as it were’.

तत्र सत्त्वं निर्मलत्वात्प्रकाशकमनामयम् । सुखसङ्गेन बध्नाति ज्ञानसङ्गेन चानघ ॥ ६ ॥
tatra sattvaṃ nirmalatvātprakāśakamanāmayam | sukhasaṅgena badhnāti jñānasaṅgena cānagha || 6 ||
Padaccheda
तत्र सत्त्वम् निर्मलत्वात् प्रकाशकम् अनामयम् सुख-सङ्गेन बध्नाति ज्ञान-सङ्गेन च अनघ
tatra sattvam nirmalatvāt prakāśakam anāmayam sukha-saṅgena badhnāti jñāna-saṅgena ca anagha
Word by word
Of these sattva, illuminating and free from harm because it is stainless, binds by attachment to happiness and by attachment to knowledge, O sinless one.

Note Śaṅkara: stainless like a crystal. Attachment to happiness is the thought ‘I am happy’, the false joining of happiness, an object, with the Self, the subject. That is ignorance, for a property of the object cannot belong to the subject; it makes the unattached as if attached. Knowledge here, named beside happiness, is likewise a property of the inner organ, the field, and not of the Self; otherwise there could be no attachment to it and no bondage.

रजो रागात्मकं विद्धि तृष्णासङ्गसमुद्भवम् । तन्निबध्नाति कौन्तेय कर्मसङ्गेन देहिनम् ॥ ७ ॥
rajo rāgātmakaṃ viddhi tṛṣṇāsaṅgasamudbhavam | tannibadhnāti kaunteya karmasaṅgena dehinam || 7 ||
Padaccheda
रजः राग-आत्मकम् विद्धि तृष्णा-सङ्ग-समुद्भवम् तत् निबध्नाति कौन्तेय कर्म-सङ्गेन देहिनम्
rajaḥ rāga-ātmakam viddhi tṛṣṇā-saṅga-samudbhavam tat nibadhnāti kaunteya karma-saṅgena dehinam
Word by word
Know rajas to be of the nature of passion, the source of thirst and attachment. It binds the embodied one, O son of Kuntī, by attachment to action.

Note Śaṅkara: rāga, from colouring, as red chalk colours. Thirst is longing for what has not been obtained; attachment (he reads āsaṅga) is the mind's fond clinging to what has. Attachment to action is being intent on actions that have seen and unseen ends.

तमस्त्वज्ञानजं विद्धि मोहनं सर्वदेहिनाम् । प्रमादालस्यनिद्राभिस्तन्निबध्नाति भारत ॥ ८ ॥
tamastvajñānajaṃ viddhi mohanaṃ sarvadehinām | pramādālasyanidrābhistannibadhnāti bhārata || 8 ||
Padaccheda
तमः तु अज्ञान-जम् विद्धि मोहनम् सर्व-देहिनाम् प्रमाद-आलस्य-निद्राभिः तत् निबध्नाति भारत
tamaḥ tu ajñāna-jam viddhi mohanam sarva-dehinām pramāda-ālasya-nidrābhiḥ tat nibadhnāti bhārata
Word by word
But know tamas to be born of ignorance, deluding all embodied ones. It binds by negligence, laziness and sleep, O descendant of Bharata.

Note Śaṅkara: deluding is causing want of discernment.

सत्त्वं सुखे सञ्जयति रजः कर्मणि भारत । ज्ञानमावृत्य तु तमः प्रमादे सञ्जयत्युत ॥ ९ ॥
sattvaṃ sukhe sañjayati rajaḥ karmaṇi bhārata | jñānamāvṛtya tu tamaḥ pramāde sañjayatyuta || 9 ||
Padaccheda
सत्त्वम् सुखे सञ्जयति रजः कर्मणि भारत ज्ञानम् आवृत्य तु तमः प्रमादे सञ्जयति उत
sattvam sukhe sañjayati rajaḥ karmaṇi bhārata jñānam āvṛtya tu tamaḥ pramāde sañjayati uta
Word by word
Sattva attaches one to happiness, rajas to action, O descendant of Bharata; but tamas, veiling knowledge, attaches one to negligence.

Note Śaṅkara: the knowledge veiled is the discernment produced by sattva. Negligence is not doing a duty that has fallen due.

रजस्तमश्चाभिभूय सत्त्वं भवति भारत । रजः सत्त्वं तमश्चैव तमः सत्त्वं रजस्तथा ॥ १० ॥
rajastamaścābhibhūya sattvaṃ bhavati bhārata | rajaḥ sattvaṃ tamaścaiva tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajastathā || 10 ||
Padaccheda
रजः तमः च अभिभूय सत्त्वम् भवति भारत रजः सत्त्वम् तमः च एव तमः सत्त्वम् रजः तथा
rajaḥ tamaḥ ca abhibhūya sattvam bhavati bhārata rajaḥ sattvam tamaḥ ca eva tamaḥ sattvam rajaḥ tathā
Word by word
Sattva arises, O descendant of Bharata, overpowering rajas and tamas; rajas, overpowering sattva and tamas; and likewise tamas, overpowering sattva and rajas.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘arises’ is ‘grows’. When one guṇa has grown over the other two it begins its own work: sattva knowledge and happiness, rajas action and thirst, tamas the veiling of knowledge.

सर्वद्वारेषु देहेऽस्मिन्प्रकाश उपजायते । ज्ञानं यदा तदा विद्याद्विवृद्धं सत्त्वमित्युत ॥ ११ ॥
sarvadvāreṣu dehe 'sminprakāśa upajāyate | jñānaṃ yadā tadā vidyādvivṛddhaṃ sattvamityuta || 11 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-द्वारेषु देहे अस्मिन् प्रकाशः उपजायते ज्ञानम् यदा तदा विद्यात् विवृद्धम् सत्त्वम् इति उत
sarva-dvāreṣu dehe asmin prakāśaḥ upajāyate jñānam yadā tadā vidyāt vivṛddham sattvam iti uta
Word by word
When light, which is knowledge, arises in all the gates in this body, then one should know that sattva has increased.

Note Śaṅkara: the gates are the organs, hearing and the rest, through which the Self perceives. The light is a state of the inner organ, the understanding; that itself is knowledge, and is the sign of sattva.

लोभः प्रवृत्तिरारम्भः कर्मणामशमः स्पृहा । रजस्येतानि जायन्ते विवृद्धे भरतर्षभ ॥ १२ ॥
lobhaḥ pravṛttirārambhaḥ karmaṇāmaśamaḥ spṛhā | rajasyetāni jāyante vivṛddhe bharatarṣabha || 12 ||
Padaccheda
लोभः प्रवृत्तिः आरम्भः कर्मणाम् अशमः स्पृहा रजसि एतानि जायन्ते विवृद्धे भरत-ऋषभ
lobhaḥ pravṛttiḥ ārambhaḥ karmaṇām aśamaḥ spṛhā rajasi etāni jāyante vivṛddhe bharata-ṛṣabha
Word by word
Greed, activity, the undertaking of actions, restlessness and longing: these arise when rajas has increased, O best of the Bharatas.

Note Śaṅkara: greed is the wish to take what belongs to another. Restlessness is the stirring of joy, passion and the like. Longing is thirst for things in general.

अप्रकाशोऽप्रवृत्तिश्च प्रमादो मोह एव च । तमस्येतानि जायन्ते विवृद्धे कुरुनन्दन ॥ १३ ॥
aprakāśo 'pravṛttiśca pramādo moha eva ca | tamasyetāni jāyante vivṛddhe kurunandana || 13 ||
Padaccheda
अप्रकाशः अप्रवृत्तिः च प्रमादः मोहः एव च तमसि एतानि जायन्ते विवृद्धे कुरु-नन्दन
aprakāśaḥ apravṛttiḥ ca pramādaḥ mohaḥ eva ca tamasi etāni jāyante vivṛddhe kuru-nandana
Word by word
Want of light, inactivity, negligence and delusion: these arise when tamas has increased, O joy of the Kurus.

Note Śaṅkara: want of light is want of discernment; inactivity is complete absence of effort, and negligence its result; delusion is dullness.

यदा सत्त्वे प्रवृद्धे तु प्रलयं याति देहभृत् । तदोत्तमविदां लोकानमलान्प्रतिपद्यते ॥ १४ ॥
yadā sattve pravṛddhe tu pralayaṃ yāti dehabhṛt | tadottamavidāṃ lokānamalānpratipadyate || 14 ||
Padaccheda
यदा सत्त्वे प्रवृद्धे तु प्रलयम् याति देहभृत् तदा उत्तम-विदाम् लोकान् अमलान् प्रतिपद्यते
yadā sattve pravṛddhe tu pralayam yāti dehabhṛt tadā uttama-vidām lokān amalān pratipadyate
Word by word
When the embodied one goes to death while sattva has increased, he reaches the stainless worlds of the knowers of the highest.

Note Śaṅkara: this shows that the fruit gained through death too is caused by attachment and passion and belongs to the guṇas. The ‘knowers of the highest’ are those who know the principles, mahat and the rest.

रजसि प्रलयं गत्वा कर्मसङ्गिषु जायते । तथा प्रलीनस्तमसि मूढयोनिषु जायते ॥ १५ ॥
rajasi pralayaṃ gatvā karmasaṅgiṣu jāyate | tathā pralīnastamasi mūḍhayoniṣu jāyate || 15 ||
Padaccheda
रजसि प्रलयम् गत्वा कर्म-सङ्गिषु जायते तथा प्रलीनः तमसि मूढ-योनिषु जायते
rajasi pralayam gatvā karma-saṅgiṣu jāyate tathā pralīnaḥ tamasi mūḍha-yoniṣu jāyate
Word by word
Going to death in rajas, he is born among those attached to action; likewise, having died in tamas, he is born in the wombs of the dull.

Note Śaṅkara: those attached to action are men; the wombs of the dull are those of cattle and the like.

कर्मणः सुकृतस्याहुः सात्त्विकं निर्मलं फलम् । रजसस्तु फलं दुःखमज्ञानं तमसः फलम् ॥ १६ ॥
karmaṇaḥ sukṛtasyāhuḥ sāttvikaṃ nirmalaṃ phalam | rajasastu phalaṃ duḥkhamajñānaṃ tamasaḥ phalam || 16 ||
Padaccheda
कर्मणः सुकृतस्य आहुः सात्त्विकम् निर्मलम् फलम् रजसः तु फलम् दुःखम् अज्ञानम् तमसः फलम्
karmaṇaḥ sukṛtasya āhuḥ sāttvikam nirmalam phalam rajasaḥ tu phalam duḥkham ajñānam tamasaḥ phalam
Word by word
They say that the fruit of action well done is stainless and of sattva; but the fruit of rajas is pain, and the fruit of tamas is ignorance.

Note Śaṅkara: action well done is action of sattva. As action is the topic, ‘of rajas’ and ‘of tamas’ mean of action of rajas and of action of tamas, which is adharma; the fruit is like its cause.

सत्त्वात्संजायते ज्ञानं रजसो लोभ एव च । प्रमादमोहौ तमसो भवतोऽज्ञानमेव च ॥ १७ ॥
sattvātsaṃjāyate jñānaṃ rajaso lobha eva ca | pramādamohau tamaso bhavato 'jñānameva ca || 17 ||
Padaccheda
सत्त्वात् संजायते ज्ञानम् रजसः लोभः एव च प्रमाद-मोहौ तमसः भवतः अज्ञानम् एव च
sattvāt saṃjāyate jñānam rajasaḥ lobhaḥ eva ca pramāda-mohau tamasaḥ bhavataḥ ajñānam eva ca
Word by word
From sattva arises knowledge, and from rajas greed; from tamas arise negligence and delusion, and ignorance too.
ऊर्ध्वं गच्छन्ति सत्त्वस्था मध्ये तिष्ठन्ति राजसाः । जघन्यगुणवृत्तस्था अधो गच्छन्ति तामसाः ॥ १८ ॥
ūrdhvaṃ gacchanti sattvasthā madhye tiṣṭhanti rājasāḥ | jaghanyaguṇavṛttasthā adho gacchanti tāmasāḥ || 18 ||
Padaccheda
ऊर्ध्वम् गच्छन्ति सत्त्व-स्थाः मध्ये तिष्ठन्ति राजसाः जघन्य-गुण-वृत्त-स्थाः अधः गच्छन्ति तामसाः
ūrdhvam gacchanti sattva-sthāḥ madhye tiṣṭhanti rājasāḥ jaghanya-guṇa-vṛtta-sthāḥ adhaḥ gacchanti tāmasāḥ
Word by word
Those who abide in sattva go upwards; those of rajas stay in the middle; those of tamas, abiding in the working of the lowest guṇa, go downwards.

Note Śaṅkara: upwards is birth in the worlds of the gods and the like; the middle is birth among men; downwards is birth among cattle and the like. The working of tamas is sleep, laziness and the rest.

नान्यं गुणेभ्यः कर्तारं यदा द्रष्टानुपश्यति । गुणेभ्यश्च परं वेत्ति मद्भावं सोऽधिगच्छति ॥ १९ ॥
nānyaṃ guṇebhyaḥ kartāraṃ yadā draṣṭānupaśyati | guṇebhyaśca paraṃ vetti madbhāvaṃ so 'dhigacchati || 19 ||
Padaccheda
न अन्यम् गुणेभ्यः कर्तारम् यदा द्रष्टा अनुपश्यति गुणेभ्यः च परम् वेत्ति मत्-भावम् सः अधिगच्छति
na anyam guṇebhyaḥ kartāram yadā draṣṭā anupaśyati guṇebhyaḥ ca param vetti mat-bhāvam saḥ adhigacchati
Word by word
When the seer sees no doer other than the guṇas, and knows what is higher than the guṇas, he attains my state.

Note Śaṅkara: having set out at length how false knowledge binds, the Lord now teaches liberation through right seeing. The guṇas, changed into the form of body, organs and objects, are the doers of all actions in every state; what is higher than they is the witness of their working.

गुणानेतानतीत्य त्रीन्देही देहसमुद्भवान् । जन्ममृत्युजरादुःखैर्विमुक्तोऽमृतमश्नुते ॥ २० ॥
guṇānetānatītya trīndehī dehasamudbhavān | janmamṛtyujarāduḥkhairvimukto 'mṛtamaśnute || 20 ||
Padaccheda
गुणान् एतान् अतीत्य त्रीन् देही देह-समुद्भवान् जन्म-मृत्यु-जरा-दुःखैः विमुक्तः अमृतम् अश्नुते
guṇān etān atītya trīn dehī deha-samudbhavān janma-mṛtyu-jarā-duḥkhaiḥ vimuktaḥ amṛtam aśnute
Word by word
Having gone beyond these three guṇas, from which the body arises, the embodied one, freed from birth, death, old age and pain, attains immortality.

Note Śaṅkara: he goes beyond them while still living; they are the limiting adjuncts that make up illusion, and the seed from which the body arises. Freed while still living, the knower attains immortality: this is how he attains ‘my state’.

अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
कैर्लिङ्गैस्त्रीन्गुणानेतानतीतो भवति प्रभो । किमाचारः कथं चैतांस्त्रीन्गुणानतिवर्तते ॥ २१ ॥
kairliṅgaistrīnguṇānetānatīto bhavati prabho | kimācāraḥ kathaṃ caitāṃstrīnguṇānativartate || 21 ||
Padaccheda
कैः लिङ्गैः त्रीन् गुणान् एतान् अतीतः भवति प्रभो किम्-आचारः कथम् च एतान् त्रीन् गुणान् अतिवर्तते
kaiḥ liṅgaiḥ trīn guṇān etān atītaḥ bhavati prabho kim-ācāraḥ katham ca etān trīn guṇān ativartate
Word by word
Arjuna said: By what marks, O Lord, is one who has gone beyond these three guṇas known? What is his conduct, and how does he pass beyond these three guṇas?
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
प्रकाशं च प्रवृत्तिं च मोहमेव च पाण्डव । न द्वेष्टि सम्प्रवृत्तानि न निवृत्तानि काङ्क्षति ॥ २२ ॥
prakāśaṃ ca pravṛttiṃ ca mohameva ca pāṇḍava | na dveṣṭi sampravṛttāni na nivṛttāni kāṅkṣati || 22 ||
Padaccheda
प्रकाशम् च प्रवृत्तिम् च मोहम् एव च पाण्डव न द्वेष्टि सम्प्रवृत्तानि न निवृत्तानि काङ्क्षति
prakāśam ca pravṛttim ca moham eva ca pāṇḍava na dveṣṭi sampravṛttāni na nivṛttāni kāṅkṣati
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: Light, activity and delusion, O son of Pāṇḍu: he does not hate them when they have arisen, nor long for them when they have ceased.

Note Śaṅkara: these are the effects of sattva, rajas and tamas. One who does not see rightly hates them: ‘a thought of tamas has arisen in me and I am deluded; painful activity of rajas has moved me from my own nature; sattva binds me to happiness.’ The one beyond the guṇas does not. This mark is not seen by others; it is known to oneself alone, for no one sees another's hatred or longing.

उदासीनवदासीनो गुणैर्यो न विचाल्यते । गुणा वर्तन्त इत्येव योऽवतिष्ठति नेङ्गते ॥ २३ ॥
udāsīnavadāsīno guṇairyo na vicālyate | guṇā vartanta ityeva yo 'vatiṣṭhati neṅgate || 23 ||
Padaccheda
उदासीन-वत् आसीनः गुणैः यः न विचाल्यते गुणाः वर्तन्ते इति एव यः अवतिष्ठति न इङ्गते
udāsīna-vat āsīnaḥ guṇaiḥ yaḥ na vicālyate guṇāḥ vartante iti eva yaḥ avatiṣṭhati na iṅgate
Word by word
He who, seated like one indifferent, is not shaken by the guṇas; who, knowing that it is only the guṇas that act, stands firm and does not move;

Note Śaṅkara: as an indifferent man takes no side, so this renouncer, a knower of the Self, is set on the path that leads beyond the guṇas and is not moved from his discernment. The guṇas, changed into body, organs and objects, act on one another. avatiṣṭhati is in the parasmaipada for the sake of the metre; another reading is anutiṣṭhati.

समदुःखसुखः स्वस्थः समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चनः । तुल्यप्रियाप्रियो धीरस्तुल्यनिन्दात्मसंस्तुतिः ॥ २४ ॥
samaduḥkhasukhaḥ svasthaḥ samaloṣṭāśmakāñcanaḥ | tulyapriyāpriyo dhīrastulyanindātmasaṃstutiḥ || 24 ||
Padaccheda
सम-दुःख-सुखः स्वस्थः सम-लोष्ट-अश्म-काञ्चनः तुल्य-प्रिय-अप्रियः धीरः तुल्य-निन्दा-आत्म-संस्तुतिः
sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ svasthaḥ sama-loṣṭa-aśma-kāñcanaḥ tulya-priya-apriyaḥ dhīraḥ tulya-nindā-ātma-saṃstutiḥ
Word by word
the same in pain and pleasure, abiding in himself, to whom a clod, a stone and gold are the same, to whom the pleasant and the unpleasant are alike, wise, to whom blame and praise of himself are alike;

Note Śaṅkara: svastha is abiding in his own Self, serene.

मानावमानयोस्तुल्यस्तुल्यो मित्रारिपक्षयोः । सर्वारम्भपरित्यागी गुणातीतः स उच्यते ॥ २५ ॥
mānāvamānayostulyastulyo mitrāripakṣayoḥ | sarvārambhaparityāgī guṇātītaḥ sa ucyate || 25 ||
Padaccheda
मान-अवमानयोः तुल्यः तुल्यः मित्र-अरि-पक्षयोः सर्व-आरम्भ-परित्यागी गुण-अतीतः सः उच्यते
māna-avamānayoḥ tulyaḥ tulyaḥ mitra-ari-pakṣayoḥ sarva-ārambha-parityāgī guṇa-atītaḥ saḥ ucyate
Word by word
the same in honour and dishonour, the same towards the sides of friend and foe, who has given up all undertakings: he is said to have gone beyond the guṇas.

Note Śaṅkara: though indifferent in his own view, in the view of others he may seem to be on the side of a friend or a foe; hence this is said. Undertakings are actions with seen and unseen ends; he gives up all action except what merely keeps the body alive. Verses 23–25 describe what the renouncer who seeks liberation must practise as the means to go beyond the guṇas, as long as it needs effort; once it is firm and known to himself, it is the mark of one who has gone beyond them.

मां च योऽव्यभिचारेण भक्तियोगेन सेवते । स गुणान्समतीत्यैतान्ब्रह्मभूयाय कल्पते ॥ २६ ॥
māṃ ca yo 'vyabhicāreṇa bhaktiyogena sevate | sa guṇānsamatītyaitānbrahmabhūyāya kalpate || 26 ||
Padaccheda
माम् च यः अव्यभिचारेण भक्ति-योगेन सेवते सः गुणान् समतीत्य एतान् ब्रह्म-भूयाय कल्पते
mām ca yaḥ avyabhicāreṇa bhakti-yogena sevate saḥ guṇān samatītya etān brahma-bhūyāya kalpate
Word by word
And he who serves me with the unswerving yoga of devotion, having gone beyond these guṇas, is fit for becoming Brahman.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘me’ is the Lord, Nārāyaṇa, who dwells in the heart of all beings; ‘he’ is a renouncer or a man of action. Devotion itself is the yoga. Becoming Brahman is liberation.

ब्रह्मणो हि प्रतिष्ठाहममृतस्याव्ययस्य च । शाश्वतस्य च धर्मस्य सुखस्यैकान्तिकस्य च ॥ २७ ॥
brahmaṇo hi pratiṣṭhāhamamṛtasyāvyayasya ca | śāśvatasya ca dharmasya sukhasyaikāntikasya ca || 27 ||
Padaccheda
ब्रह्मणः हि प्रतिष्ठा अहम् अमृतस्य अव्ययस्य च शाश्वतस्य च धर्मस्य सुखस्य ऐकान्तिकस्य च
brahmaṇaḥ hi pratiṣṭhā aham amṛtasya avyayasya ca śāśvatasya ca dharmasya sukhasya aikāntikasya ca
Word by word
For I am the abode of Brahman, the immortal and undecaying, of the eternal dharma and of unfailing happiness.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘I’ is the inner Self, in which Brahman, the supreme Self, abides; by right knowledge the inner Self is known to be the supreme Self. He takes ‘immortal’, ‘undecaying’, ‘eternal’, ‘dharma’ (what is reached by the discipline of knowledge), ‘happiness’ and ‘unfailing’ all as describing that Brahman. Or: the power of the Lord by which Brahman acts to show grace to devotees is I, Brahman itself, since a power and its holder are not different. Or: ‘Brahman’ is Brahman with distinctions, and I, without distinctions, am its abode; and the abode too of the eternal dharma, which is steadfastness in knowledge, and of the unfailing happiness born of it.
