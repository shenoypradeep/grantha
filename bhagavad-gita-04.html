चतुर्थोऽध्यायः
Caturtho 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 4 · Jñānakarmasaṃnyāsayoga
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
इमं विवस्वते योगं प्रोक्तवानहमव्ययम् । विवस्वान्मनवे प्राह मनुरिक्ष्वाकवेऽब्रवीत् ॥ १ ॥
imaṃ vivasvate yogaṃ proktavānahamavyayam | vivasvānmanave prāha manurikṣvākave 'bravīt || 1 ||
Padaccheda
इमम् विवस्वते योगम् प्रोक्तवान् अहम् अव्ययम् विवस्वान् मनवे प्राह मनुः इक्ष्वाकवे अब्रवीत्
imam vivasvate yogam proktavān aham avyayam vivasvān manave prāha manuḥ ikṣvākave abravīt
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: I taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvat; Vivasvat declared it to Manu, and Manu told it to Ikṣvāku.

Bhāṣya ‘this yoga’ is the one taught in the two preceding adhyāyas. It was taught to Vivasvat, the Sun, at the beginning of creation, to give strength to the warriors who protect the world. It is imperishable because its fruit, liberation, does not perish. Ikṣvāku was Manu's son, the first king.

एवं परंपराप्राप्तमिमं राजर्षयो विदुः । स कालेनेह महता योगो नष्टः परंतप ॥ २ ॥
evaṃ paraṃparāprāptamimaṃ rājarṣayo viduḥ | sa kāleneha mahatā yogo naṣṭaḥ paraṃtapa || 2 ||
Padaccheda
एवम् परंपरा-प्राप्तम् इमम् राजर्षयः विदुः सः कालेन इह महता योगः नष्टः परंतप
evam paraṃparā-prāptam imam rājarṣayaḥ viduḥ saḥ kālena iha mahatā yogaḥ naṣṭaḥ paraṃtapa
Word by word
Thus handed down in succession, the royal sages knew it. Through a long stretch of time that yoga was lost here, O scorcher of foes.

Bhāṣya Handed down in the line of warriors. rājarṣi: one who is both king and sage. ‘Lost’: the line of its teaching was broken.

स एवायं मया तेऽद्य योगः प्रोक्तः पुरातनः । भक्तोऽसि मे सखा चेति रहस्यं ह्येतदुत्तमम् ॥ ३ ॥
sa evāyaṃ mayā te 'dya yogaḥ proktaḥ purātanaḥ | bhakto 'si me sakhā ceti rahasyaṃ hyetaduttamam || 3 ||
Padaccheda
सः एव अयम् मया ते अद्य योगः प्रोक्तः पुरातनः भक्तः असि मे सखा च इति रहस्यम् हि एतत् उत्तमम्
saḥ eva ayam mayā te adya yogaḥ proktaḥ purātanaḥ bhaktaḥ asi me sakhā ca iti rahasyam hi etat uttamam
Word by word
That same ancient yoga I have taught you today, because you are my devotee and my friend; for this is the highest secret.

Bhāṣya The secret is the yoga, that is, knowledge.

अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
अपरं भवतो जन्म परं जन्म विवस्वतः । कथमेतद्विजानीयां त्वमादौ प्रोक्तवानिति ॥ ४ ॥
aparaṃ bhavato janma paraṃ janma vivasvataḥ | kathametadvijānīyāṃ tvamādau proktavāniti || 4 ||
Padaccheda
अपरम् भवतः जन्म परम् जन्म विवस्वतः कथम् एतत् विजानीयाम् त्वम् आदौ प्रोक्तवान् इति
aparam bhavataḥ janma param janma vivasvataḥ katham etat vijānīyām tvam ādau proktavān iti
Word by word
Arjuna said: Your birth was later, and the birth of Vivasvat earlier. How am I to understand that you taught this in the beginning?

Bhāṣya Arjuna asks as though raising an objection, in order to remove the notion, held by the foolish, that Vāsudeva is not the Lord and not all-knowing. ‘Later’: in the house of Vasudeva.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
बहूनि मे व्यतीतानि जन्मानि तव चार्जुन । तान्यहं वेद सर्वाणि न त्वं वेत्थ परंतप ॥ ५ ॥
bahūni me vyatītāni janmāni tava cārjuna | tānyahaṃ veda sarvāṇi na tvaṃ vettha paraṃtapa || 5 ||
Padaccheda
बहूनि मे व्यतीतानि जन्मानि तव च अर्जुन तानि अहम् वेद सर्वाणि न त्वम् वेत्थ परंतप
bahūni me vyatītāni janmāni tava ca arjuna tāni aham veda sarvāṇi na tvam vettha paraṃtapa
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: Many births of mine have passed, and of yours too, O Arjuna. I know them all; you do not, O scorcher of foes.

Bhāṣya Arjuna does not know them because his power of knowledge is obstructed by merit, demerit and the like. The Lord, by nature eternally pure, awake and free, has a power of knowledge that nothing covers.

अजोऽपि सन्नव्ययात्मा भूतानामीश्वरोऽपि सन् । प्रकृतिं स्वामधिष्ठाय सम्भवाम्यात्ममायया ॥ ६ ॥
ajo 'pi sannavyayātmā bhūtānāmīśvaro 'pi san | prakṛtiṃ svāmadhiṣṭhāya sambhavāmyātmamāyayā || 6 ||
Padaccheda
अजः अपि सन् अव्यय-आत्मा भूतानाम् ईश्वरः अपि सन् प्रकृतिम् स्वाम् अधिष्ठाय सम्भवामि आत्म-मायया
ajaḥ api san avyaya-ātmā bhūtānām īśvaraḥ api san prakṛtim svām adhiṣṭhāya sambhavāmi ātma-māyayā
Word by word
Though I am unborn and of imperishable nature, and though I am the Lord of beings, I take control of my own Prakṛti and come into being by my own power of illusion.

Bhāṣya This answers how the eternal Lord, who has neither merit nor demerit, can be born. avyayātmā: whose power of knowledge never wanes. His ‘own Prakṛti’ is his māyā, made of the three guṇas, under whose sway the whole world moves, and deluded by which it does not know its own Self, Vāsudeva. He seems to have a body and seems to be born, by his own māyā, and not in reality as people are.

यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत । अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् ॥ ७ ॥
yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānirbhavati bhārata | abhyutthānamadharmasya tadātmānaṃ sṛjāmyaham || 7 ||
Padaccheda
यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिः भवति भारत अभ्युत्थानम् अधर्मस्य तदा आत्मानम् सृजामि अहम्
yadā yadā hi dharmasya glāniḥ bhavati bhārata abhyutthānam adharmasya tadā ātmānam sṛjāmi aham
Word by word
Whenever there is a decline of dharma, O Bhārata, and a rising of adharma, then I send myself forth.

Bhāṣya Dharma here is that marked by the orders of society and the stages of life, the means by which living beings gain prosperity and the highest good. He sends himself forth by māyā.

परित्राणाय साधूनां विनाशाय च दुष्कृताम् । धर्मसंस्थापनार्थाय सम्भवामि युगे युगे ॥ ८ ॥
paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām | dharmasaṃsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge || 8 ||
Padaccheda
परित्राणाय साधूनाम् विनाशाय च दुष्कृताम् धर्म-संस्थापन-अर्थाय सम्भवामि युगे युगे
paritrāṇāya sādhūnām vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām dharma-saṃsthāpana-arthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge
Word by word
For the protection of the good, for the destruction of evildoers, and for the sake of establishing dharma, I come into being in age after age.

Bhāṣya The good are those who keep to the right path.

जन्म कर्म च मे दिव्यमेवं यो वेत्ति तत्त्वतः । त्यक्त्वा देहं पुनर्जन्म नैति मामेति सोऽर्जुन ॥ ९ ॥
janma karma ca me divyamevaṃ yo vetti tattvataḥ | tyaktvā dehaṃ punarjanma naiti māmeti so 'rjuna || 9 ||
Padaccheda
जन्म कर्म च मे दिव्यम् एवम् यः वेत्ति तत्त्वतः त्यक्त्वा देहम् पुनर्जन्म न एति माम् एति सः अर्जुन
janma karma ca me divyam evam yaḥ vetti tattvataḥ tyaktvā deham punarjanma na eti mām eti saḥ arjuna
Word by word
He who truly knows my divine birth and action in this way does not go to rebirth on leaving the body; he comes to me, O Arjuna.

Bhāṣya The birth is of the nature of māyā, and the action is the protection of the good and the rest; both are divine, not of the ordinary kind, belonging to the Lord. ‘He comes to me’: he is liberated.

वीतरागभयक्रोधा मन्मया मामुपाश्रिताः । बहवो ज्ञानतपसा पूता मद्भावमागताः ॥ १० ॥
vītarāgabhayakrodhā manmayā māmupāśritāḥ | bahavo jñānatapasā pūtā madbhāvamāgatāḥ || 10 ||
Padaccheda
वीत-राग-भय-क्रोधाः मद्-मयाः माम् उपाश्रिताः बहवः ज्ञान-तपसा पूताः मद्-भावम् आगताः
vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhāḥ mad-mayāḥ mām upāśritāḥ bahavaḥ jñāna-tapasā pūtāḥ mad-bhāvam āgatāḥ
Word by word
Free from attachment, fear and anger, full of me and taking refuge in me, many, purified by the austerity of knowledge, have come to my state.

Bhāṣya This path to liberation is not new. manmayāḥ: knowers of Brahman, who see no difference from the Lord. The austerity is knowledge itself, of the supreme Self; the word shows that steadfastness in knowledge needs no other austerity. ‘My state’ is the state of the Lord, liberation.

ये यथा मां प्रपद्यन्ते तांस्तथैव भजाम्यहम् । मम वर्त्मानुवर्तन्ते मनुष्याः पार्थ सर्वशः ॥ ११ ॥
ye yathā māṃ prapadyante tāṃstathaiva bhajāmyaham | mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ || 11 ||
Padaccheda
ये यथा माम् प्रपद्यन्ते तान् तथा एव भजामि अहम् मम वर्त्म अनुवर्तन्ते मनुष्याः पार्थ सर्वशः
ye yathā mām prapadyante tān tathā eva bhajāmi aham mama vartma anuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ
Word by word
In whatever way men approach me, in that very way I favour them. Men follow my path in every way, O Pārtha.

Bhāṣya This answers the charge that the Lord gives his own state to some and not to all out of attachment and aversion. He favours each by giving what each seeks: to those who want some fruit, that fruit; to those who do what is taught without wanting its fruit and seek liberation, knowledge; to the knowers who have renounced and seek liberation, liberation; to the distressed, relief. One person cannot seek both a fruit and liberation at once.

काङ्क्षन्तः कर्मणां सिद्धिं यजन्त इह देवताः । क्षिप्रं हि मानुषे लोके सिद्धिर्भवति कर्मजा ॥ १२ ॥
kāṅkṣantaḥ karmaṇāṃ siddhiṃ yajanta iha devatāḥ | kṣipraṃ hi mānuṣe loke siddhirbhavati karmajā || 12 ||
Padaccheda
काङ्क्षन्तः कर्मणाम् सिद्धिम् यजन्ते इह देवताः क्षिप्रम् हि मानुषे लोके सिद्धिः भवति कर्म-जा
kāṅkṣantaḥ karmaṇām siddhim yajante iha devatāḥ kṣipram hi mānuṣe loke siddhiḥ bhavati karma-jā
Word by word
Desiring the success of their actions, men here sacrifice to the gods; for in the human world success born of action comes quickly.

Bhāṣya This is why not all seek liberation. The gods are Indra, Agni and the rest. It is in the human world that scripture applies and that people are entitled to the dharmas of the orders of society and the stages of life.

Reading The DCS takes yajanta as a participle, yajantaḥ; it is read here as the verb yajante, as Śaṅkara does.

चातुर्वर्ण्यं मया सृष्टं गुणकर्मविभागशः । तस्य कर्तारमपि मां विद्ध्यकर्तारमव्ययम् ॥ १३ ॥
cāturvarṇyaṃ mayā sṛṣṭaṃ guṇakarmavibhāgaśaḥ | tasya kartāramapi māṃ viddhyakartāramavyayam || 13 ||
Padaccheda
चातुर्वर्ण्यम् मया सृष्टम् गुण-कर्म-विभागशः तस्य कर्तारम् अपि माम् विद्धि अकर्तारम् अव्ययम्
cāturvarṇyam mayā sṛṣṭam guṇa-karma-vibhāgaśaḥ tasya kartāram api mām viddhi akartāram avyayam
Word by word
The four orders of society were created by me according to the division of the guṇas and of actions. Though I am its author, know me to be a non-doer, changeless.

Bhāṣya The brahmin, in whom sattva prevails, has self-control, austerity and the like for his actions; the warrior, in whom rajas prevails with sattva subordinate, has valour and the like; the trader, in whom rajas prevails with tamas subordinate, has farming and the like; the servant, in whom tamas prevails with rajas subordinate, has service. Though the Lord is the author of this by way of māyā, in truth he does nothing, and so is not bound to the cycle of birth and death.

न मां कर्माणि लिम्पन्ति न मे कर्मफले स्पृहा । इति मां योऽभिजानाति कर्मभिर्न स बध्यते ॥ १४ ॥
na māṃ karmāṇi limpanti na me karmaphale spṛhā | iti māṃ yo 'bhijānāti karmabhirna sa badhyate || 14 ||
Padaccheda
न माम् कर्माणि लिम्पन्ति न मे कर्म-फले स्पृहा इति माम् यः अभिजानाति कर्मभिः न सः बध्यते
na mām karmāṇi limpanti na me karma-phale spṛhā iti mām yaḥ abhijānāti karmabhiḥ na saḥ badhyate
Word by word
Actions do not stain me, nor have I any longing for the fruit of action. He who knows me thus is not bound by actions.

Bhāṣya They do not stain him because he has no sense of ‘I’ in them. Anyone else who knows him as his own Self, thinking ‘I am not the doer, and I have no longing for the fruit of action’, is likewise not bound: for him too actions do not produce another body.

एवं ज्ञात्वा कृतं कर्म पूर्वैरपि मुमुक्षुभिः । कुरु कर्मैव तस्मात्त्वं पूर्वैः पूर्वतरं कृतम् ॥ १५ ॥
evaṃ jñātvā kṛtaṃ karma pūrvairapi mumukṣubhiḥ | kuru karmaiva tasmāttvaṃ pūrvaiḥ pūrvataraṃ kṛtam || 15 ||
Padaccheda
एवम् ज्ञात्वा कृतम् कर्म पूर्वैः अपि मुमुक्षुभिः कुरु कर्म एव तस्मात् त्वम् पूर्वैः पूर्वतरम् कृतम्
evam jñātvā kṛtam karma pūrvaiḥ api mumukṣubhiḥ kuru karma eva tasmāt tvam pūrvaiḥ pūrvataram kṛtam
Word by word
Knowing this, the ancients who sought liberation also performed action. Therefore perform action yourself, as the ancients did in earlier times.

Bhāṣya He is not to sit idle, nor to renounce. If Arjuna does not know the Self, he should act to purify himself; if he knows the truth, for the sake of holding the world together. The ancients are Janaka and the others.

किं कर्म किमकर्मेति कवयोऽप्यत्र मोहिताः । तत्ते कर्म प्रवक्ष्यामि यज्ज्ञात्वा मोक्ष्यसेऽशुभात् ॥ १६ ॥
kiṃ karma kimakarmeti kavayo 'pyatra mohitāḥ | tatte karma pravakṣyāmi yajjñātvā mokṣyase 'śubhāt || 16 ||
Padaccheda
किम् कर्म किम् अकर्म इति कवयः अपि अत्र मोहिताः तत् ते कर्म प्रवक्ष्यामि यत् ज्ञात्वा मोक्ष्यसे अशुभात्
kim karma kim akarma iti kavayaḥ api atra mohitāḥ tat te karma pravakṣyāmi yat jñātvā mokṣyase aśubhāt
Word by word
What is action? What is inaction? Even the wise are confused about this. I shall explain action to you, knowing which you will be freed from evil.

Bhāṣya He will explain both action and inaction. The evil is the cycle of birth and death.

कर्मणो ह्यपि बोद्धव्यं बोद्धव्यं च विकर्मणः । अकर्मणश्च बोद्धव्यं गहना कर्मणो गतिः ॥ १७ ॥
karmaṇo hyapi boddhavyaṃ boddhavyaṃ ca vikarmaṇaḥ | akarmaṇaśca boddhavyaṃ gahanā karmaṇo gatiḥ || 17 ||
Padaccheda
कर्मणः हि अपि बोद्धव्यम् बोद्धव्यम् च विकर्मणः अकर्मणः च बोद्धव्यम् गहना कर्मणः गतिः
karmaṇaḥ hi api boddhavyam boddhavyam ca vikarmaṇaḥ akarmaṇaḥ ca boddhavyam gahanā karmaṇaḥ gatiḥ
Word by word
For there is something to be understood about action, about forbidden action, and about inaction. The true nature of action is hard to fathom.

Bhāṣya Action is what scripture enjoins, vikarma what it forbids, and inaction is sitting still. ‘Of action’ stands for all three.

कर्मण्यकर्म यः पश्येदकर्मणि च कर्म यः । स बुद्धिमान्मनुष्येषु स युक्तः कृत्स्नकर्मकृत् ॥ १८ ॥
karmaṇyakarma yaḥ paśyedakarmaṇi ca karma yaḥ | sa buddhimānmanuṣyeṣu sa yuktaḥ kṛtsnakarmakṛt || 18 ||
Padaccheda
कर्मणि अकर्म यः पश्येत् अकर्मणि च कर्म यः सः बुद्धिमान् मनुष्येषु सः युक्तः कृत्स्न-कर्म-कृत्
karmaṇi akarma yaḥ paśyet akarmaṇi ca karma yaḥ saḥ buddhimān manuṣyeṣu saḥ yuktaḥ kṛtsna-karma-kṛt
Word by word
He who sees inaction in action, and action in inaction, is wise among men; he is a yogī, and has done all that is to be done.

Bhāṣya Action (karma) is whatever is done, mere activity; to see inaction in it is to see the absence of action. Inaction, the ceasing from activity, depends on an agent just as activity does, and all dealing with act and its factors holds only on the ground of ignorance, before the reality is reached; so he who sees action in inaction sees rightly. Such a one is praised as wise, a yogī, a doer of all action: freed from evil, he has done what was to be done.

Bhāṣya There is no contradiction in this. What is in truth inaction appears to the deluded as action, and action as inaction, as water is seen in a mirage or silver in mother-of-pearl; the Lord speaks to remove that inverted grasp. It is not that inaction rests in action as berries in a bowl. To one in a moving boat the trees standing on the bank seem to move the other way, and distant things that move seem still. So the action of body and organs is superimposed on the actionless, changeless Self: ‘I am the doer, this is my action, I shall enjoy its fruit’. The stopping of their activity, and the ease it brings, is superimposed in the same way: ‘I do nothing, I sit quiet and happy’; and this, resting on the sense of ‘I’, is action. Because the world, steeped in the inverted view, forgets the truth however often it hears it, and because the thing is hard to know, the Lord gives the answer again and again.

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara rejects another explanation: that the obligatory rites, done for the Lord, are called ‘inaction’ in a figurative sense because they have no fruit, as a cow that gives no milk is called no cow, and their omission ‘action’ because it yields sin. To know that would not free one from evil (4.16): release might come from doing the rites, not from knowing them to be fruitless, and no such knowledge is enjoined anywhere. A false view, itself an evil, cannot free from evil: darkness does not remove darkness. Had that been the meaning it could have been said in plain words, as at 2.47, and not in a riddle that misleads. Nor can sin, something positive, arise from not doing, a mere absence (2.16; Chāndogya 6.2.2). And scripture would not enjoin a rite that is fruitless and only pain; to call it fruitless and yet a means to liberation is to contradict oneself. So the verse means what it says.

यस्य सर्वे समारम्भाः कामसंकल्पवर्जिताः । ज्ञानाग्निदग्धकर्माणं तमाहुः पण्डितं बुधाः ॥ १९ ॥
yasya sarve samārambhāḥ kāmasaṃkalpavarjitāḥ | jñānāgnidagdhakarmāṇaṃ tamāhuḥ paṇḍitaṃ budhāḥ || 19 ||
Padaccheda
यस्य सर्वे समारम्भाः काम-संकल्प-वर्जिताः ज्ञान-अग्नि-दग्ध-कर्माणम् तम् आहुः पण्डितम् बुधाः
yasya sarve samārambhāḥ kāma-saṃkalpa-varjitāḥ jñāna-agni-dagdha-karmāṇam tam āhuḥ paṇḍitam budhāḥ
Word by word
He whose undertakings are all free from desire and its motives, and whose actions are burnt in the fire of knowledge: him those who know call wise.

Bhāṣya His actions are mere movements, done for the good of the world if he is engaged in life, or just to stay alive if he has withdrawn. The knowledge is the seeing of inaction in action and action in inaction; it burns up actions, good and bad. ‘Those who know’ are the knowers of Brahman.

त्यक्त्वा कर्मफलासङ्गं नित्यतृप्तो निराश्रयः । कर्मण्यभिप्रवृत्तोऽपि नैव किंचित्करोति सः ॥ २० ॥
tyaktvā karmaphalāsaṅgaṃ nityatṛpto nirāśrayaḥ | karmaṇyabhipravṛtto 'pi naiva kiṃcitkaroti saḥ || 20 ||
Padaccheda
त्यक्त्वा कर्म-फल-आसङ्गम् नित्य-तृप्तः निराश्रयः कर्मणि अभिप्रवृत्तः अपि न एव किंचित् करोति सः
tyaktvā karma-phala-āsaṅgam nitya-tṛptaḥ nirāśrayaḥ karmaṇi abhipravṛttaḥ api na eva kiṃcit karoti saḥ
Word by word
Having given up attachment to the fruit of action, ever content and depending on nothing, he does nothing at all, though he is engaged in action.

Bhāṣya He gives up both the sense of ‘I’ in actions and attachment to their fruit. nirāśraya: without the support on which one relies to gain some human end. Action done by a knower is in truth inaction, since he sees the actionless Self. Having no purpose of his own, he would give up action; but if he cannot, he goes on as before, to hold the world together or to avoid the blame of the good.

निराशीर्यतचित्तात्मा त्यक्तसर्वपरिग्रहः । शारीरं केवलं कर्म कुर्वन्नाप्नोति किल्बिषम् ॥ २१ ॥
nirāśīryatacittātmā tyaktasarvaparigrahaḥ | śārīraṃ kevalaṃ karma kurvannāpnoti kilbiṣam || 21 ||
Padaccheda
निराशीः यत-चित्त-आत्मा त्यक्त-सर्व-परिग्रहः शारीरम् केवलम् कर्म कुर्वन् न आप्नोति किल्बिषम्
nirāśīḥ yata-citta-ātmā tyakta-sarva-parigrahaḥ śārīram kevalam karma kurvan na āpnoti kilbiṣam
Word by word
Free from expectation, with mind and body controlled, having given up all possessions, and doing only the action needed to keep the body alive, he incurs no sin.

Bhāṣya This is the one who renounces action. citta is the inner organ and ātman the outer aggregate of body and organs. śārīra here is action whose only purpose is to maintain the body, and not ‘action done by the body’; he argues for this at length. kevala: without the sense of ‘I’ even in that. kilbiṣa covers merit too, since for one who seeks liberation merit also binds: he is freed from the cycle of birth and death.

Reading The DCS leaves out the word na in its analysis of this verse; it is supplied here.

यदृच्छालाभसंतुष्टो द्वंद्वातीतो विमत्सरः । समः सिद्धावसिद्धौ च कृत्वापि न निबध्यते ॥ २२ ॥
yadṛcchālābhasaṃtuṣṭo dvaṃdvātīto vimatsaraḥ | samaḥ siddhāvasiddhau ca kṛtvāpi na nibadhyate || 22 ||
Padaccheda
यदृच्छा-लाभ-संतुष्टः द्वंद्व-अतीतः विमत्सरः समः सिद्धौ असिद्धौ च कृत्वा अपि न निबध्यते
yadṛcchā-lābha-saṃtuṣṭaḥ dvaṃdva-atītaḥ vimatsaraḥ samaḥ siddhau asiddhau ca kṛtvā api na nibadhyate
Word by word
Content with what comes unsought, beyond the pairs of opposites, free from envy, and the same in success and failure, he is not bound even when he acts.

Bhāṣya This shows how such an ascetic gets the food and the like that keep the body alive. Though struck by cold, heat and the rest, his mind is not cast down. He goes about for alms seeing all the while that the Self is not the doer; people ascribe agency to him, but by his own experience he does nothing.

गतसङ्गस्य मुक्तस्य ज्ञानावस्थितचेतसः । यज्ञायाचरतः कर्म समग्रं प्रविलीयते ॥ २३ ॥
gatasaṅgasya muktasya jñānāvasthitacetasaḥ | yajñāyācarataḥ karma samagraṃ pravilīyate || 23 ||
Padaccheda
गत-सङ्गस्य मुक्तस्य ज्ञान-अवस्थित-चेतसः यज्ञाय आचरतः कर्म समग्रम् प्रविलीयते
gata-saṅgasya muktasya jñāna-avasthita-cetasaḥ yajñāya ācarataḥ karma samagram pravilīyate
Word by word
For one whose attachment is gone, who is free, whose mind is established in knowledge, and who acts for the sake of sacrifice, action is wholly dissolved.

Bhāṣya Free of the bonds of merit, demerit and the like. samagra: ‘together with its tip’, that is, its fruit.

ब्रह्मार्पणं ब्रह्महविर्ब्रह्माग्नौ ब्रह्मणा हुतम् । ब्रह्मैव तेन गन्तव्यं ब्रह्मकर्मसमाधिना ॥ २४ ॥
brahmārpaṇaṃ brahmahavirbrahmāgnau brahmaṇā hutam | brahmaiva tena gantavyaṃ brahmakarmasamādhinā || 24 ||
Padaccheda
ब्रह्म अर्पणम् ब्रह्म हविः ब्रह्म-अग्नौ ब्रह्मणा हुतम् ब्रह्म एव तेन गन्तव्यम् ब्रह्म-कर्म-समाधिना
brahma arpaṇam brahma haviḥ brahma-agnau brahmaṇā hutam brahma eva tena gantavyam brahma-karma-samādhinā
Word by word
The means of offering is Brahman; the oblation is Brahman; by Brahman it is offered in the fire that is Brahman. Brahman alone is to be reached by him who is absorbed in action as Brahman.

Bhāṣya This explains why action done by such a one is dissolved without producing its fruit. The knower of Brahman sees that the ladle, the oblation, the fire, the offerer, the act of offering and its result do not exist apart from the Self, as one sees that there is no silver in the mother-of- pearl. brahma-karma-samādhi: he whose absorption is in the action that is Brahman.

Bhāṣya Even when it is done by one who wishes to hold the world together, such action is in truth inaction, the notion of Brahman having crushed it. And for one who has ceased from action and renounced all of it, to present knowledge as a sacrifice, in praise of right vision, fits all the better: what in the sacrifice is known as the ladle and the rest is, for him who sees the truth, Brahman within. Otherwise, since everything is Brahman, there would be no point in calling the ladle and the rest Brahman in particular. No action called a sacrifice is found without the notion of its factors, the sense of being the doer and the aim at a fruit; here the notion of difference between act, factors and fruit is gone, so it is inaction (4.18, 4.20, 3.28, 5.8). As an agnihotra done from desire ceases to be one when the desire is gone, so the knower's action, a mere outward movement, becomes inaction; hence ‘it is wholly dissolved’ (4.23).

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara rejects the view that Brahman itself stands as the five factors and performs the action, so that the notion of ladle and the rest is not removed but the idea of Brahman is laid on them, as that of Viṣṇu on an image or of Brahman on name. That might be so if the context were not in praise of the sacrifice of knowledge (4.33). On that view the knowledge would have the ladle and the rest for its object and would not be the knowledge of Brahman; liberation is not reached by a knowledge that lays one view on another, yet the verse says that Brahman is to be reached; and the adhyāya takes up right vision at 4.18 and ends in its praise (4.33, 4.39).

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara takes brahma arpaṇam and brahma haviḥ as separate words, and brahmāgnau as a compound; the DCS treats all three as compounds.

दैवमेवापरे यज्ञं योगिनः पर्युपासते । ब्रह्माग्नावपरे यज्ञं यज्ञेनैवोपजुह्वति ॥ २५ ॥
daivamevāpare yajñaṃ yoginaḥ paryupāsate | brahmāgnāvapare yajñaṃ yajñenaivopajuhvati || 25 ||
Padaccheda
दैवम् एव अपरे यज्ञम् योगिनः पर्युपासते ब्रह्म-अग्नौ अपरे यज्ञम् यज्ञेन एव उपजुह्वति
daivam eva apare yajñam yoginaḥ paryupāsate brahma-agnau apare yajñam yajñena eva upajuhvati
Word by word
Some, men of action, perform only the sacrifice to the gods. Others offer the self, by the self alone, in the fire that is Brahman.

Bhāṣya Other sacrifices are now listed in order to praise right vision, just presented as a sacrifice. The second kind is that of the knowers of Brahman: yajña is here a name of the Self. To see the self that is joined to adjuncts such as the intellect as nothing but the supreme Brahman, free of adjuncts, is to offer it in that fire. This is done by the renouncers who are steadfast in seeing the oneness of Brahman and the Self.

श्रोत्रादीनीन्द्रियाण्यन्ये संयमाग्निषु जुह्वति । शब्दादीन्विषयानन्य इन्द्रियाग्निषु जुह्वति ॥ २६ ॥
śrotrādīnīndriyāṇyanye saṃyamāgniṣu juhvati | śabdādīnviṣayānanya indriyāgniṣu juhvati || 26 ||
Padaccheda
श्रोत्र-आदीनि इन्द्रियाणि अन्ये संयम-अग्निषु जुह्वति शब्द-आदीन् विषयान् अन्ये इन्द्रिय-अग्निषु जुह्वति
śrotra-ādīni indriyāṇi anye saṃyama-agniṣu juhvati śabda-ādīn viṣayān anye indriya-agniṣu juhvati
Word by word
Others offer hearing and the other senses in the fires of restraint; others offer sound and the other objects in the fires of the senses.

Bhāṣya The plural ‘fires’ is used because restraint differs for each sense. The second group regard as an offering the taking in, by the senses, of objects that are not forbidden.

सर्वाणीन्द्रियकर्माणि प्राणकर्माणि चापरे । आत्मसंयमयोगाग्नौ जुह्वति ज्ञानदीपिते ॥ २७ ॥
sarvāṇīndriyakarmāṇi prāṇakarmāṇi cāpare | ātmasaṃyamayogāgnau juhvati jñānadīpite || 27 ||
Padaccheda
सर्वाणि इन्द्रिय-कर्माणि प्राण-कर्माणि च अपरे आत्म-संयम-योग-अग्नौ जुह्वति ज्ञान-दीपिते
sarvāṇi indriya-karmāṇi prāṇa-karmāṇi ca apare ātma-saṃyama-yoga-agnau juhvati jñāna-dīpite
Word by word
Others offer all the actions of the senses and the actions of the life-breath in the fire of the yoga of self-restraint, kindled by knowledge.

Bhāṣya The actions of the breath are contracting, expanding and the like. The fire is kindled by discerning knowledge, as a lamp is by oil; they dissolve those actions in it.

द्रव्ययज्ञास्तपोयज्ञा योगयज्ञास्तथापरे । स्वाध्यायज्ञानयज्ञाश्च यतयः संशितव्रताः ॥ २८ ॥
dravyayajñāstapoyajñā yogayajñāstathāpare | svādhyāyajñānayajñāśca yatayaḥ saṃśitavratāḥ || 28 ||
Padaccheda
द्रव्य-यज्ञाः तपः-यज्ञाः योग-यज्ञाः तथा अपरे स्वाध्याय-ज्ञान-यज्ञाः च यतयः संशित-व्रताः
dravya-yajñāḥ tapaḥ-yajñāḥ yoga-yajñāḥ tathā apare svādhyāya-jñāna-yajñāḥ ca yatayaḥ saṃśita-vratāḥ
Word by word
Some sacrifice with wealth, some with austerity, some with yoga; others, ascetics of strict vows, sacrifice with study and with knowledge.

Bhāṣya The first give away wealth at holy places as a sacrifice. Yoga here is the regulation of the breath, the withdrawal of the senses and the like. svādhyāya is the regular recitation of the Ṛgveda and the other Vedas; jñāna is thorough knowledge of the meaning of the scriptures.

अपाने जुह्वति प्राणं प्राणेऽपानं तथापरे । प्राणापानगती रुद्ध्वा प्राणायामपरायणाः ॥ २९ ॥
apāne juhvati prāṇaṃ prāṇe 'pānaṃ tathāpare | prāṇāpānagatī ruddhvā prāṇāyāmaparāyaṇāḥ || 29 ||
Padaccheda
अपाने जुह्वति प्राणम् प्राणे अपानम् तथा अपरे प्राण-अपान-गती रुद्ध्वा प्राणायाम-परायणाः
apāne juhvati prāṇam prāṇe apānam tathā apare prāṇa-apāna-gatī ruddhvā prāṇāyāma-parāyaṇāḥ
Word by word
Others offer the out-breath in the in-breath, and likewise the in-breath in the out-breath; checking the courses of both, they are intent on the regulation of the breath.

Bhāṣya The first is the regulation of breath called filling (pūraka), the second that called emptying (recaka); checking the outward course of the breath through mouth and nose and its downward course is that called holding (kumbhaka).

अपरे नियताहाराः प्राणान्प्राणेषु जुह्वति । सर्वेऽप्येते यज्ञविदो यज्ञक्षपितकल्मषाः ॥ ३० ॥
apare niyatāhārāḥ prāṇānprāṇeṣu juhvati | sarve 'pyete yajñavido yajñakṣapitakalmaṣāḥ || 30 ||
Padaccheda
अपरे नियत-आहाराः प्राणान् प्राणेषु जुह्वति सर्वे अपि एते यज्ञ-विदः यज्ञ-क्षपित-कल्मषाः
apare niyata-āhārāḥ prāṇān prāṇeṣu juhvati sarve api ete yajña-vidaḥ yajña-kṣapita-kalmaṣāḥ
Word by word
Others, of measured diet, offer the breaths in the breaths. All these know what sacrifice is, and have their sins destroyed by sacrifice.

Bhāṣya Whichever of the vital breaths has been mastered, the others are offered into it and seem to enter it.

यज्ञशिष्टामृतभुजो यान्ति ब्रह्म सनातनम् । नायं लोकोऽस्त्ययज्ञस्य कुतोऽन्यः कुरुसत्तम ॥ ३१ ॥
yajñaśiṣṭāmṛtabhujo yānti brahma sanātanam | nāyaṃ loko 'styayajñasya kuto 'nyaḥ kurusattama || 31 ||
Padaccheda
यज्ञ-शिष्ट-अमृत-भुजः यान्ति ब्रह्म सनातनम् न अयम् लोकः अस्ति अयज्ञस्य कुतः अन्यः कुरुसत्तम
yajña-śiṣṭa-amṛta-bhujaḥ yānti brahma sanātanam na ayam lokaḥ asti ayajñasya kutaḥ anyaḥ kurusattama
Word by word
Those who eat the nectar that remains of the sacrifice go to the eternal Brahman. Not even this world is for one who does not sacrifice; how then the other, O best of the Kurus?

Bhāṣya Having performed these sacrifices, they eat in the time that remains the food prescribed, called nectar. They go to Brahman if they seek liberation, and, as the sense requires, in the course of time.

एवं बहुविधा यज्ञा वितता ब्रह्मणो मुखे । कर्मजान्विद्धि तान्सर्वानेवं ज्ञात्वा विमोक्ष्यसे ॥ ३२ ॥
evaṃ bahuvidhā yajñā vitatā brahmaṇo mukhe | karmajānviddhi tānsarvān evaṃ jñātvā vimokṣyase || 32 ||
Padaccheda
एवम् बहुविधाः यज्ञाः वितताः ब्रह्मणः मुखे कर्म-जान् विद्धि तान् सर्वान् एवम् ज्ञात्वा विमोक्ष्यसे
evam bahuvidhāḥ yajñāḥ vitatāḥ brahmaṇaḥ mukhe karma-jān viddhi tān sarvān evam jñātvā vimokṣyase
Word by word
Thus sacrifices of many kinds are spread out in the mouth of the Veda. Know them all to be born of action; knowing this, you will be freed.

Bhāṣya Brahman here is the Veda; the sacrifices are known through it. They arise from the actions of body, speech and mind, not from the Self, for the Self does not act. Knowing ‘these are not my activity; I am actionless and uninvolved’, one is freed, by this right vision, from the bondage of the cycle of birth and death.

श्रेयान्द्रव्यमयाद्यज्ञाज्ज्ञानयज्ञः परंतप । सर्वं कर्माखिलं पार्थ ज्ञाने परिसमाप्यते ॥ ३३ ॥
śreyāndravyamayādyajñājjñānayajñaḥ paraṃtapa | sarvaṃ karmākhilaṃ pārtha jñāne parisamāpyate || 33 ||
Padaccheda
श्रेयान् द्रव्य-मयात् यज्ञात् ज्ञान-यज्ञः परंतप सर्वम् कर्म अखिलम् पार्थ ज्ञाने परिसमाप्यते
śreyān dravya-mayāt yajñāt jñāna-yajñaḥ paraṃtapa sarvam karma akhilam pārtha jñāne parisamāpyate
Word by word
Better than the sacrifice made with material things is the sacrifice of knowledge, O scorcher of foes. All action without exception, O Pārtha, is completed in knowledge.

Bhāṣya A sacrifice made with material things produces a fruit; the sacrifice of knowledge does not, and so is better. All action is contained in knowledge, the means to liberation, which is like the flood of water on every side (2.46).

तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया । उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥ ३४ ॥
tadviddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā | upadekṣyanti te jñānaṃ jñāninastattvadarśinaḥ || 34 ||
Padaccheda
तत् विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानम् ज्ञानिनः तत्त्व-दर्शिनः
tat viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā upadekṣyanti te jñānam jñāninaḥ tattva-darśinaḥ
Word by word
Learn that by prostration, by questioning and by service. The wise who see the truth will teach you knowledge.

Bhāṣya One approaches teachers, bows low before them, and asks ‘How does bondage come about? How liberation? What is knowledge, and what ignorance?’ Some who have knowledge see the truth as it is and some do not; hence ‘who see the truth’. Only knowledge taught by those of right vision is effective.

यज्ज्ञात्वा न पुनर्मोहमेवं यास्यसि पाण्डव । येन भूतान्यशेषेण द्रक्ष्यस्यात्मन्यथो मयि ॥ ३५ ॥
yajjñātvā na punarmohamevaṃ yāsyasi pāṇḍava | yena bhūtānyaśeṣeṇa drakṣyasyātmanyatho mayi || 35 ||
Padaccheda
यत् ज्ञात्वा न पुनः मोहम् एवम् यास्यसि पाण्डव येन भूतानि अशेषेण द्रक्ष्यसि आत्मनि अथ उ मयि
yat jñātvā na punaḥ moham evam yāsyasi pāṇḍava yena bhūtāni aśeṣeṇa drakṣyasi ātmani atha u mayi
Word by word
Knowing which, O son of Pāṇḍu, you will not fall into such delusion again, and by which you will see all beings without exception in the Self, and also in me.

Bhāṣya All beings, from Brahmā down to a tuft of grass, will be seen directly in the inward Self, and in Vāsudeva, the supreme Lord: that is, he will see the oneness of the individual knower and the Lord, which all the Upaniṣads teach.

अपि चेदसि पापेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यः पापकृत्तमः । सर्वं ज्ञानप्लवेनैव वृजिनं संतरिष्यसि ॥ ३६ ॥
api cedasi pāpebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ pāpakṛttamaḥ | sarvaṃ jñānaplavenaiva vṛjinaṃ saṃtariṣyasi || 36 ||
Padaccheda
अपि चेत् असि पापेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यः पाप-कृत्तमः सर्वम् ज्ञान-प्लवेन एव वृजिनम् संतरिष्यसि
api cet asi pāpebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ pāpa-kṛttamaḥ sarvam jñāna-plavena eva vṛjinam saṃtariṣyasi
Word by word
Even if you are the most sinful of all sinners, you will cross over all sin by the raft of knowledge alone.

Bhāṣya Sin is here an ocean to be crossed. For one who seeks liberation, merit too is called sin.

यथैधांसि समिद्धोऽग्निर्भस्मसात्कुरुतेऽर्जुन । ज्ञानाग्निः सर्वकर्माणि भस्मसात्कुरुते तथा ॥ ३७ ॥
yathaidhāṃsi samiddho 'gnirbhasmasātkurute 'rjuna | jñānāgniḥ sarvakarmāṇi bhasmasātkurute tathā || 37 ||
Padaccheda
यथा एधांसि समिद्धः अग्निः भस्मसात् कुरुते अर्जुन ज्ञान-अग्निः सर्व-कर्माणि भस्मसात् कुरुते तथा
yathā edhāṃsi samiddhaḥ agniḥ bhasmasāt kurute arjuna jñāna-agniḥ sarva-karmāṇi bhasmasāt kurute tathā
Word by word
As a blazing fire reduces fuel to ashes, O Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge reduces all actions to ashes.

Bhāṣya It makes them seedless; knowledge cannot literally burn actions like fuel. The actions that have produced the present body have begun to bear fruit and are exhausted only by being lived through. What knowledge destroys are those that have not begun to bear fruit: those done before it arose, those done along with it, and those of many past births.

न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रमिह विद्यते । तत्स्वयं योगसंसिद्धः कालेनात्मनि विन्दति ॥ ३८ ॥
na hi jñānena sadṛśaṃ pavitramiha vidyate | tatsvayaṃ yogasaṃsiddhaḥ kālenātmani vindati || 38 ||
Padaccheda
न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशम् पवित्रम् इह विद्यते तत् स्वयम् योग-संसिद्धः कालेन आत्मनि विन्दति
na hi jñānena sadṛśam pavitram iha vidyate tat svayam yoga-saṃsiddhaḥ kālena ātmani vindati
Word by word
For there is no purifier here equal to knowledge. One who has been made perfect by yoga finds it of himself, in himself, in time.

Bhāṣya Made fit by the yoga of action and the yoga of absorption, the seeker of liberation gains that knowledge after a long time.

श्रद्धावांल्लभते ज्ञानं तत्परः संयतेन्द्रियः । ज्ञानं लब्ध्वा परां शान्तिमचिरेणाधिगच्छति ॥ ३९ ॥
śraddhāvāṃllabhate jñānaṃ tatparaḥ saṃyatendriyaḥ | jñānaṃ labdhvā parāṃ śāntimacireṇādhigacchati || 39 ||
Padaccheda
श्रद्धावान् लभते ज्ञानम् तद्-परः संयत-इन्द्रियः ज्ञानम् लब्ध्वा पराम् शान्तिम् अचिरेण अधिगच्छति
śraddhāvān labhate jñānam tad-paraḥ saṃyata-indriyaḥ jñānam labdhvā parām śāntim acireṇa adhigacchati
Word by word
One who has faith, who is intent on it and has his senses restrained, gains knowledge. Having gained knowledge, he attains the supreme peace before long.

Bhāṣya This is the sure means of gaining knowledge. A man of faith may still be slow, hence ‘intent on it’, devoted to attending on a teacher and the like; and one so intent may still not have mastered his senses. Prostration and the rest (verse 34) are outward and may be put on by a deceiver; faith and these cannot. The supreme peace is liberation, which follows quickly on right vision.

अज्ञश्चाश्रद्दधानश्च संशयात्मा विनश्यति । नायं लोकोऽस्ति न परो न सुखं संशयात्मनः ॥ ४० ॥
ajñaś cāśraddadhānaś ca saṃśayātmā vinaśyati | nāyaṃ loko 'sti na paro na sukhaṃ saṃśayātmanaḥ || 40 ||
Padaccheda
अ-ज्ञः च अ-श्रद्दधानः च संशय-आत्मा विनश्यति न अयम् लोकः अस्ति न परः न सुखम् संशय-आत्मनः
a-jñaḥ ca a-śraddadhānaḥ ca saṃśaya-ātmā vinaśyati na ayam lokaḥ asti na paraḥ na sukham saṃśaya-ātmanaḥ
Word by word
One who does not know, who has no faith, and whose mind is given to doubt, perishes. For the doubter there is neither this world nor the next, nor happiness.

Bhāṣya One who does not know the Self and one without faith in the words of the teacher and in scripture also perish, but not as the doubter does; he is the worst of all.

योगसंन्यस्तकर्माणं ज्ञानसंछिन्नसंशयम् । आत्मवन्तं न कर्माणि निबध्नन्ति धनंजय ॥ ४१ ॥
yogasaṃnyastakarmāṇaṃ jñānasaṃchinnasaṃśayam | ātmavantaṃ na karmāṇi nibadhnanti dhanaṃjaya || 41 ||
Padaccheda
योग-संन्यस्त-कर्माणम् ज्ञान-संछिन्न-संशयम् आत्मवन्तम् न कर्माणि निबध्नन्ति धनंजय
yoga-saṃnyasta-karmāṇam jñāna-saṃchinna-saṃśayam ātmavantam na karmāṇi nibadhnanti dhanaṃjaya
Word by word
Actions do not bind one who has renounced actions through yoga, whose doubt is cut through by knowledge, and who is vigilant, O Dhanaṃjaya.

Bhāṣya The yoga is the seeing of the supreme truth, by which he has renounced actions, that is, merit and demerit. The knowledge is the seeing of the oneness of the Self and the Lord. Actions, seen by him as the mere play of the guṇas, produce no result for him.

तस्मादज्ञानसम्भूतं हृत्स्थं ज्ञानासिनात्मनः । छित्त्वैनं संशयं योगमातिष्ठोत्तिष्ठ भारत ॥ ४२ ॥
tasmādajñānasambhūtaṃ hṛtsthaṃ jñānāsinātmanaḥ | chittvainaṃ saṃśayaṃ yogamātiṣṭhottiṣṭha bhārata || 42 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मात् अज्ञान-सम्भूतम् हृद्-स्थम् ज्ञान-असिना आत्मनः छित्त्वा एनम् संशयम् योगम् आतिष्ठ उत्तिष्ठ भारत
tasmāt ajñāna-sambhūtam hṛd-stham jñāna-asinā ātmanaḥ chittvā enam saṃśayam yogam ātiṣṭha uttiṣṭha bhārata
Word by word
Therefore, with the sword of knowledge, cut through this doubt of yours, born of ignorance and lodged in the heart; resort to yoga, and arise, O Bhārata.

Bhāṣya The heart is the intellect. The sword is knowledge, right vision, which removes grief, delusion and such faults. The doubt is ‘your own’ because it concerns the Self. The yoga is the performance of action, the means to right vision. ‘Arise’: now, for battle.
