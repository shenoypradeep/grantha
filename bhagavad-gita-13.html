त्रयोदशोऽध्यायः
Trayodaśo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 13 · Kṣetrakṣetrajñavibhāgayoga
श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
इदं शरीरं कौन्तेय क्षेत्रमित्यभिधीयते । एतद्यो वेत्ति तं प्राहुः क्षेत्रज्ञ इति तद्विदः ॥ १ ॥
idaṃ śarīraṃ kaunteya kṣetramityabhidhīyate | etadyo vetti taṃ prāhuḥ kṣetrajña iti tadvidaḥ || 1 ||
Padaccheda
इदम् शरीरम् कौन्तेय क्षेत्रम् इति अभिधीयते एतत् यः वेत्ति तम् प्राहुः क्षेत्रज्ञः इति तत्-विदः
idam śarīram kaunteya kṣetram iti abhidhīyate etat yaḥ vetti tam prāhuḥ kṣetrajñaḥ iti tat-vidaḥ
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: This body, O son of Kuntī, is called the field. Him who knows it, those who know them call the knower of the field.

Introduction Introducing the adhyāya: the seventh adhyāya pointed to two Prakṛtis of the Lord: the lower, of the three guṇas, divided eightfold, the cause of the cycle of birth and death; and the higher, become the jīva, the knower of the field, of the Lord's own nature. Through these two the Lord is the cause of the arising, staying and dissolving of the world. This adhyāya is begun to determine the truth of the Lord who has them, by setting out the field and its knower. And the last adhyāya, from 12.13 to its end, told how the renouncers who know the truth live; this one tells by what knowledge of the truth they become dear to the Lord. Prakṛti, of the three guṇas, changed into all effects, organs and objects, is put together as body and senses for the Puruṣa's experience and liberation; that aggregate is this body.

Bhāṣya It is called the field (kṣetra) because it protects from injury (kṣata-trāṇa), because it wastes away (kṣaya), because it perishes (kṣaraṇa), or because the fruits of action ripen in it as in a field. He who knows it makes it, from the soles of the feet to the head, an object of his knowledge, whether that knowledge is natural or comes from instruction. ‘Those who know them’ are those who know both the field and its knower.

Reading Some editions place a verse before this one (prakṛtiṃ puruṣaṃ caiva …, Arjuna's question), which makes 35 verses. Śaṅkara does not comment on it, and it is not counted here.

क्षेत्रज्ञं चापि मां विद्धि सर्वक्षेत्रेषु भारत । क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञयोर्ज्ञानं यत्तज्ज्ञानं मतं मम ॥ २ ॥
kṣetrajñaṃ cāpi māṃ viddhi sarvakṣetreṣu bhārata | kṣetrakṣetrajñayorjñānaṃ yattajjñānaṃ mataṃ mama || 2 ||
Padaccheda
क्षेत्रज्ञम् च अपि माम् विद्धि सर्व-क्षेत्रेषु भारत क्षेत्र-क्षेत्रज्ञयोः ज्ञानम् यत् तत् ज्ञानम् मतम् मम
kṣetrajñam ca api mām viddhi sarva-kṣetreṣu bhārata kṣetra-kṣetrajñayoḥ jñānam yat tat jñānam matam mama
Word by word
And know the knower of the field in all fields to be me, O descendant of Bharata. The knowledge of the field and of the knower of the field: that I hold to be knowledge.

Bhāṣya Know the knower of the field, who seems divided by the many fields from Brahmā down to a clump of grass, to be me, the supreme Lord, who is not subject to the cycle of birth and death, free of every limiting adjunct and beyond the reach of words such as ‘being’ and ‘non-being’. Since nothing remains to be known apart from the true nature of the field, its knower and the Lord, this alone is right knowledge.

Bhāṣya The objection: if the one Lord is in all fields and there is no other experiencer, then either the Lord is subject to saṃsāra, or, there being no one else to be subject to it, there is no saṃsāra. Scripture on bondage, liberation and their causes would then be useless, and perception and inference, which show pleasure, pain and the variety of the world, would be contradicted. The reply: knowledge and ignorance are different things with opposite results (Kaṭha 1.2.4, 1.2.2; the two paths taught by Vyāsa; the two kinds of steadfastness here), and ignorance with its effect is to be given up through knowledge, as śruti (Kena 2.5, Śvetāśvatara 3.8 and 6.20, Taittirīya 2.7 and 2.9, Muṇḍaka 3.2.9, Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.10), smṛti (Gītā 5.15, 5.19, 13.28) and reasoning show.

Bhāṣya So the knower of the field, being the Lord himself, only seems subject to saṃsāra, through the adjuncts that ignorance makes, as the Self seems to be the body. The sense of Self in the body is settled in all creatures and is the work of ignorance, like taking a post for a man; and that does not give the post the properties of the man. It is objected that post and man are both things known, while here the known is superimposed on the knower, so that the properties of the body may really become the knower's. Then the Self would be unconscious; and one would have to say why pleasure, pain and desire become the Self's while old age and death do not: all are superimposed by ignorance alike. Saṃsāra, which is being a doer and an experiencer, sits in the known and is superimposed on the knower; it stains him no more than the dirt that children see in the sky.

Bhāṣya Nor is the knower of the field subject to saṃsāra as the one who has the ignorance. Ignorance is of tamas: a notion that veils, grasping wrongly, raising doubt or not grasping at all, and absent when the light of discernment is present. It belongs to an instrument, as dimness of sight belongs to the eye and not to the one who sees; and being something experienced, like the light of a lamp, it is not a property of the experiencer. All schools grant that in soleness, when every instrument is gone, there is no ignorance; were it the Self's own property, as heat is of fire, it could never be parted from it. So the knower of the field is always the Lord (13.31).

Bhāṣya On the charge that scripture becomes useless: it falls on all who hold a Self, for all grant that the liberated have nothing to do with saṃsāra or with scripture. As for the dualists scripture has its purpose only in the state of bondage, so for us in the sphere of ignorance. Nor can the dualist say that for him bondage and liberation are real states of the Self. They cannot be together; if they come in turn without a cause there is no release, and if with a cause they are not the Self's own and not ultimately real; a bondage without beginning but with an end, and a liberation with a beginning but without end, are against all proof; and what passes from one state to another is not eternal.

Bhāṣya Scripture is not useless, because it speaks to the ordinary person who does not know. Only one who sees the Self in fruit and cause, which are not the Self, is addressed by injunction and prohibition; one who sees the Self as other than them is not, as Viṣṇumitra does not take up an order given to Devadatta. The knower of Brahman does not engage in action, nor does one who denies a Self and a next world; the one who acts is he who infers a Self from scripture, does not know its true nature, thirsts for the fruit of action and has faith. The discerning are few, and the deluded do not follow them: activity depends on attachment and comes of one's nature (5.14).

Bhāṣya So saṃsāra is nothing but ignorance and concerns things as they are commonly seen. Ignorance and its effect do not belong to the knower of the field taken by himself, and a false notion cannot spoil what is ultimately real: mirage water does not turn desert ground to mud. If even the learned think ‘I am this, this is mine’, their learning is only seeing the Self in the field; if they saw the changeless knower of the field they would want neither enjoyment nor action. There is another sort of learning: ‘the knower of the field is the Lord, but I am subject to saṃsāra and must end it by knowing field and knower and by meditation’. One who thinks so, and believes he is giving scripture its sense, is the worst of the learned, a slayer of the Self: without the tradition of the meaning of scripture he gives up what is taught and invents what is not. Though he knows all the sciences he is to be disregarded like a fool.

Bhāṣya Whose is the ignorance? His to whom it appears. The question is idle: if you see ignorance you see the one who has it, as one who sees the owner of the cows does not ask whose they are. If you say ‘it is mine’, then you know ignorance and yourself; but the tie between the knower and the ignorance he knows cannot itself be grasped, for that would need another knower, and that one another, without end. The known is only known and the knower only knower; ignorance and pain do not stain him. Nor is it a fault merely to be the knower of a faulty field: the changeless one, whose nature is knowing, is called a knower only by a figure, as fire is said to heat by merely being hot. So act, factors and fruit are superimposed on the Self by ignorance (2.19, 3.27, 5.15) and action is for one who does not know, as he will show at 18.11 and 18.50.

तत्क्षेत्रं यच्च यादृक्च यद्विकारि यतश्च यत् । स च यो यत्प्रभावश्च तत्समासेन मे शृणु ॥ ३ ॥
tatkṣetraṃ yacca yādṛkca yadvikāri yataśca yat | sa ca yo yatprabhāvaśca tatsamāsena me śṛṇu || 3 ||
Padaccheda
तत् क्षेत्रम् यत् च यादृक् च यत्-विकारि यतः च यत् सः च यः यत्-प्रभावः च तत् समासेन मे शृणु
tat kṣetram yat ca yādṛk ca yat-vikāri yataḥ ca yat saḥ ca yaḥ yat-prabhāvaḥ ca tat samāsena me śṛṇu
Word by word
What that field is, of what kind, what its modifications are, and what arises from what; and who he is and what his powers are: hear that from me in brief.

Bhāṣya This verse sums up what the chapter will explain. ‘From what, what’: from which cause which effect arises. ‘He’ is the knower of the field; his powers are those produced by his limiting adjuncts.

ऋषिभिर्बहुधा गीतं छन्दोभिर्विविधैः पृथक् । ब्रह्मसूत्रपदैश्चैव हेतुमद्भिर्विनिश्चितैः ॥ ४ ॥
ṛṣibhirbahudhā gītaṃ chandobhirvividhaiḥ pṛthak | brahmasūtrapadaiścaiva hetumadbhirviniścitaiḥ || 4 ||
Padaccheda
ऋषिभिः बहुधा गीतम् छन्दोभिः विविधैः पृथक् ब्रह्मसूत्र-पदैः च एव हेतुमद्भिः विनिश्चितैः
ṛṣibhiḥ bahudhā gītam chandobhiḥ vividhaiḥ pṛthak brahmasūtra-padaiḥ ca eva hetumadbhiḥ viniścitaiḥ
Word by word
It has been sung in many ways by the seers, distinctly by the various Vedic hymns, and by the words that point to Brahman, which are reasoned and certain.

Bhāṣya The seers are Vasiṣṭha and others; the chandas are the Ṛk and the other Vedas. brahma-sūtra-pada: sentences that indicate Brahman, called pada because by them Brahman is reached, that is, known; such as ‘One should meditate on it as the Self alone’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.7). They are free of doubt and give rise to certain knowledge.

महाभूतान्यहंकारो बुद्धिरव्यक्तमेव च । इन्द्रियाणि दशैकं च पञ्च चेन्द्रियगोचराः ॥ ५ ॥
mahābhūtānyahaṃkāro buddhiravyaktameva ca | indriyāṇi daśaikaṃ ca pañca cendriyagocarāḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
महाभूतानि अहंकारः बुद्धिः अव्यक्तम् एव च इन्द्रियाणि दश एकम् च पञ्च च इन्द्रिय-गोचराः
mahābhūtāni ahaṃkāraḥ buddhiḥ avyaktam eva ca indriyāṇi daśa ekam ca pañca ca indriya-gocarāḥ
Word by word
The great elements, the sense of ‘I’, understanding and the unmanifest; the ten organs and the one, and the five objects of the senses;

Bhāṣya The great elements here are the subtle ones, great because they pervade all modifications; the gross ones are meant by ‘objects of the senses’. The sense of ‘I’ is their cause, understanding is its cause, and the unmanifest, the Lord's power called illusion (7.14), is the cause of that. The ten organs are the five of knowledge (hearing and the rest) and the five of action (speech, hands and the rest); ‘the one’ is the mind. The five objects are sound and the rest. These are the twenty-four principles of the Sāṃkhyas.

इच्छा द्वेषः सुखं दुःखं संघातश्चेतना धृतिः । एतत्क्षेत्रं समासेन सविकारमुदाहृतम् ॥ ६ ॥
icchā dveṣaḥ sukhaṃ duḥkhaṃ saṃghātaścetanā dhṛtiḥ | etatkṣetraṃ samāsena savikāramudāhṛtam || 6 ||
Padaccheda
इच्छा द्वेषः सुखम् दुःखम् संघातः चेतना धृतिः एतत् क्षेत्रम् समासेन स-विकारम् उदाहृतम्
icchā dveṣaḥ sukham duḥkham saṃghātaḥ cetanā dhṛtiḥ etat kṣetram samāsena sa-vikāram udāhṛtam
Word by word
desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the aggregate, awareness and firmness: this, in brief, is declared to be the field with its modifications.

Bhāṣya What the Vaiśeṣikas call qualities of the self are in fact properties of the field, because they are objects of knowledge. The aggregate is the combination of body and organs. Awareness is the state of the inner organ that shows itself in that aggregate, pervaded by a reflection of the Self's consciousness as a heated lump of iron is by fire. Firmness is that by which the tired body and organs are held up. Desire and the rest stand for all properties of the inner organ.

अमानित्वमदम्भित्वमहिंसा क्षान्तिरार्जवम् । आचार्योपासनं शौचं स्थैर्यमात्मविनिग्रहः ॥ ७ ॥
amānitvam adambhitvam ahiṃsā kṣāntirārjavam | ācāryopāsanaṃ śaucaṃ sthairyamātmavinigrahaḥ || 7 ||
Padaccheda
अमानित्वम् अदम्भित्वम् अहिंसा क्षान्तिः आर्जवम् आचार्य-उपासनम् शौचम् स्थैर्यम् आत्म-विनिग्रहः
amānitvam adambhitvam ahiṃsā kṣāntiḥ ārjavam ācārya-upāsanam śaucam sthairyam ātma-vinigrahaḥ
Word by word
Absence of pride, absence of pretence, non-injury, forbearance, uprightness, service of the teacher, purity, steadiness, restraint of body and organs;

Bhāṣya Verses 7–11 enjoin the means to knowledge, themselves called ‘knowledge’ because they lead to it; the renouncer devoted to them is one established in knowledge. Pride is praise of oneself; pretence is making a show of one's own virtue. Forbearance is remaining unchanged when wronged by another. Purity is washing the body with earth and water and, within, removing attachment and the like from the mind by dwelling on their opposites. Steadiness is resolve in the path of liberation alone. ātman here is the aggregate of body and organs, which by nature runs everywhere and is held to the right path.

इन्द्रियार्थेषु वैराग्यमनहंकार एव च । जन्ममृत्युजराव्याधिदुःखदोषानुदर्शनम् ॥ ८ ॥
indriyārtheṣu vairāgyamanahaṃkāra eva ca | janmamṛtyujarāvyādhiduḥkhadoṣānudarśanam || 8 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रिय-अर्थेषु वैराग्यम् अनहंकारः एव च जन्म-मृत्यु-जरा-व्याधि-दुःख-दोष-अनुदर्शनम्
indriya-artheṣu vairāgyam anahaṃkāraḥ eva ca janma-mṛtyu-jarā-vyādhi-duḥkha-doṣa-anudarśanam
Word by word
dispassion towards the objects of the senses, absence of the sense of ‘I’, and seeing the evil in birth, death, old age, sickness and pain;

Bhāṣya The objects are enjoyments seen and unseen. One reflects on the evil in each: in birth, lying in the womb and coming out through it; in old age, the loss of intelligence, strength and vigour, and being despised. Or: seeing in birth and the rest the evil that is pain, since they are causes of pain. From this comes dispassion, and then the organs turn towards the inner Self.

असक्तिरनभिष्वङ्गः पुत्रदारगृहादिषु । नित्यं च समचित्तत्वमिष्टानिष्टोपपत्तिषु ॥ ९ ॥
asaktiranabhiṣvaṅgaḥ putradāragṛhādiṣu | nityaṃ ca samacittatvamiṣṭāniṣṭopapattiṣu || 9 ||
Padaccheda
असक्तिः अनभिष्वङ्गः पुत्र-दार-गृह-आदिषु नित्यम् च सम-चित्तत्वम् इष्ट-अनिष्ट-उपपत्तिषु
asaktiḥ anabhiṣvaṅgaḥ putra-dāra-gṛha-ādiṣu nityam ca sama-cittatvam iṣṭa-aniṣṭa-upapattiṣu
Word by word
non-attachment, not identifying oneself with son, wife, home and the like, and constant evenness of mind when the wished-for and the unwished-for come;

Bhāṣya Sakti is mere liking for objects; abhiṣvaṅga is a stronger attachment, the thought that another is not other than oneself: ‘when he is happy or suffers, I am happy or suffer; when he lives or dies, I live or die’. ‘And the like’ takes in others who are very dear, such as servants.

मयि चानन्ययोगेन भक्तिरव्यभिचारिणी । विविक्तदेशसेवित्वमरतिर्जनसंसदि ॥ १० ॥
mayi cānanyayogena bhaktir avyabhicāriṇī | viviktadeśasevitvamaratirjanasaṃsadi || 10 ||
Padaccheda
मयि च अनन्य-योगेन भक्तिः अव्यभिचारिणी विविक्त-देश-सेवित्वम् अरतिः जन-संसदि
mayi ca ananya-yogena bhaktiḥ avyabhicāriṇī vivikta-deśa-sevitvam aratiḥ jana-saṃsadi
Word by word
unswerving devotion to me with undivided absorption, resorting to secluded places, no delight in the company of people;

Bhāṣya Ananya-yoga is the firm conviction ‘there is none higher than the Lord Vāsudeva, so he alone is our goal’. A secluded place is one free, by nature or by cleaning, from impurity and from snakes, tigers and the like: a forest, a river bank, a temple; there the mind grows clear. ‘People’ are the worldly, uncultivated and undisciplined; the company of the cultivated and disciplined helps knowledge.

अध्यात्मज्ञाननित्यत्वं तत्त्वज्ञानार्थदर्शनम् । एतज्ज्ञानमिति प्रोक्तमज्ञानं यदतोऽन्यथा ॥ ११ ॥
adhyātmajñānanityatvaṃ tattvajñānārthadarśanam | etajjñānamiti proktam ajñānaṃ yadato 'nyathā || 11 ||
Padaccheda
अध्यात्म-ज्ञान-नित्यत्वम् तत्त्व-ज्ञान-अर्थ-दर्शनम् एतत् ज्ञानम् इति प्रोक्तम् अज्ञानम् यत् अतः अन्यथा
adhyātma-jñāna-nityatvam tattva-jñāna-artha-darśanam etat jñānam iti proktam ajñānam yat ataḥ anyathā
Word by word
constancy in the knowledge of the Self, and keeping in view the goal of the knowledge of the truth: this is declared to be knowledge; what is contrary to it is ignorance.

Bhāṣya The knowledge of the truth comes when the practice of these means has ripened; its goal is liberation, the end of the cycle of birth and death. One who reflects on that fruit applies himself to the means. Their opposites, pride, pretence, injury and the rest, are to be known as ignorance and avoided, since they keep that cycle going.

Reading The DCS leaves out the words proktam ajñānam in its analysis of this verse; they are supplied here.

ज्ञेयं यत्तत्प्रवक्ष्यामि यज्ज्ञात्वामृतमश्नुते । अनादिमत्परं ब्रह्म न सत्तन्नासदुच्यते ॥ १२ ॥
jñeyaṃ yattatpravakṣyāmi yajjñātvāmṛtamaśnute | anādimatparaṃ brahma na sattannāsaducyate || 12 ||
Padaccheda
ज्ञेयम् यत् तत् प्रवक्ष्यामि यत् ज्ञात्वा अमृतम् अश्नुते अनादिमत् परम् ब्रह्म न सत् तत् न असत् उच्यते
jñeyam yat tat pravakṣyāmi yat jñātvā amṛtam aśnute anādimat param brahma na sat tat na asat ucyate
Word by word
I shall declare what is to be known, knowing which one attains immortality: the supreme Brahman, without beginning. It is called neither being nor non-being.

Bhāṣya Linking the verse with 13.7–11: humility and the rest are restraints and observances; they do not define what is to be known. They were called knowledge because they are its cause and work together with it. ‘Attains immortality’: does not die again; the fruit is named to turn the hearer towards what follows.

Bhāṣya Anādimat is ‘having no beginning’. Some divide the words as anādi mat-param, ‘without beginning, having me (the power called Vāsudeva) as its supreme power’, on the ground that -mat adds nothing to the bahuvrīhi. That cannot be: Brahman is here taught by the denial of every distinction, and to show it as having a particular power contradicts that. The suffix only fills the metre.

Bhāṣya After so loud an announcement, ‘neither being nor non-being’ is still the fitting thing to say. In all the Upaniṣads Brahman is pointed out only by the denial of distinctions, ‘not this, not this’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.3.6), ‘not gross, not fine’ (3.8.8), since it is beyond the reach of speech. It does not follow that what is not called ‘is’ does not exist: it is no object of the notion ‘is not’ either. Only what the senses reach, a pot and the like, is the object of a cognition that goes with ‘it is’ or ‘it is not’; this is beyond the senses and known through scripture alone. Nor is the statement a contradiction: ‘it is other than the known and above the unknown’ (Kena 1.3). A text such as ‘who knows whether there is anything in that world or not’ (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.1.1) is an arthavāda that goes with an injunction.

Bhāṣya By reasoning: a word conveys its meaning through class (cow, horse), action (cooks, reads), quality (white, black) or relation (rich, owning cows). Brahman has no class; it is without qualities; it is without action (Śvetāśvatara 6.19); and being one, without a second, not an object, and the Self, it has no relation. So no word expresses it (Taittirīya 2.9).

सर्वतःपाणिपादं तत्सर्वतोऽक्षिशिरोमुखम् । सर्वतःश्रुतिमल्लोके सर्वमावृत्य तिष्ठति ॥ १३ ॥
sarvataḥpāṇipādaṃ tatsarvato'kṣiśiromukham | sarvataḥśrutimalloke sarvamāvṛtya tiṣṭhati || 13 ||
Padaccheda
सर्वतः-पाणि-पादम् तत् सर्वतः-अक्षि-शिरः-मुखम् सर्वतः-श्रुतिमत् लोके सर्वम् आवृत्य तिष्ठति
sarvataḥ-pāṇi-pādam tat sarvataḥ-akṣi-śiraḥ-mukham sarvataḥ-śrutimat loke sarvam āvṛtya tiṣṭhati
Word by word
With hands and feet on every side, with eyes, heads and mouths on every side, with ears on every side, it stands among living beings, pervading all.

Bhāṣya Lest it be thought not to exist because it is not the object of the word ‘being’, its existence is shown through the organs of all living beings, its limiting adjuncts. The hands, feet and the rest belong to the field and are false in the knower; they are spoken of as if they were his properties so that his existence may be grasped. As those who know the tradition say: ‘That which is free of all diversity is set forth by superimposition and its denial.’

सर्वेन्द्रियगुणाभासं सर्वेन्द्रियविवर्जितम् । असक्तं सर्वभृच्चैव निर्गुणं गुणभोक्तृ च ॥ १४ ॥
sarvendriyaguṇābhāsaṃ sarvendriyavivarjitam | asaktaṃ sarvabhṛccaiva nirguṇaṃ guṇabhoktṛ ca || 14 ||
Padaccheda
सर्व-इन्द्रिय-गुण-आभासम् सर्व-इन्द्रिय-विवर्जितम् असक्तम् सर्व-भृत् च एव निर्गुणम् गुण-भोक्तृ च
sarva-indriya-guṇa-ābhāsam sarva-indriya-vivarjitam asaktam sarva-bhṛt ca eva nirguṇam guṇa-bhoktṛ ca
Word by word
It appears through the functions of all the organs, yet is devoid of all organs; unattached, yet supporting all; without guṇas, yet the experiencer of the guṇas.

Bhāṣya ‘all the organs’ includes the inner ones, understanding and mind. It seems to be active through their functions (determining, intending, hearing, speaking), as the śruti says: ‘it thinks, as it were; it moves, as it were’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.3.7). It supports all because everything rests on being; even a mirage is not without a ground. The guṇas are sattva, rajas and tamas, which, turned into pleasure, pain and delusion through sound and the other objects, it perceives.

बहिरन्तश्च भूतानामचरं चरमेव च । सूक्ष्मत्वात्तदविज्ञेयं दूरस्थं चान्तिके च तत् ॥ १५ ॥
bahirantaśca bhūtānāmacaraṃ carameva ca | sūkṣmatvāt tad avijñeyaṃ dūrasthaṃ cāntike ca tat || 15 ||
Padaccheda
बहिः अन्तः च भूतानाम् अचरम् चरम् एव च सूक्ष्मत्वात् तत् अविज्ञेयम् दूर-स्थम् च अन्तिके च तत्
bahiḥ antaḥ ca bhūtānām acaram caram eva ca sūkṣmatvāt tat avijñeyam dūra-stham ca antike ca tat
Word by word
It is outside and inside beings, the unmoving and also the moving. Because of its subtlety it is not to be known. It is far away, and it is near.

Bhāṣya ‘outside’ and ‘inside’ are said with the body, taken through ignorance to be the self, as the limit. The moving and unmoving bodies too are only that, appearing as a rope appears as a snake. Though it is the very thing to be known, it is unknown to the ignorant, being subtle like space; for them it is far, unreachable in thousands of millions of years. To the knowers it is ever known and near, being their own Self.

अविभक्तं च भूतेषु विभक्तमिव च स्थितम् । भूतभर्तृ च तज्ज्ञेयं ग्रसिष्णु प्रभविष्णु च ॥ १६ ॥
avibhaktaṃ ca bhūteṣu vibhaktamiva ca sthitam | bhūtabhartṛ ca tajjñeyaṃ grasiṣṇu prabhaviṣṇu ca || 16 ||
Padaccheda
अविभक्तम् च भूतेषु विभक्तम् इव च स्थितम् भूत-भर्तृ च तत् ज्ञेयम् ग्रसिष्णु प्रभविष्णु च
avibhaktam ca bhūteṣu vibhaktam iva ca sthitam bhūta-bhartṛ ca tat jñeyam grasiṣṇu prabhaviṣṇu ca
Word by word
Undivided, yet standing as if divided in beings, what is to be known is the supporter of beings, that which swallows them and that which brings them forth.

Bhāṣya It is one in every body, like space, and seems divided because it shows itself only in bodies. It supports beings while they last, swallows them at dissolution and brings them forth at creation, as a rope does the snake falsely imagined in it.

ज्योतिषामपि तज्ज्योतिस्तमसः परमुच्यते । ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं ज्ञानगम्यं हृदि सर्वस्य विष्ठितम् ॥ १७ ॥
jyotiṣāmapi tajjyotis tamasaḥ paramucyate | jñānaṃ jñeyaṃ jñānagamyaṃ hṛdi sarvasya viṣṭhitam || 17 ||
Padaccheda
ज्योतिषाम् अपि तत् ज्योतिः तमसः परम् उच्यते ज्ञानम् ज्ञेयम् ज्ञान-गम्यम् हृदि सर्वस्य विष्ठितम्
jyotiṣām api tat jyotiḥ tamasaḥ param ucyate jñānam jñeyam jñāna-gamyam hṛdi sarvasya viṣṭhitam
Word by word
It is the light even of lights, and is said to be beyond darkness. Knowledge, what is to be known and what is reached by knowledge: it is seated in the heart of everyone.

Bhāṣya The sun and other lights shine only when kindled by the light of the Self's consciousness. Darkness is ignorance, which does not touch it. Knowledge is absence of pride and the rest (7–11); what is to be known is what was taught from verse 12; that same, once known, is the fruit of knowledge and so ‘what is reached by knowledge’. All three are found in the heart, the understanding, of every living being.

Reading The DCS leaves out the words taj jyotis tamasaḥ in its analysis of this verse; they are supplied here.

इति क्षेत्रं तथा ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं चोक्तं समासतः । मद्भक्त एतद्विज्ञाय मद्भावायोपपद्यते ॥ १८ ॥
iti kṣetraṃ tathā jñānaṃ jñeyaṃ coktaṃ samāsataḥ | madbhakta etadvijñāya madbhāvāyopapadyate || 18 ||
Padaccheda
इति क्षेत्रम् तथा ज्ञानम् ज्ञेयम् च उक्तम् समासतः मत्-भक्तः एतत् विज्ञाय मत्-भावाय उपपद्यते
iti kṣetram tathā jñānam jñeyam ca uktam samāsataḥ mat-bhaktaḥ etat vijñāya mat-bhāvāya upapadyate
Word by word
Thus the field, and likewise knowledge and what is to be known, have been told in brief. My devotee, having understood this, becomes fit for my state.

Bhāṣya The field is verses 5–6, knowledge 7–11, what is to be known 12–17; this much is the whole meaning of the Veda and of the Gītā. The devotee is one who has given his whole self to the Lord, Vāsudeva, the all-knowing supreme teacher, and is held by the conviction that whatever he sees, hears or touches is the Lord alone. ‘My state’ is the state of the supreme Self: he goes to liberation.

प्रकृतिं पुरुषं चैव विद्ध्यनादी उभावपि । विकारांश्च गुणांश्चैव विद्धि प्रकृतिसंभवान् ॥ १९ ॥
prakṛtiṃ puruṣaṃ caiva viddhyanādī ubhāvapi | vikārāṃśca guṇāṃścaiva viddhi prakṛtisaṃbhavān || 19 ||
Padaccheda
प्रकृतिम् पुरुषम् च एव विद्धि अनादी उभौ अपि विकारान् च गुणान् च एव विद्धि प्रकृति-संभवान्
prakṛtim puruṣam ca eva viddhi anādī ubhau api vikārān ca guṇān ca eva viddhi prakṛti-saṃbhavān
Word by word
Know that Prakṛti and the Puruṣa are both without beginning; and know that the modifications and the guṇas are born of Prakṛti.

Bhāṣya These are the Lord's two Prakṛtis, the lower and the higher of 7.4–5, here the field and its knower. As the Lord's lordship is eternal, so are they; having these two is what makes him the Lord. Some take anādī as ‘not the first cause’, to keep the Lord as cause; that is wrong, for before they arose he would have nothing to rule, and the cycle of birth and death would be without a cause. The modifications run from understanding down to body and organs; the guṇas here are the forms of pleasure, pain and delusion. Prakṛti is the Lord's power of illusion, made of the three guṇas.

कार्यकारणकर्तृत्वे हेतुः प्रकृतिरुच्यते । पुरुषः सुखदुःखानां भोक्तृत्वे हेतुरुच्यते ॥ २० ॥
kāryakāraṇakartṛtve hetuḥ prakṛtirucyate | puruṣaḥ sukhaduḥkhānāṃ bhoktṛtve heturucyate || 20 ||
Padaccheda
कार्य-कारण-कर्तृत्वे हेतुः प्रकृतिः उच्यते पुरुषः सुख-दुःखानाम् भोक्तृत्वे हेतुः उच्यते
kārya-kāraṇa-kartṛtve hetuḥ prakṛtiḥ ucyate puruṣaḥ sukha-duḥkhānām bhoktṛtve hetuḥ ucyate
Word by word
Prakṛti is said to be the cause in the producing of effect and cause; the Puruṣa is said to be the cause in the experiencing of pleasures and pains.

Bhāṣya Śaṅkara reads kārya-karaṇa: the effect is the body, the instruments are the thirteen organs in it; the elements and objects fall under ‘effect’, and the guṇas, which rest on the organs, under ‘instrument’. He also gives the reading kārya-kāraṇa, shown here: the effect is a modification and the cause what is modified; or the sixteen modifications are the effect and the seven that are both source and product the cause.

Bhāṣya Puruṣa, jīva, knower of the field and experiencer are synonyms. The cycle of birth and death is the experiencing of pleasure and pain; it exists only when Prakṛti, changed into body, organs, pleasure and pain, is joined through ignorance with the conscious Puruṣa as its experiencer.

पुरुषः प्रकृतिस्थो हि भुङ्क्ते प्रकृतिजान्गुणान् । कारणं गुणसङ्गोऽस्य सदसद्योनिजन्मसु ॥ २१ ॥
puruṣaḥ prakṛtistho hi bhuṅkte prakṛtijānguṇān | kāraṇaṃ guṇasaṅgo 'sya sadasadyonijanmasu || 21 ||
Padaccheda
पुरुषः प्रकृति-स्थः हि भुङ्क्ते प्रकृति-जान् गुणान् कारणम् गुण-सङ्गः अस्य सत्-असत्-योनि-जन्मसु
puruṣaḥ prakṛti-sthaḥ hi bhuṅkte prakṛti-jān guṇān kāraṇam guṇa-saṅgaḥ asya sat-asat-yoni-janmasu
Word by word
For the Puruṣa, seated in Prakṛti, experiences the guṇas born of Prakṛti. Attachment to the guṇas is the cause of his births in good and evil wombs.

Bhāṣya ‘seated in Prakṛti’ means having taken Prakṛti, which is ignorance changed into body and organs, to be himself. He experiences the guṇas as ‘I am happy, I suffer, I am deluded, I am learned’. Good wombs are those of gods and the like, evil ones those of animals and the like; human wombs, being both, are included. So ignorance, called being seated in Prakṛti, and desire, the attachment to the guṇas, are the cause of the cycle of birth and death; they are stated so that they may be given up, by knowledge and dispassion together with renunciation.

उपद्रष्टानुमन्ता च भर्ता भोक्ता महेश्वरः । परमात्मेति चाप्युक्तो देहेऽस्मिन्पुरुषः परः ॥ २२ ॥
upadraṣṭānumantā ca bhartā bhoktā maheśvaraḥ | paramātmeti cāpyukto dehe 'sminpuruṣaḥ paraḥ || 22 ||
Padaccheda
उपद्रष्टा अनुमन्ता च भर्ता भोक्ता महा-ईश्वरः परम-आत्मा इति च अपि उक्तः देहे अस्मिन् पुरुषः परः
upadraṣṭā anumantā ca bhartā bhoktā mahā-īśvaraḥ parama-ātmā iti ca api uktaḥ dehe asmin puruṣaḥ paraḥ
Word by word
The supreme Puruṣa in this body is the witness, the approver, the supporter, the experiencer, the great Lord, and is also called the supreme Self.

Bhāṣya Upadraṣṭṛ is one who watches from close by without himself acting, like the expert who sits by at a sacrifice and notes what priests and sacrificer do well or badly; or the innermost seer, beyond body, eye, mind and understanding. The approver is content with what they do, or never restrains them. He is the supporter because body, organs, mind and understanding hold together for the sake of the conscious Self; the experiencer because the understanding's thoughts of pleasure, pain and delusion arise as if lit up by his consciousness. ‘Supreme’: higher than the unmanifest. This is the one taught at 13.2, ‘know the knower of the field to be me’.

य एवं वेत्ति पुरुषं प्रकृतिं च गुणैः सह । सर्वथा वर्तमानोऽपि न स भूयोऽभिजायते ॥ २३ ॥
ya evaṃ vetti puruṣaṃ prakṛtiṃ ca guṇaiḥ saha | sarvathā vartamāno 'pi na sa bhūyo 'bhijāyate || 23 ||
Padaccheda
यः एवम् वेत्ति पुरुषम् प्रकृतिम् च गुणैः सह सर्वथा वर्तमानः अपि न सः भूयः अभिजायते
yaḥ evam vetti puruṣam prakṛtim ca guṇaiḥ saha sarvathā vartamānaḥ api na saḥ bhūyaḥ abhijāyate
Word by word
He who thus knows the Puruṣa, and Prakṛti together with the guṇas, is not born again, in whatever way he may live.

Bhāṣya He knows the Puruṣa directly as himself, ‘I am this’, and knows Prakṛti, which is ignorance, with its modifications as brought to nothing by knowledge. When the knower's body falls he takes no other. ‘Even’: how much more one who keeps to his proper conduct.

Bhāṣya (objection) actions done before knowledge arose, after it, and in past births cannot perish without giving fruit. (Reply) śruti and smṛti (4.37) teach that all the knower's actions are burnt; actions sprout in a new birth only from the seed of ignorance and desire. Those that have begun the present body go on until their force is spent, like an arrow already shot; those that have not begun to bear fruit are made seedless by knowledge, like an arrow set on the bow but not released.

ध्यानेनात्मनि पश्यन्ति केचिदात्मानमात्मना । अन्ये सांख्येन योगेन कर्मयोगेन चापरे ॥ २४ ॥
dhyānenātmani paśyanti kecidātmānamātmanā | anye sāṃkhyena yogena karmayogena cāpare || 24 ||
Padaccheda
ध्यानेन आत्मनि पश्यन्ति केचित् आत्मानम् आत्मना अन्ये सांख्येन योगेन कर्म-योगेन च अपरे
dhyānena ātmani paśyanti kecit ātmānam ātmanā anye sāṃkhyena yogena karma-yogena ca apare
Word by word
Some see the Self in the understanding by the mind, through meditation; others by the yoga of discernment; and others by the yoga of action.

Bhāṣya Meditation is withdrawing the organs from their objects into the mind, and the mind into the inner conscious Self, and then thinking of it one-pointedly, in an unbroken stream like flowing oil. ātmanā is the inner organ refined by meditation. Discernment is the reflection ‘these guṇas, sattva, rajas and tamas, are seen by me; I am other than they, the witness of their working, eternal’. Action done with the thought of offering it to the Lord is called yoga because it leads to yoga; these see through purity of mind and the rise of knowledge.

अन्ये त्वेवमजानन्तः श्रुत्वान्येभ्य उपासते । तेऽपि चातितरन्त्येव मृत्युं श्रुतिपरायणाः ॥ २५ ॥
anye tv evam ajānantaḥ śrutvānyebhya upāsate | te 'pi cātitarantyeva mṛtyuṃ śrutiparāyaṇāḥ || 25 ||
Padaccheda
अन्ये तु एवम् अजानन्तः श्रुत्वा अन्येभ्यः उपासते ते अपि च अतितरन्ति एव मृत्युम् श्रुति-परायणाः
anye tu evam ajānantaḥ śrutvā anyebhyaḥ upāsate te api ca atitaranti eva mṛtyum śruti-parāyaṇāḥ
Word by word
But others, not knowing thus, hear of it from others and meditate; they too, devoted to what they have heard, cross beyond death.

Bhāṣya Not knowing the Self by any of these ways, they hear from teachers, are told ‘think on this alone’, and do so with faith. Death is the cycle of birth and death. For them hearing is the highest means: they rely on another's teaching alone, without discernment of their own. How much more, then, do the discerning cross beyond death.

यावत्संजायते किंचित्सत्त्वं स्थावरजङ्गमम् । क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञसंयोगात्तद्विद्धि भरतर्षभ ॥ २६ ॥
yāvatsaṃjāyate kiṃcitsattvaṃ sthāvarajaṅgamam | kṣetrakṣetrajñasaṃyogāttadviddhi bharatarṣabha || 26 ||
Padaccheda
यावत् संजायते किंचित् सत्त्वम् स्थावर-जङ्गमम् क्षेत्र-क्षेत्रज्ञ-संयोगात् तत् विद्धि भरत-ऋषभ
yāvat saṃjāyate kiṃcit sattvam sthāvara-jaṅgamam kṣetra-kṣetrajña-saṃyogāt tat viddhi bharata-ṛṣabha
Word by word
Whatever being is born, unmoving or moving, know that it is born from the union of the field and the knower of the field, O best of the Bharatas.

Bhāṣya This union is not contact, as of a rope with a pot, for the knower is partless like space; nor inherence, as of threads and cloth, for they are not cause and effect. It is the superimposition of each and its properties on the other, from failure to distinguish their natures, as a snake is superimposed on a rope or silver on a shell: it is false knowledge. For one who separates the knower from the field as a stalk from muñja grass, and sees the field as unreal, like an elephant made by magic or a thing seen in a dream, false knowledge goes; and with that cause of birth gone, he is not born again.

समं सर्वेषु भूतेषु तिष्ठन्तं परमेश्वरम् । विनश्यत्स्वविनश्यन्तं यः पश्यति स पश्यति ॥ २७ ॥
samaṃ sarveṣu bhūteṣu tiṣṭhantaṃ parameśvaram | vinaśyatsvavinaśyantaṃ yaḥ paśyati sa paśyati || 27 ||
Padaccheda
समम् सर्वेषु भूतेषु तिष्ठन्तम् परम-ईश्वरम् विनश्यत्सु अविनश्यन्तम् यः पश्यति सः पश्यति
samam sarveṣu bhūteṣu tiṣṭhantam parama-īśvaram vinaśyatsu avinaśyantam yaḥ paśyati saḥ paśyati
Word by word
He who sees the supreme Lord abiding the same in all beings, not perishing among the perishing, he sees.

Bhāṣya ‘the same’: without difference, in all beings from Brahmā to the unmoving. Birth is the root of all changes of a thing and destruction the last; by denying the last, all the earlier ones are denied too, so the Lord is wholly unlike beings, without difference, and one. ‘He sees’: everyone sees, but wrongly, as one with an eye disease sees many moons; he who sees the one moon alone sees.

समं पश्यन्हि सर्वत्र समवस्थितमीश्वरम् । न हिनस्त्यात्मनात्मानं ततो याति परां गतिम् ॥ २८ ॥
samaṃ paśyanhi sarvatra samavasthitamīśvaram | na hinastyātmanātmānaṃ tato yāti parāṃ gatim || 28 ||
Padaccheda
समम् पश्यन् हि सर्वत्र समवस्थितम् ईश्वरम् न हिनस्ति आत्मना आत्मानम् ततः याति पराम् गतिम्
samam paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram na hinasti ātmanā ātmānam tataḥ yāti parām gatim
Word by word
For, seeing the Lord present alike everywhere, the same, he does not destroy himself by himself; therefore he goes to the supreme goal.

Bhāṣya (objection) no living being destroys itself; why deny it? (Reply) every ignorant man sets aside the Self, which is plain and immediate, takes what is not the Self to be himself, and, having done good and evil, kills that self and takes up a new one, and again another; and the real Self too is as if killed by ignorance, since its fruit is not had. So all the ignorant are slayers of the self. The one who sees the Self does neither. The supreme goal is liberation.

प्रकृत्यैव च कर्माणि क्रियमाणानि सर्वशः । यः पश्यति तथात्मानमकर्तारं स पश्यति ॥ २९ ॥
prakṛtyaiva ca karmāṇi kriyamāṇāni sarvaśaḥ | yaḥ paśyati tathātmānamakartāraṃ sa paśyati || 29 ||
Padaccheda
प्रकृत्या एव च कर्माणि क्रियमाणानि सर्वशः यः पश्यति तथा आत्मानम् अकर्तारम् सः पश्यति
prakṛtyā eva ca karmāṇi kriyamāṇāni sarvaśaḥ yaḥ paśyati tathā ātmānam akartāram saḥ paśyati
Word by word
And he who sees that actions are in every way done by Prakṛti alone, and likewise that the Self is not the doer, he sees.

Bhāṣya Prakṛti is the Lord's illusion, made of the three guṇas, changed into the form of body and organs; actions are those begun by speech, mind and body. The Self, the knower of the field, is free of every limiting adjunct. He sees the highest truth: of what is without guṇas, not a doer and without difference, like space, there is no proof of division.

यदा भूतपृथग्भावमेकस्थमनुपश्यति । तत एव च विस्तारं ब्रह्म सम्पद्यते तदा ॥ ३० ॥
yadā bhūtapṛthagbhāvamekasthamanupaśyati | tata eva ca vistāraṃ brahma sampadyate tadā || 30 ||
Padaccheda
यदा भूत-पृथग्भावम् एक-स्थम् अनुपश्यति ततः एव च विस्तारम् ब्रह्म सम्पद्यते तदा
yadā bhūta-pṛthagbhāvam eka-stham anupaśyati tataḥ eva ca vistāram brahma sampadyate tadā
Word by word
When he sees the separate existence of beings as resting in the One, and their spreading forth as from that alone, then he becomes Brahman.

Bhāṣya He sees it directly, following (anu) the teaching of scripture and teacher: ‘all this is the Self alone’ (Chāndogya 7.25.2). The spreading forth is their arising from the Self: ‘from the Self is breath, from the Self hope, … from the Self food’ (Chāndogya 7.26.1).

अनादित्वान्निर्गुणत्वात्परमात्मायमव्ययः । शरीरस्थोऽपि कौन्तेय न करोति न लिप्यते ॥ ३१ ॥
anāditvānnirguṇatvātparamātmāyamavyayaḥ | śarīrastho 'pi kaunteya na karoti na lipyate || 31 ||
Padaccheda
अनादित्वात् निर्गुणत्वात् परम-आत्मा अयम् अव्ययः शरीर-स्थः अपि कौन्तेय न करोति न लिप्यते
anāditvāt nirguṇatvāt parama-ātmā ayam avyayaḥ śarīra-sthaḥ api kaunteya na karoti na lipyate
Word by word
Being without beginning and without guṇas, this supreme Self is undecaying. Though dwelling in the body, O son of Kuntī, it neither acts nor is tainted.

Bhāṣya ‘beginning’ is cause. What has a cause decays in its own nature, and what has qualities decays by their loss; this has neither. Not acting, it is not touched by the fruit of action. Who then acts and is tainted? It is only ignorance that gives rise to the usage ‘it acts, it is tainted’ (5.14); in truth there is none of it in the one supreme Self. So those established in knowledge, the wandering renouncers who have set aside the dealings of ignorance, have no duty of action.

यथा सर्वगतं सौक्ष्म्यादाकाशं नोपलिप्यते । सर्वत्रावस्थितो देहे तथात्मा नोपलिप्यते ॥ ३२ ॥
yathā sarvagataṃ saukṣmyādākāśaṃ nopalipyate | sarvatrāvasthito dehe tathātmā nopalipyate || 32 ||
Padaccheda
यथा सर्व-गतम् सौक्ष्म्यात् आकाशम् न उपलिप्यते सर्वत्र अवस्थितः देहे तथा आत्मा न उपलिप्यते
yathā sarva-gatam saukṣmyāt ākāśam na upalipyate sarvatra avasthitaḥ dehe tathā ātmā na upalipyate
Word by word
As all-pervading space is not tainted, because of its subtlety, so the Self, present everywhere in the body, is not tainted.
यथा प्रकाशयत्येकः कृत्स्नं लोकमिमं रविः । क्षेत्रं क्षेत्री तथा कृत्स्नं प्रकाशयति भारत ॥ ३३ ॥
yathā prakāśayatyekaḥ kṛtsnaṃ lokamimaṃ raviḥ | kṣetraṃ kṣetrī tathā kṛtsnaṃ prakāśayati bhārata || 33 ||
Padaccheda
यथा प्रकाशयति एकः कृत्स्नम् लोकम् इमम् रविः क्षेत्रम् क्षेत्री तथा कृत्स्नम् प्रकाशयति भारत
yathā prakāśayati ekaḥ kṛtsnam lokam imam raviḥ kṣetram kṣetrī tathā kṛtsnam prakāśayati bhārata
Word by word
As the one sun illumines this whole world, so the lord of the field illumines the whole field, O descendant of Bharata.

Bhāṣya The lord of the field is the supreme Self; the field runs from the great elements to firmness (5–6). The sun serves as an example in two ways: like the sun, the Self is one in all fields, and is not tainted.

क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञयोरेवमन्तरं ज्ञानचक्षुषा । भूतप्रकृतिमोक्षं च ये विदुर्यान्ति ते परम् ॥ ३४ ॥
kṣetrakṣetrajñayorevamantaraṃ jñānacakṣuṣā | bhūtaprakṛtimokṣaṃ ca ye viduryānti te param || 34 ||
Padaccheda
क्षेत्र-क्षेत्रज्ञयोः एवम् अन्तरम् ज्ञान-चक्षुषा भूत-प्रकृति-मोक्षम् च ये विदुः यान्ति ते परम्
kṣetra-kṣetrajñayoḥ evam antaram jñāna-cakṣuṣā bhūta-prakṛti-mokṣam ca ye viduḥ yānti te param
Word by word
Those who thus know, with the eye of knowledge, the difference between the field and the knower of the field, and the ceasing of Prakṛti, the source of beings, go to the supreme.

Bhāṣya The eye is the knowledge of the Self born of the teaching of scripture and teacher. The Prakṛti of beings is ignorance, called the unmanifest; its mokṣa is its passing into non-existence. The supreme is Brahman, the truth of the supreme Self: they do not take a body again.
