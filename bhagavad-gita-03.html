तृतीयोऽध्यायः
Tṛtīyo 'dhyāyaḥ · Adhyāya 3 · Karmayoga
अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
ज्यायसी चेत्कर्मणस्ते मता बुद्धिर्जनार्दन । तत्किं कर्मणि घोरे मां नियोजयसि केशव ॥ १ ॥
jyāyasī cetkarmaṇaste matā buddhirjanārdana | tatkiṃ karmaṇi ghore māṃ niyojayasi keśava || 1 ||
Padaccheda
ज्यायसी चेत् कर्मणः ते मता बुद्धिः जनार्दन तत् किम् कर्मणि घोरे माम् नियोजयसि केशव
jyāyasī cet karmaṇaḥ te matā buddhiḥ janārdana tat kim karmaṇi ghore mām niyojayasi keśava
Word by word
Arjuna said: If you hold knowledge to be superior to action, O Janārdana, why then do you engage me in this terrible action, O Keśava?

Note Śaṅkara: buddhi here is knowledge. The action is terrible because it is cruel and involves killing. He takes Arjuna's question as proof that the Lord had not taught knowledge and action as one combined means: had he done so, Arjuna could not have set the one above the other.

व्यामिश्रेणैव वाक्येन बुद्धिं मोहयसीव मे । तदेकं वद निश्चित्य येन श्रेयोऽहमाप्नुयाम् ॥ २ ॥
vyāmiśreṇaiva vākyena buddhiṃ mohayasīva me | tadekaṃ vada niścitya yena śreyo 'hamāpnuyām || 2 ||
Padaccheda
व्यामिश्रेण एव वाक्येन बुद्धिम् मोहयसि इव मे तत् एकम् वद निश्चित्य येन श्रेयः अहम् आप्नुयाम्
vyāmiśreṇa eva vākyena buddhim mohayasi iva me tat ekam vada niścitya yena śreyaḥ aham āpnuyām
Word by word
With words that seem mixed you confuse my understanding, as it were. Decide, and tell me the one thing by which I may attain the highest good.

Note Śaṅkara: the Lord speaks distinctly, but to Arjuna's slow understanding his words seem mixed. ‘The one thing’: either knowledge or action, whichever suits Arjuna's capacity and condition.

Note The text of the bhāṣya reads vyāmiśreṇeva, ‘as though mixed’; the critical edition has vyāmiśreṇaiva.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
लोकेऽस्मिन्द्विविधा निष्ठा पुरा प्रोक्ता मयानघ । ज्ञानयोगेन सांख्यानां कर्मयोगेन योगिनाम् ॥ ३ ॥
loke 'smindvividhā niṣṭhā purā proktā mayānagha | jñānayogena sāṃkhyānāṃ karmayogena yoginām || 3 ||
Padaccheda
लोके अस्मिन् द्विविधा निष्ठा पुरा प्रोक्ता मया अनघ ज्ञान-योगेन सांख्यानाम् कर्म-योगेन योगिनाम्
loke asmin dvividhā niṣṭhā purā proktā mayā anagha jñāna-yogena sāṃkhyānām karma-yogena yoginām
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: In this world, O sinless one, a twofold path of steadfastness was taught by me of old: by the yoga of knowledge for the men of discernment, and by the yoga of action for the men of action.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘of old’: at the beginning of creation, when the Lord brought forth beings and revealed the Vedic tradition. The sāṃkhyas are those who discern the Self from what is not the Self, who have renounced straight from the student's life and abide in Brahman; the yogins are those who perform rites. Since the two paths are taught for different persons, knowledge and action are not to be combined by one person for one end.

न कर्मणामनारम्भान्नैष्कर्म्यं पुरुषोऽश्नुते । न च संन्यसनादेव सिद्धिं समधिगच्छति ॥ ४ ॥
na karmaṇāmanārambhānnaiṣkarmyaṃ puruṣo 'śnute | na ca saṃnyasanādeva siddhiṃ samadhigacchati || 4 ||
Padaccheda
न कर्मणाम् अनारम्भात् नैष्कर्म्यम् पुरुषः अश्नुते न च संन्यसनात् एव सिद्धिम् समधिगच्छति
na karmaṇām anārambhāt naiṣkarmyam puruṣaḥ aśnute na ca saṃnyasanāt eva siddhim samadhigacchati
Word by word
A man does not attain freedom from action by not undertaking actions; nor does he reach perfection by renunciation alone.

Note Śaṅkara: naiṣkarmya is the state without action: steadfastness in the yoga of knowledge, abiding as the actionless Self. Actions such as sacrifice, done in this or an earlier birth, destroy past wrong, purify the mind, and so lead to knowledge; hence the yoga of action is the means to the yoga of knowledge. ‘By renunciation alone’: by merely giving up actions, without knowledge.

न हि कश्चित्क्षणमपि जातु तिष्ठत्यकर्मकृत् । कार्यते ह्यवशः कर्म सर्वः प्रकृतिजैर्गुणैः ॥ ५ ॥
na hi kaścitkṣaṇamapi jātu tiṣṭhatyakarmakṛt | kāryate hyavaśaḥ karma sarvaḥ prakṛtijairguṇaiḥ || 5 ||
Padaccheda
न हि कश्चित् क्षणम् अपि जातु तिष्ठति अ-कर्म-कृत् कार्यते हि अवशः कर्म सर्वः प्रकृति-जैः गुणैः
na hi kaścit kṣaṇam api jātu tiṣṭhati a-karma-kṛt kāryate hi avaśaḥ karma sarvaḥ prakṛti-jaiḥ guṇaiḥ
Word by word
For no one ever remains even for a moment without acting; everyone is helplessly made to act by the guṇas born of Prakṛti.

Note Śaṅkara: the guṇas are sattva, rajas and tamas. ‘Everyone’ means everyone who does not know the Self; the knowers, whom the guṇas do not move, have been set apart. So the yoga of action is for the ignorant, not for the knowers.

कर्मेन्द्रियाणि संयम्य य आस्ते मनसा स्मरन् । इन्द्रियार्थान्विमूढात्मा मिथ्याचारः स उच्यते ॥ ६ ॥
karmendriyāṇi saṃyamya ya āste manasā smaran | indriyārthānvimūḍhātmā mithyācāraḥ sa ucyate || 6 ||
Padaccheda
कर्मेन्द्रियाणि संयम्य यः आस्ते मनसा स्मरन् इन्द्रिय-अर्थान् विमूढ-आत्मा मिथ्या-आचारः सः उच्यते
karmendriyāṇi saṃyamya yaḥ āste manasā smaran indriya-arthān vimūḍha-ātmā mithyā-ācāraḥ saḥ ucyate
Word by word
He who restrains the organs of action but sits dwelling in his mind on the objects of the senses is deluded, and is called a man of false conduct.

Note Śaṅkara: the organs of action are the hands and the rest. mithyācāra: one whose conduct is false and sinful.

यस्त्विन्द्रियाणि मनसा नियम्यारभतेऽर्जुन । कर्मेन्द्रियैः कर्मयोगमसक्तः स विशिष्यते ॥ ७ ॥
yastvindriyāṇi manasā niyamyārabhate 'rjuna | karmendriyaiḥ karmayogam asaktaḥ sa viśiṣyate || 7 ||
Padaccheda
यः तु इन्द्रियाणि मनसा नियम्य आरभते अर्जुन कर्मेन्द्रियैः कर्म-योगम् अ-सक्तः सः विशिष्यते
yaḥ tu indriyāṇi manasā niyamya ārabhate arjuna karmendriyaiḥ karma-yogam a-saktaḥ saḥ viśiṣyate
Word by word
But he who controls the senses by the mind, O Arjuna, and, unattached, undertakes the yoga of action with the organs of action, excels.

Note Śaṅkara: this is the one entitled to action, who does not know the Self. The senses controlled are the senses of knowledge; the organs of action are speech, hands and the rest. ‘Unattached’: without aiming at the fruit. He excels the man of false conduct.

नियतं कुरु कर्म त्वं कर्म ज्यायो ह्यकर्मणः । शरीरयात्रापि च ते न प्रसिध्येदकर्मणः ॥ ८ ॥
niyataṃ kuru karma tvaṃ karma jyāyo hyakarmaṇaḥ | śarīrayātrāpi ca te na prasidhyedakarmaṇaḥ || 8 ||
Padaccheda
नियतम् कुरु कर्म त्वम् कर्म ज्यायः हि अकर्मणः शरीर-यात्रा अपि च ते न प्रसिध्येत् अ-कर्मणः
niyatam kuru karma tvam karma jyāyaḥ hi akarmaṇaḥ śarīra-yātrā api ca te na prasidhyet a-karmaṇaḥ
Word by word
Perform the action that is prescribed for you, for action is better than inaction. Even the maintenance of your body would not be possible through inaction.

Note Śaṅkara: niyata: the obligatory action taught by scripture, for which one is qualified and for which no particular fruit is stated.

यज्ञार्थात्कर्मणोऽन्यत्र लोकोऽयं कर्मबन्धनः । तदर्थं कर्म कौन्तेय मुक्तसङ्गः समाचर ॥ ९ ॥
yajñārthātkarmaṇo 'nyatra loko 'yaṃ karmabandhanaḥ | tadarthaṃ karma kaunteya muktasaṅgaḥ samācara || 9 ||
Padaccheda
यज्ञ-अर्थात् कर्मणः अन्यत्र लोकः अयम् कर्म-बन्धनः तद्-अर्थम् कर्म कौन्तेय मुक्त-सङ्गः समाचर
yajña-arthāt karmaṇaḥ anyatra lokaḥ ayam karma-bandhanaḥ tad-artham karma kaunteya mukta-saṅgaḥ samācara
Word by word
This world is bound by action other than action done for the sake of sacrifice. Perform action for that purpose, O son of Kuntī, free from attachment.

Note Śaṅkara: the sacrifice is the Lord, for scripture says ‘sacrifice is Viṣṇu’; action done for his sake does not bind. ‘Free from attachment’: without attachment to the fruit of action.

सहयज्ञाः प्रजाः सृष्ट्वा पुरोवाच प्रजापतिः । अनेन प्रसविष्यध्वमेष वोऽस्त्विष्टकामधुक् ॥ १० ॥
sahayajñāḥ prajāḥ sṛṣṭvā purovāca prajāpatiḥ | anena prasaviṣyadhvameṣa vo 'stviṣṭakāmadhuk || 10 ||
Padaccheda
सह-यज्ञाः प्रजाः सृष्ट्वा पुरा उवाच प्रजापतिः अनेन प्रसविष्यध्वम् एषः वः अस्तु इष्ट-कामधुक्
saha-yajñāḥ prajāḥ sṛṣṭvā purā uvāca prajāpatiḥ anena prasaviṣyadhvam eṣaḥ vaḥ astu iṣṭa-kāmadhuk
Word by word
Of old the Lord of creatures, having created creatures together with sacrifice, said: “By this you shall multiply; let this be the yielder of all you desire.”

Note Śaṅkara: the creatures are the three orders; ‘of old’: at the beginning of creation. This is a further reason why one who is entitled to action must act.

देवान्भावयतानेन ते देवा भावयन्तु वः । परस्परं भावयन्तः श्रेयः परमवाप्स्यथ ॥ ११ ॥
devānbhāvayatānena te devā bhāvayantu vaḥ | parasparaṃ bhāvayantaḥ śreyaḥ paramavāpsyatha || 11 ||
Padaccheda
देवान् भावयत अनेन ते देवाः भावयन्तु वः परस्परम् भावयन्तः श्रेयः परम् अवाप्स्यथ
devān bhāvayata anena te devāḥ bhāvayantu vaḥ parasparam bhāvayantaḥ śreyaḥ param avāpsyatha
Word by word
“Nourish the gods by this, and let the gods nourish you. Nourishing one another, you will attain the highest good.”

Note Śaṅkara: the gods, Indra and the rest, nourish men with rain and the like. The highest good is liberation, reached in time through the gaining of knowledge; or else heaven.

इष्टान्भोगान्हि वो देवा दास्यन्ते यज्ञभाविताः । तैर्दत्तानप्रदायैभ्यो यो भुङ्क्ते स्तेन एव सः ॥ १२ ॥
iṣṭānbhogānhi vo devā dāsyante yajñabhāvitāḥ | tairdattānapradāyaibhyo yo bhuṅkte stena eva saḥ || 12 ||
Padaccheda
इष्टान् भोगान् हि वः देवाः दास्यन्ते यज्ञ-भाविताः तैः दत्तान् अ-प्रदाय एभ्यः यः भुङ्क्ते स्तेनः एव सः
iṣṭān bhogān hi vaḥ devāḥ dāsyante yajña-bhāvitāḥ taiḥ dattān a-pradāya ebhyaḥ yaḥ bhuṅkte stenaḥ eva saḥ
Word by word
“For the gods, nourished by sacrifice, will give you the enjoyments you wish for. He who enjoys what they give without offering to them in return is indeed a thief.”

Note Śaṅkara: ‘without offering in return’: without discharging his debt. Such a man only gratifies his own body and senses, and robs the gods of what is theirs.

यज्ञशिष्टाशिनः सन्तो मुच्यन्ते सर्वकिल्बिषैः । भुञ्जते ते त्वघं पापा ये पचन्त्यात्मकारणात् ॥ १३ ॥
yajñaśiṣṭāśinaḥ santo mucyante sarvakilbiṣaiḥ | bhuñjate te tvaghaṃ pāpā ye pacantyātmakāraṇāt || 13 ||
Padaccheda
यज्ञ-शिष्ट-आशिनः सन्तः मुच्यन्ते सर्व-किल्बिषैः भुञ्जते ते तु अघम् पापाः ये पचन्ति आत्म-कारणात्
yajña-śiṣṭa-āśinaḥ santaḥ mucyante sarva-kilbiṣaiḥ bhuñjate te tu agham pāpāḥ ye pacanti ātma-kāraṇāt
Word by word
The good, who eat what remains of the sacrifice, are freed from all sins. But the wicked, who cook for their own sake, eat sin.

Note Śaṅkara: what remains after the sacrifices to the gods and the rest is the food called ‘nectar’. The sins are those incurred at the five places of slaughter in a household, the hearth and the rest, and others arising from injury done through carelessness.

अन्नाद्भवन्ति भूतानि पर्जन्यादन्नसंभवः । यज्ञाद्भवति पर्जन्यो यज्ञः कर्मसमुद्भवः ॥ १४ ॥
annādbhavanti bhūtāni parjanyādannasaṃbhavaḥ | yajñādbhavati parjanyo yajñaḥ karmasamudbhavaḥ || 14 ||
Padaccheda
अन्नात् भवन्ति भूतानि पर्जन्यात् अन्न-संभवः यज्ञात् भवति पर्जन्यः यज्ञः कर्म-समुद्भवः
annāt bhavanti bhūtāni parjanyāt anna-saṃbhavaḥ yajñāt bhavati parjanyaḥ yajñaḥ karma-samudbhavaḥ
Word by word
From food creatures come into being; from rain food is produced; from sacrifice comes rain; and sacrifice arises from action.

Note Śaṅkara: action is what turns the wheel of the world. ‘Sacrifice’ here is the unseen result (apūrva) of the rite, which arises from action, the activity of priest and sacrificer.

कर्म ब्रह्मोद्भवं विद्धि ब्रह्माक्षरसमुद्भवम् । तस्मात्सर्वगतं ब्रह्म नित्यं यज्ञे प्रतिष्ठितम् ॥ १५ ॥
karma brahmodbhavaṃ viddhi brahmākṣarasamudbhavam | tasmātsarvagataṃ brahma nityaṃ yajñe pratiṣṭhitam || 15 ||
Padaccheda
कर्म ब्रह्म-उद्भवम् विद्धि ब्रह्म अक्षर-समुद्भवम् तस्मात् सर्व-गतम् ब्रह्म नित्यम् यज्ञे प्रतिष्ठितम्
karma brahma-udbhavam viddhi brahma akṣara-samudbhavam tasmāt sarva-gatam brahma nityam yajñe pratiṣṭhitam
Word by word
Know that action arises from the Veda, and the Veda arises from the Imperishable. Therefore the all-pervading Veda is always established in sacrifice.

Note Śaṅkara: brahman in this verse means the Veda, which makes action known. The Imperishable is Brahman, the supreme Self, from which the Veda came forth like a man's breath. The Veda is all-pervading because it reveals all things, and is established in sacrifice because the rules of sacrifice are its main subject.

एवं प्रवर्तितं चक्रं नानुवर्तयतीह यः । अघायुरिन्द्रियारामो मोघं पार्थ स जीवति ॥ १६ ॥
evaṃ pravartitaṃ cakraṃ nānuvartayatīha yaḥ | aghāyurindriyārāmo moghaṃ pārtha sa jīvati || 16 ||
Padaccheda
एवम् प्रवर्तितम् चक्रम् न अनुवर्तयति इह यः अघायुः इन्द्रिय-आरामः मोघम् पार्थ सः जीवति
evam pravartitam cakram na anuvartayati iha yaḥ aghāyuḥ indriya-ārāmaḥ mogham pārtha saḥ jīvati
Word by word
He who does not, here, help to turn the wheel thus set turning, whose life is sin and who delights in the senses, lives in vain, O Pārtha.

Note Śaṅkara: the wheel of the world was set turning by the Lord on the basis of the Veda and sacrifice. This sums up the section: one who is entitled to action and does not know the Self must perform action, until he becomes fit for steadfastness in knowledge of the Self.

यस्त्वात्मरतिरेव स्यादात्मतृप्तश्च मानवः । आत्मन्येव च संतुष्टस्तस्य कार्यं न विद्यते ॥ १७ ॥
yastvātmaratireva syādātmatṛptaśca mānavaḥ | ātmanyeva ca saṃtuṣṭastasya kāryaṃ na vidyate || 17 ||
Padaccheda
यः तु आत्म-रतिः एव स्यात् आत्म-तृप्तः च मानवः आत्मनि एव च संतुष्टः तस्य कार्यम् न विद्यते
yaḥ tu ātma-ratiḥ eva syāt ātma-tṛptaḥ ca mānavaḥ ātmani eva ca saṃtuṣṭaḥ tasya kāryam na vidyate
Word by word
But the man who delights in the Self alone, who is satisfied with the Self and content in the Self alone: for him there is nothing to be done.

Note Śaṅkara: this is the sāṃkhya, the renouncer steadfast in knowledge of the Self. His delight is in the Self and not in objects; he is satisfied by the Self and not by food and drink; his contentment does not depend on gaining outward things.

नैव तस्य कृतेनार्थो नाकृतेनेह कश्चन । न चास्य सर्वभूतेषु कश्चिदर्थव्यपाश्रयः ॥ १८ ॥
naiva tasya kṛtenārtho nākṛteneha kaścana | na cāsya sarvabhūteṣu kaścidarthavyapāśrayaḥ || 18 ||
Padaccheda
न एव तस्य कृतेन अर्थः न अकृतेन इह कश्चन न च अस्य सर्व-भूतेषु कश्चित् अर्थ-व्यपाश्रयः
na eva tasya kṛtena arthaḥ na akṛtena iha kaścana na ca asya sarva-bhūteṣu kaścit artha-vyapāśrayaḥ
Word by word
He has nothing to gain here by what is done, and nothing to lose by what is not done; nor does he depend on any being for any end.

Note Śaṅkara: for one who delights in the supreme Self, action serves no purpose, and not acting brings no harm. He need not rely on any being, from Brahmā down to plants, to gain anything. But Arjuna, he adds, does not stand in this right vision, which is like the flood of water on every side (2.46).

तस्मादसक्तः सततं कार्यं कर्म समाचर । असक्तो ह्याचरन्कर्म परमाप्नोति पूरुषः ॥ १९ ॥
tasmād asaktaḥ satataṃ kāryaṃ karma samācara | asakto hyācarankarma paramāpnoti pūruṣaḥ || 19 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मात् अ-सक्तः सततम् कार्यम् कर्म समाचर अ-सक्तः हि आचरन् कर्म परम् आप्नोति पूरुषः
tasmāt a-saktaḥ satatam kāryam karma samācara a-saktaḥ hi ācaran karma param āpnoti pūruṣaḥ
Word by word
Therefore always perform, unattached, the action that ought to be done; for a man who performs action unattached attains the highest.

Note Śaṅkara: the action meant is the obligatory duty, performed for the Lord's sake. He attains the highest, liberation, by way of purity of mind.

कर्मणैव हि संसिद्धिमास्थिता जनकादयः । लोकसंग्रहमेवापि संपश्यन्कर्तुमर्हसि ॥ २० ॥
karmaṇaiva hi saṃsiddhimāsthitā janakādayaḥ | lokasaṃgrahamevāpi saṃpaśyankartumarhasi || 20 ||
Padaccheda
कर्मणा एव हि संसिद्धिम् आस्थिताः जनक-आदयः लोक-संग्रहम् एव अपि संपश्यन् कर्तुम् अर्हसि
karmaṇā eva hi saṃsiddhim āsthitāḥ janaka-ādayaḥ loka-saṃgraham eva api saṃpaśyan kartum arhasi
Word by word
For it was by action that Janaka and others set out for perfection. You ought to act, too, having regard to the holding together of the world.

Note Śaṅkara: Janaka, Aśvapati and the others were learned warriors of old; perfection is liberation. If they had already gained right vision, they went on to it together with action, without renouncing, for the sake of the world, since their action had already begun; if not, they reached it in due course by action as the means of purifying the mind. lokasaṃgraha is keeping the world from going astray.

यद्यदाचरति श्रेष्ठस्तत्तदेवेतरो जनः । स यत्प्रमाणं कुरुते लोकस्तदनुवर्तते ॥ २१ ॥
yadyadācarati śreṣṭhastattadevetaro janaḥ | sa yatpramāṇaṃ kurute lokastadanuvartate || 21 ||
Padaccheda
यत् यत् आचरति श्रेष्ठः तत् तत् एव इतरः जनः सः यत् प्रमाणम् कुरुते लोकः तत् अनुवर्तते
yat yat ācarati śreṣṭhaḥ tat tat eva itaraḥ janaḥ saḥ yat pramāṇam kurute lokaḥ tat anuvartate
Word by word
Whatever the best man does, that the others do too; what he sets up as authority, the world follows.

Note Śaṅkara: the authority may be worldly or Vedic.

न मे पार्थास्ति कर्तव्यं त्रिषु लोकेषु किंचन । नानवाप्तमवाप्तव्यं वर्त एव च कर्मणि ॥ २२ ॥
na me pārthāsti kartavyaṃ triṣu lokeṣu kiṃcana | nānavāptamavāptavyaṃ varta eva ca karmaṇi || 22 ||
Padaccheda
न मे पार्थ अस्ति कर्तव्यम् त्रिषु लोकेषु किंचन न अनवाप्तम् अवाप्तव्यम् वर्ते एव च कर्मणि
na me pārtha asti kartavyam triṣu lokeṣu kiṃcana na anavāptam avāptavyam varte eva ca karmaṇi
Word by word
For me, O Pārtha, there is nothing in the three worlds that has to be done, nothing unattained that has to be attained; yet I am engaged in action.
यदि ह्यहं न वर्तेयं जातु कर्मण्यतन्द्रितः । मम वर्त्मानुवर्तन्ते मनुष्याः पार्थ सर्वशः ॥ २३ ॥
yadi hyahaṃ na varteyaṃ jātu karmaṇyatandritaḥ | mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ || 23 ||
Padaccheda
यदि हि अहम् न वर्तेयम् जातु कर्मणि अतन्द्रितः मम वर्त्म अनुवर्तन्ते मनुष्याः पार्थ सर्वशः
yadi hi aham na varteyam jātu karmaṇi atandritaḥ mama vartma anuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ
Word by word
For if I were ever not engaged in action, unwearied, men, O Pārtha, would follow my path in every way.
उत्सीदेयुरिमे लोका न कुर्यां कर्म चेदहम् । संकरस्य च कर्ता स्यामुपहन्यामिमाः प्रजाः ॥ २४ ॥
utsīdeyurime lokā na kuryāṃ karma cedaham | saṃkarasya ca kartā syām upahanyāmimāḥ prajāḥ || 24 ||
Padaccheda
उत्सीदेयुः इमे लोकाः न कुर्याम् कर्म चेत् अहम् संकरस्य च कर्ता स्याम् उपहन्याम् इमाः प्रजाः
utsīdeyuḥ ime lokāḥ na kuryām karma cet aham saṃkarasya ca kartā syām upahanyām imāḥ prajāḥ
Word by word
These worlds would perish if I did not perform action; I should be the author of confusion and should destroy these creatures.

Note Śaṅkara: the worlds would perish for want of the action that sustains them. Having set out to favour creatures he would be destroying them, which would not befit the Lord.

सक्ताः कर्मण्यविद्वांसो यथा कुर्वन्ति भारत । कुर्याद्विद्वांस्तथासक्तश्चिकीर्षुर्लोकसंग्रहम् ॥ २५ ॥
saktāḥ karmaṇy avidvāṃso yathā kurvanti bhārata | kuryād vidvāṃs tathāsaktaś cikīrṣur lokasaṃgraham || 25 ||
Padaccheda
सक्ताः कर्मणि अ-विद्वांसः यथा कुर्वन्ति भारत कुर्यात् विद्वान् तथा अ-सक्तः चिकीर्षुः लोक-संग्रहम्
saktāḥ karmaṇi a-vidvāṃsaḥ yathā kurvanti bhārata kuryāt vidvān tathā a-saktaḥ cikīrṣuḥ loka-saṃgraham
Word by word
As the unwise act, attached to action, O Bhārata, so should the wise man act, unattached, wishing to hold the world together.

Note Śaṅkara: the unwise act thinking ‘the fruit of this action will be mine’. The wise man is the knower of the Self; though he has nothing of his own to gain, he should act for the good of others.

न बुद्धिभेदं जनयेदज्ञानां कर्मसङ्गिनाम् । जोषयेत्सर्वकर्माणि विद्वान्युक्तः समाचरन् ॥ २६ ॥
na buddhibhedaṃ janayedajñānāṃ karmasaṅginām | joṣayetsarvakarmāṇi vidvānyuktaḥ samācaran || 26 ||
Padaccheda
न बुद्धि-भेदम् जनयेत् अज्ञानाम् कर्म-सङ्गिनाम् जोषयेत् सर्व-कर्माणि विद्वान् युक्तः समाचरन्
na buddhi-bhedam janayet ajñānām karma-saṅginām joṣayet sarva-karmāṇi vidvān yuktaḥ samācaran
Word by word
The wise man should not unsettle the understanding of the ignorant who are attached to action. Performing all actions himself with care, he should have them perform them.

Note Śaṅkara: the understanding that would be unsettled is their conviction, ‘I must do this, and enjoy its fruit’.

प्रकृतेः क्रियमाणानि गुणैः कर्माणि सर्वशः । अहंकारविमूढात्मा कर्ताहमिति मन्यते ॥ २७ ॥
prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ | ahaṃkāravimūḍhātmā kartāhamiti manyate || 27 ||
Padaccheda
प्रकृतेः क्रियमाणानि गुणैः कर्माणि सर्वशः अहंकार-विमूढ-आत्मा कर्ता अहम् इति मन्यते
prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ ahaṃkāra-vimūḍha-ātmā kartā aham iti manyate
Word by word
Actions are in every way done by the guṇas of Prakṛti; but one whose mind is deluded by the sense of ‘I’ thinks, “I am the doer.”

Note Śaṅkara: prakṛti is the primal matter, the state in which the three guṇas are in balance; its guṇas are its modifications in the form of body and organs. ahaṃkāra is the notion that the aggregate of body and organs is the Self; through ignorance such a man takes their actions to be his own.

Note The DCS treats kartāham as one word; it is divided here as kartā aham.

तत्त्ववित्तु महाबाहो गुणकर्मविभागयोः । गुणा गुणेषु वर्तन्त इति मत्वा न सज्जते ॥ २८ ॥
tattvavittu mahābāho guṇakarmavibhāgayoḥ | guṇā guṇeṣu vartanta iti matvā na sajjate || 28 ||
Padaccheda
तत्त्व-वित् तु महा-बाहो गुण-कर्म-विभागयोः गुणाः गुणेषु वर्तन्ते इति मत्वा न सज्जते
tattva-vit tu mahā-bāho guṇa-karma-vibhāgayoḥ guṇāḥ guṇeṣu vartante iti matvā na sajjate
Word by word
But one who knows the truth, O mighty-armed, about the divisions of the guṇas and of actions understands that it is the guṇas that act among the guṇas, and is not attached.

Note Śaṅkara: the guṇas in the form of the organs act upon the guṇas in the form of objects; the Self does not.

प्रकृतेर्गुणसंमूढाः सज्जन्ते गुणकर्मसु । तानकृत्स्नविदो मन्दान्कृत्स्नविन्न विचालयेत् ॥ २९ ॥
prakṛterguṇasaṃmūḍhāḥ sajjante guṇakarmasu | tānakṛtsnavido mandānkṛtsnavinna vicālayet || 29 ||
Padaccheda
प्रकृतेः गुण-संमूढाः सज्जन्ते गुण-कर्मसु तान् अकृत्स्न-विदः मन्दान् कृत्स्न-वित् न विचालयेत्
prakṛteḥ guṇa-saṃmūḍhāḥ sajjante guṇa-karmasu tān akṛtsna-vidaḥ mandān kṛtsna-vit na vicālayet
Word by word
Those who are deluded by the guṇas of Prakṛti are attached to the actions of the guṇas. One who knows the whole should not unsettle those slow-witted ones, who do not know the whole.

Note Śaṅkara: they think, ‘we do the action for its fruit’. Those who do not know the whole see only the fruit of action; the knower of the whole is the knower of the Self.

मयि सर्वाणि कर्माणि संन्यस्याध्यात्मचेतसा । निराशीर्निर्ममो भूत्वा युध्यस्व विगतज्वरः ॥ ३० ॥
mayi sarvāṇi karmāṇi saṃnyasyādhyātmacetasā | nirāśīrnirmamo bhūtvā yudhyasva vigatajvaraḥ || 30 ||
Padaccheda
मयि सर्वाणि कर्माणि संन्यस्य अध्यात्म-चेतसा निराशीः निर्ममः भूत्वा युध्यस्व विगत-ज्वरः
mayi sarvāṇi karmāṇi saṃnyasya adhyātma-cetasā nirāśīḥ nirmamaḥ bhūtvā yudhyasva vigata-jvaraḥ
Word by word
Laying down all actions in me with a discerning mind, free from expectation and from the sense of ‘mine’, fight, with your fever gone.

Note Śaṅkara: this is how the ignorant seeker of liberation who is entitled to action should act. ‘In me’: in Vāsudeva, the supreme Lord, all-knowing, the Self of all. adhyātmacetas is the discerning thought, ‘I, the doer, act for the Lord as a servant’. The fever is anguish and grief.

ये मे मतमिदं नित्यमनुतिष्ठन्ति मानवाः । श्रद्धावन्तोऽनसूयन्तो मुच्यन्ते तेऽपि कर्मभिः ॥ ३१ ॥
ye me matamidaṃ nityamanutiṣṭhanti mānavāḥ | śraddhāvanto 'nasūyanto mucyante te 'pi karmabhiḥ || 31 ||
Padaccheda
ये मे मतम् इदम् नित्यम् अनुतिष्ठन्ति मानवाः श्रद्धावन्तः अनसूयन्तः मुच्यन्ते ते अपि कर्मभिः
ye me matam idam nityam anutiṣṭhanti mānavāḥ śraddhāvantaḥ anasūyantaḥ mucyante te api karmabhiḥ
Word by word
Men who always follow this teaching of mine, full of faith and not finding fault, are also freed from actions.

Note Śaṅkara: not finding fault with Vāsudeva, the supreme teacher. The actions from which they are freed are merit and demerit.

ये त्वेतदभ्यसूयन्तो नानुतिष्ठन्ति मे मतम् । सर्वज्ञानविमूढांस्तान्विद्धि नष्टानचेतसः ॥ ३२ ॥
ye tvetadabhyasūyanto nānutiṣṭhanti me matam | sarvajñānavimūḍhāṃstānviddhi naṣṭānacetasaḥ || 32 ||
Padaccheda
ये तु एतत् अभ्यसूयन्तः न अनुतिष्ठन्ति मे मतम् सर्व-ज्ञान-विमूढान् तान् विद्धि नष्टान् अचेतसः
ye tu etat abhyasūyantaḥ na anutiṣṭhanti me matam sarva-jñāna-vimūḍhān tān viddhi naṣṭān acetasaḥ
Word by word
But those who find fault with this teaching of mine and do not follow it: know them to be deluded in all knowledge, lost and without discernment.
सदृशं चेष्टते स्वस्याः प्रकृतेर्ज्ञानवानपि । प्रकृतिं यान्ति भूतानि निग्रहः किं करिष्यति ॥ ३३ ॥
sadṛśaṃ ceṣṭate svasyāḥ prakṛterjñānavānapi | prakṛtiṃ yānti bhūtāni nigrahaḥ kiṃ kariṣyati || 33 ||
Padaccheda
सदृशम् चेष्टते स्वस्याः प्रकृतेः ज्ञानवान् अपि प्रकृतिम् यान्ति भूतानि निग्रहः किम् करिष्यति
sadṛśam ceṣṭate svasyāḥ prakṛteḥ jñānavān api prakṛtim yānti bhūtāni nigrahaḥ kim kariṣyati
Word by word
Even a man of knowledge acts in accordance with his own nature. Beings follow their nature; what will restraint do?

Note Śaṅkara: this answers why people do not follow the Lord's teaching. prakṛti here is the impressions left by merit, demerit and the like done before, showing themselves at the start of the present birth. Nature is hard to restrain, whether by the Lord's prohibition or by another's.

इन्द्रियस्येन्द्रियस्यार्थे रागद्वेषौ व्यवस्थितौ । तयोर्न वशमागच्छेत्तौ ह्यस्य परिपन्थिनौ ॥ ३४ ॥
indriyasyendriyasyārthe rāgadveṣau vyavasthitau | tayorna vaśamāgacchet tau hyasya paripanthinau || 34 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियस्य इन्द्रियस्य अर्थे राग-द्वेषौ व्यवस्थितौ तयोः न वशम् आगच्छेत् तौ हि अस्य परिपन्थिनौ
indriyasya indriyasya arthe rāga-dveṣau vyavasthitau tayoḥ na vaśam āgacchet tau hi asya paripanthinau
Word by word
Attachment and aversion are fixed towards the object of each of the senses. One should not come under their sway, for they are one's enemies on the road.

Note Śaṅkara: if everyone simply followed his nature, human effort and the teaching of scripture would have no scope; this verse shows their scope. Nature moves a man through attachment and aversion; one who checks these by their opposite comes to see by the light of scripture and is no longer under the sway of nature. They obstruct the path to the good like robbers on a road.

श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात् । स्वधर्मे निधनं श्रेयः परधर्मो भयावहः ॥ ३५ ॥
śreyānsvadharmo viguṇaḥ paradharmātsvanuṣṭhitāt | svadharme nidhanaṃ śreyaḥ paradharmo bhayāvahaḥ || 35 ||
Padaccheda
श्रेयान् स्वधर्मः विगुणः पर-धर्मात् सु-अनुष्ठितात् स्वधर्मे निधनम् श्रेयः पर-धर्मः भय-आवहः
śreyān svadharmaḥ viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt su-anuṣṭhitāt svadharme nidhanam śreyaḥ para-dharmaḥ bhaya-āvahaḥ
Word by word
Better is one's own dharma, though imperfect, than the dharma of another well performed. Death in one's own dharma is better; the dharma of another brings danger.

Note Śaṅkara: death while keeping to one's own dharma is better than life in the dharma of another, which brings the danger of hell and the like.

अर्जुन उवाचarjuna uvāca
अथ केन प्रयुक्तोऽयं पापं चरति पूरुषः । अनिच्छन्नपि वार्ष्णेय बलादिव नियोजितः ॥ ३६ ॥
atha kena prayukto 'yaṃ pāpaṃ carati pūruṣaḥ | anicchannapi vārṣṇeya balādiva niyojitaḥ || 36 ||
Padaccheda
अथ केन प्रयुक्तः अयम् पापम् चरति पूरुषः अन्-इच्छन् अपि वार्ष्णेय बलात् इव नियोजितः
atha kena prayuktaḥ ayam pāpam carati pūruṣaḥ an-icchan api vārṣṇeya balāt iva niyojitaḥ
Word by word
Arjuna said: By what, then, is a man impelled to commit sin, even against his will, O descendant of Vṛṣṇi, as though driven by force?

Note Śaṅkara: as a servant is driven by a king.

श्रीभगवानुवाचśrībhagavān uvāca
काम एष क्रोध एष रजोगुणसमुद्भवः । महाशनो महापाप्मा विद्ध्येनमिह वैरिणम् ॥ ३७ ॥
kāma eṣa krodha eṣa rajoguṇasamudbhavaḥ | mahāśano mahāpāpmā viddhyenamiha vairiṇam || 37 ||
Padaccheda
कामः एषः क्रोधः एषः रजः-गुण-समुद्भवः महा-अशनः महा-पाप्मा विद्धि एनम् इह वैरिणम्
kāmaḥ eṣaḥ krodhaḥ eṣaḥ rajaḥ-guṇa-samudbhavaḥ mahā-aśanaḥ mahā-pāpmā viddhi enam iha vairiṇam
Word by word
The Blessed Lord said: It is desire, it is anger, born of the guṇa of rajas, all-devouring and greatly sinful. Know this to be the enemy here.

Note Śaṅkara: desire is the enemy of the whole world, the cause of every evil that befalls creatures. When thwarted by something it turns into anger, so anger is the same thing. It is born of rajas, or else it gives rise to rajas: once arisen, desire stirs up rajas and so drives a man. ‘Here’: in the cycle of birth and death.

धूमेनाव्रियते वह्निर्यथादर्शो मलेन च । यथोल्बेनावृतो गर्भस्तथा तेनेदमावृतम् ॥ ३८ ॥
dhūmenāvriyate vahniryathādarśo malena ca | yatholbenāvṛto garbhastathā tenedamāvṛtam || 38 ||
Padaccheda
धूमेन आव्रियते वह्निः यथा आदर्शः मलेन च यथा उल्बेन आवृतः गर्भः तथा तेन इदम् आवृतम्
dhūmena āvriyate vahniḥ yathā ādarśaḥ malena ca yathā ulbena āvṛtaḥ garbhaḥ tathā tena idam āvṛtam
Word by word
As fire is covered by smoke, a mirror by dust, and an unborn child by the womb, so is this covered by that.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘this’ is explained in the next verse: it is knowledge, covered by desire.

आवृतं ज्ञानमेतेन ज्ञानिनो नित्यवैरिणा । कामरूपेण कौन्तेय दुष्पूरेणानलेन च ॥ ३९ ॥
āvṛtaṃ jñānametena jñānino nityavairiṇā | kāmarūpeṇa kaunteya duṣpūreṇānalena ca || 39 ||
Padaccheda
आवृतम् ज्ञानम् एतेन ज्ञानिनः नित्य-वैरिणा काम-रूपेण कौन्तेय दुष्पूरेण अनलेन च
āvṛtam jñānam etena jñāninaḥ nitya-vairiṇā kāma-rūpeṇa kaunteya duṣpūreṇa analena ca
Word by word
Knowledge is covered, O son of Kuntī, by this constant enemy of the wise man, in the form of desire, which is hard to fill and insatiable.

Note Śaṅkara: it is the constant enemy of the wise, not of the fool. The wise man knows beforehand that desire is leading him to harm, and so always suffers; the fool looks on desire as a friend while he thirsts, and learns otherwise only when the pain comes. anala: ‘for which there is no “enough”’.

इन्द्रियाणि मनो बुद्धिरस्याधिष्ठानमुच्यते । एतैर्विमोहयत्येष ज्ञानमावृत्य देहिनम् ॥ ४० ॥
indriyāṇi mano buddhirasyādhiṣṭhānamucyate | etairvimohayatyeṣa jñānamāvṛtya dehinam || 40 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियाणि मनः बुद्धिः अस्य अधिष्ठानम् उच्यते एतैः विमोहयति एषः ज्ञानम् आवृत्य देहिनम्
indriyāṇi manaḥ buddhiḥ asya adhiṣṭhānam ucyate etaiḥ vimohayati eṣaḥ jñānam āvṛtya dehinam
Word by word
The senses, the mind and the intellect are said to be its seat. By these it covers knowledge and deludes the embodied one.

Note Śaṅkara: when the enemy's stronghold is known, he is easily destroyed.

तस्मात्त्वमिन्द्रियाण्यादौ नियम्य भरतर्षभ । पाप्मानं प्रजहिह्येनं ज्ञानविज्ञाननाशनम् ॥ ४१ ॥
tasmāttvamindriyāṇyādau niyamya bharatarṣabha | pāpmānaṃ prajahihyenaṃ jñānavijñānanāśanam || 41 ||
Padaccheda
तस्मात् त्वम् इन्द्रियाणि आदौ नियम्य भरत-ऋषभ पाप्मानम् प्रजहिहि एनम् ज्ञान-विज्ञान-नाशनम्
tasmāt tvam indriyāṇi ādau niyamya bharata-ṛṣabha pāpmānam prajahihi enam jñāna-vijñāna-nāśanam
Word by word
Therefore, O best of the Bharatas, control the senses first, and cast off this sinful thing, the destroyer of knowledge and realisation.

Note Śaṅkara: jñāna is the understanding of the Self and the rest gained from scripture and from a teacher; vijñāna is the direct experience of it.

इन्द्रियाणि पराण्याहुरिन्द्रियेभ्यः परं मनः । मनसस्तु परा बुद्धिर्यो बुद्धेः परतस्तु सः ॥ ४२ ॥
indriyāṇi parāṇyāhur indriyebhyaḥ paraṃ manaḥ | manasastu parā buddhir yo buddheḥ paratastu saḥ || 42 ||
Padaccheda
इन्द्रियाणि पराणि आहुः इन्द्रियेभ्यः परम् मनः मनसः तु परा बुद्धिः यः बुद्धेः परतः तु सः
indriyāṇi parāṇi āhuḥ indriyebhyaḥ param manaḥ manasaḥ tu parā buddhiḥ yaḥ buddheḥ parataḥ tu saḥ
Word by word
The senses, they say, are superior; superior to the senses is the mind; superior to the mind is the intellect; and he who is beyond the intellect is the Self.

Note Śaṅkara: the five senses are superior to the gross, outward, limited body, being subtler, more inward and wider in reach. The mind is of the nature of intention and doubt, the intellect of the nature of decision. ‘He’ is the supreme Self, the seer of the intellect, more inward than everything that is seen.

एवं बुद्धेः परं बुद्ध्वा संस्तभ्यात्मानमात्मना । जहि शत्रुं महाबाहो कामरूपं दुरासदम् ॥ ४३ ॥
evaṃ buddheḥ paraṃ buddhvā saṃstabhyātmānamātmanā | jahi śatruṃ mahābāho kāmarūpaṃ durāsadam || 43 ||
Padaccheda
एवम् बुद्धेः परम् बुद्ध्वा संस्तभ्य आत्मानम् आत्मना जहि शत्रुम् महा-बाहो काम-रूपम् दुरासदम्
evam buddheḥ param buddhvā saṃstabhya ātmānam ātmanā jahi śatrum mahā-bāho kāma-rūpam durāsadam
Word by word
Thus knowing the Self, which is beyond the intellect, and steadying the self by the self, slay the enemy, O mighty-armed, in the form of desire, so hard to overcome.

Note Śaṅkara: ‘steadying the self by the self’: settling the mind well by one's own purified mind. durāsada: hard to lay hold of, for its many forms are hard to make out.
